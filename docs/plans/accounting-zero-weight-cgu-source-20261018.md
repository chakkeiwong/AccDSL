# Zero-weight CGU reversal — bounded source investigation

18 October 2026. P17/R03–R04; MC17/19/24/26/29/30/35.
Documentary research only; manuscript and delivered finite methods unchanged.

## Question and baseline

Can an inspected applicable technical source prescribe allocation of a CGU
impairment reversal when every eligible non-goodwill asset has zero carrying
amount but at least two assets have positive reversal capacity? The existing
EU 2023/1803 IAS36.116–125 reading requires changed estimates, excludes goodwill,
uses relative carrying amounts and imposes individual caps. The denominator
in paragraph 122 is zero in this case. Positive capacities alone do not
select an allocation. An individual-asset reversal is a different supported
method and cannot be substituted for a CGU allocation.

Prior inspections: impairment-floors (14 October), impairment-tax-repairs,
finite-method-decisions/corporate and extended-method-exchanges/corporate-residuals
(15–16 October). None records a supported all-zero fallback. Do not repeat the
same primary paragraphs as if newly resolving the question.

## Skeptical audit and assumption review

The actual baseline is IAS36.122's carrying-weight instruction, not a chosen
equal-weight or capacity-weight algorithm. A worked example with positive
carrying amounts cannot answer the zero-denominator question. A third-party
manual is interpretation rather than authoritative standard wording. An
asset-level recoverable amount changes the facts and may change the unit of
account. Historic sources do not establish operative October 2026 law or actual
company applicability. These are the material risks; no empirical metric,
training/default change, numerical campaign or installation is proposed.

Audit passes for a bounded inquiry with explicit unresolved disposition. The
first cheap diagnostic is whether a retrieved technical passage actually
addresses zero carrying weights. Familiar pro-rata wording alone fails this
screen. A search snippet is a lead, not support.

## Evidence contract and intent ledger

Candidate mechanism: a source-supported allocation or an explicit explanation
of why this boundary cannot arise under the source's premises. Expected failure:
new sources repeat ordinary carrying-weight allocation without the boundary.
Primary pass: inspect and preserve the precise technical argument, including
conditions, caps and unit of account. Promotion veto: no explicit boundary
argument, missing facts, incompatible authority or unsupported generalisation.
Continuation veto for this inquiry: inaccessible or corrupted full text; this
does not stop independent manuscript review. Repair trigger: an applicable
argument changes the current unresolved method disposition. Explanatory only:
search hits, source counts, ordinary positive-weight examples and source age.
No conclusion about IFRS compliance, complete current law, actual valuation or
whole-method readiness follows from access or a negative search.

Material choices: retain the declared two-or-more-asset CGU (task hypothesis,
not proof of real applicability); compare exact zero amounts rather than a
numerical epsilon (mathematical boundary, not a rounding policy); inspect a
professional interpretation as a new lead (secondary evidence, not authority).
The failure check for each is an explicit statement of source premises before
using its rule. No replacement allocation is introduced by convenience.
