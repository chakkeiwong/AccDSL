# R03-M — amendment dates, award modifications and net hedges

Documentary methods and exact synthetic checks only; runtime remains held.

Question: how do delayed/conditional inducements, beneficial award changes
and group/net hedges differ from the immediate-conversion, cancellation and
single-item cases already specified? Baseline is those existing procedures;
the required outcome is a complete conditional ordering and a discriminating
case for each named residual, not another generic financing example.

Skeptical audit: do not equate announcing an offer with effective amendment,
amendment with debt extinguishment, or delayed consideration with later-date
measurement. An equity modification retains original grant-date cost and
separately dated incremental cost. A net hedge designation must identify
gross components, timing and admissible risk; it cannot be represented only
by a net amount. Nil positions and layers retain additional requirements.
The audit passes with these constraints; actual legal/valuation facts stay
conditional and no general option-pricing method is inferred from a quote.

Primary criterion: inspected IAS32 AG35/IFRS9 and IFRS2 modification/group
hedge clauses plus exact conditional expense, equity/liability and reserve
reconciliations. Missing legal classification, wrong measurement date,
duplicate grant cost or undisclosed netting vetoes its case and triggers
repair. Exact arithmetic does not prove the supplied valuation or hedge
eligibility. No stochastic ranking or default numerical policy is proposed.

Material assumptions: explicit share/cash obligations, service intervals,
zero taxes, no credit dominance, no excluded forward component and supplied
fair values are case hypotheses. Early checks separate legal discharge,
original/incremental service and gross/net cash. The evidence directory is

## Source-informed audit and bounded case design

The 15 October continuation inspected IAS32.15–27/AG32–35, IFRS9.3.3.1–4/
B3.3.1–6, 6.6.1–6/B6.6.1–16, and IFRS2.B42–B44C in the retained EU2023/1803
text. No new effective-date claim is inferred from that historical compilation.

The initial three examples need two discriminating extensions. A simultaneous
net hedge alone would hide B6.6.9/15's earlier-sale reserve; add a two-period
sale/expense case with separately identified gross amounts. A single beneficial
award example would hide differing service clocks; add additional instruments
with a shorter service period while the original award is unchanged, and a
separate cash-to-equity modification. The latter follows
B44A rather than importing the original equity grant floor.

Inducement examples supply explicit legal release at binding acceptance, with
later administrative share delivery, or fixed cash consideration payable later.
The offer has no binding effect before the supplied approval/acceptance
condition. Conditional instruments that already bind the issuer require their
own IAS32 classification; absence of a completed conversion is not evidence
that no obligation exists. A delayed discharge cannot be inferred merely from
delayed delivery. Unresolved legal effects stay unresolved.

Assumption audit: zero interest/discount, no tax, linear service and constant
second-period FX are convenience choices, used only to isolate timing. Values
before/after amendment are supplied valuation inputs, not an option model.
Forecast sales and expenses are external, individually eligible and highly
probable; actual net risk management, contemporaneous designation and hedge
effectiveness are explicit conditional inputs. Check gross effects and equity
bridges first. A missing or contradictory input vetoes application of that
case, not continued specification. Both fixed cash and share consideration
retain separate classified obligations. No empirical ranking is possible.

Human contract: the reader has seen immediate conversion, grant-date expense
and the cumulative hedge-reserve method. Explain why four conversion dates
differ, why an award amendment has two cost clocks, and why a net hedge still
needs gross and period-specific amounts. Add each subsection beside that prior
method, preserving the existing section hierarchy and all predecessor sources.
