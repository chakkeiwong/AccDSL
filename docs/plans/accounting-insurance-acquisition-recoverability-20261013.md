# Insurance acquisition assets and overlapping impairment tests

13 October 2026. P17/E07 and P18; MC08/10/11/13/16/19/22/28/30/32–35,
G4/G6/G7/G15/G16. Continue from Version 2.55; runtime remains held.

Question: how should acquisition cash flows allocated to expected renewal
groups remain identifiable before group recognition, and how do the two
recoverability tests avoid recognising the same impairment twice? The current
proposal identifies acquisition balances but has no worked B35D example.

Comparator and source: Version 2.55 and the retained December 2022 AASB17
compilation, paragraphs 28A–28F, 32(a), B35A–B35D and relevant presentation
rules. Inspect the complete cross-page provision chain before calculation.
Use ordinary reporting-date recoverability, not transition C14D/C24A as a
shortcut. Historical edition and actual entity applicability stay explicit.

Skeptical audit: the group-level and renewal-level tests overlap; simply adding
their unadjusted deficits double counts impairment. Group cash inflows and
renewal-subset cash inflows must also be consistent. A general allowance to
capitalise arbitrary selling expenses, unsupported renewals, a universal zero
for missing data, or an assumed refund on reversal would be wrong. The earliest
diagnostic is a source-bound two-group table separating allocations, group
net inflows, renewal-subset net inflows and losses already recognised.

Evidence contract: primary success is a sourced conditional allocation and
impairment calculation, component-preserving entries and a bounded reversal
case. Verify exact arithmetic, retained asset totals, no double counting and
unchanged cash at impairment/reversal. Unsupported cost eligibility, allocation,
group identities, inconsistent populations or wrong impairment timing veto
the affected conclusion. A later group recognition must derecognise the related
asset rather than leave it counted separately. Hash/build checks establish
integrity only. No actual insurer recoverability, estimated renewal rate,
current-operative authority or complete acquisition/reinsurance model follows.

Default audit: small currency amounts are authored, not data or numerical
defaults. Assume eligible paid acquisition cash flows, a supported allocation
to two not-yet-recognised groups, impairment indicators, no immediate-expense
election, and expected net inflows measured under the specified rule. Distinguish
the complete future groups from their renewal subsets. For reversal, retain
the original allocation and no intervening group recognition or other movement;
inspect B35B's allocation-update obligations before accepting that assumption.
