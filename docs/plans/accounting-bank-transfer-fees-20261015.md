# R03-I — revolving drawings, fees and transferred assets

15 October 2026. Protected Version 3.06. MC13/15/17/19/20/24–28/30/32–35.
Documentary methods and deterministic examples only.

Question: can the bank design distinguish an undrawn facility from actual
funding, a renewed draw from an old exposure, fees in effective interest from
service fees, and partial derecognition from continued exposure? Comparator:
the earlier full commitment/draw and full-transfer methods, not a product run.

Skeptical audit: a drawn percentage is not automatically the proportion of ECL
that should move to a loan; contractual cancellation rights may not define a
revolving facility's credit-exposure period. A loan fee's label does not decide
whether it is yield or service revenue. Retained cash flows require fair-value
allocation and the transfer/risk/reward/control decision before derecognition;
cash received alone is not proof of a sale. A guarantee is not automatically
the same as retaining substantially all risk.

1. Inspect retained AASB9 3.2.2–3.2.23, B3.2 continuing-involvement guidance,
   B5.4.1–3 and 5.5.20/B5.5.39–40; reconcile existing bank specifications.
2. Specify dates, exposure splits, capacity rollforward, fee purpose and
   classification; include a continuing-involvement conditional method.
3. Derive minimal exact adverse/positive cases, with supplied fair values/ECL
   and explicit transfer eligibility. No actual credit calibration is inferred.
4. Integrate a protected successor, verify preserved prior content, build and
   inspect affected pages; refresh remaining methods and extended exchanges.

Pass requires checked conditional procedures and journal/capacity conservation.
Wrong asset recognition, exposure transfer, fee timing or duplicated provision
vetoes its case and triggers repair. Missing actual contractual facts blocks
application, not independent method work. No numerical ranking, runtime,
current-rule admission or bank completeness follows from these checks.

Defaults: amounts are synthetic scale choices; fair values and losses are
supplied hypotheses; capacity replenishment is a contract-specific rule, not
universal; no-tax cases isolate recognition; price/par ratios are not ECL
probabilities. Earliest check is separate gross loan, allowance, commitment
provision and remaining legal capacity after each draw/repayment.
