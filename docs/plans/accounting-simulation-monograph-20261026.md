simulation without hiding unidentified histories, invalid financing, information
leakage or computational approximation? Proposed approach: joint history
families, explicit event/contract state, actor-specific information, declared
policies, query-driven calculation and distinct exact/approximate results.

## Narrative chain and chapter scope

1. The same statements, different companies: what a simulation must explain.
2. Reconstructing admissible histories: observations, aggregation and constraints.
3. Learning over time: evidence clocks, actor knowledge and persistent ambiguity.
4. Scenarios and decisions: conditioning, intervention and information discipline.
5. Debt as a dated contract: proceeds, promises, pricing, refinancing and default.
6. Equity and ownership: funding, dilution, rights, costs and book/value separation.
7. From economic events to financial statements: timing, recognition and group state.
8. Compact uncertainty: constraints, DAGs, exact abstraction and learning preservation.
9. Approximate inference and simulation: particles, rare events, bounds and failures.
10. Decisions under ambiguity: robust policies, dynamic consistency and information.
11. Numerical solution: equations, complementarity, fixed points and learned solvers.
12. Validation and adversarial cases: evidence, experiments and implementation design.
