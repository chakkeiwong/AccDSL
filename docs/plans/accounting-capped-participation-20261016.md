## Mathematical audit before executing the selector

For E≥f let m be the participating weight and c its residual cap. Ordinary EPS
is max((E−f)/(n+m),(E−f−c)/n). For candidate target EPS r≥0, both branches
are at most r exactly when the same additive sum of (a_i−r*b_i) satisfies
two constant bounds. Selecting each negative term minimises that sum for both
bounds. Therefore an optimum exists among prefixes ordered by gross a_i/b_i;
the fixed-additive OrdinaryEPS theorem is not reused without this derivation.
Test every prefix and every finite subset independently and retain ties rather
than inventing a strict improvement. With E<f and nonnegative gross additions,
each addition makes the negative ordinary EPS less negative, so exclude all.
The calculator requires nonnegative gross adjustments, positive share additions,
fixed rights and all series outstanding for the whole period. This is a finite
local proof; no theorem is claimed for arbitrary state-dependent gross effects.

Before implementation, verify the optimum-prefix argument, exact example
amounts, source passages and the relation to IAS33.44's individual ordering.
If the source/derivation cannot support selection, retain the allocation method
and conditional world calculations and leave selection unresolved; continue
other ready work. A graph of feasible exercise states is not automatically an
IAS33-compliant dilution procedure.

## Source and selector audit completed before freezing

Inspected IAS33.9–15,33–47 and A13–A16 in the retained text. Paragraph35
explicitly requires consequential income/expense changes, such as an employee
profit-sharing expense after conversion. Such consequences can make gross
adjustments nonadditive and are outside the theorem above. They are a concrete
next F1 source-supported repair lead, not permission to treat supplied single-
series adjustments as complete. Actual exercise dependencies remain separate.

For this family, both fractional branches have the same candidate increment a
and denominator increment b. If the current ordinary EPS is r≥0, a candidate
strictly reduces it exactly when a−r*b<0: the previously active branch rises
when that expression is positive, while both branches fall below r when it is
negative. An included candidate leaves the new EPS above a/b. Thus ascending
gross a/b ordering and the updated exact comparison retain the global minimum;
the threshold proof supplies the independent finite-subset characterization.
Here gross a is the complete after-tax adjustment before reallocating earnings
between the two classes, not a fixed increment of ordinary-class earnings.
The algorithm redoes A14 allocation after every assumption. Equality is excluded
under41/43. This is a local derivation applying the inspected allocation and
dilution objectives, not a quotation of a general theorem from IAS33.

The source/math audit passes for that explicit family. It would fail for
state-dependent gross adjustments, changing participation terms, loss-sharing
