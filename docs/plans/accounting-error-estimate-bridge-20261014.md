## Intent, assumptions and evidence contract

Question: how can the same apparent change in a depreciable balance lead to
different current expense and comparative/opening balances because one is new
information and the other is an error in earlier available information?

Comparator: AASB108 operative definitions and application/disclosure paragraphs,
plus existing AASB116 depreciation method. Mechanism: explicit decision facts,
unchanged original reports, comparative-restatement layer, opening-equity bridge
and prospective revised estimate, with separate current ledger adjustments.
First choose the source-supported profile; then fix numerical dates and amounts.

Primary pass: fully reconstructible comparative/current statements and EPS
where included, exact journals/bridges, no duplicated correction, clear tax scope,
and positive/adverse classifications. Missing information about original facts,
authority, materiality, tax or practicability leaves dependent claims unresolved.
Known misuse of current-profit correction or hindsight vetoes that candidate and
triggers repair. Broader unknown actual facts do not stop conditional internal
design. No completed journal alone establishes correct classification.

Explanatory checks: balances, hashes, build and fixture counts. Not concluded:
actual materiality, current IFRS/US/PRC admission, tax advice, actual historical
truth, full accounting-engine conformance or human readiness. No stochastic
ranking or empirical experiment.
