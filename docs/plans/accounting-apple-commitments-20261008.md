## Skeptical audit and research intent

The earlier A18 interpretation correctly limits Note 12 to remaining terms over
one year, but would be misleading if treated as the full-file evidence on
shorter commitments. Annual MD&A reports manufacturing obligations 53.0 billion
(52.9 within twelve months) and other purchases 12.0 (4.1 within twelve months).
The plan is revised to inspect those passages and the interim manufacturing
update, rather than repeating a blanket unquantified-short-term claim. Different
category/term definitions block subtracting the 11.226-billion Note 12 table
from 12.0 and calling the residual short-term obligations without a mapping.

The interim notes have no new lease maturity table. Its MD&A says contractual
cash requirements have not changed materially except in three named categories;
that is neither an exact zero change nor a new lease measurement. Annual 2025
maturities, rolling twelve-month requirements and remaining contract terms are
different time descriptions. Lease cost, payments, ROU assets, discounted
liabilities, undiscounted maturities and uncommenced obligations are distinct.
The disclosed rate is an aggregate description, not a contract discount curve.

Mechanism under test: conditional delivery, new-order and payment timing can
produce the same selected commitment totals with different inventory/payable/
cash consequences. The issuer does not disclose the complete commitment
roll-forward or definitions needed to infer actual deliveries. An example must
therefore state its balance meaning and extinguishment event as assumptions.
A failed arithmetic witness triggers repair, not rejection of the wider design.
No ranking or stochastic comparison is planned. This audit passes with the
expanded MD&A scope and explicit conditional model; the tempting balance-change
equals-cash shortcut is excluded before running calculations.

## Reader task, argument and pre-mortem

The reader is a technically prepared accounting-system designer who has just
seen why revenue does not determine collections. The next problem is whether
liability balances and purchase schedules determine future cash needs. The
new discussion should move from discounted versus undiscounted lease amounts,
to commencement and output uncertainty, to the broader purchase disclosures,
then to delivery and payment histories. This is one mechanism extension,
expected to add roughly three pages to Chapter 2; the exact page count is not
an acceptance criterion. Canonical baseline is the frozen 135-page Version 2.7.

Before the new section, a reader may know that sources need context but not why
the 12.430 lease liability, 14.790 maturity total and 15.6 cash-requirement
disclosure differ. Explain those definitions before arithmetic. Preserve every
Note 12 contingency and supply restriction in the sidecar and summarise their
consequences in the manuscript; do not let tables substitute for the mechanism.

Cold-reader questions: Why do the three lease amounts differ? What is unknown
about the 849 million? Why is 13.8 billion variable cost not next year's fixed
cash requirement? Why does the 11.226 purchase table omit relevant cash needs?
Can the 53.0-to-39.8 decline identify purchases or payments? What additional
facts turn a commitment into inventory, expense, a payable or a cash outflow?

A successful run could still mislead through matched arithmetic over different
populations, conflating costs with cash, equating no material change with zero,
or treating unresolved litigation as zero exposure. Exact qualifiers, original
tables and conditional examples are the cheap early checks. Independent reader
and human acceptance remain pending; contact sheets establish navigation/layout
inspection, not continuous scholarly reading.
