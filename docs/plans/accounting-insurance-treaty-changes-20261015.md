# R03-J — partial treaties, estimate changes and finance presentation

15 October 2026. Protected Version3.07. MC13/15/18–20/24–28/30/32–35.
Documentary method and exact synthetic calculations only.

Question: how should partial/changing reinsurance coverage and changed cash
flow/discount estimates alter distinct issued and held groups? Comparator:
the current full homogeneous50% treaty and fixed-value examples. Supplied risk
adjustments and rates must not masquerade as a complete valuation/process law.

Skeptical audit: treaty recovery percentage cannot be applied to all issued
losses if only some contracts are covered. New contracts and contract changes
are not the same as updated estimates. Financial changes must not enter CSM
as though they were future nonfinancial service. OCI election must preserve
total insurance finance and the required systematic allocation. Held and
issued recognition times, loss components and loss-recovery components are
distinct. A source rule for one GMM branch cannot cover VFA/PAA automatically.

1. Inspect retained IFRS17 reinsurance, modifications, future/past service,
   loss allocation and profit/OCI finance clauses plus application guidance.
2. Specify coverage/date joins, allocation, estimate ordering and admissible
   methods; derive minimum discriminating examples with supplied valuations.
3. Verify issued/held balances, CSM/loss components and total profit/OCI; failed
   recognition target or missing required input vetoes that case and triggers
   repair. It does not invalidate independent methods.
4. Integrate a protected successor; check preservation, build/render and
   refresh remaining groups, complex valuations and closed event exchanges.

Assumptions: group composition, coverage fraction, expected recovery, no-tax
and no-investment-component cases are explicit convenience choices; actual
contract facts are unknown. Rates and RA are supplied and do not claim a
calibration. A pass supports the conditional procedure, not an insurer's actual
statement, full process law, implementation or empirical superiority.
