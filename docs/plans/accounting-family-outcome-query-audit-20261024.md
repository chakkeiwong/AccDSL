# Audit possible and necessary outcomes of complete conditional families

24 October 2026. S2–S4; MC08/09/10/11/16/18/24/26/28/35,
G1/G2/G3/G16 and P17-08.01/.02/.03/.04/.06.

Question: can a query distinguish an outcome that occurs in some history from
one that holds throughout the complete declared family? Existing family
assessments provide feasibility witnesses and coverage; their witnesses do not
give outcome bounds or necessity. Inspect the current exact solver's extra
constraints, optimization and certificates before designing a successor.

Skeptical audit: a successful first witness and equal values at two selected
witnesses cannot prove constancy. Do not manufacture a family by independent
account ranges, infer empty from unvisited branches or say an empty family
establishes a meaningful company outcome. The original complete typed-history
and sequential native compilers, plus independent exact replay, are baselines.
Existing generic solver functionality should be reused after its behavior is
inspected; do not add another solver or change frozen parents.

Research intent: distinguish existence, impossibility, necessity and unknown
for an explicitly typed account predicate under the complete family. Candidate
mechanism is shared-family intersection with the predicate and its negation.
Expected failure: first-witness certainty, missing intermediate constraints,
wrong account/date types or resource exhaustion mistaken for an answer.
Promotion requires exact original replay of witnesses/counterexamples and
checked infeasibility of all branches for the corresponding universal result.
Any lost parent constraint or unproved global exhaustion vetoes promotion and
triggers a successor repair. A corrupt baseline is a continuation veto; a
failed query candidate is a repair trigger. Timing and counts explain execution
only. No probabilities, source admission, actual future prediction, general
company completeness or solver superiority may be concluded.

Defaults audit: use explicit inherited finite budgets as engineering ceilings,
not scientific thresholds. Record exact comparator and family identity, known
or incomplete premise state, endpoint strictness and fixed event topology.
The first diagnostic should show terminal inventory60 necessary within the
sequence, intermediate inventory65 possible but not necessary, and an empty
unfunded family distinct from a necessary claim. Examine an open endpoint and
partial-budget case before any general outcome interface is implemented.
