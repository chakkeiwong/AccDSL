## Pre-calculation skeptical audit and intent

Question: when should a shorter useful life change only current/future expense,
and when does it expose an error requiring restated comparative/opening equity?
Comparator: the original historical information and inspected AASB108.5/32–53,
116.43–62A and 112 tax allocation; not a target ending asset balance.

The initial candidate discovery date of 1 January 2025 was rejected: 2024
statements need not yet have been authorized. The repaired chronology fixes
authorization on 31 March 2025 and discovery/new information on 1 April 2025.
No issued 2025 interim statements exist. Current-quarter catch-up is separate
from the correction of prior annual periods. New estimate information concerns
conditions newly arising on 1 April, not conditions at the 2024 reporting date.
This does not assert a separate IAS10 subsequent-event classification.

Audit passes for a conditional historical method after EPS scope capture.
Arithmetic, a balanced journal or a successful PDF cannot determine original
availability, materiality, remaining life, tax law or practicability. These are
express assumptions with missing-input refusals. The primary criterion is
reconstructible independent book and comparative paths to identical corrected
balances, with current expense and equity origins kept distinct. Wrong dates,
hindsight, duplicated correction or an unsupported tax origin veto the case
and trigger repair. Missing actual-company facts veto actual-company admission,
not this synthetic comparison. No stochastic inference or performance claim.

## Fixed facts and assumption audit

Use currency units and one for-profit private Tier 1 entity. No public shares
or public-issuance filing; no voluntary EPS. Initial capital is 200; cash
purchases an administrative asset for 120, available for use on 1 January
2023. Zero residual, one component, no impairment/revaluation/restoration,
no capitalization of depreciation into inventory; straight-line consumption.
The entity receives 50 annual cash operating income before depreciation.
No other working capital, distributions or comprehensive-income items.
Original annual depreciation was 10 in both 2023 and 2024; statements were
authorized on 31 March of the following year. One comparative year, 2024,
is displayed in the 2025 report; 2023 remains the opening bridge.

Estimate history: twelve years was reasonable using all then-available
information, including each annual review. On 1 April 2025 a newly enacted
operating restriction imposes retirement at 31 December 2028. It did not exist
and was not anticipated at the earlier dates. For the teaching case assume
recoverability is independently satisfied; a shorter life does not itself
complete impairment analysis. Remaining life is 45 months.

Error history: a reliable original approved schedule already specified six
years from 1 January 2023. It was available and reasonably obtainable for both
authorizations; an input/transcription failure used twelve years instead.
The mistake is material, every comparative amount is practicably measurable,
and six years remains appropriate. This is a different fact history, not
an alternative accounting election on the same fully known facts.

Tax law is a supplied conditional schedule: deduction 20 annually in 2023–28,
enacted 25% rate for all relevant recovery periods, current tax paid on the
following 31 March, no uncertainty or other differences. Initial book/tax
bases are both 120. Recovery is through use, with no initial-recognition
exception issue or DTA. Missing law/rate/recovery inputs produce tax-unsupported,
never a zero-tax default. Cash flow categories are illustrative under the
already stated company convention, not a new presentation-standard claim.

## Procedure, state and expected consequences

Classify first. Persist source/time/authorization facts and classification
decision separately from numbers. Unknown original availability, materiality
or treatment keeps classification unresolved. Policy changes use a distinct
route; 108.35 is not an error-versus-estimate fallback. Missing local evidence
cannot establish impracticability. Proved period-specific or cumulative
impracticability needs the distinct 108.44/45 route and further specification;
do not fabricate a full restatement.

Estimate: retain original 2023/2024 statements and the 2025 opening. Charge
3/12 of annual 10 to March; allocate remaining 97.5 over 45 months, giving
19.5 for April–December and 22 for 2025. Future complete years 2026–28 each
carry 26; the four future allocations exhaust 97.5.

Error: correct comparative 2024 by using annual 20, and restate 1 January
2024 opening for the 2023 shortfall. Cumulative prior-year book correction
at 1 January 2025 is accumulated depreciation +20, DTL -5, retained earnings
-15. The current system posts an April discovery adjustment with attribution
to opening equity; it does not backdate its ingestion or rewrite old books.
At discovery, also correct first-quarter current depreciation from 2.5 to5
through current expense. Its 0.625 deferred-tax benefit is current P&L.
Then record April–December depreciation 15. No second charge to profit for
the cumulative prior-year 20.

The book bridge has a stable identity containing entity, affected periods,
original-close identity, accepted treatment and target restatement. Exact
retry returns its receipt before freshness checks; same identity/different
payload is a conflict; a new identifier cannot duplicate the same target
correction. A comparative view is derived presentation with no ledger-post
permission. Old publications and committed books remain retrievable with
the explicit superseded/wrong classification. This is a semantic design
composition using existing contracts, not a new portable OperationRequest
encoding or runtime implementation.
