# PPE preparation cost, sample goods and available-use depreciation

MC02/04/07/13–16/19/20/28/30/32–35; P17-04/E07 with P18 integration.
This is a documentary method and exact synthetic example, not runtime behavior.

The equipment and the goods produced while preparing it are separate assets.
Admit the equipment's recognition facts, cash-price components, direct purpose,
date and cost model. A cost-population record gives each receipt/resource an
identity, amount, allocation and source. AASB116.16–17 admits necessary delivery,
installation and functional testing;19–22 excludes the stated startup/incidental
costs. Paragraph20A sends saleable test-goods measurement to AASB102 and their
proceeds and cost to profit under the applicable standards. Do not count one
resource both as machine testing and as goods production. A genuinely shared
resource needs an evidenced allocation whose shares sum to its cost; it is not
automatically assigned wholly to both or equally between them.

The selected sources are historical AASB116 compilation6 (annual starts
2023–2026), AASB102 compilation3 (2022–2026), AASB15 compilation8 (starts before
1July2026 from2023) and AASB107 compilation5 (2024–2025). Their intersection
includes the synthetic calendar2024 year. Official copies, application pages,
selected passages and amendment details are in the accompanying source manifest.
This does not establish current IFRS, US GAAP or ASBE compliance.

The manufacturer starts with cash/equity300. It pays100 for equipment,5 for
delivery,12 for necessary installation and3 for separate functional testing.
Recognition is admitted: benefits are probable and cost reliably measured.
Sample materials4 and contracted conversion2 create the sole saleable batch;
there are no other production overheads in these facts. A separate abnormal
production waste1 is expensed under AASB102.16(a), together with staff training3
and advertising2. All amounts are paid. Cost6 is below NRV8; no completion or
selling costs remain. One enforceable ordinary-product contract meets
AASB15.9, fixes price8, and transfers the entire batch's title, possession,
acceptance and control on30June; cash is collected then. There are no other
promises, returns, concessions or financing components in this contract.

Debit PPE120/credit cash120. Debit inventory6/credit cash6, and debit period
expense6/credit cash6. At sale debit cash8/credit revenue8; separately debit
cost of sales6/credit inventory6. Retain the sample batch's cost and sale
identity after inventory reaches zero. Cash is176, PPE120, inventory0 and
pretax loss4 before depreciation. Netting the samples' margin2 against PPE
would wrongly produce cost118 and omit their income and expense.

The equipment is capable of intended operation on1July. It remains idle to
31December; no later production occurs in this case. Even consumption including
obsolescence supports straight-line depreciation over60months, residual0.
AASB116.55 starts depreciation on availability and permits no idle-period
suspension for this method; a properly admitted usage method can have zero
charge at zero production. The monthly convention yields120/60×6=12. Debit
depreciation expense12/credit accumulated depreciation12. PPE closes at108,
profit is8−6−6−12=−16, and closing equity300−16=284 equals cash176+PPE108.
There are no liabilities, tax or OCI in this explicitly pretax slice.

Cash-flow presentation retains the same economic classifications: investing
outflow120 for PPE, operating receipt8 less goods payments6 and period
payments6, hence CFO−4. Indirect CFO is profit−16 plus depreciation12=−4.
Financing cash is0 because capital300 is already in the opening balance.
Closing cash300−120−4=176. Income8 and goods expense6 remain separate in profit.

Balanced adverse variants demonstrate why arithmetic alone is inadequate.
Netting goods cash into PPE gives118, depreciation11.8, net PPE106.2 and
profit−17.8; total cash still176 but operating/investing classifications become
−6/−118. Waiting until productive use omits depreciation12 and overstates PPE
and equity12. Capitalizing excluded period costs gives126, depreciation12.6,
profit−10.6 and equity289.4. Each can balance; all differ from the admitted
recognition/measurement target.

Missing availability evidence leaves the depreciation start unresolved; a
first invoice or first profitable month does not supply it. Missing overheads,
overlapping resource assignments or an uncertain allocation block exact cost
admission. Missing control evidence blocks the claimed sale, and receipt of
cash alone may require a contract liability under the existing method. These
are documentary admission obligations, not claims that production code already
rejects these inputs. Real facts about taxes, restoration, grants, impairment,
component lives or financing need their own existing methods and authority.
