\section{A control history must fit the reporting method}
\label{sim:control-group-scope}

A supplied history of control can answer the continuity question without yet supplying a method for consolidated reporting. The existing selected group-period calculation supports a stable perimeter and full ownership. A conditional history must therefore be checked against that scope before the simulator uses the calculation. Matching opening and closing control edges cannot replace the intervening history.

The checked connection carries the original history request, actor, exact interval and source identities into the group-report request. It identifies the same parent and subsidiary in both. It also requires an explicit relation between the history's reporting basis and the book-composition measurement basis. Similar labels do not establish that those two premises mean the same thing.

Time precision matters here too. The book period uses calendar dates, whereas the control history can use exact instants. The reference requires an explicit fixed-offset calendar interpretation and compares the resulting midnight boundaries exactly. A partial-day acquisition cannot be rounded into whole-period control merely because the annual report's date fields contain no time of day.

The outcome depends on the whole interval. Missing coverage, conflicting evidence or unresolved actor availability leave the control-history requirement unresolved. Continuous absence of control stays outside this parent--subsidiary reporting route, even if an ownership record says 100 per cent. A gain or loss requires changing-perimeter methods; this includes a loss followed by regain when the opening and closing assessments are identical. Continuous control with partial ownership requires non-controlling-interest methods beyond the present full-ownership reference.

Only continuous supplied control together with a separate full-ownership premise fits the existing method's control and ownership scope. Even that result is a candidate for further report checks. It supplies no missing legal-book report, acquisition measurement or accounting authority, and the scope check itself produces no consolidated statement. The original report can still refuse a request whose control scope fits but whose other inputs are absent or incompatible.

This separates two sources of failure. If a valid unchanged-period fixture is refused, the existing report or its inputs need diagnosis. If a history contains a mid-period acquisition and the stable-perimeter report refuses it, the refusal exposes a missing financial method. The next work must specify acquisition or loss accounting, dated member and flow allocation, ownership and non-controlling interests, and the required measurements. A source-history check cannot manufacture those amounts or authorize a journal merely because its identities and dates match.\footnote{The conditional history-to-group connection, complete expected routes and unchanged/changed-perimeter baseline checks are retained in \path{data/accounting-master/control-group-boundary-20261027/}. The selected comparison covers 23 cases and 15 refusal controls. Every scope result withholds source, accounting, posting and report authorization; it is preparation for financial-method composition.}
