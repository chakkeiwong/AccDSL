\section{Writing, revising and dating the company path}
\label{sec:company-path-authoring}

The interrupted second year creates two practical questions. What can another
implementation recover from that result, and what must an author supply to
continue it? The completed first year establishes opening cash and equity for
the second. An unknown payroll amount leaves those openings known and preserves
the operations already applied. It supplies neither a second-year closing nor
a third-year opening. An exchange must retain that distinction even when all
three years were requested together.

Each available annual outcome therefore identifies its exact input, predecessor,
applied operations and report. A complete year also carries its conditional
closing memory. An interrupted year has no such closing; the remaining periods
stay explicitly unexecuted. In the payroll example, the first eleven second-year
operations remain, while the 27 annual-result lines are unresolved. The two
known opening lines are not erased. A supplier-payment failure has a different
stopping reason and preserves its own shorter prefix.

Supplying the missing payroll amount creates a new assumption branch. Its
revision record names the parent and child inputs, the affected year, the exact
replacement and the reason. Comparing the full input bodies establishes that
no other year or source opening changed. Earlier annual results remain
identical; the affected suffix is recomputed from the original historical
opening. Restoring the original payroll input reproduces the complete baseline
path. This is an explicit repair of a conditional scenario, without changing
what the company historically reported or claiming a durable database commit.

Content identities alone are insufficient for that conclusion. A deliberately
changed cash report can be given internally consistent new hashes while still
failing to follow its operations. The exchange distinguishes binding checks
from trusted local replay. The first establishes which bytes refer to one
another; the second recomputes their financial consequences. The extended
example preserves the preceding complete evidence and exchanges five paths,
five revisions and thirteen available annual outcomes, including both failures.

\paragraph{A collection rule changes a different instruction.}
Consider the second-year price revision from 11 to 12. Each of the two deliveries
now recognises a receivable of 192. The fixed input still collects 79.2 per claim
in each of two instalments, or 158.4 in total. Each claim therefore leaves 33.6
uncollected. This is the fixed-allocation result described in the preceding
section, and it remains available unchanged.

An author may instead specify that 90 percent of each named invoice should be
collected in two equal instalments. This optional rule generates 172.8 per
claim, split into payments of 86.4, and leaves 19.2 uncollected. Relative to the
fixed amounts, each of four allocations increases by 7.2. Cash consequently
rises by 28.8 and receivables fall by 28.8. Revenue, cost, profit and the remaining
annual report lines are unchanged. The rule alters settlement, not recognition.
The 90 percent fraction is inherited from the illustrative example; it is not
an estimate of how Northstar's customers would actually pay.

The policy names the parent path, year, deliveries, claims, collection events,
fraction and instalment shares. Materialization creates a separate input path
and records every old and generated allocation, invoice total and remainder.
It changes no unspecified event date, older claim or following-year instruction.
Unknown inputs remain unresolved. Another collection against a targeted claim
outside these named events is unsupported until its priority and effect on the
remaining amount have been specified. A generated path still needs accounting
execution: a valid policy formula does not establish sufficient cash for all
subsequent payments.

\paragraph{The text names the operation and its claim.}
The corresponding authoring subset preserves the typed operation path rather
than hiding that settlement decision in a formula. Its outer declaration names
the profile, company path and scenario assumption. An initial-year block gives
the source opening, claims, assets and annual inputs. Continuation blocks give
the following years' choices and events; they cannot replace the carried opening
with newly authored balances. Every field required by the typed profile must
be declared, including explicit markers for arrays.

For the price-12 scenario, one delivery and one allocation in its first fixed
collection are written as follows:
\begin{lstlisting}
operation delivery-1-a delivery 2026-01-03T09:00:00Z 16/1 12/1 true sales-ar-2026-a;
operation collection-1-a collection 2026-01-04T09:00:00Z;
allocate collection-1-a sales-ar-2026-a 396/5;
\end{lstlisting}
The listing wraps long lines for display; each declaration occupies one line
in the executable text. The allocation names both the
previously declared collection and the receivable it settles. Other claims and
operations still require their own declarations. Materializing the illustrative
90 percent rule changes this allocation to \(432/5\), or 86.4. Editing the
delivery price alone does not regenerate it.

Each declaration ends with a semicolon. Quoted text preserves spaces and
punctuation. Amounts use canonical reduced fractions, truth values use
\code{true}, \code{false} or \code{unknown}, and missing numeric values or
instants remain \code{unknown}. The sequence of operation declarations preserves
precedence at equal instants. Allocation lines attach to an already declared
settlement; they neither create a claim nor reorder events. Duplicate operation
or allocation identities and initial-opening declarations inside a continuation
are rejected.

The complete baseline text contains all 63 operations over three years.
Six examples, including both interrupted paths and the policy-generated path,
lower to the exact saved typed inputs and reproduce their reports. Parsing
still answers a narrower question than accounting execution. A well-typed
negative purchase quantity is preserved so that execution can reject its
economic meaning. The text parser evaluates no expressions, executes no shell
commands and opens no source file named by the input. General expressions,
endogenous branching and wider accounting profiles remain separate language
requirements. The following extension supplies a bounded family wrapper and
pure expressions for the declared collection policy.

\paragraph{The assumptions also have an information date.}
The source cutoff in the preceding section does not date the collection rule
or other future inputs. An information declaration must identify the exact
path header and each annual definition, then distinguish when those bytes were
authored, publicly available, ingested and accepted. Changing one year changes
that component's identity; its old timing evidence cannot admit the replacement.
Unchanged years can retain their own evidence. This whole-year unit is coarse:
it deliberately avoids claiming that an earlier subset admits a later complete
definition.

The synthetic clock example authors the assumptions at 09:00 UTC on 6 March
2025, ingests them at 10:00 and accepts them at 11:00. Public availability is
known only to the calendar day 7 March. A system-as-of query at 11:00 on 6 March
can pass the synthetic ingestion and acceptance tests even though a public
information query cannot yet do so. During 7 March, public availability remains
unresolved; the next midnight is late enough for that day-precision declaration.
Both views still require an eligible source opening.

Chronology matters independently of the cutoff. Exact equal timestamps can
satisfy nondecreasing authoring, ingestion and acceptance order. Overlapping
calendar-day bounds may leave the order unresolved. A publication day ends
just before the following midnight, so authorship at that midnight cannot
precede publication within the earlier day. Missing or untrusted observations
cannot be repaired by assigning an early date in the declaration.

These times are authored test clocks, not observed historical records. Passing
their checks establishes the selector's behavior on the example, without
admitting a historical forecast or authenticating an author's identity. The
separate settlement policy also requires its own information evidence; the
path's dates cannot supply it automatically. The present design therefore
retains archival synthetic simulation while making the missing temporal
requirements explicit.

\path{company-information-20261023},
\path{company-policy-20261023} and
