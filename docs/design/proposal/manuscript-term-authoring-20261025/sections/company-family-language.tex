\section{Keeping company choices and their consequences together}
\label{sec:company-family-language}

The fixed collection amounts and the 90-percent rule answer different questions
about the same company opening. An analyst comparing them needs each choice's
cash, receivables, profit and later balances together. Selecting favorable
amounts independently would describe a company that no stated choice produces.
A named scenario family therefore retains the entire input path and result of
each member, including its unresolved years.

The bounded family contains the baseline, two price revisions, the unknown
payroll path, the supplier-payment failure and the policy-generated path. Four
members complete their accounting; two remain partial. Every member has the
same historical source, opening record, entity, book, currency and reporting
periods. Opening claims, asset memory, drawn facility balance and debt principal
must also agree. Future instructions may differ. These checks establish the
common declared opening; they do not reconstruct an unobserved real history.

The text places an existing company path inside a named member block. Typed
constructors embed the same path record. A family query returns each member's
complete report vector for the requested year and retains its path and result
identities. If a member never reached that year, its response says why the
report is unavailable. The full family result also retains the preceding and
following years, so a later amount cannot be detached from the path that
generated it.

These are authored exogenous scenarios. Six names imply neither equal
probabilities nor exhaustive coverage of possible futures. Two declarations
that produce identical results can remain separately named assumptions without
acquiring extra probability mass. An empty authored selection means that the
author supplied no members; it does not establish that no history is feasible.
Historical reconstruction and justified probability laws retain their separate
requirements from Chapter~\ref{ch:math}.

\paragraph{A policy expression has units and dependencies.}
The collection fraction can now be written as a calculation rather than supplied
only as a final number. In the ordinary scenario it is one minus a declared
discount of one tenth. A second branch supplies four fifths. Both branches use
two equal instalments. The guard is an explicit Boolean assumption: it does
not infer whether customers will behave normally.

The expression graph contains exact rational or Boolean inputs, literals,
references and pure arithmetic, comparison and conditional operations. Text
and typed construction preserve the same graph. Static analysis checks all
references, operator arities, cycles and types, including both conditional
arms. The resulting fraction and instalment shares pass through the existing
settlement policy's domain and claim-allocation checks. The expressions do
not create new collection dates or decide which contracts should exist.

Numeric types carry units and an explicit context. A quantity of goods times
a price in dollars per good gives dollars; a quantity cannot be added to cash.
Addition and comparison require matching dimensions and context. A
context-free dimensionless fraction can scale a company amount. Multiplication
does not silently convert currencies or combine different entities. A ratio
of contextual amounts retains that context and cannot automatically become
an unrestricted policy parameter.

For Northstar's two revised invoices, the total amount is 384. The ordinary
fraction of nine tenths collects 345.6; four fifths collects 307.2. The latter
therefore reduces cash by 38.4 and increases receivables by the same amount,
with unchanged profit. The exact arithmetic agrees with the generated path's
full accounting result. The example establishes the consequence of the two
stated collection assumptions, without estimating which assumption is more
likely.

A known guard evaluates only its selected arm, after both arms have passed
static checks. An unknown guard leaves the policy output unresolved; it is
not false. This bounded evaluator does not prove that equal-looking arms are
symbolically equivalent. A valid but undefined calculation in an unselected
arm is not evaluated, while division by known zero in a selected calculation
is invalid. Missing declarations differ from declared unknown values. The
former are invalid references; the latter remain visible dependencies.

Each output records all declared input dependencies and the subset actually
evaluated. Thus an unselected branch's assumptions remain inspectable even
when they do not determine the current value. Logical \code{and} and
\code{or} conservatively propagate unknown operands in this subset; they do
not introduce implicit short-circuit or SQL missing-value semantics. General
functions, date arithmetic, source maps, richer types and endogenous event
scheduling remain language work.

\paragraph{Reuse a calculation without changing its meaning.}
The collection example can express the ordinary fraction through a reusable
function: given a dimensionless discount \(d\), return \(1-d\). A second
function selects the ordinary or stress fraction from the explicit Boolean
assumption; a third returns the equal instalment share \(1/2\). Calling
these functions with the existing inputs yields the same \(9/10\), \(4/5\)
and \(1/2\) values as the earlier expression graph. Their reuse changes
how an assumption is authored, not how it is estimated.

The optional \code{CompanyFunctions/1} declaration names each function's
ordered, explicitly typed parameters, return type, local expression nodes
and output. A call supplies argument-node identities in parameter order.
Function bodies can read their parameters and literals; they cannot capture
an undeclared company input. Expansion gives each call fresh local identities
and lowers its operations to the existing expression evaluator. Parameter
and return types must agree exactly, including units and company context.
Every definition receives static checks, even if no selected output uses it.

Evaluation follows the existing graph's demand for values. An unused argument
does not have to be evaluated, although its references and types must be valid.
For example, a function that ignores one argument can return a known constant
even when that argument contains a well-typed division by zero. If the result
uses that division, it is invalid. Likewise, the stress collection branch can
remain known when its unused discount is unknown. Each call retains all
declared argument dependencies separately from the dependencies that reach its
result; output evaluation records the inputs actually used.

The checked comparison preserves complete three-year results for the ordinary
and stress policies. Moving from \(9/10\) to \(4/5\) reduces cash by
\(192/5\) and raises receivables by \(192/5\), with unchanged profit.
An unknown selected discount or guard prevents policy materialization. A
negative generated fraction is rejected by the existing collection policy's
domain checks. Compilation supplies neither a collection assumption nor a
missing fact.

The bounded reference accepts at most 32 functions, 16 parameters per
function and 256 source nodes per scope. Call depth is at most 16; both
local and expanded dependency depth are at most 64. Validation and final
expansion together permit at most 2,048 emitted nodes and 2,048 scope visits,
including calls that merely pass an argument through. Serialized requests
are limited to 262,144 UTF-8 bytes. These are explicit convenience limits,
not measured scalability claims. Cycles, recursion and exceeded limits are
refused; there is no implicit numerical solution or event scheduling.

The function request, compiler and generated legacy graph retain separate
identities and definition/call origins. Their information evidence does not
inherit the earlier policy's dates. This increment provides typed declarations;
matched textual function syntax, parser spans, richer types and module dispatch
remain further language work. Its 23 focused methods, 79 predecessor checks
and 46 policy/accounting comparisons are retained in
\path{data/accounting-master/company-functions-20261025/}.


\paragraph{Locate each declaration in the source that was supplied.}
The matched text extension now expresses these same bounded functions. For
example, the ordinary collection fraction is written as follows:
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\scriptsize,breaklines=true]
function complement;
parameter discount '{"context":null,"dimensions":{},"kind":"number"}';
returns '{"context":null,"dimensions":{},"kind":"number"}';
param_ref d discount;
literal one '{"context":null,"dimensions":{},"kind":"number"}' 1/1;
node r sub one d;
result r;
end_function;
\end{lstlisting}
The quoted JSON type denotes a number with no dimensions and no company
context. The parameter reference binds the local name \code{d} to the declared
discount; \code{r} subtracts it from one. The result declaration identifies
the returned node, and \code{end\_function} closes the function. In the
surrounding program, \code{call regular complement discount;} supplies the
main program's discount node and names the resulting call \code{regular}.
Input declarations and the other functions remain necessary for the full
collection policy. The displayed wrapping does not introduce extra source
lines: each declaration occupies one physical line and ends in a semicolon.

Function parameters precede the return type and body; a result ends the body.
Blocks cannot nest, but a call can name a function declared later. Main inputs,
nodes and outputs use the earlier expression notation, within a
\code{CompanyFunctions/1} program ending in \code{end\_program}.
The parser rejects duplicate names and JSON keys, incomplete blocks, unsupported
declarations and semantic errors reported by the pinned typed compiler.
Quoted command-looking characters are text: parsing performs no shell
expansion, arithmetic evaluation or event generation. A validly typed division
by zero can therefore compile and later fail when its value is requested.

Each accepted declaration records its path within the typed request, physical
line and exact original UTF-8 byte interval. Byte offsets are zero-based and
end-exclusive; line and Unicode-codepoint columns are one-based, with the ending
column exclusive. The slice includes the terminal semicolon and excludes
surrounding spaces, tabs and the line ending. Its hash and the complete source
hash bind these locations to the supplied bytes. This distinction matters when
non-ASCII names use several bytes per character, or a source uses CRLF rather
than LF. Both line-ending forms are accepted without Unicode normalization.

Canonical rendering uses LF and deterministic quoting. It reproduces the same
typed request but can have a different source hash and different locations.
Consequently, original locations are never assigned to re-rendered text.
The generated expression's function and node origins can be joined to these
declarations; token-level debugging and runtime exception locations remain
separate work. Source locations also supply no evidence of when a policy was
written, published or used.

The text frontend bounds source size to 524,288 bytes, each physical line to
16,384 bytes and accepted declarations to 12,000 before the typed compiler's
own limits apply. These are scoped engineering limits. Eighteen focused test
methods, 23 typed-function regression methods, 79 expression checks and 285
source/financial comparisons cover six policy cases, preserving complete
three-year results where available and unresolved or rejected paths elsewhere.
The exact examples, declaration slices and financial comparisons are retained
in \path{data/accounting-master/company-function-text-20261025/}. The checked
extension still does not supply dates, polymorphism, module dispatch or
endogenous economic decisions.


\paragraph{Let the next sale depend on the stock that remains.}
A reusable calculation becomes a company decision when it reads the current
book and proposes an economic event. Consider a hypothetical company holding
two units of a homogeneous, divisible inventory lot. The lot has a carrying
amount and tax base of USD~20, and cash is USD~452. Management's authored rule
keeps half a unit in reserve and sells the lesser of declared demand and stock
above that reserve. Demand, the reserve and the price are scenario assumptions;
the example constructor requires them to be nonnegative. The rule applies only
when its separate Boolean switch is enabled.

With demand of one unit on each of three successive decision dates and a price
of USD~15 per unit, the first decision sells one unit. The second reads the
remaining unit and sells only half. The third reads the remaining half unit
and proposes no sale. Total proceeds are USD~22.50, released inventory cost is
USD~15 and profit before any subsequent tax assessment is USD~7.50. Cash becomes
USD~474.50, while the retained half unit carries USD~5. Reusing the opening stock
for all three decisions would give the wrong quantities. These exact fractions
provide an independent check on the state read at each decision.

The bounded reference \texttt{StateDecisionEvent/1} separates this choice from
accounting for its consequences. It receives the complete conditional annual
book, its content hash, a decision date, a typed function program and explicit
input bindings. A binding can read the selected lot's current quantity or the
book's cash balance, or supply a scenario value with provenance and an
availability date. Quantity, currency and company context must agree with the
program's declared types. Every program input starts as an unknown placeholder;
the binding determines which value the rule may read.

A value dated after the decision, or with unknown availability, stays unknown
for evaluation while its original record remains inspectable. The model itself
also needs a declared availability date. These dates express the authored
scenario; they do not establish when a real company knew a fact. The caller
must further state that no intervening economic event is omitted between the
snapshot and the decision. The reference neither verifies that premise against
disclosures nor discovers event dates. Decision and sale share one calendar
date within the current fiscal period; explicit order distinguishes decisions
on the same day.

The program supplies three outputs: whether to act, the units sold and the
proceeds. The compiler checks the entire program before evaluation. A false
decision guard leaves the book exactly unchanged and need not read an unused
future price. An unknown guard leaves the decision unresolved. Only a true
guard requires the units and proceeds; unknown values remain unresolved, while
invalid arithmetic refuses the proposal. This distinction prevents missing
information from silently becoming a zero sale.

A known proposal still needs the existing sale method's six findings:
proportional allocation selected, homogeneous units, divisible units, unchanged
measurement basis, completed performance, and tax base equal to carrying amount.
The decision rule supplies none of these findings by itself. Application
recomputes the complete proposal against the same state and request, then
dispatches it to the unchanged native sale transition. A stale snapshot or an
altered proposal refuses even if the caller updates its hash. Unknown findings
remain unresolved, false findings remain unsupported, and a refused proposal
is retained separately from its accounting outcome.

In the checked single and repeated cases, the proposed operations, complete
native transitions and financial statement fields equal those produced by
manually authored sales. The report describes the recorded decision prefix and
retains the annual interface's unresolved questions. A sequence stops at its
first refusal, preserving earlier successful decisions, no-actions and remaining
requests. Its decision cursor can advance through a no-action even though the
book's last event date does not change; a later request cannot move behind that
cursor. The reference accepts at most 64 decisions and 32 input bindings per
decision. These are engineering limits, not a capacity claim.

Twenty-eight focused test methods, 14 native-accounting regression methods,
23 function regression methods and 64 exact comparisons across eight cases are
retained in \path{data/accounting-master/state-decision-events-20261025/}.
They check this composition of an authored rule and a conditional accounting
method. The example leaves demand estimation, general financing decisions,
simultaneous choices, event scheduling and actual historical execution open.
No database commit, complete fiscal event population or forecast accuracy
follows from the successful sequence.


\paragraph{Bind a scheduled decision when its date arrives.}
The preceding sequence fixes the state read by each decision, but its caller
must supply the corresponding future book hashes. An authored simulation should
instead supply its starting book and a calendar, then calculate those later
states as it proceeds. The bounded \texttt{StateDecisionSchedule/1} extension
does this by materializing each decision request only at its turn. Its expected
state hash is the hash of the current book, after earlier successful events.

A schedule supplies a named rule, explicit priority and either a list of
calendar dates or a start date, exclusive end date and positive integer number
of days between occurrences. For example, daily decisions from 2 January 2027
up to, but excluding, 5 January produce the same three decisions as the explicit
dates 2, 3 and 4 January. Calendar arithmetic includes leap days. It introduces
no month-end adjustment, business calendar, timezone conversion or contractual
day-count convention. All occurrences must fall within the current fiscal
period and no earlier than the book's last event.

Different decisions on the same day need distinct explicit priorities. An
unresolved tie stops scheduling before economic execution; rearranging the input
array does not choose an order. Each decision and sale receives a stable identity
derived from the declared root and schedule names and its occurrence number.
The complete schedule also retains its own content identity, so an equivalent
list of dates and recurrence can produce identical execution while remaining
different authored documents. Every declared policy is statically checked
before the first event, including a later policy that an earlier refusal might
otherwise prevent the runner from reaching.

The checked calendar reproduces the manually assembled stock/reserve sequence:
one unit sold, then half a unit, then no action. Cash is USD~474.50. The selected
lot retains USD~5 of carrying amount, while the inventory control totals USD~65:
the opening USD~80 also included USD~60 outside that lot. With a zero reserve,
the same rule sells both units over two decisions and cash reaches USD~482;
the next decision observes depletion and takes no action. Distinguishing a
selected lot from the company's inventory control is necessary when checking
the full balance sheet.

An unresolved or refused decision stops the sequence with the exact current
book, earlier outcomes and pending calendar retained. The stop record includes
the materialized request, its state hash and the proposed event where available.
A no-action advances the decision cursor without changing the book's event
date. The scheduler accepts up to 32 schedules and 64 total decisions; the
existing request checker also bounds serialized size, nesting and structure.
These limits keep the reference finite and do not establish production capacity.

Eighteen focused scheduler methods, 28 direct decision regression methods and
49 exact comparisons across five cases preserve manually assembled requests,
complete native outcomes and all financial report fields. The evidence is in
\path{data/accounting-master/state-decision-schedule-20261025/}. The calendar
retains the explicit premise that no external economic event is omitted.
Combining decisions with separately supplied financing, tax, purchase or period
events requires those events' own timing and accounting rules. General calendar
generation, historical completeness and durable restart remain separate work.


\paragraph{Declare the company book before applying its methods.}
The preceding native examples use a fixed company context and chart of accounts.
A reusable calendar does not remove that restriction. A company may keep its cash
under account \texttt{101}, another under \texttt{bank.alpha}; their contract
records may also have different structures. The optional
\texttt{CompanyBook/1} reference makes those choices explicit before a method
reads balances or proposes an entry. It declares the entity, book, currency,
conditional case, period, account classifications and selected cash account.
Balances use the existing debit-positive convention and sum to zero. Each scalar
in the method's memory has a declared type and an address within a closed schema.

The payment example separates the accounting relation from those local names.
One synthetic company maps the cash and liability roles to
\texttt{bank.alpha} and \texttt{note.alpha}, with the outstanding amount under
\texttt{loan}. Another maps the roles to \texttt{101} and \texttt{230}, with the
amount under \texttt{contracts/obligation}. Both start with cash of USD~100 and an
outstanding liability of USD~30. A payment of USD~3.50 credits cash and debits
the liability by the same amount, leaving cash of USD~96.50 and a liability of
USD~26.50. The contract record falls to USD~26.50 and its cumulative payments
rise to USD~3.50. A second payment of USD~26.50 extinguishes the liability and
leaves cash of USD~70. Other accounts and unrelated contract records are unchanged.
The example assumes sufficient cash and a positive payment no larger than the
outstanding amount; the generic book does not impose a universal funding policy.

\texttt{CompanyBookMethod/1} declares an injective mapping from account roles to
the book's accounts, prerequisite predicates, monetary calculations, memory
replacements and postconditions. Bindings select declared account balances,
typed memory values or supplied event inputs. Their company, dimensions and
scale must agree exactly with the method's input declarations. A dollar amount
cannot become an inventory quantity merely because both are rational numbers.
The existing monetary interpreter derives the journal and separate gross cash
flows. The memory interpreter then replaces the specified scalar fields; this
profile rejects dynamic paths, new collection members and container writes.
Nested prerequisite and monetary definitions carry the enclosing method's
identity and version. A literal memory replacement is interpreted in the
destination's declared type.

\texttt{CompanyBookTransition/1} checks the entire request before assessing its
prerequisites. It derives monetary effects, stages memory changes and constructs
a new book from the same starting state. Only then does it evaluate the
postconditions against that proposed book. In the payment case, a postcondition
requires the credit balance of the liability account to equal the outstanding
contract amount. A failed postcondition returns no applied book, although the
staged calculation remains available for diagnosis. An unknown applicability
finding remains unresolved; a false finding can make the chosen method
unsupported. An inactive guarded prerequisite does not, by itself, cancel the
economic event: a separate decision determines whether an event should occur.

The request identifies both the starting book and the complete method by digest.
Application recomputes the proposal and compares its canonical JSON representation,
including Boolean and numeric types. Changing a saved journal, memory value or
authorization flag therefore cannot pass merely by replacing its digest. Event
identities cannot repeat, dates cannot precede the current book, and each event
must fall within the declared period. This is an atomic construction of a
provisional value; the separate posting-authorization and durable-storage rules
still govern any admitted or persisted transaction.

Five comparisons connect this reference to the retained native methods. Two
current-tax payments and three complete-lot sales reproduce their native journals,
gross flows and entire resulting states through explicit mappings. The mappings
retain every native field outside the declared changes; both payment cases also
reproduce the complete native financial reports. These checks concern the selected
synthetic cases. General reporting and closing, source admission, dynamic contract
collections, currency conversion and the broader corporate package still require
their own methods. The declared book supplies a common place to compose those
methods without weakening the older profiles' invariants.

\paragraph{Bind a named method to the event that actually occurred.}
The declared book separates company-specific accounts from a method's financial
roles. Selecting that method also requires an unambiguous connection to the event.
\texttt{CompanyMethodRegistry/1} names the available data-only method definitions,
their schemas, expected account classifications and input types. Each entry
identifies its complete method body. The optional \texttt{CompanyMethodCall/1}
selects one entry under the book's company context and checks every entry's
structure before evaluating the selected method's prerequisites. An invalid unused
entry therefore cannot remain hidden until a later event happens to select it.

Some inputs are supplied facts; others describe the event itself. Keeping these
sources distinct matters for a sale after a tax close. Suppose the event is dated
15 June, but a separately supplied date says 1 September. A predicate comparing
that supplied date with a 1 July close could accept the wrong timing. The named
binder instead derives designated date and identity inputs from the event's own
metadata. It rejects a supplied input with the same name. In the checked case,
the 15 June sale consequently fails the closed-period prerequisite. A genuine
change to the event date changes the predicate's input with it.

The binder materializes the ordinary company-book transition request, including
the current state and selected method identities. The existing prerequisite,
monetary and memory engines then determine the result. Two company payment layouts
and a complete-lot sale reproduce their explicitly authored requests and entire
provisional transitions; unknown and false applicability findings retain their
different consequences. Repeated calls read the resulting book, so the next
payment uses the reduced outstanding amount. Application recomputes the complete
named call and uses the same type-sensitive JSON comparison as the underlying
transition.

The current registry supports a directly dated event. Assessment intervals,
measurement dates and period carry are different clock families and remain
unsupported here. The registry also leaves native applicability to an explicit
adapter. For example, a reusable inventory-sale adapter must validate all lot
allocations, disposition and tax-base conditions, the selected sale method and the
native close date, as well as preserve every unmapped field. Matching one native
journal cannot establish those conditions. The selected comparisons establish the
named binding mechanism; they do not turn the retained test projections into
general native-method admission.

\paragraph{Carry the entire native book through the selected sale.}
A complete state check precedes this sale. The selected
\texttt{NativeCompanySale/1} adapter extends the earlier test projection to the complete retained native whole-lot sale domain. It validates
the native state's structure and accounting controls before importing every
existing lot into a declared company book. The native account classifications
and company context supply an explicit mapping. Fields outside the sale remain
bound to the original state and are restored when the proposed book is exported.
The adapter then validates the entire restored native state again.

Preserving every lot makes the comparison discriminating. In a two-lot example,
the inventory account holds USD~20: USD~15 belongs to the selected lot and USD~5
to a second lot. Selling the selected lot for USD~40 raises cash from USD~454
to USD~494, reduces inventory to USD~5 and raises cost of sales from USD~56 to
USD~71. The second lot is unchanged. Selling it later for USD~8 leaves cash of
USD~502 and no inventory. The declared method reconciles the inventory account
with the carrying amounts of all existing lots and other inventory before and
after each sale; it replaces only the selected lot's units, carrying amount,
tax base and disposition fields.

The generic monetary and memory interpreters compute these changes through the
named method binder. The native transition remains the comparison calculation:
a test disables it while the adapter still proposes and applies the sale. Across
23 accepted and refused cases, complete native states, journals, gross cash flows
and refusal reasons agree. Unknown performance or proceeds remain unresolved;
false performance and a fractional sale remain unsupported. Native cash-funding
failure, duplicate identities, backdating, tax-close restrictions and inconsistent
lot or control balances retain their native consequences.

This adapter preserves the fixed native profile's domain rather than inferring
accounting applicability from the general book's type checks. It creates no new
lot and supports only a complete-lot sale. Source admission, partial disposals,
new collection members and a general company's full reporting package remain
separate work. Its contribution is a checked route through the common book that
preserves the native conditions and every field outside the selected transaction.

\paragraph{A conditional history gives a conditional opening.}
Importing a known book and selecting a possible history answer different
questions. Suppose the disclosed constraints permit closing cash of USD~15.
One compatible synthetic history starts with USD~10 and adds sales of USD~2
and USD~3; another starts with USD~8 and adds sales of USD~3 and USD~4.
Both reach the same cash balance, yet their cumulative-sales memory is USD~5
and USD~7 respectively. A continuation that reads that memory must retain
which history supplied it. Equal closing statements do not erase the difference.

\texttt{ConditionalCompanyOpening/1} accepts an explicit witness for the
typed history and measurement attachment. It replays the complete supplied
constraints before constructing an opening. Every account maps once to a
uniquely identified account with an explicitly declared classification; every
numeric memory field maps once to a nonoverlapping path. Signed amounts,
dimensions and scale are preserved. Missing or duplicate mappings refuse
rather than creating an unallocated balancing amount. In the example, both
openings contain cash of USD~15 and equity credit of USD~15, while their
separate sales memories remain USD~5 and USD~7. A checked inventory example
similarly retains one physical unit separately from inventory of USD~2 and
cash of USD~21.

The boundary between the representations needs explicit premises. This first
adapter selects the final declared end-of-day snapshot and starts the book
on the next day, assuming no unrecorded event across that boundary. Entity
and book must agree, and monetary amounts must already use the same currency
at unit scale. The target context is explicitly synthetic. The source
accounting-basis context and the stated mapping premise remain attached to
it; changing the representation does not establish a basis conversion.
Income and expense accounts must already have zero opening balances.
Otherwise the adapter refuses: it does not invent fiscal closing entries.

The returned provenance retains the original history-and-measurement
definition, selected witness, source snapshot, mapping premises, exclusions
and unresolved obligations. Missing premises yield an unresolved result.
Remaining target obligations or unmapped observations stay visible in a
candidate opening. A valid opening balance report checks the mapped book;
it does not admit the history or authorize a financial continuation. No
investor information cutoff or forecast admissibility has been checked.

This distinction matters for receivables. An aggregate balance can be mapped
exactly without supplying invoice identities, counterparties or due dates.
Those facts must come from an explicit declared or source-supported contract
population before an identified collection method can use them. The adapter
therefore provides a checked conditional starting state while preserving the
remaining method and contract obligations. Other compatible histories remain
represented by the unchanged parent definition; this selected witness neither
enumerates them nor assigns them probabilities.


\paragraph{Keep the selected history attached when the book changes.}
The conditional opening can support a further explicitly assumed transaction,
provided that the method's premises are supplied and no target obligation
remains unresolved in this bounded composition. The
\texttt{ConditionalCompanyContinuation/1} wrapper recomputes the complete
opening result and compares it with the supplied result before invoking the
ordinary book transition. A stale state, altered witness record or different
company context refuses. Missing opening premises or remaining target
obligations return an unresolved result. The wrapper retains the complete
opening evidence alongside the financial result; a copied provenance label
alone would not establish that connection.

For the two preceding histories, assume a cash distribution of USD~1 on
2~January 2025. This synthetic method checks the explicitly selected
applicability premise, a positive amount and sufficient cash. It credits cash
and debits the declared equity account by USD~1, leaving cash of USD~14 and
an equity credit of USD~14. The financing cash flow is minus USD~1 and profit
is zero. In the statement reconciliation, the direct movement in reported
equity is minus USD~1, although the ledger debit is positive. The common
report rejects an explanation with the opposite sign. Neither history's
sales memory changes: USD~5 and USD~7 still distinguish their pasts.

The checked wrapper agrees exactly with the unchanged transition and report
under both account charts. Unknown event inputs retain their reasons, false
applicability remains unsupported, and insufficient cash refuses without
changing the supplied book. This is a declared financial continuation of a
selected conditional history. It does not establish lawful distributions,
distributable reserves, accounting compliance or admitted execution. A future
method that depends on unresolved invoice terms needs those terms explicitly;
the successful distribution example cannot supply them.


\paragraph{Report the declared accounts and explain their movements.}
A common book also needs a reporting method that respects each company's chart.
\texttt{CompanyBookReport/1} takes opening and closing books for the same company,
period and account population, together with a declared statement map. Every
account belongs to exactly one line of the same classification: asset, liability,
equity, income or expense. A missing account, overlapping line or conflicting
classification prevents the report. Different memory schemas can describe the
opening and closing books, since a period may create a new lot; each book is
validated independently, and reporting does not establish that memory migration.

The report follows the book's debit-positive convention. Assets and expenses use
their stored signs; liabilities, direct equity and income reverse them. Profit
equals income less expenses, and reported equity includes that profit. Contra
balances remain signed. The first profile requires an opening book at the period
start with closed income and expense accounts and no current-period cash flows.
It therefore describes a recorded period, rather than an arbitrary comparison
between two accumulated income statements.

Cash flows preserve each method's declared activity and origin. For operating,
investing and financing activities separately, the report sums receipts and
payments before computing the net movement. The aggregate must equal the change
in the selected cash account. Each flow must refer to an event recorded after
the opening history; a prior event cannot be reused to explain current-period
cash. Direct equity movements require explicit account, event, date, amount and
description. They must explain every change in the direct equity accounts.
Profit enters the equity reconciliation separately, so a missing contribution
or distribution cannot disappear into an unexplained residual.

An authored sale and contribution illustrate all four statements. Opening cash
is USD~100, inventory USD~20, another asset USD~10, liabilities USD~30 and equity
USD~100. Selling inventory with carrying amount USD~15 for USD~40 produces
USD~25 profit. A subsequent USD~5 owner contribution brings cash to USD~145.
Closing assets of USD~160 equal liabilities of USD~30 plus equity of USD~130;
opening equity of USD~100, the USD~5 contribution and USD~25 profit explain that
closing equity. Operating cash receipts are USD~40 and financing receipts USD~5.
The same quantities reconcile under two distinct company charts. In a compatible
retained annual case, every asset, liability, income and expense line and all
direct cash-flow activities agree with the native report: profit is USD~49.50,
cash USD~491.50 and equity USD~671.50.

These equalities establish the recorded amounts' consistency. They cannot reveal
omitted offsetting events or establish the economic cause of a supplied equity
explanation. The report therefore retains its recorded-period scope and never
claims fiscal completeness. Note requirements remain explicitly missing,
unsupported or unresolved; this profile cannot declare a note package complete.
The indirect operating-cash bridge, source and accounting admission, comparative
presentation and explicit closing and carry methods remain separate obligations.

\paragraph{Close the recorded balances under explicit carry policies.}
The period report leaves profit in the temporary income and expense accounts.
\texttt{CompanyBookCarry/1} transfers those recorded balances to a named equity
account and constructs the next provisional opening. The caller identifies that
account explicitly; an account label cannot select it. Each temporary account
receives the negative of its closing balance, while the selected equity account
receives their sum. With debit-positive storage, profit therefore produces an
additional equity credit and a loss an equity debit. The journal sums to zero,
and cash and the other permanent accounts remain unchanged.

In the preceding company example, profit of USD~25 is transferred to equity,
leaving USD~130 of opening equity for the next period and zero income and expense
balances. An otherwise analogous USD~3 loss reduces equity by USD~3. If the
company names a separate retained-income account, the contribution account remains
unchanged. Zero profit produces no net retained-equity transfer, although offsetting
income and expense balances still require closing entries. The next opening report
has no current-period profit and reconciles to the preceding closing equity.

The method recomputes the period report and requires the closing snapshot to be
dated the last included day before the period's exclusive endpoint. The next
period starts at that endpoint and ends at an explicitly supplied later date;
calendar years are not assumed. This date rule does not establish that the event
record is complete. The method carries recorded balances provisionally and keeps
fiscal-completeness and accounting-admission flags false.

Every typed memory leaf needs its own declared policy: preserve its value, reset
it to an explicitly typed literal, or, for a date leaf, set it to the next period's
start. Each policy records its rationale. Missing policies and type mismatches
prevent carry. The engine does not silently reset payment counters, advance tax
clocks or infer an opening tax basis. Such choices can be type-correct yet
substantively wrong, so their provenance remains part of the proposed result.
The existing scalar memory interpreter applies the declared replacements.

The next book clears prior-period cash flows but retains lifetime event identities
and adds a new close identity. A returned archive record links the prior book,
report, closing journal result, next opening and memory policies by digest.
Application recomputes the full proposal. This is a returned provisional history
record, not a durable storage commit. Comparisons reproduce the retained native
carry's balances, period and imported lot memory; its other tax, interest and
archive conventions remain separate native rules. Consecutive-period tests retain
earlier event and close identities rather than making them available for reuse.

\paragraph{Run the declared company through successive periods.}
A company model needs to use these methods in one sequence, with each operation
reading the book produced by its predecessors. The finite
\texttt{DeclaredCompanyProgram/1} combines an opening book, named method registry,
statement map and an authored list of events, reports and provisional carries.
An event names its method and supplies dated, typed inputs. A report names its
cutoff, direct equity explanations and unresolved note requirements. A carry also
names the retained-equity account, next period endpoint and every memory policy.
The program constructs each method request from the current book at that turn;
future balance hashes and expected journals are not authored inputs.

An interim report can follow the last supplied transaction by several months.
Its explicit cutoff advances only the observation date of a provisional copy;
it creates no journal, cash flow or memory change. The trace records that advance,
and a failed report leaves the preceding book unchanged. Reaching December's
last day does not prove that all December events were supplied. Reports continue
to use the actual opening of their current fiscal period. After a successful
carry, its returned next opening becomes that basis; earlier income and cash
flows do not enter the new period's report. Supplied equity explanations and note
requirements belong to each report rather than being copied silently across years.

Consider the preceding declared book before its illustrative owner contribution:
cash is USD~100, inventory USD~20, another asset USD~10, liabilities USD~30 and
equity USD~100. A supplied sale for USD~40 releases USD~15 of inventory and earns
USD~25. After an interim report, paying USD~3.50 of principal leaves year-end cash
USD~136.50, inventory USD~5, liabilities USD~26.50 and equity USD~125. Carry closes
the temporary accounts. In the next year, selling the remaining inventory for
USD~8 earns USD~3; paying the remaining USD~26.50 leaves cash USD~118, no inventory
or liability, the other asset USD~10 and equity USD~128. A second carry retains the
lifetime payment total of USD~30 and all event and close identities. These exact
amounts, every constructed request and every engine result agree with separately
written calls under two different account charts. The sale's aggregate carrying
cost and applicability finding are supplied assumptions; this example does not
infer a lot valuation or tax treatment.

The bounded program checks all command shapes and method definitions before
execution, but evaluates economic prerequisites against the book at their actual
turn. Same-day operations follow their explicit sequence positions. An unknown
finding, false prerequisite, invalid date, repeated identity or failed carry
returns the exact refused command alongside the entire successful prefix. Report
and carry changes become visible together only after that command succeeds.
Canonical replay recomputes the complete result, including report and archive
references; changing an input or substituting a result prevents verification.
The initial limits of 64 commands and an 8~MiB trace bound execution for this
reference. Exceeding the trace limit retains the prefix and identifies the
oversized result by digest; it makes no scalability or durable-recovery claim.


\paragraph{Author the sequence while retaining its declared definitions.}
The matched \texttt{DeclaredCompanyText/1} frontend expresses those same commands
as text. Four separately checked content hashes identify the opening book,
schemas, method registry and statement map. These definitions remain imported
data; the following language authors their operation sequence. For example,
after its program header and four import declarations, a sale can be written as:
\begin{lstlisting}
event "sale.2027" method "sell" on "2027-02-01";
  input "proceeds" known "40/1" because "Synthetic assumption";
  input "cost" known "15/1" because "Synthetic assumption";
  input "approved" known true because "Synthetic assumption";

\end{lstlisting}
Each declaration occupies one physical line. Each amount's type comes from
the selected registry entry, including its company context, currency and units.
A finding can instead be explicitly unknown, with its reason and provenance;
compilation preserves it for the method to evaluate at the actual command.

A report command supplies its cutoff, direct-equity explanations and note
requirements. A carry adds its retained-equity account, next period endpoint and
one policy for each memory leaf. Preserve, typed reset and next-period-start
policies retain the same meanings as their data-record counterparts. The compiler
resolves reset types from the declared memory schema rather than guessing units.
Missing policy coverage remains a carry refusal, so earlier successful commands
remain visible. Repeated event identities and invalid chronology likewise retain
the checked engine's exact refusal and preceding book.

Compilation verifies every imported hash and returns the resolved program,
dependency identities, source hash and physical declaration locations. Unknown
fields, repeated declarations, malformed scalar values and unchecked imports are
rejected. It neither evaluates Python nor treats a serialized object as a command.
The two company charts reproduce their full data programs and execution results;
an independently authored sale and interim report also recover cash USD~140,
profit USD~25 and equity USD~125. Complete and stopped programs are compared,
including unknown findings, missing carry policies and changed ordering. These
checks establish the finite frontend's correspondence; authoring complete method
algorithms, broader accounting policies and all company disclosures remains work
for the wider language design.


\paragraph{Follow purchasing, sales and wages through one book.}
The next declared methods connect six ordinary operations: purchasing inventory
on credit, delivering it on credit, collecting receivables, paying suppliers,
accruing wages and paying those wages. Each method is a data definition interpreted
by the same checked requirements, monetary and memory engines. An explicit role
map identifies the accounts. Quantity and unit cost carry different dimensions,
so their product is a currency amount; substituting a money input for units fails
the type check. The selected synthetic premise is a positive, constant inventory
unit cost stored in company memory. It does not establish an inventory valuation
policy for heterogeneous lots.

Purchasing increases inventory and supplier payables without moving cash.
Delivery increases receivables and revenue, while transferring its cost from
inventory to cost of sales. Collection reduces receivables and increases cash.
Supplier and wage payments reduce their respective liabilities and cash; wage
accrual creates the expense and payable before payment. The methods check available
inventory, receivables, obligations and cash at the event's actual turn. They also
retain an explicit applicability finding: unknown remains unresolved, and false
remains unsupported. Other accounts and all typed memory remain unchanged.

Start with cash USD~100, inventory USD~20 and equity USD~120, at a unit cost of
USD~5. Purchasing six units creates inventory and payables of USD~30. Delivering
five units for USD~50 creates that receivable and releases USD~25 of inventory.
Collect USD~35, pay the supplier USD~20, accrue wages of USD~10 and pay USD~8.
Cash ends at USD~107, receivables at USD~15 and inventory at USD~25. Supplier
payables of USD~10 and wage payables of USD~2 leave equity of USD~135, including
profit of USD~15. Assets of USD~147 equal liabilities of USD~12 plus equity.
Operating cash of USD~7 also reconciles to profit less the USD~15 receivable
increase and USD~5 inventory increase, plus the two payable increases of USD~10
and USD~2. This bridge explains the supplied cycle; it does not select a general
indirect cash-flow policy.

Every journal, complete account balance and cash movement agrees with separate
calls to the retained \texttt{DemoCorporate/1} methods under the same premises.
A second chart produces identical financial quantities through its own explicit
mapping, and the text frontend reproduces the data program. Additional checks
retain nonzero plant, accumulated depreciation and debt while these operations
run. This is monetary conformance for the selected methods, not a conversion of
all native asset, history, tax or information-selection semantics.

A payment exceeding available cash preserves the preceding book. The current
requirement interpreter returns a primitive invalid result for its failed cash
condition. A separate diagnostic identifies an isolated liquidity shortage as
an economic funding failure and retains that exact underlying result; another
failed condition is never hidden by that explanation. A general typed default
outcome remains a separate execution obligation. Maturity, ageing, impairment,
tax and disclosure completeness also remain unresolved until their own facts and
methods are supplied. The next step is to derive explicit working-capital
roll-forwards from these verified events, rather than treating balanced totals
as complete notes.


\paragraph{Explain working-capital movements from the verified history.}
The operating cycle now supports four reconciled movement schedules rather than
only its closing totals. \texttt{DeclaredWorkingCapital/1} first recomputes the
complete program result and selects one successful report, including the report
inside a provisional carry. Its actual opening book defines the period. Earlier
interim reports add no activity; a preceding carry establishes a new opening.
If execution later stops, an earlier successful report can still be selected,
while the failed command cannot stand in for a report.

For the illustrative cycle, receivables open at zero, increase by USD~50 of credit
sales and fall by USD~35 of collections, closing at USD~15. Inventory opens at
USD~20, receives USD~30 of purchases and releases USD~25 to cost of sales, closing
at USD~25. Supplier payables increase from zero by USD~30 and fall by USD~20;
wage payables increase by USD~10 and fall by USD~8. Their closing amounts are
USD~10 and USD~2. Every movement retains its event identity, date, typed inputs,
provenance, exact method definition and verified request/result references.
Liabilities use positive presentation even though the book stores credits as
negative balances. Purchases and deliveries also retain the supplied quantities
and the declared unit-cost premise.

Classification depends on the complete checked method definition, not its registry
nickname. Renaming a method key therefore preserves its meaning; changing the
method cannot masquerade as the old operation. An unsupported movement in a scoped
account prevents the schedule, even if another event offsets it. Gross posting
checks also detect an unsupported debit and credit that cancel within one event.
That last case exposed and repaired a first-draft error: inspecting only the net
journal had hidden the movement. The preserved counterexample distinguishes the
repair from a claim based only on successful ordinary examples.

After carry, collecting the remaining USD~15 and paying the remaining USD~10 of
supplier balances produces the next period's movements, with no repetition of
prior sales or purchases. Inventory remains USD~25 and wage payables USD~2.
The four working-capital changes explain a USD~5 cash contribution. In general,
this contribution is only part of an indirect cash reconciliation: a separate
noncash expense can leave a residual between profit, these four changes and direct
operating cash. The schedule displays that residual instead of forcing it to zero.

These schedules account for recorded movements. They retain unresolved receivable
ageing and impairment, inventory valuation and quantity coverage, maturity and
payment terms, related parties, tax and source-population completeness. An exact
roll-forward supplies useful note evidence, but cannot make those absent disclosures
complete. Both note-completeness and fiscal-completeness indicators remain false.


\paragraph{Accrue interest before changing the principal it prices.}
The same company book can now join its operating cycle to borrowing, repayment,
interest, capital contributions and dividends. The illustrative borrowing has a
fixed annual simple rate of 7.3 percent and an explicit actual-days/365 convention.
These are declared synthetic terms. They provide an exact timing example; fees,
effective-interest valuation, compounding, contractual maturity and source
admission require their own methods.

Interest for each declared interval is principal times the annual rate times its
calendar days divided by 365. A date stored in company memory marks the first day
not yet accrued. Each interval starts at that date and ends exclusively at its
declared boundary. The event occurs at the end of the preceding calendar day and
advances the stored date to the boundary. Principal changes occur at the beginning
of their event date and require that same date in memory. Thus a repayment cannot
change the principal used to price an earlier, unaccrued interval. A second memory
value must equal signed ledger debt; an unexplained principal posting prevents the
next financing method.

Issue USD~100 on 1 January 2027. The 90 days to 1 April accrue
$100\times0.073\times90/365=1.8$ dollars of interest. At that boundary, repay
USD~40 and borrow USD~20: principal becomes USD~80. The next 91 days accrue
USD~1.456, and the remaining 184 days accrue USD~2.944. Total interest is USD~6.2.
The stored date reaches 1 January 2028, covering all 365 days. Repeating or skipping
an interval, changing its event date, or changing principal before the preceding
days have accrued is refused. The convention counts a leap day as another day
while retaining the stated denominator of 365.

The method builder calculates each fraction from the exact dates and embeds it
in the typed definition. The caller supplies neither an interest amount nor an
unchecked year fraction. Rate and elapsed years have inverse dimensions, leaving
a currency amount when multiplied by principal. Each interval remains one finite
declared method; this is not yet a general instrument calendar. A report or
provisional carry does not itself accrue omitted interest. Their balanced totals
therefore remain separate from whether the stored date covers the reporting cutoff.

\paragraph{Keep financing, profit and direct equity distinct.}
Debt receipts and repayments change cash and principal. Interest accrual changes
expense and interest payable; payment reduces that payable and cash. A capital
contribution uses supplied shares times price. Dividend declaration reduces
retained equity and creates a payable; payment settles it. The contribution and
declaration need explicit explanations in the equity statement. A supplied
applicability finding remains unresolved when unknown and unsupported when false.
It does not establish a legal right to distribute retained earnings. Payments
cannot exceed the recorded obligation or available cash.

Combine these financing events with the preceding six operating events. Add a
USD~30 contribution, declare USD~12 of dividends, pay USD~5 of those dividends
and USD~4 of interest. Cash closes at USD~208, receivables at USD~15 and inventory
at USD~25. Debt is USD~80, unpaid interest USD~2.2 and unpaid dividends USD~7,
alongside supplier and payroll payables of USD~12. Profit is USD~8.8 and equity
USD~146.8: assets of USD~248 equal liabilities of USD~101.2 plus that equity.
With interest payments explicitly classified as operating, operating cash is
USD~3 and financing cash USD~105. Selecting the supported financing classification
for interest changes the category, while preserving total cash and the expense.

Every monetary transition is compared with the retained corporate engine under
the same premises. Separate calculations check the amounts and date coverage;
the older engine's acceptance alone cannot establish timing because it accepts a
supplied year fraction. Two charts and matched text produce the same financial
results. Unpaid interest and dividends remain visible across provisional carry;
maturity, valuation, outstanding-share reconciliation, lawful distributions and
complete disclosures remain unresolved.

\paragraph{Compose bounded definitions without weakening each method's checks.}
The combined registry exceeded the original record's structural limit before any
event ran. The successor \texttt{CompanyMethodRegistry/2} checks each method with
the unchanged earlier materializer, including unused methods. Its enclosing
record retains the one-megabyte input limit and the 32-entry limit. Each method
retains its own depth, scalar and 4,096-node bounds. The complete registry identity
remains bound to every call; selecting a valid method cannot conceal a malformed
unused definition.

\texttt{DeclaredCompanyProgram/2} applies the same component-by-component validation
to the opening, schemas, registry, statement map and commands. Its 64-command and
8\,MiB trace bounds remain. \texttt{DeclaredCompanyText/2} uses the same command
grammar and exact hashed imports with the successor registry and runner.
Compatible smaller programs retain the complete monetary transition and report
results; only the declared composition version and its provenance change.
This permits the demonstrated common book while leaving scalability and durable
execution for separate validation.


\paragraph{Reconcile financing movements and show the dates still covered.}
A verified company trace can explain the financing balances at the same time as it explains the operating cycle. `DeclaredFinancingSchedules/1` first recomputes the v2 program and chooses a successful report. It then rebuilds the declared financing definitions from the exact policy, intervals and role map. Registry names do not classify an event; the method definition hash does.

The schedule records debt issues and repayments, interest accruals and payments, capital contributions, dividend declarations and dividend payments. Each row retains its event date, typed inputs, method, request and result hashes. Signed ledger credits are presented as positive liabilities. Opening plus increases less decreases equals the selected closing balance for debt, interest payable, capital, retained direct movements and dividends.

Interest coverage is a separate result. In the example, the cursor starts on 1 January 2027 and advances through intervals of 90, 91 and 184 days to 1 January 2028. The three rows therefore cover 365 days. A balanced report with a cursor that stops earlier remains a valid provisional balance report with an explicit accrual gap. A carry cannot silently complete the missing suffix. Unknown methods that change a scoped account, tracked debt or cursor refuse classification, even when their net journal is zero.

The four operating schedules remain compatible with the v2 program. Their partial cash bridge leaves the unpaid-interest residual visible under the selected operating-interest policy. This is an explanation for the synthetic example, not a general indirect cash-flow rule. Contract maturity, fees, valuation, outstanding shares, lawful distributions, source completeness and full notes remain unresolved.

\paragraph{Distinguish collecting a claim from assessing whether it is overdue.}
The same receivable balance can imply different collection problems at different
dates. Consider two explicitly assumed claims: USD~60 due on 31~January 2027
and USD~40 due on 28~February. A receipt of USD~20 against the first claim on
15~January leaves USD~40 against that claim. The selected synthetic policy treats
a remaining amount as overdue only after its due date. Thus neither claim is
overdue at the end of January. A further receipt of USD~10 against the second
claim in February leaves aggregate receivables of USD~70. At 28~February, USD~40
is overdue under this policy; the other USD~30 becomes overdue on the next day.
These classifications describe the assumed payment terms. They do not measure
an allowance or establish a right to write off either claim.

\texttt{DeclaredCollectionClock/1} keeps a fixed declared population of claims,
with separate memory for each due date, remaining amount and last assessed
amount. The opening claims must sum exactly to the selected receivable account.
A collection identifies one claim, cannot exceed its recorded remainder, and
posts debit cash and credit receivables. It changes that claim's remainder while
preserving the previous assessment. An assessment instead reads the remainders
at its declared date, stores the resulting overdue amounts and makes no journal
entry or cash movement. Unknown required event inputs retain their reasons and
stop that command; an explicitly false applicability finding refuses the method.

Assessment windows have an inclusive start and an exclusive end. The assessment
occurs on the final included date, and the saved cursor must equal that window's
start before it can advance to the end. Windows must be positive and contiguous.
This makes a repeated, overlapping or skipped assessment detectable. Within one
date, supplied event order is retained, but an assessment seals that date against
a later collection: a payment intended to affect that observation must precede
it. The journal engine also rejects events earlier than the current book date
or outside the current fiscal period.

A report can advance its observation date without supplying the missing
assessment. It then identifies the last assessment as historical, or records
that none exists. Provisional period carry preserves unpaid claims, due dates
and assessment memory; it cannot reset the cursor or turn year-end into a new
assessment. In the example, a December assessment records overdue amounts of
USD~40 and USD~30. After carrying those balances, collecting the first USD~40
in January 2028 leaves USD~30 outstanding. A declared January assessment then
records overdue amounts of zero and USD~30. Cash has risen from USD~100 to
USD~170, receivables have fallen from USD~100 to USD~30, and total assets and
equity remain USD~200 throughout.

The existing command language can author these events, reports and carries
through its hashed definitions. The guarded execution compares exact method
definitions, so renaming a registry entry does not change its meaning. A foreign
method that writes claim memory or posts to the receivable account refuses,
including offsetting postings with no net balance change. Only the successful
prefix remains available after refusal. The checked fragment supports eight
declared opening claims and sixteen assessment windows within the existing work
limits. New claim insertion, revisions to payment terms, complete invoice
coverage, impairment, tax and source admission remain distinct requirements.


\paragraph{A credit sale must create the claim that can later be collected.}
The fixed population above can follow claims already present at opening. A new
sale poses a different problem: the invoice identifier, customer and payment
terms must enter the state when the receivable arises. Increasing only the
aggregate receivable account leaves later collection without an identified
claim. Reserving zero-valued invoices before they exist would instead confuse
absence with an existing claim whose balance happens to be zero.

The additive \texttt{CompanyMemoryTypes/1} declaration distinguishes fixed
records, exact scalar values and finite maps of typed records. An empty map
contains no claims. Once a sale inserts an invoice, each of its actual fields
has an explicit type, including the company and currency context of its
monetary amount. \texttt{CompanyBookTransition/2} checks that these declarations
agree with the current book and its memory schema. It permits insertion of a
complete map entry and replacement of a scalar field. It refuses duplicate
identities, incomplete records, incompatible types and excess capacity. Map
keys remain single identities even when they contain slashes or tildes.
The frozen schema validator does not express the declared capacity, so the
successor checks the typed population again after staging and before returning
the proposed book. Monetary effects, memory changes and postconditions must
all succeed; a failure leaves the supplied book unchanged.

Consider a separate synthetic company opening with cash of USD~100, inventory
of USD~20 and equity of USD~120. It sells two units costing USD~5 each on
2~January 2027 for consideration of USD~30, due on 31~January. The declared sale
creates the named customer's invoice for USD~30 and records its date and sale
event. At the same step it debits receivables and credits revenue by USD~30,
then debits cost of sales and credits inventory by USD~10. There is no cash
movement. A receipt of USD~10 on 15~January debits cash, credits receivables
and reduces only this invoice to USD~20. Receipts identify both the invoice
and its declared customer; neither another customer's label nor a payment
larger than the remainder is accepted.

\texttt{DeclaredReceivableLifecycle/1} requires the sum of invoice remainders
to equal the receivable account before and after each successful command.
It begins with an explicitly empty claim population; the preceding profile
retains its separate opening-claim example. Assessment uses the same declared
strictly-after-due-date convention and contiguous half-open windows. An
assessment on 31~January records zero overdue; one on 28~February records
USD~20. These operations update invoice memory without a journal or cash
flow. Each invoice records whether it has been assessed and at which date.
An invoice created after an earlier assessment therefore remains visibly
unassessed until a subsequent assessment includes it. A receipt preserves
the old snapshot rather than silently recomputing it. Assessment seals its
date against later sales or receipts intended to change that observation.

With no further transactions in 2027, cash is USD~110, receivables USD~20 and
inventory USD~10. Assets and equity are USD~140, profit is USD~20 and operating
cash flow is USD~10. Provisional year-end carry transfers the profit to
retained earnings and preserves every existing invoice field and the
assessment cursor. Collecting the remaining USD~20 in January 2028 raises
cash to USD~130, clears the receivable and produces operating cash flow of
USD~20 in that year. It leaves the prior overdue snapshot historical until
the next declared assessment records zero.

The versioned \texttt{DeclaredReceivableText/1} adapter uses the existing
event, report and carry syntax with explicitly hashed lifecycle imports.
Its checked executions reproduce the structured requests on two different
charts of accounts. An unresolved or refused command retains earlier
successful transactions and reports. These examples establish a bounded
sale-to-collection composition, including identity creation and carry; they
do not supply source-backed opening invoices, revised payment terms,
impairment, tax, arbitrary method authoring or production durability. Those
remain substantive requirements of the wider company simulator.


\paragraph{A claim needs evidence as well as a place in memory.}
The preceding lifecycle explains what an identified sale does to the book.
It does not establish where the invoice identity, amount or due date came
from. An implementer needs to preserve the source reading separately from the
decision to recognize a sale. Otherwise a correction to a document can be
mistaken for a second transaction, or a missing payment term can acquire an
invented value merely because execution requires one.

The bounded \texttt{ReceivableSourceContract/1} supplies this separation for
explicitly constructed JSON documents. Each reading binds an exact UTF-8 byte
span and its hash, the company/book/currency context, an assertion identity,
and a stable claim and sale-event identity. Original and corrected assertions
remain present. A named information cutoff controls which assertion was
available; a separate ingestion cutoff controls which version the system had
received. Unknown timing, competing corrections, altered source bytes or
unreadable terms remain unresolved or refuse as appropriate. Recognition
approval, receipts and assessment policy are separately supplied premises.

Extend the synthetic company by a second sale on 3~January 2027: consideration
of USD~40, cost of USD~10, due on 28~February, to the same named customer but
under a different invoice identity. After the earlier USD~10 receipt, the two
claims are USD~20 and USD~40. Cash is USD~110, inventory is zero, profit is
USD~50 and assets and equity are USD~170. Carry preserves both claims. The
subsequent receipts of USD~20 and USD~40 raise cash to USD~170 and clear the
claims, with no new profit and operating cash flow of USD~60. These balances
follow from the declared transactions; they are not readings of a real company.

Now suppose a corrected assertion changes the first invoice's due date to
31~March. It becomes available at noon UTC on 15~March and is ingested at
09:00 UTC on 16~March. An earlier information or ingestion view retains the
original assertion. A later view selects the correction and reconstructs a
separate history from the same opening. Its February overdue assessment is
zero instead of USD~20; its journals and financial statements are unchanged.
Three assertions therefore still describe two claims. This operation corrects
a reading of the original terms. A genuine renegotiation with its own effective
date would require an amendment method; it cannot be represented by silently
rewriting the original sale.

\paragraph{New payment terms leave earlier assessments intact.}
A prospective amendment answers a different question from a corrected source
reading: what changes from the amendment date onward? Return to the earlier
single-invoice example, before the illustrative second sale. The original
invoice was due on 31~January 2027. Its remaining USD~20 was assessed as
overdue on 28~February. Suppose the company and customer are now modeled as
extending its due date on 10~March to 30~April. The February assessment remains
USD~20 overdue under the original terms. A fresh assessment on 31~March records
zero overdue under the extension. Replacing the old due date in place would
lose the information needed to explain why both assessments are correct in
their respective histories.

The bounded \texttt{ReceivableTermHistory/1} reference supplies this separate
prospective operation. Its migration starts from a valid legacy claim book
and an explicit premise that no earlier amendment occurred. On that premise,
it imports the existing due date as the initial term and preserves the last
stored assessment. It does not reconstruct earlier assessments. The imported
assessment's event identity is recorded as unknown because the old summary
does not identify that event. The source book and its hash remain available;
the migration leaves its balances, cash flows, claim identities and original
scalar observations unchanged. Consequently, even a successful migration
leaves completeness of the earlier term history unresolved.

Each claim then has an append-only map of term versions and an append-only
map of assessments. A term names its due date, effective date, originating
event, predecessor and supplied premise. The current-term reference identifies
the version used for a new assessment. Each assessment retains its own term
reference, assessment date, remaining amount at that date and overdue amount.
The validator checks the term applicable at that historical date; it does not
recompute every old observation using the current due date or current balance.
The familiar scalar assessment fields remain the summary of the latest
assessment, while the current remaining balance can change independently.

For example, a second extension on 10~April moves the due date to 31~May.
A receipt of USD~5 on 15~April reduces the claim to USD~15, while the March
assessment still records the USD~20 that remained when it was made. The
30~April assessment records zero overdue; the 30~June assessment records
USD~15 overdue. A 31~December assessment retains that amount. Year-end carry
preserves the three term versions and all assessment records, and opens a new
assessment window. Collection of the final USD~15 on 10~January 2028 clears
the claim; the 31~January assessment records zero. Final cash is USD~130,
receivables are zero and retained earnings are USD~20, as in the unamended
single-invoice example: these date changes altered the assessment history,
while the supplied sale and collection amounts determined the monetary result.

The immediate amendment takes effect on its event date. It requires the
current state hash, existing claim and customer identities, the expected
current term, a new term identity and explicit typed inputs. It refuses
backdating, a due date before effectiveness, duplicate identities and two
terms with the same effective date. An assessment seals its day against later
amendments or collections. An amendment followed by assessment on that same
day is allowed, so the order is explicit. Unknown required input remains
unresolved. The bounded maps admit at most 32~terms and 64~assessments per
claim; this reference does not silently discard old records when those limits
are reached.

These constructed date-only amendments and assessments produce no journal
entry. That is the declared scope of this method; an actual contract change
may require a separately specified measurement and accounting treatment.

remain outside this reference. The exact typed requests, historical states,
receipts, carry and negative cases are retained for two account charts in

without treating either operation as evidence for a real invoice population.



\paragraph{Term commands retain the meaning of the typed operations.}
An author can express this same lifecycle through
\texttt{ReceivableTermText/1}. The text imports four explicitly hashed
definitions: the current book, its schemas, the term-state configuration and
the period-report context. The configuration preserves the migration premise
and historical uncertainty already recorded in the state. The report context
supplies the opening book and account mapping needed for carry. None of these
facts is inferred from the spelling of a command.

Within those imports, an event selects \texttt{amend}, \texttt{assess} or
\texttt{collect}, names its identity and date, and states each input with its
provenance. For the March extension, these inputs identify the claim and
customer, the expected current term, the new term identity and due date,
the supplied premise and the explicit applicability choice. The compiler
assigns their declared types and retains unknown input as unknown. A carry
command names the closing identity, retained-earnings account and next period
boundary. Its cutoff must equal the current book date; its preservation policy
comes from the checked term operation. This profile does not let command text
replace that policy or silently advance the closing book.

meaning: the completed prefix and its state remain available, together with
the command that stopped execution. An unknown applicability input, for
example, stops at that event without turning the unknown into approval or
undoing an earlier valid extension. Although the reused grammar can express
standalone reports, this narrower term profile explicitly refuses that command.

\paragraph{A payment can change the decision that follows it.}
Separately supplied economic events can alter the state read by a scheduled
rule. The bounded \texttt{MixedEventTimeline/1} joins directly dated native
payments and inventory sales with the preceding decision calendar. It preserves
the native accounting operation and materializes each decision against the book
at its turn. Every action has an explicit date and priority; a same-day tie
remains unresolved rather than inheriting the order of the input array.

An order-sensitive example starts with cash of USD~452 and tax payable of
USD~10.25, split between carried vintages of USD~5.25 and USD~5. Management's
authored rule permits a one-unit inventory sale at USD~15 only if cash is below
USD~450. Paying USD~4 from the first tax vintage before the decision reduces
cash to USD~448 and enables the sale. Cash then becomes USD~463, total inventory
falls from USD~80 to USD~70, and the selected lot's carrying amount falls from
USD~20 to USD~10. The sale contributes USD~5 of profit; the payment reduces cash
and payable without creating a new expense.

Reverse the explicit same-day order and the decision sees cash of USD~452,
so it takes no action. The later payment leaves cash of USD~448 and inventory
of USD~80. In both cases tax payable is USD~6.25, with vintages of USD~1.25 and
USD~5. These differences follow from the specified threshold and order, not an
estimated company policy. The comparison checks the complete native journals,
state histories and financial reports as well as these independent controls.

The initial adapters accept allocated payments of carried tax vintages and the
existing proportional inventory sale. Their declared schedule date must match
the date inside the native event. Structural checks cover every supplied entry
before execution, but remaining cash, stock, payable and accounting findings
are assessed against the book at the relevant action. A later unknown vintage
or unavailable authorization therefore preserves the earlier successful prefix
and the exact failed event. An unresolved decision after a successful payment
retains that payment; it is not rolled back merely because the subsequent sale
could not be evaluated.

Assessment and period-boundary operations need different treatment. A current
tax assessment names an interval, a deferred measurement binds to a particular
state, and a carry changes the fiscal period. The mixed reference leaves these
operations, and nested return/lease envelopes, unsupported rather than assigning
them an arbitrary event date or rewriting a stale measurement. This is an
explicit implementation gap in the integrated timeline; the separate native
methods retain their existing scope.

Seventeen focused methods, 18 scheduler regression methods and 61 exact

the combined population to 64 actions, including at most 32 supplied native
entries, and retains the current book, successful outcomes, failed action and
remaining timeline on refusal. Its closed-path premise covers both supplied
events and decisions. General financing, richer calendars, historical
completeness and production durability remain open.


\paragraph{Available inputs do not establish a saved past calculation.}
The path's information date cannot also date the separate policy body or its
materializer. The synthetic extension gives the policy public availability on
10 March 2025 and the method on 12 March, both at calendar-day precision.
Even if the parent path is already available, the public dependency selection
cannot pass until 13 March. The query binds observations to the exact policy
and method bytes, then recomputes the generated path and its accounting result.
Changing the rule or materializer prevents reuse of the old observations.

That result is a consequence calculated from the eligible synthetic inputs.
It receives no invented authoring, publication, ingestion or acceptance date
of its own. Showing that a calculation actually existed before a cutoff needs
separate historical execution evidence. Eligible dependencies can also produce
a partial accounting result: availability does not supply missing payroll,
closing memory or a third-year opening.

The expression program and its assumptions introduce further dependencies.
Their information evidence is not inherited from the directly supplied
90-percent policy. The current expression and family examples therefore retain
archival scenario meaning, with historical, temporal and forecast admission
unestablished. Their draft content hashes use the existing company engine's
encoding, including its non-ASCII escaping. Import into the separately defined
portable record encoding requires an explicit identity mapping;
Section~\ref{sec:company-module-language} supplies that mapping for these
legacy drafts and retains the separate verification levels.

\paragraph{Interrupted work differs from incomplete accounting.}
Suppose evaluation stops after two members. A checkpoint records those exact
member results and the next member to evaluate, bound to the original family,
source and method. Resume validates the stored prefix by replay and evaluates
the requested number of new members. The ordering and identities prevent a
member from being skipped or counted twice. Different budget splits reproduce
the same final family.

The unknown-payroll and supplier-failure members are finished evaluations with
partial financial results. They are not unfinished computational work. Once
stored, they remain part of the completed prefix, and recovery continues with
the next member. This distinction is necessary to avoid repeatedly evaluating
a known unresolved case while silently excluding it from the family. Recovery
inside a member's year or event sequence remains outside this checkpoint.

The scheduling budget counts newly evaluated members. Validating a saved
prefix also takes work, recorded separately; the budget is not a claim about
total runtime. A changed source, model or method requires a new operation,
and a rehashed false prefix fails trusted replay. Neither changed semantics
nor a schema migration can be disguised as resume.

Publication of the checkpoint has its own work owner and generation. One
reference transition publishes the new checkpoint and immutable request
decision together. If acknowledgement is lost, either nothing was published
or the complete transition occurred. Retrying the exact request publishes it
in the first case and returns the saved response in the second. That response
remains the same after a later request advances the work head. Reusing the
identity with different request bytes conflicts; a new request with a stale
head does not advance it.

The reference rejects a world with only part of the checkpoint, decision and
generation update. It checks these publication meanings through explicit
before-and-after states and complete replay, without establishing physical
storage durability or changing the company's accounting state. A persistent
implementation, efficient recovery and compatible migrations remain distinct
engineering obligations.

\paragraph{Resolve a sector assessment without turning it into a common ledger.}
The common company world names a legal book and a component handle, but that
name alone does not tell us what the handle contains. A bank assessment can
carry account balances and an insurer assessment can carry only a head pointing
to a separately recorded balance digest. Treating both as one generic state
would lose the distinction that later accounting and reporting methods need.

The boundary therefore resolves one selected native case through its own
content-addressed registry. The request names the component and the exact case;
the resolver checks the native packet, schema, record identity, dependency
closure, source blobs, owner, book, currency, state, source and information
view. A changed record body, missing dependency, wrong schema, altered blob or
different book refuses before any method is called. The resulting world retains
the unrelated books and every unresolved obligation. Reference resolution is a
copy-and-identity operation, not a calculation.

The retained bank case illustrates why the context travels with the state. It
uses a synthetic US legal book, USD millions, a supplied US GAAP/CECL basis and
a solo perimeter. Its selected accounting state contains balances, while its
assessment profile declares \code{accounting\_effect=none}. The retained insurer
case uses a synthetic legal book, USD illustrative millions and a historical
EU IFRS~17 authority; its accounting head records a balances hash rather than
materialized balances, and its assessment profile also has no accounting
effect. These cases are not numerically compared. Their matching currency
labels do not establish matching units, reporting bases, dates, legal facts or
consolidation eligibility.

This distinction changes the implementation decision. A resolved assessment
can be cited by a later method through its native profile and view, but it
cannot post to the company book or authorize consolidation. The world remains
conditional when source, law, entity admission or method obligations are
unresolved. A later adapter must declare how a native state maps to the common
book, which accounts and memory it changes, and which evidence proves that
mapping. Until then, the exact native records are preserved beside the common
world rather than flattened into a convenient balance sheet.

The checked resolver covers one named bank case and one named insurer case.
It verifies 34 bank records and 11 insurer records in their selected closures,
including 21 and 6 source blobs respectively, and retains their byte hashes.
Those counts establish identity for the selected examples; they do not measure
sector coverage, accounting compliance, valuation validity, or a complete bank
or insurance engine. The native exchange validations remain the source of their
own conditional method results. The common boundary contributes a safe route
to those references and records the unresolved work required before posting.



\paragraph{A member's balances can depend on state held by the group.}
The corporate group introduces a different reason to preserve the native
representation. Its parent and subsidiary have separate balances, but their
state also includes shared inventory lots, returns, a compound instrument,
tax calculations and ownership changes. Copying each balance dictionary into
an independent company book would leave those dependencies behind. A later
event could then use a valid-looking opening balance without the facts needed
to determine its accounting consequences.

The reference adapter therefore selects a member within the complete group
state. A selector names the exact state record and the path
\code{/books/P} or \code{/books/S}; its identity differs from the identity of
the group record itself. The common company's reference points to that
selector. The result retains the selected balances, the whole native state
and its information view together. Record schemas, dependency references and
source bytes are checked before a selection is returned. The selector gives
access to a member's balances without asserting that they form a separately
executable book.

The two retained snapshots also show why time belongs in the relationship.
On 1 January 2025, the supplied parent ownership fraction is four-fifths and
the parent and subsidiary cash balances are 500 and 100. On 31 December 2025,
ownership is nine-tenths and cash is 491.5 and 152.5. Binding either fraction
to the entire supplied control interval would erase an ownership change.
The adapter instead attaches it to the selected snapshot day. It preserves
the supplied control premise and the archival, synthetic information view;
it does not determine control, recalculate consolidation or admit current
source evidence.

\paragraph{An elimination worksheet leaves the legal books intact.}
Separately valid company books permit a different operation. Consider a new
constructed example in USD units at 31 December 2025. Parent P holds cash 80,
an investment in subsidiary S of 100 and a receivable from S of 20; its equity
is 200. S holds cash 70 and equipment 50, owes P 20 and has capital of 100.
Both books balance. Assume that P controls and fully owns S, the books use
compatible accounting policies and measurement bases, the reciprocal balances
refer to the same obligation, and the investment equals the subsidiary's
capital with no acquisition difference. These are supplied facts of the
illustration. They do not describe the earlier four-fifths or nine-tenths
ownership case.

Adding the legal books gives assets of 320, liabilities of 20 and equity of
300. The worksheet then records two explicit adjustments. First, it credits
the investment by 100 and debits the subsidiary capital by 100. Second, it
credits the receivable by 20 and debits the payable by 20. Under the declared
signed-balance convention, each adjustment sums to zero. The resulting assets
are cash 150 and equipment 50; liabilities are zero and equity is 200. Cash
does not change. Neither legal book receives an entry: the adjustments belong
to the separate worksheet, alongside the unadjusted contributions.

The implementation requires a complete map from each local account to the
declared common chart. It checks the legal owners, case, currency, period,
snapshot date and classifications, and preserves each book's memory and cash
flow history. Every paired adjustment names its source accounts, expected
full balances and economic premise. A source account can be used only once;
the pair must come from different books and contain exactly opposite,
nonzero balances. This first operation supports the two declared pair roles,
receivable--payable and investment--equity. It refuses cash elimination,
partial allocations, unmatched balances, unresolved components and unsupported
sector books rather than supplying a balancing amount. A worksheet with no
declared adjustments remains an unadjusted combination.

Two versions of the illustration use different local account names and the
same explicit common-chart meanings. Both reproduce the complete signed
unadjusted balances, elimination deltas and adjusted balances; replay also
checks that the legal books remain unchanged. These checks establish the
finite worksheet calculation. The truth of the supplied economic premises
still requires evidence, and classification equality does not prove policy
equivalence. Noncontrolling interests, goodwill, tax, foreign exchange,
intragroup profit and period cash-flow eliminations require additional methods.
The result therefore remains a snapshot worksheet, not a complete consolidated
statement package or an executable group book.

\paragraph{Correct net cash can conceal incorrect gross cash flows.}
The snapshot worksheet leaves a period question unanswered: which legal-book
receipts and payments cross the group's boundary? An internal loan can produce
one company's investing payment and another company's financing receipt.
Adding the cash changes gives the right net amount, but retaining both flows
would misstate the group's gross activity. Matching activity labels is therefore
neither necessary nor sufficient to identify an internal transfer.

A separate constructed period illustrates the extension. At the start of
2025, P has cash 100, an investment in S of 100 and equity of 200; S has cash
100 and capital of 100. Continue to assume stable full ownership and control,
compatible policies and measurement bases, and investment cost equal to S's
capital. P then lends 30 to S, and S receives 20 from an external customer,
recognising income of 20. At year end P has cash 70 and a receivable of 30;
S has cash 150, a payable of 30 and income of 20. The investment and capital
remain unchanged. This constructed period has no interest, impairment,
acquisition difference or other transactions.

The opening worksheet removes the investment and capital of 100, leaving
group cash and equity of 200. The closing worksheet also removes the reciprocal
receivable and payable of 30, leaving cash 220 and equity including profit of
220. The period operation first obtains each legal book's own checked report.
It preserves all three original cash flows and names the internal payment and
receipt as a supplied pair. Removing that pair leaves an external operating
receipt of 20, no external payment and a net cash increase of 20. Profit of
20 also reconciles opening and closing group equity. The result is the same
whether both internal flows were labelled operating or the lender recorded
investing and the borrower financing.

A pair identifies the owner, flow identity and exact expected record on each
side, together with the supplied explanation connecting the transfers.
The dates and currencies must match; the amounts must be opposite and nonzero;
the owners must differ; and a flow cannot be removed twice. A changed record,
partial amount, duplicate reference or unexplained cash or equity movement
refuses. Opening and closing books must share the same period, chart,
ownership perimeter and compatibility assumptions. Each legal report retains
its own equity explanations and missing notes.

If the caller supplies no pair, the operation keeps the original gross flows.
It does not infer that two equal amounts describe the same transaction.
Likewise, an omitted closing reciprocal-balance adjustment leaves an extra
receivable and payable in the worksheet, even though equity still reconciles.
These cases remain visibly incomplete. By contrast, omitting the closing
investment adjustment breaks the declared equity bridge and refuses. The
distinction matters: reconciliation can expose contradictions without finding
every missing internal transaction.

The checked extension covers two account charts and both activity
classifications, with complete legal reports and group results preserved for
replay under \path{data/accounting-master/} in
\path{group-period-report-20261025}. It is a conditional period summary.
Actual source evidence, the completeness of paired transactions, indirect
operating cash flow, full notes and the wider group methods remain unresolved.
