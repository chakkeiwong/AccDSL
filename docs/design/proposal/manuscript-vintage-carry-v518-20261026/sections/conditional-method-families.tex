\section{A balanced return still needs a quantity limit}
\label{sec:conditional-method-families}

A return can be described by balanced monetary postings even when its quantity
exceeds the number of units sold. The preceding histories preserve unknown
amounts and explicit stock conditions. Applying an accounting method to those
amounts also requires a connection between its monetary calculations and its
selected economic prerequisites. This section develops that connection for a
restricted, exact example of returned goods.

The example retains an authored settlement dated 1 February 2025. Let \(n\)
be the returned quantity, \(p\) the refund price per unit, \(c\) the original
unit cost, \(d\) the reduction in value per unit and \(h\) the handling cost
per unit. Quantities are in units of goods; unit amounts are in dollars per
unit. Let \(N\) be the total recoverable amount of the returned goods in
dollars. The assumed calculations give refund cash \(R\), handling cash \(H\)
and inventory carrying amount \(V\):
\begin{align}
\label{eq:method-family-amounts}
 R&=np, & H&=nh, & V&=\min\{n(c-d),N\}.
\end{align}
These are the retained synthetic method's calculations. Its applicability to
an actual company's policy and contract still requires the corresponding facts
and authority. The purpose here is to preserve all conditional amounts within
this specified method and test its selected prerequisites.

\subsection{Keep both sides of the carrying-value minimum}

Suppose \(p=20\), \(c=12\), \(d=2\) and \(h=4\) dollars per unit. The
authored domains permit \(0\leq n\leq12\) units and \(5\leq N\leq30\)
dollars, with endpoints included. Refund and handling cash are linear in
\(n\). The minimum is represented by two disjoint cases:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:method-family-minimum}
 \begin{cases}
  V=10n, & 10n\leq N,\\
  V=N,   & N<10n.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Equality belongs to the first case. Every possible pair \((n,N)\) belongs
to exactly one case, so the compiler neither drops equality nor counts it as a
separate alternative. Each case retains the entire original input domain and
all monetary calculations. Nested minima retain the combinations of their
cases, subject to the declared finite branch limit.

The complete \path{PiecewiseMethodFamily/1} definition includes the selected
\path{MethodMonetaryEffects/1} record and its digest, one explicit interval and
premise for every input, output measurements, target obligations and the branch
limit. Entity, book, date, dimensions and scale are checked before arithmetic
is lowered to rational formulas. A closed singleton domain can supply a fixed
coefficient while its original declaration remains in the definition. Thus an
unknown \(n\) multiplied by the fixed \(p=20\) is supported. If both \(n\)
and \(p\) vary, their product is outside this affine profile; it is reported
as unsupported, rather than replaced by a guessed price.

The postings provide a separate algebraic check. Let \(L\) be the refund
liability amount being released and \(A\) the recovery asset being removed,
both expressed as positive dollar amounts. With debit-positive signs, the
retained method posts \(L\) to the refund liability, \(-A\) to the recovery
asset, \(V\) to inventory, \(R-L\) to revenue, \(A-V\) to cost of goods
sold, \(H\) to selling expense and \(-R-H\) to cash. Their sum is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:method-family-balance}
 L-A+V+(R-L)+(A-V)+H-R-H=0.
\end{equation}
Each term cancels regardless of the proposed return quantity. The compiler
requires this balance, and the cash-flow reconciliation, to simplify exactly;
it does not add a balancing amount. Equation~\eqref{eq:method-family-balance}
also explains why balance cannot supply the missing quantity restriction.

\subsection{Intersect the amounts with selected prerequisites}

The selected \path{MethodRequirements/3} definition supplies that restriction
through the sold quantity \(s\), as well as conditions on dates, component
identity, settlement state and rights. The joined
\path{PrerequisiteMethodFamily/1} embeds both complete definitions. Every
requirement input maps either to a shared monetary input or to an explicit
external known or unknown value. A shared mapping requires exactly the same
complete type and the original source reference on each side. Its proposed
premise explains the correspondence; matching labels alone does not establish
that two reported quantities refer to the same goods.

For the retained example, the relevant quantity and cash conditions include
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:method-family-selected-conditions}
 0<n\leq s,\qquad np=R_{\mathrm{cash}},\qquad
 nh=H_{\mathrm{cash}},
\end{equation}
where \(R_{\mathrm{cash}}\) and \(H_{\mathrm{cash}}\) are the supplied
external cash facts. With \(s=10\), \(R_{\mathrm{cash}}=40\),
\(H_{\mathrm{cash}}=8\), \(p=20\) and \(h=4\), the refund condition
requires \(20n=40\), hence \(n=2\). The handling condition independently
agrees: \(4n=8\). The quantity limit is also satisfied.

Both cases in equation~\eqref{eq:method-family-minimum} survive. For
\(20\leq N\leq30\), carrying amount is 20 dollars; for
\(5\leq N<20\), it equals \(N\). The result therefore retains a family
of possible carrying amounts and their dependence on recoverable value, even
though refund cash has fixed the returned quantity. Neither the two cases nor
the number of retained witnesses supplies a probability distribution.

Now fix \(n=11\), keep \(s=10\), and supply consistent refund and handling
cash of 220 and 44 dollars. Also fix \(N=20\), \(L=40\) and \(A=16\).
The method gives \(V=\min\{110,20\}=20\). Its eight posting amounts are
40, \(-16\), 20, 180, \(-4\), 44, \(-220\) and \(-44\) dollars;
they sum to zero. The monetary family consequently contains a balanced
assignment. The selected quantity predicate requires \(11\leq10\), so
the joined conditional family is empty. All inputs are singleton in this
counterexample, and constant evaluation leaves one minimum case to check.
A hypothetical quantity inconsistent with an imposed constraint makes this
intersection empty; it does not make the supplied record structurally malformed.

External findings retain a different role. In the selected settlement, a known
false finding that all return rights have closed yields the original
\path{unsupported} consequence. An unknown finding remains unresolved.
The solver cannot choose either finding to rescue an otherwise attractive
monetary assignment. Known dates and component identifiers likewise retain
the exact original comparisons. Across fixed findings the original consequence
priority is invalid, then unsupported, then unresolved.

The first joined profile evaluates fixed guards with the original evaluator.
An inactive body adds no condition, while the separate requirement that an
input be known still applies. A guard with unknown external support is
unresolved; a guard depending on a varying monetary input is unsupported in
this version. Numeric equalities and inequalities, conjunctions and products
with at most one varying operand can restrict the shared quantities.
General symbolic disjunctions and products of two varying quantities need
further methods.

A value in a feasible assignment is known \emph{within that hypothetical
assignment}. This satisfies the selected requirement's known-value test during
replay, but does not turn an unknown actual transaction into an observed fact.
Missing external facts, missing mapping premises and residual source or native
state obligations remain visible. A feasible numerical candidate with such
target obligations cannot establish that the intended actual history exists.

\subsection{Check the complete assignment and preserve unresolved search}

Every feasible branch is replayed through the unchanged monetary evaluator and
the unchanged prerequisite evaluator, using the same exact input assignment.
The checker also substitutes that assignment into every generated constraint.
It retains the parent branch identities, external values and reasons, mappings,
original expressions and source references. Another valid witness is acceptable;
reproducing the solver's first chosen value is not the membership criterion.
An infeasibility result retains named conflicting constraints and rechecks their
joint inconsistency.

A global finite cell budget and elapsed-time policy apply across the retained
branches. A witness from one visited branch establishes candidate existence,
while unvisited branches still prevent a complete-search claim. Conditional
emptiness requires every retained branch to be checked infeasible. Zero or
exhausted budgets leave the search unknown. Bounded Python validation and exact
replay are not hard-preempted at the elapsed deadline, and repeated searches near
that boundary can report different coverage.
