# Connected company-state survey: questions and source procedure

28 October 2026. This is the live protocol for S00–S09 in the unified program.
The source ledgers are an index of actual reading and remaining work, not a
claim that a systematic review has been completed. Previous source manifests
remain intact, with their historical reading scope.

## Questions that determine inclusion

Q1: What must a company remember to produce the required decisions, payments,
journals and reports? Include economic event/REA models, inverse transaction
reconstruction, dynamic-firm state, sufficient statistics and sector-specific
cohort/age/maturity representations.

Q2: What can observations identify? Include noisy/incomplete accounting reports,
observability, partial identification, Bayesian and set-valued updating, filtering
and smoothing, parameter learning, structural changes and missingness.

Q3: What information is required to simulate intervention and choice? Include
latent confounding, endogenous policy/disclosure, actor information, incomplete
contracts, equilibrium financing and nonanticipative control.

Q4: How do the same histories connect to cash flows and value? Include
accounting-based fundamental valuation, residual income, dividend/DCF identities,
terminal assumptions, operating/financing separation, stochastic discount
factors and structural credit. Forecastability, correct prices and identification
are separate questions.

Q5: How should the observation model represent unreliable reports? Include
accrual estimation errors, earnings quality, misstatements, real activities,
forensic reconstruction and source corroboration. Distinguish evidence of
anomalies, evidence of incorrect reporting and evidence of intent.

Q6: How can the alternatives be represented and computed? Include factored
models, symbolic constraints, exact state equivalence, POMDP beliefs, particles,
robust ambiguity sets and adapted scenario reduction. Compare both full
conditional output laws and decision feasibility, not just forecast means.

## Search, screening and source checks

Search the preserved local library and its existing ledgers first, then author,
publisher and institutional sites and bibliographic indexes. Search phrases
combine the six questions with accounting, company/firm, financial statements,
cash flow, valuation, hidden state, observability, fraud, simulation and
control. Log exact queries and results; a failed or empty search is not a
negative finding about the literature. Preserve source versions and deduplicate
working papers versus articles without silently replacing either.

Classify every screened candidate as foundational, direct method, competitor,
survey/tutorial, software, empirical example, background, peripheral,
superseded, blocked or quarantined. Title screening only decides the next
reading action. A paper enters technical support only after the relevant
model/method, assumptions, derivation/theory, evaluation and appendix portions
are inspected to the depth required by the local claim. For implementation
choices, inspect official code and licence/version as well.

For each seed, inspect related work and comparison sections and follow all
references plausibly material to the questions. Forward screening records
highly cited and recent citing work, extensions, corrections and negative
results. The initial five-high/five-recent query is a bounded discovery batch,
not a saturation claim. Continue until every material branch has foundational,
direct and competing arguments and all consequential omission risks have a
defensible disposition. Retain accessible full text; record failure/status
without treating abstracts or metadata as technical evidence.

Check publisher/deposit versions and correction/retraction metadata before
promoting a paper's claims. An index field saying no retraction is a dated
metadata observation, not a complete integrity check. Citation counts and venue
metadata describe visibility; missing values stay unavailable, not zero.
Source access failures block only the claims that need that source.

## Required records and synthesis

The directory contains source-support.json, citation-venue.json,
backward-snowball.json, forward-snowball.json, claim-support.json and
omitted-paper-risk.json. search-log.json records retrieval and screening.
Each primary reading retains exact local identity and allowed/forbidden use.
Every consequential manuscript claim names its source section/equation or its
local derivation. Separate a source's claim from a local transfer to AccDSL.

The comparative synthesis asks the same questions of every branch: company
state, observation process, actor information, identified quantities, actions,
forecast/valuation target, uncertainty, failure, computation and evidence.
Compare competing treatments on shared cases. In particular, the finite-horizon
DCF/residual-income comparison must inspect the Penman–Sougiannis versus
Lundholm–O'Keefe exchange and Penman's reply, not inherit a one-sided summary.

The initial batch reuses two 28 October source manifests, inspects Givan et al.
sections2.2–3.3 and appendix proofs5/7, and visually reads Kaelbling et al.
sections3.1–3.4 (printed105–108). The latter PDF has almost no usable extracted

sections2–3.1; its garbled extracted formulas require visual review before
any equation is adopted. Empirical claims remain unevaluated.

The final hostile review asks whether a skeptical reader would find a missing
foundational/competing paper, unsupported transfer, inconsistent pricing
measure, prior-driven identification, circular use of market prices, hindsight
in evidence, or unexamined reporting incentive. Failures create repair tasks.
Full-paper audits, additional sectors and broader forensic primary readings
remain internal work; survey infrastructure alone does not close MC29.
