Company Histories,
Accounting Interpretations,

Product and implementation design

Version 1.0   2 October 2026




This document specifies a system that translates company disclosures into traceable evidence,
represents several compatible economic and accounting histories, and simulates their future
journals and financial statements under explicit economic scenarios.
The core requirements, data contracts, semantics and acceptance cases govern implementation.
Proposed libraries can be replaced while preserving those contracts. Sector rule packages
require their own accounting review before they can be described as compliant with a reporting
standard.

Chapter 1

The product decision

A company’s accounts describe the financial consequences of its activities, but they leave much
of the activity itself unobserved. Revenue combines many sales, a borrowing balance combines
contracts with different terms, and a provision combines estimates about uncertain future
payments. Aggregation is necessary for reporting. It also makes the inverse task—recovering
economic history from published reports—fundamentally different from entering known trans-
actions into a ledger.
AccDSL will preserve this distinction. Its first product is an evidence-backed representation of
a company’s historical record. Its second is a set of compatible histories and current economic
states. Its third is a collection of conditional simulations, each explaining how a particular his-
tory, accounting treatment and business response produces future journals and reports. These
products share identifiers and provenance, so an analyst can move from a forecast balance to
the assumption or disclosure responsible for it.
The recommended design is a small, typed accounting language over a shared intermediate
representation, combined with an evidence database, contract calculators and replaceable in-
ference engines. A language earns its place by making recurring accounting decisions explicit
and checkable: which entity and period an amount belongs to, whether it is known or assumed,
which rule applies, and how an event changes a balanced ledger. The language does not supply
missing facts or determine a company’s future behaviour.


1.1 Why journals are necessary but insuﬀicient

Consider a company with a borrowing of 100 currency units and annual interest expense of
5. Assume that the entire principal was outstanding throughout the year, interest was paid at
year-end, and there were no fees, defaults, hedges or foreign-exchange effects. Both a fixed-rate
loan at 5% and a floating-rate loan whose rate happened to be 5% generate the same entries:

                         Account                          Debit   Credit
                         Cash, on initial borrowing         100
                         Borrowing liability                         100
                         Interest expense, at year-end        5
                         Cash                                           5

Even the full historical journal would not distinguish these two contracts. Now impose a
scenario in which the floating rate resets immediately to 7% for the whole next year. The fixed

loan produces interest of 5; the floating loan produces interest of 7. If the disclosure is also
compatible with any fixed-rate fraction α ∈ [0, 1], future interest is

                           I(α) = 100[0.05α + 0.07(1 − α)] = 7 − 2α.                          (1.1)

The historical accounts are compatible with the whole interval [5, 7] of future interest. This is a
range of feasible amounts. It becomes a probability distribution only when a distribution for α
is supplied. Neither the number of generated interpretations nor a language model’s confidence
provides that distribution.
Journals remain essential because simulated economic events must have coherent accounting
consequences. However, the simulated state must also contain contract terms, maturities, cus-
tomer or borrower cohorts, collateral, unrecognised commitments, and management decisions.
These determine what the journals will be under a changed economy. Reconstructing every
historical transaction is unnecessary when a smaller state preserves the responses needed for
the intended scenarios. Establishing that smaller state is an explicit modelling task, examined
in Chapter 5.
The analogy with competing legal interpretations is useful at the level of representation. Sev-
eral readings may survive the evidence and require separate reasoning. In accounting, some
alternatives concern the meaning of a disclosure; others concern undisclosed facts or future
behaviour. The system must identify which kind of uncertainty is involved. A known contrac-
tual term must not become an arbitrary interpretive choice merely because another choice is
convenient for a simulation.


1.2 Users, questions and deliverables

An accounting analyst needs to inspect a number in its original table, verify its entity, currency
and period, and see how it reconciles with the notes. A financial modeller needs to distinguish
a reported balance from assumptions about its composition. A language engineer needs de-
terministic semantics and tests. A researcher needs to compare explanations under the same
scenario, including explanations that fit history equally well.
The first release must answer the following complete workflow. An analyst loads a set of
reports and specifies an information cutoff. AccDSL preserves the original documents and
extracts source-linked observations. It identifies reconciliation failures and constructs an ex-
plicitly bounded family of compatible histories. The analyst declares additional assumptions
where simulation requires them. The system executes each requested scenario and returns
statements, journal traces, explanation-specific distributions or bounds, and unresolved limita-
tions. When a later filing arrives, the analyst can compare the revised interpretation with the
earlier information set.

with cash, receivables, payables, inventory, fixed assets, ordinary debt and ordinary equity.
Banks and insurers must be representable in the core data model from the outset, but their
executable measurement and behavioural packages follow dedicated milestones. Until a package
supports an instrument, the result is explicitly incomplete.


1.3 What constitutes a useful first implementation

The first demonstrator should reconstruct three reporting dates and the intervening annual
and interim flows for one company, including at least one restatement or revised comparative.
It must preserve a borrowing-composition ambiguity and execute the fixed/floating example
through both journals and all affected statement lines. A synthetic company supplies known
hidden history; a public company supplies realistic disclosure and extraction problems. Their
roles differ: the synthetic case tests preservation and rejection of known alternatives, while the
public case tests traceability and practical coverage.
Success is a reproducible chain of evidence and computation, with identified gaps. A convincing-
looking complete ledger reconstructed from incomplete disclosures would fail this product con-
tract if its assumptions were hidden. Forecast accuracy requires a later evaluation with histor-
ical information cutoffs and suitable baselines; it is not established by the first demonstrator.

2.1 The record is a collection of assertions

The original record includes financial statements, notes, accounting policies, auditor opinions,
management discussion, segment tables, security terms, regulatory returns and subsequent an-
nouncements. A complete ingestion process accounts for every page and every extracted block.
A complete financial model is a stronger claim: it would require economically relevant infor-
mation that public reports may never disclose. AccDSL therefore reports document coverage,
semantic coverage and simulation coverage separately.
Each source span receives a disposition: structured observation, narrative assertion, relation-
ship, policy evidence, contextual material, duplicate, or unresolved extraction. “Unresolved”
remains visible. A commitment buried in a note is not discarded because it has no current
balance-sheet line. An auditor’s qualification is retained as evidence about the record; it does
not become an automatic numerical haircut. Management’s forecast is a dated assertion with
an author and modality, not an observed future outcome.
An observation is an immutable record with the fields below. Values inferred from observations
are separate records, linked by a derivation. This avoids overwriting a company’s statement
with an analyst’s preferred reconstruction.

A narrative assertion adds actor, predicate, object, scope, quantifier and modality. For example,
“substantially all debt is fixed rate” has a source span and a qualitative quantifier. It does not
assert α = 1. A reviewed interpretation can introduce a numerical restriction such as α ≥ a,
but the threshold a remains an analyst assumption unless the source defines it. Boilerplate
policy language also does not prove that a company holds a particular instrument.


2.2 Different names, different measurements

An accounting concept is more than a row label. “Debt” can refer to legal principal, amortised
carrying amount, undiscounted contractual cash flows or a fair-value disclosure. These amounts
can all be correct at the same date. Likewise, “revenue” may be reported gross or net, a segment
measure may differ from the consolidated measure, and an alternative performance measure
may exclude selected expenses. A mapping must state its relationship: equivalent meaning,
component of, broader than, presentation reclassification, or measurement transformation.
The common vocabulary will preserve source concepts as first-class identifiers. Company exten-
sions may inherit a broad economic category without being declared identical to a standardised
concept. For instance, an extension can be a component of borrowings while retaining an unre-
solved split between lease liabilities and other financing. Many-to-many mappings are allowed;
each mapping has applicable dates, dimensions and evidence. An aggregation mapping does
not silently become a measurement conversion.
Standards differ in recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure. Consequently, con-
version between IFRS, US GAAP and Chinese Accounting Standards for Business Enterprises
(ASBE) is not generally a dictionary lookup. The original basis remains preserved, and any
comparison basis is a separate derived book. The same principle applies to tax and prudential
capital. A tax loss carryforward, accounting deferred tax asset and regulatory capital deduction
can concern the same economic loss while belonging to different measurement systems.


2.3 Time, revisions and information availability

At least four times matter. A machine may enter service in November; its cost appears in the
accounts for the year ending December; the annual report is published in March; and AccDSL
ingests the report in April. A later report may revise the December comparative. These times
answer different questions and must remain distinct.
The storage model uses validity intervals for economic assertions and transaction intervals for
database versions, following the distinction in Snodgrass and Ahn (1986). Publication and
availability timestamps are additional domain fields. Database transaction time records what
AccDSL contained; it cannot substitute for what investors could have known. An “as known
on” query applies an explicit availability rule and does not include a later restatement simply
because its economic period is earlier.
CHAPTER 2. REPRESENTING A COMPANY’S HISTORICAL RECORD                                           6


AccDSL supports three named views: the original filing view; the latest restated view, with a
stated information cutoff; and a harmonised research view, with explicit transformations. No
view changes the stored source facts. Corrections have links to the replaced assertion and a
reason. Where two sources conflict without a clear supersession relationship, both survive with
a conflict record.
Instant values and interval flows have different types. A balance at 30 June is not a six-month
flow. Calendar intervals use half-open boundaries [t0 , t1 ) internally; a filing period ending 30
June maps to the next boundary at 1 July in its reporting calendar. Quarter-only earnings
may be derived from year-to-date values only if the periods, basis, perimeter, units and revision
vintage are compatible. The derivation retains both source observations and their correlated
rounding uncertainty. Fifty-two/fifty-three-week calendars, stub periods and reporting-date
changes require explicit calendar objects.
Accounting-policy changes, estimate changes and error corrections also have different temporal
effects. The applicable rule package decides whether a change is prospective, requires restate-
ment, or adjusts opening balances. The engine never infers that effect from a generic “revision”
flag.


2.4 The company boundary changes

Legal entity, reporting group and economic exposure are separate dimensions. Acquisitions,
disposals, changes in control, joint arrangements, special-purpose vehicles and non-controlling
interests can change the reporting perimeter. A group’s increase in property need not be capital
expenditure; it may reflect an acquisition, a currency translation or a revaluation. The bridge
between opening and closing property must identify these movements before a scenario model
can extrapolate investment.
The company graph therefore stores dated ownership, control and counterparty relationships.
Control is an accounting conclusion with supporting evidence, not a rule that blindly consol-
idates every majority-owned entity. Group reporting combines entity books with a separate
adjustment and elimination book. Intragroup receivables and payables require matching coun-
terparties, currencies and dates. Unmatched eliminations remain unresolved; a balancing “plug”
is not a recovered transaction.
Acquired opening balances, goodwill, purchase-price allocation, divested operations and non-
controlling interests each have their own event types. Analyses can preserve reported scope or
define a constant perimeter, but the latter is a derived modelling assumption, not the original
record.


2.5 Further problems that the representation must handle

Several diﬀiculties extend beyond differences in standards, industries and company labels.
Recognition and settlement occur on different dates: a sale, invoice, cash receipt and im-
pairment can belong to four periods. Net balances hide gross flows: repeated borrowing and
repayment can leave the same closing debt. Netting can conceal offsetting exposures and
liquidity needs. Different contractual maturities and repricing dates can share the same cur-
rent carrying amount. Guarantees, undrawn credit facilities, litigation and covenants can be
economically important before an asset or liability is recognised.
Foreign currency introduces transaction currency, functional currency and presentation cur-
rency, together with different translation conventions for stocks and flows. Currency translation
CHAPTER 2. REPRESENTING A COMPANY’S HISTORICAL RECORD                                         7


effects must not be mistaken for cash receipts. Inventory and depreciation depend on vintages
or lots; an aggregate stock and average age may be insuﬀicient under a shock. Tax bases can
evolve differently from carrying amounts. Deferred tax recognition requires assumptions about
recoverability, rather than multiplication by a tax rate alone.
Public information is selectively disclosed and rounded. Duplicate XBRL facts may agree,
overlap within rounding bounds or contradict one another. A dash can mean zero, immaterial,
unavailable or inapplicable. Tables can mix units, span pages and embed signs in parentheses.
Text and table references can be wrong in the source itself. AccDSL retains these distinctions
and gives an analyst a local repair path without pretending that extraction confidence settles
economic interpretation.
Finally, strategic behaviour matters. A firm may refinance, cut investment, raise equity, change
prices, breach a covenant or fail. A bank may preserve a capital ratio by shrinking assets; an
insurer may change reinsurance or asset allocation. Such responses cannot be deduced from the
balance-sheet identity. They require explicitly calibrated or assumed policies, and alternative
policies must remain separate when the available record does not distinguish them.
Chapter 3

What the literature contributes

The research problem crosses accounting, databases, programming languages, econometrics
and document understanding. Each field resolves part of it. The review below distinguishes
results that can be adopted directly, engineering ideas that need adaptation, and evidence that
does not establish the intended product capability. Source versions and technical anchors are
recorded in the accompanying research ledgers. The search includes work outside the original
collection, with public-index searches and backward and forward citation searches through 2
October 2026. It is a scoped design survey, rather than a claim to enumerate every paper in
these fields.


3.1 Accounting structure and company dynamics

Nehmer and Robinson (1997), especially Sections 2–5, supplies a useful algebraic starting point:
balances occupy a structured state space, transactions change that state, and aggregation re-
lates detailed accounts to reports. This supports a distinction between conservation constraints
and permitted transactions. An entry can conserve the accounting total and still be an inad-
missible transaction. AccDSL adopts that distinction in its posting validator. Its particular
event vocabulary, evidence model and inverse-history semantics are new design decisions, not
results established by the algebraic paper.
Doshi (2024) develops an accounting DSL with time, transaction logs and correction mecha-
nisms. The relevant material is in Chapters 4 and 7–9 of the 2024 thesis; the original collec-
tion’s filename misleadingly suggests 2026. It demonstrates why corrections and dates belong
in the language rather than in informal comments. The thesis does not establish recovery of
an entire company history from public reports or inference over economically distinct histories.
Babcinețchi et al. (2022), pp. 342–345, offers a much smaller accounting language and grammar
proposal. It is useful as a syntax precedent, but it provides insuﬀicient semantics or evaluation
to govern AccDSL by itself.
Shahnazarian (2004) connects accounting dynamics with behavioural and econometric equa-
tions. Sections 2.3–2.8 describe the accounting relationships; the optimisation and econometric
discussion in Chapter 3 and the data and evaluation material in Chapters 4 and 8 show how the
model is used with standardised Swedish corporate data. This is an important precedent for
simulating companies under changed conditions. Its standardised tax-data starting point differs
from heterogeneous public-report extraction, and its working-capital funds formulation should
not be imported as a general modern cash-flow statement implementation. The transferable
idea is the explicit separation of accounting evolution and behavioural assumptions.

CHAPTER 3. WHAT THE LITERATURE CONTRIBUTES                                                    9


Pierson (2020), Sections 3–4 and the reporting structures, makes that separation operational
in system dynamics. Receivables, payables and wages accumulate differences between accrual
and payment flows; reporting-period accumulators turn instantaneous flows into statements.
AccDSL can use these structures as examples of coherent movement schedules. The model’s
payment delays, inventory approximations and simplified equity treatment remain modelling
choices. They are not universal defaults for companies or evidence that an aggregate state is
suﬀicient for all shocks.
Bank models demonstrate why journal generation needs economic state. Halaj (2016), Section
3, combines funding shocks, fire sales, loans, securities and risk constraints. The homogeneous
portfolios and specified funding process make the model tractable, but limit instrument-level
interpretation. Behn et al. (2019), Section 2, makes managers’ choices over asset growth, debt,
risk and payouts depend on capital and liquidity constraints. These models motivate interfaces
for management policy and regulatory calculations. Their parameterisations must be calibrated
and evaluated for the target application; passing their accounting identities would not validate
an AccDSL forecast.
Two important neighbouring traditions remain explicit coverage qualifications. McCarthy’s
1982 REA model is a foundational candidate for the relation between resources, events and
agents; Caiani and co-authors’ stock-flow-consistent agent-based benchmark is a candidate for
simulation across interacting sectors. Verified primary PDFs could not be obtained in this
retrieval campaign after institutional and publisher attempts. Their records are retained in the
omission register, and no technical result from either is used to justify the design. AccDSL’s
event and counterparty records are specified and justified directly below. A later multi-company
equilibrium engine would require expansion of the stock-flow-consistent literature before imple-
mentation.


3.2 Incomplete information and provenance

Financial disclosures are incomplete databases in a substantive sense. Multiple underlying
records can yield the same published observations. Libkin (2014), Sections 2–5, explains the
semantics of incomplete databases, labelled nulls, conditional tables and certain answers. Re-
peated occurrences of the same unknown must share a value. Conditional tables can represent
dependence between alternatives that independent missing cells cannot express. This is directly
relevant to a debt note and a cash-flow table that refer to the same unknown repayment.
AccDSL adopts shared symbolic unknowns and conditions. It extends the database idea with
accounting constraints, continuous amounts, temporal transitions and economic event models.
A “certain” answer means a statement that holds for every history in the declared feasible
family. The family itself may omit unknown mechanisms; certainty is therefore always relative
to a model scope. Imieliński and Lipski’s original 1984 paper is a foundational backward lead
whose publisher copy was inaccessible in this campaign. The inspected Libkin paper supplies
the technical discussion actually used here.
Green et al. (2007), Definitions 3.1–3.2 and Section 4, treats provenance as annotations that
compose through relational operations. Combining sources and alternative derivations can
therefore have formal semantics, rather than being a list of unrelated citations. This is use-
ful for report aggregation and explanation queries. Positive relational operations covered by
provenance semirings do not by themselves specify every operation AccDSL needs: negation,
nonlinear valuation, optimisation and probabilistic conditioning require their own derivation
records. Accordingly, the implementation starts with a typed dependency graph and may use
semiring annotations for supported relational subqueries. A dependency edge is not a posterior
CHAPTER 3. WHAT THE LITERATURE CONTRIBUTES                                                       10


probability.
Snodgrass and Ahn (1986) distinguishes historical validity from the time facts are stored. The
archived paper is Temporal Databases, by Snodgrass and Ahn, not the later Jensen–Snodgrass
paper initially returned by a search. Its snapshot, rollback, historical and temporal examples
support the need to recover both old business facts and old database beliefs. AccDSL adds
public availability time because a database insertion date cannot establish whether a historical
investor could have seen a filing.


3.3 Inverse problems, identification and probability

Stuart (2010), Section 2.2, starts from an observation equation y = G(u) and distinguishes
inversion, regularisation and Bayesian inference. The discussion makes explicit that a regular-
ising norm or prior is an additional assumption. AccDSL has a similar observation problem,
although its unknowns include discrete accounting interpretations and event structures as well
as continuous parameters. The infinite-dimensional well-posedness theorems in that paper are
not automatically applicable to this mixed, potentially discontinuous model.
The relevant issue is often identification rather than computation alone. Giacomini and Kita-
gawa (2021), Sections 2.1–2.6 and Theorem 1 of the archived 2020 working version, shows how
a class of priors can preserve uncertainty within an identified set. A likelihood that is flat over
the unresolved part does not learn the conditional prior over that part. Posterior lower and
upper probabilities summarise a class of posterior beliefs. The paper’s regularity conditions
and application to structural vector autoregressions do not prove that an arbitrary account-
ing program admits the same algorithms or guarantees. The design borrows the distinction
between learnable quantities and assumption-dependent distributions; the simple accounting
bounds in Chapter 5 are derived directly.
Hoeting et al. (1999), equations (1)–(3), gives Bayesian model averaging as a weighted sum
of model-conditional distributions, with weights determined by model priors and marginal
likelihoods. Sections 3 and 5 discuss computation and prior specification. This supports
an optional weighted mode, provided AccDSL specifies the likelihood, priors and candidate
model space. It does not justify giving equal mass to every syntactically different journal
reconstruction. Two encodings of the same economic history must not gain extra weight by
duplication. Model averaging also remains conditional on the considered model family, rather
than repairing missing mechanisms.
Scenario semantics need similar care. Pearl (2009), Section 3.2.1 and equations (5)–(6), distin-
guishes an intervention that replaces a structural equation from ordinary conditioning. AccDSL
will distinguish a supplied exogenous path, an observational conditional forecast and an inter-
vention. A rise in rates imposed by a stress test is not automatically distributed as the collection
of historical periods in which rates happened to be high. Identifying a causal effect requires
assumptions beyond reproducing accounts.


3.4 Languages for rules and contracts

van Deursen et al. (2000) surveys DSL benefits, risks and implementation options. Mernik
et al. (2005), Sections 1.1 and 2.2–2.5 of the archived CWI report, treats application libraries
as serious competitors and distinguishes deciding, analysing, designing and implementing a
DSL. Its implementation decision tree explicitly includes embedding in a host language. The
literature therefore supports a conditional design decision: a DSL is attractive when domain
CHAPTER 3. WHAT THE LITERATURE CONTRIBUTES                                                      11


constraints and repeated notation justify it, but an ordinary typed library may be cheaper and
equally expressive. AccDSL will implement one shared representation accessible from both a
Python API and a textual language, so syntax does not become a prerequisite for testing the
accounting model.
Merigoux et al. (2021), Sections 3–4 and Figures 8–9, provides Catala’s scopes, exceptions,
default calculus and translation into a conventional functional target. Its explicit empty and
conflict outcomes are valuable precedents for accounting rules with exceptions. They are differ-
ent from probabilistic uncertainty. AccDSL will preserve this distinction: conflicting applicable
rules produce a diagnostic, while an evidence-supported alternative reading produces a condi-
tional model branch. The paper’s compiler correctness results do not establish correctness of
our accounting rules or of an automatic translation from disclosures.
The inspected LegalMath code offers reusable ideas in its shared typed interpretation, partial
observations, source references and validity/knowledge times. Its ordinary frontend execution
selects a reading. AccDSL needs an additional execution mode that retains a family of readings,
continuous unknowns and economic state. A bounded candidate generator is useful for search,
but its candidate limit does not prove completeness.
Peyton Jones et al. (2000), Sections 3–5, builds complex financial contracts from combinators
and separates their compositional meaning from a chosen valuation model. The ACTUS tech-
nical specification similarly separates contract states, scheduled events, state transitions and
payoffs, with risk factors supplied through an interface (ACTUS Financial Research Founda-
tion, 2026). These are strong reasons to represent contractual obligations independently of
accounting classifications. Neither contract cash flows nor a derivative price determine the
recognition, measurement and presentation rules of a complete IFRS or US GAAP book.


3.5 Extraction and financial reasoning

The document literature supports a staged ingestion process. PubTables-1M (Smock et al.,

3.6 Solvers and probabilistic execution

de Moura and Bjørner (2008), Sections 2–3, describes Z3’s interfaces and theory-solving archi-
tecture. It supports choosing an SMT solver for logical choices and arithmetic constraints, but
does not make arbitrary nonlinear simulation tractable. The first solver fragment is deliberately
bounded: finite choices, exact linear arithmetic and selected calendar relations. Unsupported
formulas remain in the representation with a solver diagnostic.
van de Meent et al. (2018) develops probabilistic programs through sampling, observations
and inference; the inspected material includes the first-order language and weighted sampling
discussion. Stan (Carpenter et al., 2017), its model language and sampler-output discussion,
and Pyro (Bingham et al., 2019), Sections 2–3, show different ways to separate a probabilistic
model from inference machinery. They are candidates for optional inference over an already-
defined economic model. Exact accounting identities and discrete rule selection should not be
turned into nearly satisfied likelihood penalties merely to fit an inference library. Nor should
successful sampler execution be described as evidence that all interpretations were explored.


3.7 The remaining design problem

The combined literature provides algebra for journals, semantics for missing information, tem-
poral storage, provenance, contract languages, rule languages, inference methods and extraction
tools. The unresolved integration problem is to retain their distinctions while connecting them:
a disclosure constrains economic histories; a history supplies contract and behavioural state;
a scenario drives events; accounting rules produce journals; reporting rules render statements;
and every step remains explainable.
The design is therefore an integration proposal with several local mathematical constructions.
Its originality and empirical advantage have not been established by this survey. Its practical
merit will depend on the acceptance cases, accounting review and comparative implementation
work in Chapter 10.

4.4 Responsibilities that AccDSL must implement

AccDSL owns the evidence and temporal model, typed accounting concepts, shared unknowns
and alternatives, rule-package selection, journal validation, consolidation provenance and re-
port explanations. It also owns the distinction between feasible sets and distributions. These
responsibilities cannot be delegated implicitly to whichever parser or solver is convenient.
External components receive narrow requests and return typed responses. A contract adapter
returns scheduled events, cash-flow quantities, state changes and source/model references; it
does not directly mutate the ledger. A valuation adapter returns a measure, currency, date,
method and numerical status; the accounting package decides whether and where to recog-
nise the change. A solver returns feasibility or bounds with scope and completeness status.
A language model proposes source-linked mappings or interpretations; it cannot commit an
unsupported assertion as fact.
The architecture consequently permits deterministic reference execution, alternative solvers and
later acceleration without changing the meaning of a historical observation or a journal entry.
Performance work begins only after the same semantic cases pass through these interfaces.

5.1 Define what is observed before inverting it

Let Dk be the disclosures available under information cutoff k. Each numeric observation di has
a context: entity or group perimeter, concept, measurement basis, dimensions, currency, period
and precision. These contexts are part of the observation operator, not additional columns that
can be discarded after extraction.
A candidate history h contains an opening economic and accounting state, events through
the last historical date T , contract terms, accounting policies, consolidation relationships and
latent quantities. Let M denote the declared model scope: its event templates, admissible rule
versions and allowed unknowns. It excludes arbitrary events that the chosen model has no way
to represent. The reporting operator Oi (h) produces the quantity to which observation i refers,
including its basis and perimeter.
Reported numbers are often rounded. If a number vi is rounded to the nearest unit qi , an
illustrative admissible interval is [vi − qi /2, vi + qi /2) under the corresponding tie convention.
The actual rounding convention, scale and XBRL accuracy metadata govern the imported
constraint. A disclosure with unspecified precision does not acquire a convenient tolerance
silently. Write Ii for the justified observation interval, and CM (h) for the joint accounting,
contractual and semantic constraints. Then the historical compatibility set is

              HM (Dk ) = {h ∈ HM : CM (h) ∧ Oi (h) ∈ Ii for every applicable i}.              (5.1)

Qualitative assertions enter as reviewed logical conditions, rather than numeric intervals. In-
compatible source contexts are never forced into the same equation.
Equation (5.1) distinguishes three outcomes. An empty set means that the observations and
declared model are inconsistent. A singleton means that this model identifies a history at its
chosen resolution. A larger set preserves unresolved alternatives. An empty set does not prove
that the company’s accounts are false: a wrong extraction, missing event type or incorrect
perimeter can cause the inconsistency. A singleton does not prove that the model contains the
real history.

5.2 Why published statements cannot identify all transactions

The simplest derivation uses fixed account mappings and a fixed basis. Let z ∈ Rn contain
unknown historical movement amounts. For m disclosed aggregates, let A ∈ Rm×n be the
aggregation matrix and y = Az ∈ Rm . If there is a nonzero vector v with Av = 0, then

                                    A(z + v) = Az + Av = y.

Both movement vectors give the same reports whenever both satisfy the remaining constraints.
Thus the feasible family is the aﬀine set z+ker A intersected with those constraints. Accounting
requirements can shrink this family, but do not generally eliminate it. For example, cash
collections in a receivables bridge are constrained by

      ART = AR0 + credit sales − collections − write-offs + other classified movements.

If the note does not distinguish acquisitions, currency effects and write-offs, several collections
paths may fit the same closing balance.
There is a second ambiguity beyond aggregation. In Equation (1.1), identical historical move-
ments arise from different contractual reset terms. Even recovering z exactly would leave these
terms unidentified. AccDSL therefore infers economically relevant states and contract terms
alongside journal movements. A linear inverse solver over statement lines alone cannot solve
this second problem.


5.3 The accounting state and its invariants

For one entity book with na accounts in one functional currency, let bt ∈ Qna be signed account
balances, with debit amounts positive and credit amounts negative. Exact decimal amounts
can be represented as rationals with a power of ten denominator. A journal batch jt contains
the signed functional-currency amounts posted by one atomic event or explicitly grouped event
batch. Its balance condition is

                                   1T jt = 0,       b t = bt − + j t .                        (5.2)

If 1T b0 = 0, substitution gives 1T bt = 1T bt− + 1T jt = 0. Induction establishes conservation
through every committed batch. Opening balances must therefore be supplied as a balanced,
explained entry or a balanced imported state with unresolved composition explicitly identified.
This proof establishes a bookkeeping invariant. It does not establish that an amount belongs
in the chosen account, that a contract exists, or that an accounting standard permits a treat-
ment. Posting validation additionally checks account roles, ownership, currency conversions,
applicability of rules and event preconditions.
The balance vector includes temporary revenue and expense accounts during the period. Period
closing transfers their net balance to equity through balanced entries. A reporting function
can include current profit in equity before closing, or use the closed book afterwards, but
must not include it twice. Income and cash-flow reports use classified movements and exclude
mechanical closing transfers from operating flows. Consequently statements are functions of
balances, journals, contract state and reporting rules:

                              Y[u,v) = R(bv− , J[u,v) , Xv− , ρ, [u, v)).                     (5.3)

Here ρ specifies the reporting basis and perimeter, and X contains economic state beyond
ledger balances. Cash-flow classification is part of R, not recoverable from the closing cash
balance alone.

5.6 Resolution and computational limits

For a requested horizon H and scenario family S, histories may be grouped only when they
have the same conditional law for all requested outputs:

             h1 ∼H,S h2      ⇐⇒    Kh1 ,s = Kh2 ,s on those outputs for every s ∈ S.

This definition explains why individual sales can sometimes be replaced by cohorts, while fixed
and floating loans usually cannot be merged for an interest-rate stress. Exact equivalence can
be diﬀicult to prove. A grouping justified only by approximate numerical agreement carries
an error criterion and is not labelled exact. Source provenance remains available even when
execution shares a common state.
Bounds require comparable honesty. A linear program can provide certified extrema for a
supported linear model. A set of feasible sampled histories provides observed extrema: its
CHAPTER 5. HISTORICAL COMPATIBILITY AND FUTURE SIMULATION                                       21


minimum is no smaller than the true infimum, and its maximum is no larger than the true
supremum. It therefore supplies an inner exploration of possibilities, not a certified outer bound.
Interval methods may provide outer bounds that no single history attains. Every result records
which of these meanings applies.
The initial implementation offers exact bounded choices and linear constraints, symbolic con-
tinuous families such as α ∈ [0, 1], and explicit scenario execution at selected feasible states.
General nonlinear partial identification, automatic model discovery and exhaustive enumera-
tion of all economic histories are research extensions, not promises made by the core language.

The accounting language

6.1 Scope of the first language

AccDSL Core 0 describes entities and books, accounts, observations, shared unknowns, con-
straints, alternative interpretations, contract instances, accounting rules and reporting map-
pings. It has pure expressions and a restricted posting effect. Economic forecasting algorithms
can live in typed plugins; unrestricted numerical code does not need to be reimplemented in
the DSL. The grammar and interface details in Appendix A are normative for the first parser.
The compiler has six ordered stages: parsing with source locations; name and module resolu-
tion; type and temporal-context checking; lowering to the IR; constraint and rule-applicability
analysis; and execution-plan construction. The compiler never silently supplies an economic
assumption to make a program compile. A program can be valid as a historical representation
while requiring additional assumptions before a particular simulation can run.
The language’s meaning is defined by its IR and evaluator, rather than by the surface spelling
alone. Formatting changes cannot change a model’s semantic hash. A changed source span or
reviewer decision changes evidence provenance even if the numerical model remains the same.
The system therefore maintains separate evidence, semantic-model and execution identifiers.

6.3 A small model with a continuous interpretation family

The following source uses a synthetic observation whose full context is stored in the evidence
registry. The example imports the demonstration debt package, which uses one-year simple
accrual, no fees and immediate rate resets. It is not an IFRS rule package.

 use DemoDebt version "1";
 source note7 = external("synthetic-note-7");
 entity Acme functional USD;
 book Local entity Acme basis DemoDebt version "1";
 account Cash : Asset book Local;
 account Debt : Liability book Local;
 account InterestExpense : Expense book Local;

 observation debt : Money<USD> instant @2025-12-31
   = 100 USD from note7;
 symbol alpha : Real in [0, 1]
   because "The fixed-rate proportion is not disclosed";

The remaining declarations use this same unknown fraction in both loan components. A change
to the fraction therefore changes the whole debt mix consistently.
 let fixedPrincipal = alpha * value(debt);
 let floatingPrincipal = (1 - alpha) * value(debt);
 constraint split:
   fixedPrincipal + floatingPrincipal == value(debt);

   principal = value(debt);
   fixed_fraction = alpha;
   fixed_rate = 0.05 per year;
   initial_floating_rate = 0.05 per year;
 }

 alternative allFloating { assume alpha == 0; }

   set market_rate = constant(0.07 per year);
 }


The parent model retains every α ∈ [0, 1]. The two named alternatives are useful conditional
views of that family, not a declaration that the endpoints exhaust it. Requesting the parent
family’s exact bounds produces [5, 7] for the next year’s expense in this simple model. Request-
ing a conditional run of allFixed supplies α = 1. Requesting a weighted mixture requires a
separate weight model; the two labels do not create equal weights.
An observation declaration is a reference to evidence plus a proposed typed reading. The
compiler checks that the registry context agrees with the declared period and type. If the
evidence is rounded, value(debt) denotes a shared constrained value rather than a silently
exact 100. This synthetic registry explicitly declares an exact value, which keeps the illustration
deterministic.


6.4 Posting rules and explicit exceptions

An event type declares the information needed by a rule. The example below assumes an event
representing interest accrued and paid on the same date. Separate accrual and settlement
events are needed when those dates differ.
 event InterestPaid(principal: Money<USD>,
                    rate: Rate<annual>,
                    fraction: YearFraction) book Local;

 rule recogniseInterest for InterestPaid as e
 when e.principal >= 0 USD {
   let charge = roundMoney(e.principal * e.rate * e.fraction,
                          currencyScale(USD), "half_even");
   require charge >= 0 USD;
   post {
     debit InterestExpense charge;
     credit Cash charge;
   }
 }


The rule cannot perform file I/O, mutate another entity or sample a random number. It re-
turns a proposed batch. The runtime verifies balance and all preconditions before committing
CHAPTER 6. THE ACCOUNTING LANGUAGE                                                             25


state. Negative interest requires an explicit supported rule; it is not mishandled by this inten-
tionally restricted example. Rounding is a declared demonstration convention, not a universal
accounting default.
Rules can declare overrides ruleName. The resulting override graph must be acyclic. For a con-
crete event, the evaluator determines all applicable rules and removes any that are overridden
by another applicable rule. Exactly one maximal rule must remain for a required recognition
operation. An empty set of applicable rules produces UnsupportedTreatment; more than one
maximal rule produces RuleConflict. File order and a hidden numeric priority never break
ties.
If a guard depends on a symbolic value, the solver partitions the current feasible family into
the guard and its negation, subject to the existing constraints. This is a logical partition,
not a random draw. An incomplete solver returns UnknownApplicability, not a guessed rule.
Statically provable disjointness or total coverage can be cached; otherwise the runtime performs
the same checks on every concrete event.


6.5 Alternative readings, assumptions and updates

An alternative adds constraints and possibly a supported semantic mapping to its parent model.
Its record contains a rationale, supporting spans, opposing evidence, author, creation time
and scope. Child alternatives inherit all parent constraints; they cannot quietly discard an
inconvenient disclosure. A deliberate source rejection is a separate review decision with an
explicit reason and a new evidence view.
Two independent alternatives may coexist, while two mutually exclusive readings of the same
text require an explicit choice group. The representation stores these relationships in a con-
straint graph. It does not take a Cartesian product blindly. For example, interpreting an
instrument as a liability affects both its balance-sheet account and the treatment of its peri-
odic payments; those choices must be linked through one shared treatment variable.
New evidence narrows an existing family by conjunction when it concerns the same definitions
and reporting basis. If it changes the basis or corrects an earlier assertion, the system creates
a revised evidence view and records the relationship. Posterior reweighting, when enabled, is a
separate operation with likelihood provenance. A branch that becomes inconsistent is retained
as historically considered and rejected, with the evidence that rejected it.


6.6 Modules and stable intermediate representation

A package identifies its namespace, semantic version, dependencies, effective dates, jurisdiction,
supported entity classes, recognition operations and measurement policies. Standard packages
define allowable treatments; industry packages add economic objects and specialised rules;
company packages map source concepts and declare evidenced elections. A company extension
cannot override a mandatory standard rule merely because its package is loaded last. An
override is valid only at a declared extension point and within the selected standard’s policy
domain.
The IR has separate node classes for evidence, contextual observations, expressions, domains,
constraints, alternatives, contracts, economic events, measurement results, rules, journal
batches, report mappings and runs. Each node has a stable identifier and explicit input
references. No expression stores executable host-language text. Plugins are referenced by
versioned identifiers with typed signatures. This permits a second implementation to execute
CHAPTER 6. THE ACCOUNTING LANGUAGE                                                          26


the same supported program without evaluating arbitrary embedded code.
The IR serialisation version is independent of the DSL version and of any accounting-standard
edition. Migrations preserve the original payload and record changed meanings. A syntax
migration can be automatic when semantics are unchanged. A changed recognition policy
requires a new model version, even if the source file still parses.

7.1 Ingest, reconcile and construct histories

The historical procedure starts with a filing manifest. It records documents, reporting dates,
publication dates, revisions and expected statements and notes. Tagged filings are parsed
through an XBRL adapter; PDF and HTML sources are segmented into text, tables, captions
and references. The original bytes and extraction output remain immutable. Re-running an
improved extractor creates a new extraction version with links to the same source.
For each table, the importer first resolves headers, units, signs, periods and dimensions. It then
proposes concept mappings. A deterministic validator checks subtotal arithmetic, duplicate
facts, comparative consistency and cross-note references. Every proposed repair creates a new
reading. The system may suggest that an OCR value is 10 rather than 70, but only a reviewed
correction or an explicit alternative allows that reading into a validated model.
Reconciliation proceeds from local totals to movement schedules. A debt bridge may contain
opening debt, issuance, repayment, acquired debt, interest accretion, currency effects, fair-
value changes and closing debt. Every component receives a movement type. An unexplained
residual is stored as a residual with a source and period; it is not automatically classified as
repayment or issuance. A model can introduce an unknown decomposition of that residual,
subject to evidence and economic constraints.
The reconstruction engine then performs the following finite procedure within a declared model
scope:

  1. Build shared variables for observations, opening states, contract terms and supported
     movement amounts. Attach original rounding domains.
  2. Add identities, roll-forwards, account restrictions, contractual relationships and cross-
     period links. Add each semantic reading as a condition on the same joint model.
  3. Propagate types, units, intervals and simple equalities. Reject context mismatches before
     invoking a numerical solver.
  4. Ask the solver for feasibility. On infeasibility, return a suﬀicient conflicting subset of
     constraints, its source spans and model assumptions. A solver’s conflict core need not be
     the smallest possible explanation.
  5. Retain finite alternatives and continuous solution families symbolically. Materialise ex-


                                               27
CHAPTER 7. RECONSTRUCTION AND EXECUTION PROCEDURES                                               28


      ample histories only when needed for review or execution.
  6. Check whether each requested simulation has suﬀicient economic state. Missing maturi-
     ties, reset dates or cohort composition become declared assumptions or explicit unresolved
     inputs.

Unrestricted journal enumeration would be unbounded: arbitrary offsetting transactions can
be inserted without changing reports. The model therefore declares allowed event types, time
resolution, relevant accounts, lot/cohort resolution and limits on latent contract instances.
These limits describe the represented family; they are not evidence that all real possibilities
were searched. The compiler canonicalises syntactic duplicates and equivalences established
by its supported rewrite rules. It does not decide arbitrary economic equivalence. Probability
measures attach to declared economic hypotheses or parameters, never to a count of encodings;
adding a duplicate representation therefore cannot create extra probability mass or coverage.
The first implementation uses symbolic linear bridges and finite structural choices. Nonlinear
economic models can be evaluated on feasible states through plugins. A solver timeout preserves
the unresolved family and returns SolverIncomplete; it does not prune the family as impossible.
Search budgets belong to run configuration and are recorded with the results.


7.2 Condition a run without rewriting history

A run selects an evidence view and cutoff, a historical family, a forecast starting date, scenario
definitions, accounting packages, management policies, output queries and numerical settings.
A run may use a concrete history, an exact symbolic family, a finite set of conditional histories,
or weighted draws from a declared distribution. The output records which mode was used.
Suppose no debt maturity is disclosed. An analyst may run a conditional case that assumes all
debt matures after the forecast horizon. The run is valid under that assumption even though
the historical representation remains incomplete. The interface must display the assumption
next to the result and provide sensitivity cases when the assumption is material. It must not
label the assumed maturity as a discovered historical fact.
Management behaviour normally uses only information available at the decision date. A perfect-
foresight stress case is a separate, explicitly labelled policy. Scenarios should share the same un-
derlying innovations when estimating paired differences where appropriate, with stable stream
identifiers so branch order does not change the draws. Common random numbers improve
the comparability of paired simulations; they do not imply that every branch has the same
economic event counts or shock distribution.


7.3 Schedule and commit events

Each event carries an economic occurrence time, accrual interval when relevant, settlement
time, entity, contract or cohort identifier, currency, inputs and provenance. Its ordering key
consists of date, declared phase, dependency edges and a deterministic tie identifier. The
reference phases are: opening and scheduled contractual changes; market observations and rate
resets; business events and decisions; accrual and measurement; settlement; period closing and
reporting. A contract can require a more specific dependency. These phases are an engineering
starting point, not a universal description of market timing.
Two events may share a tie order only if their order is either specified or irrelevant to the re-
sult. If payment before remeasurement and payment after remeasurement differ, an unresolved
CHAPTER 7. RECONSTRUCTION AND EXECUTION PROCEDURES                                              29


ordering is a model ambiguity or an error. The engine does not conceal it behind alphabetical
event IDs.
 execute_event(event, state, run_context):
     verify prerequisites and information availability
     resolve applicable measurement and recognition rules
     compute proposed economic changes and measurements
     compute proposed journal batch(es)
     validate types, contexts, balance, constraints and idempotency
     atomically commit state, journals and explanation edges

The atomic unit includes the economic state and its accounting effects. If posting fails, neither
state changes. A repeated event ID with identical inputs returns the existing result; the same
ID with different inputs is a conflict. Consolidation runs after entity books reach the required
date and stores its adjustments separately. A failure in one scenario branch cannot mutate
another branch’s state.


7.4 Funding, tax and other simultaneous relations

Some financial models have circular dependencies. Interest affects taxable profit; tax and
investment affect funding needs; funding affects interest. The reference executor first tries to
resolve these through explicit timing. For example, beginning-of-period debt can determine
current interest, with new borrowing affecting the next accrual period. That is acceptable only
when the model’s timing assumption is stated and appropriate.
Where the economic model genuinely requires simultaneous decisions, the dependency graph
identifies the strongly connected component and delegates it to a declared equation or optimi-
sation solver. The solver must return a solution, residuals, admissibility checks and uniqueness
or multiplicity status. A contraction iteration requires a justified convergence condition; an
arbitrary fixed iteration count is not a mathematical solution. Multiple admissible equilibria
become alternatives when material. No solution produces a model outcome such as financing
failure, or an explicit numerical failure, depending on the cause.
Funding policy must include available facilities, limits, covenants, collateral and allowed capital
actions. The program cannot issue unlimited debt to keep cash positive. An inability to
meet payments may be a legitimate default outcome. Default ends or changes contractual
and accounting processes under the relevant event rules; it is not quietly discarded as a bad
simulation draw.

7.6 Worked example: a bond has several relevant amounts

A two-period zero-coupon bond raises cash of 100 and promises 110.25 at maturity. Assume
a 5% effective rate per period, no transaction costs, no default, and amortised-cost treatment
under a demonstration rule. The effective-interest schedule follows directly from 100(1.05)2 =
110.25:

 Date                                 Opening liability    Interest accretion   Closing liability
 Issue                                               0                      0                100
 End of period 1                                   100                      5                105
 End of period 2, before payment                   105                   5.25             110.25
 Redemption                                     110.25                      0                  0

Issuance debits cash and credits the liability by 100. Each accretion debits interest expense
and credits the liability. Redemption debits the liability and credits cash by 110.25. Legal face
amount, carrying amount and market fair value remain separate fields. A market-rate shock
may change fair value without changing this contractual payment schedule or this demonstra-
tion amortised-cost path.
Cash-flow reporting uses the actual payment event and its economic components; the annual
accretion is non-cash. The applicable reporting package decides how to classify the maturity
payment, rather than inferring it solely from the liability debit. Fees, modifications, early
redemption, embedded options, hedges and foreign currency require additional event and mea-
surement rules.
Ordinary equity issuance similarly separates cash, share quantity and legal capital. Ten new
shares at 12, with par value 1, produce cash of 120, share capital of 10 and premium of 110
under a simple par-value profile. Shares outstanding increase by ten units. Direct issuance
costs of 3 reduce the specified equity account and cash under the chosen illustrative treatment.
The accounting rule and the cost’s eligibility must be explicit; this example does not prescribe
every jurisdiction’s treatment.

The reporting engine produces the balance sheet, income statement, comprehensive income,
cash-flow statement, changes in equity and note schedules when the necessary modules are

present. Each output carries its basis, perimeter, period, scenario and interpretation. A
comparative table cannot combine an original prior-year figure and a restated current-year

An explanation is a dependency graph with typed edges: extracted from, interpreted as, as-

8.1 A common core with distinct accounting books

The common core governs evidence, time, money, events, journals and provenance. Reporting
standards govern recognition, measurement and presentation within that core. The design
preserves three levels of meaning: economic terms such as a promise to pay cash; an accounting
treatment under a particular standard; and a company’s reported label or extension. These
levels are connected by explicit rules and mappings, rather than collapsed into one universal
chart of accounts.
A rule package includes a standards register with jurisdiction, issuer class, effective dates,
transition provisions, permitted elections, paragraph references, implementation version and
tests. The package selector uses the entity, reporting basis, period and adopted elections. It
must distinguish a standard’s mandatory effective date from permitted early adoption. For
example, the IFRS Foundation’s IFRS 18 overview states an effective date of 1 January 2027
with earlier application permitted (IFRS Foundation, 2026c). A 2026 historical report cannot
be assigned that presentation regime solely because the latest installed package includes it.
IFRS, US GAAP and ASBE packages share economic contracts and events where appropriate,
but produce separate accounting books. A reconciliation between books consists of explained
adjustments with origins in differing treatments, not a renaming of accounts. The first imple-
mentation can preserve all three source bases even while only a selected subset has executable
rules. Such a source is represented as observed information with unsupported simulation treat-
ments, rather than being silently converted to the supported basis.
The oﬀicial IFRS overviews for IFRS 9, IAS 32 and IFRS 17 establish the relevant scope of
financial-instrument measurement, presentation and insurance contracts (IFRS Foundation,
2026d,a,b). They are orientation sources, not substitutes for reviewed paragraph-level im-
plementations. Public retrieval of the selected Chinese Ministry of Finance page and SEC
API documentation failed during this campaign; the source register records these access gaps.
ASBE and US GAAP semantic modules therefore have explicit primary-text acquisition and
accounting-review tasks before they can be marked executable and compliant.

8.2 Non-financial companies

The initial industry package covers sales, collections, purchases, payments, inventory move-
ments, payroll, capital expenditure, depreciation and basic financing. Revenue recognition is a
separate rule from invoicing and cash collection. A service contract may generate a performance
event before an invoice, creating a different asset from an unconditional receivable. Customer
advances create obligations before revenue. Returns, rebates and variable consideration need
separate assumptions and subsequent adjustments.
Inventory state includes quantity, cost, location, ownership and lot or cohort. A costing method
is selected within the reporting package’s permissible domain. An aggregate stock can support
a simple model only if its approximation is declared. A commodity-price shock can expose dif-
ferences between old and new inventory that an average value suppresses. Fixed-asset state like-
wise requires acquisition dates, components, useful lives, residual values, depreciation method,
tax basis and impairment history when those affect the requested forecast.
Industry extensions add economic mechanisms without bypassing the accounting core. Con-
struction requires performance and completion states; extractive industries require reserves,
depletion and restoration obligations; utilities may require rate-regulation treatment; software
businesses may require contract liabilities and development-cost decisions. The module mani-
fest states what is represented, what is executable, and what remains aggregated.


8.3 Debt and hybrid financing

The contract layer for a debt instrument stores issuer and holder perspective, currency, is-
sue and settlement dates, issue proceeds and legal face amount. Its payment terms include
coupon/index/spread, reset and fixing dates, day-count convention, payment calendar, amorti-
sation schedule and maturity. Its rights and protections include seniority, collateral, covenants,
options, conversion terms, guarantees and linked hedges. Missing terms are explicit unknowns.
A single carrying amount cannot stand in for this record.
The accounting layer stores the classification decision and its evidence, initial measurement, car-
rying amount, accrued interest, effective-rate method, unamortised fees or discounts, fair-value
disclosures, impairment or credit adjustments where applicable, and presentation mappings.
Contractual interest, effective-interest expense, realised cash payments and fair-value changes
are separate quantities. The zero-coupon example in Chapter 7 provides a minimal confor-
mance case for this separation.
The lifecycle must support issuance, recognition, accrual, reset, coupon, principal repayment,
modification, derecognition, default, recovery and conversion. A modification event first de-
termines which accounting operation the selected standard requires; it does not always mean
issuing a new loan. An embedded option can affect valuation and classification without being
exercised. A derivative linked as a hedge requires both the economic contract and any applica-
ble accounting designation; economic risk reduction alone does not establish hedge-accounting
eligibility.
Hybrid instruments make interpretation particularly consequential. IAS 32’s overview discusses
liability/equity classification and the separation of a compound instrument (IFRS Foundation,
2026a). The executable classification must consider the full terms and the selected standard
version. US GAAP or ASBE treatment must be implemented under its own rules rather than
inferred from an IFRS classification. Where the terms are not disclosed suﬀiciently, alterna-
tive classifications can coexist only if each is actually compatible with the known terms and
applicable rules.

8.4 Equity and ownership

Equity has both monetary accounts and security quantities. The security register records share
class, issued and outstanding quantities, treasury holdings, par or stated value where applica-
ble, voting rights, dividend rights, redemption features, options and conversion relationships.
Monetary equity accounts include contributed capital, reserves, retained earnings and other
components required by the selected basis. These records evolve together through issuance,
repurchase, cancellation, split, dividend and conversion events.
A stock split changes quantities and per-share measures without necessarily changing aggregate
monetary equity. A cash dividend affects distributions and cash rather than current operating
expense under the corresponding ordinary equity treatment. Repurchases and employee share
awards require their own rules. Earnings per share uses a dated quantity schedule and rules
for potential ordinary shares; simply dividing profit by the closing share count is insuﬀicient
when shares change during the period.
Funding simulations must connect the quantity register to market assumptions. An equity
issue at a scenario-dependent price changes cash raised, shares and dilution. The model must
state whether it targets a fixed cash amount, a fixed share count or a capital ratio. These
are different management decisions even when the historical statement would report the same
aggregate proceeds.


8.5 Banks: accounting capital is not regulatory capital

A bank package needs instrument and cohort states for loans, securities, deposits, wholesale
funding, repos, derivatives, collateral and commitments. For interest-rate analysis, currency
and repricing buckets matter alongside remaining maturity. For credit analysis, exposure,
default state, loss severity, recoveries, guarantees and their dependencies matter. For liquidity
analysis, deposit behaviour, collateral eligibility, encumbrance and payment timing matter.
These states can be represented at an explicitly chosen aggregation level; the public record
rarely identifies every contract.
Loan origination can debit loans and credit a customer deposit in the bank’s book. Subse-
quent transfer to another bank produces settlement flows. Treating all loan growth as an
immediate cash outflow would therefore encode a specific funding assumption, not a universal
banking identity. A system-wide model must also link counterparties and settlement assets;
single-company simulation records external counterparties without claiming to close the whole
economy.
An allowance calculation is a dated measurement based on forecast information. For a single
horizon, let EAD be a fixed exposure at default, PD the probability of default, and LGD the
mean loss fraction conditional on default. Ignoring discounting, the illustrative expected loss
in currency units is
                                  L = EAD × P D × LGD.
With exposure 100, default probability 0.02 and loss given default 0.4, it produces 0.8. Chang-
ing those inputs to 0.04 and 0.5 produces 2, so the allowance increase is 1.2 if there are no
other movements. This calculation is a demonstration model. A standard-specific expected-
credit-loss implementation must determine horizon, staging or other classification, discounting,
forward-looking information, write-offs and purchased-credit-impaired or other special cases.
The simple formula is not an IFRS 9 or US GAAP compliance implementation.
Regulatory capital is computed in a separate prudential layer with its own perimeter, eligibility,
deductions, risk weights and transition provisions. Accounting equity feeds a reconciliation to
CHAPTER 8. STANDARDS, INDUSTRIES AND FINANCING INSTRUMENTS                                   35


capital; it is not automatically Common Equity Tier 1. Liquidity and capital constraints
influence the management policy, which in turn generates asset sales, funding or capital events.
A breached ratio can be an economically meaningful scenario outcome. No hidden capital
injection is permitted to repair it.


8.6 Insurance: cohorts and future service

An insurer requires policy cohorts or groups with issue dates, coverage, premiums, benefits,
expenses, lapses, claims development, guarantees and reinsurance relationships. Asset port-
folios need their own contracts and valuations. The liability state must distinguish expected
future cash flows, discounting, risk adjustments or margins, unearned profit measures where
applicable, incurred claims and movements between opening and closing states. The IFRS 17
overview’s distinction between measuring future cash flows and recognising profit as service is
provided motivates this separation (IFRS Foundation, 2026b).
The module API accepts policy/cohort state, economic assumptions, non-economic assump-
tions and information date. It returns a measurement vector, movement attribution and pro-
posed recognition events. Experience differences, changes in assumptions, finance effects and
service effects remain separately identifiable. Reinsurance held has its own record and mea-
surement; it is not an automatic percentage offset to every insurance liability.
Scenarios can alter discount curves, mortality, morbidity, lapse rates, inflation, catastrophe
losses and policyholder behaviour. Their joint dependence and cohort exposure must be de-
clared. A portfolio total can match history while hiding very different guarantee and duration
profiles. This makes alternative economic histories especially valuable, but also limits what
public-data reconstruction can identify. A full insurance package needs actuarial and account-
ing review beyond the core release.

9.4 Plugin interfaces

Every plugin declares a version, supported types, input information dates, determinism, nu-
merical measure and capabilities. A contract plugin implements schedule, observe, transition
and payoff; it may return future conditional events. A behaviour plugin implements decide
and declares which state and scenario information it uses. A measurement plugin implements
measure and returns an amount or vector with a basis and numerical status. A reporting plugin
maps classified journals and balances to output contexts.
The plugin response must include all inputs that materially affect results. An external cal-
culator’s global evaluation date, market-curve cache, calendar version or random seed cannot
remain invisible. For example, QuantLib’s floating coupon interface makes fixing-date con-
ventions and pricing objects relevant inputs; the adapter records them rather than relying on
process-global state. ACTUS risk-factor observations receive explicit dates and identifiers.
The constraint interface accepts a typed formula and a requested operation: feasibility, witness,
enumeration of declared finite choices, or bound. It returns sat, unsat or unknown, plus evidence
appropriate to the operation. Timeouts and unsupported theories return unknown. A witness
proves existence within the formula, not completeness of a recovered historical family.


9.5 Human review and model-assisted extraction

The review interface should put the source passage beside the proposed observation, context
and reconciliation impact. An analyst can accept a mapping, add an alternative, reject an ex-
traction, define a symbolic unknown or request more evidence. These actions create structured
changes with reasons. The useful unit of review is a coherent disclosure and its links, not an
isolated number detached from its note.

A language model can identify candidate tables, draft concept mappings, propose contract
terms and suggest alternative readings. Its output must reference source spans and a schema.
Deterministic checks reject fabricated source references, invalid units, inconsistent periods and
unbalanced postings. A model-generated interpretation that passes those checks remains a pro-
posal until its evidentiary status is established. Token probabilities and self-reported confidence
are never automatically used as weights over economic histories.
For routine local research use, a lightweight interface and immutable source versions are suf-
ficient. Multi-user approval hierarchies, cryptographic attestations and deployment security
systems are outside this initial design. They can be added for a concrete operational require-
ment without changing the scientific representation.


9.6 Failure recovery and reproducibility

Extraction jobs and simulation jobs write explicit progress records and atomic results. Restart-
ing a failed document conversion reuses its document hash and creates either the same idempo-
tent extraction result or a new version under changed settings. Restarting a simulation resumes
only from a committed state whose model and input hashes match the run. Numerical failures
retain the inputs needed for a focused diagnostic.
A run manifest records code revision when available, package lock, source hashes, cutoff rule,
model hash, scenario data, priors or weights, seed/stream scheme, solver settings, decimal and
rounding conventions, calendars, environment and result locations. Absence of a usable Git
revision is recorded as unavailable with content hashes, rather than inventing a commit.

10.1 Three different questions require different evidence

Engineering correctness asks whether the implementation follows the specified types, transi-
tions and arithmetic. Accounting validity asks whether the selected rules faithfully implement
the applicable standards and disclosed policies. Scientific validity asks whether the economic
assumptions and uncertainty representation support the intended forecasts or comparisons. A
balanced journal answers only part of the first question.
The implementation must therefore maintain separate validation results. Compilation and
deterministic examples cannot establish forecasting quality. Agreement with a public report
cannot identify an undisclosed contract term. A lower extraction error on a document bench-
mark cannot establish that the engine preserved every materially different scenario response.

10.3 Extraction and reconstruction evaluation

A company corpus must include different standards, industries, languages, filing formats, exten-
sions, interim calendars and restatements. Company and time splits are required so repeated
report templates or revised versions do not leak into evaluation. Public benchmark tables are
useful development material, while acceptance uses complete disclosure packages that include
notes and their relationships.
Reviewers annotate source facts, contexts, mappings, required relationships, known ambigui-
ties and unsupported assertions. Relevant measures include correct source-linked values and
contexts, unresolved extraction volume, coverage of note obligations, reconciliation failures and
invented facts. Report each separately. A single overall score can hide a serious error in a small
but consequential financing note.
Historical reconstruction should test whether the known synthetic history remains feasible,
whether known incompatible histories are rejected, and whether economically distinct alterna-
tives are preserved. On real companies, public data generally cannot establish the true journal.
Review instead assesses evidential compatibility, unsupported commitments and conditional
economic consequences. Later disclosures may provide discriminating evidence for previously
ambiguous terms.
No universal numerical threshold for semantic coverage or extraction accuracy is fixed in this
document. Before a release evaluation, an accounting-reviewed test corpus and task-specific
acceptance thresholds must be recorded in an evidence contract. Hard conformance failures,
fabricated citations, silent discarding of valid alternatives and undocumented accounting ad-
justments veto acceptance regardless of an aggregate score.


10.4 Evaluate the DSL against the strongest practical baseline

The comparison uses the same IR, accounting examples and tasks. One group of models is
authored through the typed Python API; another through the DSL. Both receive equivalent
documentation and examples. Tasks include encoding a movement schedule, adding an alter-
native interpretation, changing an effective date and tracing a simulated amount. Measure
correctness, material omissions, time to a reviewed result, ease of explaining a change and
maintenance effort.
This comparison tests whether the notation and compiler help users; it is not a comparison
between careful DSL code and unstructured spreadsheets. If the DSL offers little advantage,
retain the shared IR and library and narrow the textual language. Small pilots nominate im-
provements. A claim of superiority requires a predeclared comparison and uncertainty evidence,
not a few favourable demonstrations.

10.5 Forecasting evaluation comes later

Forecast evaluation uses historical information cutoffs, fixed data vintages, declared economic
scenarios and proper separation of training and testing periods. Baselines should include a
naive persistence or ratio model, a tuned conventional financial model, the structural model
with a single selected interpretation, and the same model preserving alternatives. This identifies
whether any benefit comes from better economics, more information or the representation of
ambiguity.
Construct the inexpensive comparators before choosing a complex forecasting method. A
no-growth operating forecast with scheduled contractual financing tests whether the model
adds value beyond existing obligations. A constant margin and turnover model tests whether
detailed operations improve on familiar financial ratios. A trailing-average growth model tests
whether fitted dynamics improve on recent experience. A fixed-exposure contractual stress
model tests whether estimated management responses help beyond repricing the existing book.
Each comparator must generate coherent accounts through the same reporting rules and use
the same historical information. In synthetic cases, the known hidden state also supplies an
oracle conditional on the stipulated scenario.
Evaluate these comparisons separately under stable conditions, large rate resets, refinancing
pressure, credit deterioration and changes in reporting perimeter. A pooled average can conceal
failure in the situation the product is intended to explain. Supported underperformance against
a cheap comparator in a declared critical situation vetoes promotion of the complex forecasting
method and must lead the result discussion. These checks are falsification tests, not training
targets or evidence of superiority when merely passed.
Evaluate point forecasts where they are a stated target, distributional calibration when prob-
abilities are justified, and decision-relevant robustness when only feasible sets are available.
A wide feasible set is not automatically a good prediction, and an unweighted set cannot be
assessed as if it were a 95% predictive interval. In multi-scenario stress tests, scenario severity
and probability remain separate inputs.
Any serious numerical comparison must follow the repository’s research plan, assumption au-
dit, baseline, veto, uncertainty and result-record policies. The present document runs only
deterministic checks of its own examples. It neither selects a scientific forecasting default nor
reports comparative performance.

A.3 Typing and partial-information rules

For expressions x and y in the same currency C, the money addition rule is

                                  x : MoneyhCi y : MoneyhCi
                                                            .
                                       x + y : MoneyhCi

Context compatibility is a separate premise for observations. There is no analogous implicit
rule for different currencies. Explicit conversion uses a dated measurement and records its rate
and purpose. A rate-times-principal calculation is typed through a declared accrual convention;
the example’s annual simple rate and year fraction produce money in the principal’s currency.
Known expressions evaluate normally. Shared symbols evaluate to symbolic expressions sub-
ject to a joint constraint store. Missing source values cannot enter arithmetic until the model
supplies a symbol or a conditional assumption. Boolean guards over symbols produce formu-
las, not the value false. This avoids the common error in which “unknown whether impaired”
silently means “not impaired.” Division requires a proven nonzero denominator or a parti-
tioned domain that excludes zero; otherwise the expression remains unsupported for concrete
execution.
Logical queries first require a nonempty feasible family. An empty family returns Infeasible; it
must not produce vacuous answers to substantive questions. For a nonempty family, constraint
evaluation has three results. entailed means every state in the declared feasible family satisfies
the proposition. refuted means none does. undetermined means both remain feasible or the
solver has not decided; the response distinguishes those causes. Probabilistic truth is a separate
query requiring a measure. A language-level Boolean value must never be inferred from an
arbitrary probability threshold.


A.4 Operational rule selection

Let V (e, h) be the applicable rules for event e in concrete history h, after package/date selection
and guard evaluation. Let r ≺ r′ mean that r′ validly overrides r through the declared acyclic
graph. Define
                            Max(V ) = {r ∈ V : ∄r′ ∈ V with r ≺ r′ }.
For one required recognition operation, a singleton selects that rule; the empty set yields
UnsupportedTreatment; a larger set yields RuleConflict. Multiple distinct operations, such as
APPENDIX A. CORE GRAMMAR, RECORDS AND EXECUTION DETAILS                                         50


measurement and recognition, are evaluated through separate declared interfaces. A rule that
intentionally produces no postings must belong to a supported non-recognition event operation,
whose economic state effect is still recorded.
Given a selected rule, the evaluator computes all local bindings and preconditions, emits pro-
posed postings, verifies Equation (5.2), validates account/context restrictions and commits
the event transaction. Failure returns the original state plus a diagnostic. This defines the
atomicity requirement independently of the storage technology.

B.2 Version and access qualifications

Several discrepancies mattered. Doshi’s thesis is dated 2024 despite the original filename. The
archived Mernik text is the 2003 CWI report underlying the 2005 article. Giacomini and
Kitagawa’s copy is the April 2020 working paper underlying the 2021 article. The Docling
v5 copy lists Auer as first author. The probabilistic-programming tutorial is an arXiv version
revised in 2021, first submitted in 2018; a merged index DOI was not used. The Libkin
author PDF has a footer DOI that resolves to another paper; title/author metadata from the
indexes resolves the cited work to DOI 10.1145/2594538.2594561. These discrepancies remain
documented rather than being hidden by a cleaned bibliography.
McCarthy (1982), Imieliński and Lipski (1984), Tamer (2010), and Caiani and co-authors (2016)
are retained as important blocked candidates. Access failures and rejected wrong-content down-
loads are recorded. The design does not use their uninspected technical claims. More specialised
work on accounting measurement, process mining, multi-company equilibrium, actuarial infer-
ence and learned forecasting is recorded as expansion work for the corresponding implemen-
tation stage. The survey supports the core architecture without pretending that those later
modules have a complete literature or rule base.
OpenAlex retraction flags and available Crossref correction/version relations were inspected
APPENDIX B. RESEARCH BASIS AND OUTSTANDING REVIEW                                          53


alongside local versions. No reviewed source was excluded for a discovered retraction. Missing
correction metadata is marked unavailable; absence of a discovered notice does not certify
that no notice exists. Citation counts are dated index metadata, and venue rankings were not
available in this campaign. Neither is used as evidence of correctness.


B.3 Companion records and status

The six research ledgers are source-support.json, citation-venue.json, backward-snowball.json,
forward-snowball.json, claim-support.json, and omissions.json. They record technical anchors,
metadata sources, considered leads, omitted or blocked candidates and the distinction between
source claims and this document’s derivations. Citation leads with checked seed references are
distinguished from cross-survey additions and topic expansion leads. Forward searches retain
the most-cited and most-recent results returned by the recorded bounded queries; they do not
establish exhaustive citation coverage. The separate tools.json records software evidence. Raw
retrievals and text extracts permit a later reviewer to revisit the decisions.
This document specifies the product; it does not report a completed engine. The companion re-
view records cover deterministic example arithmetic, bibliography/build checks, rendered-page
inspection and author review. Human comprehension review, independent accounting review
and implementation conformance remain separate future evidence. They are not replaced by
a clean LaTeX build or by the quantity of literature collected.
