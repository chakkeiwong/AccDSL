26.22
When the subsidiary earns the internal margin
Now let the subsidiary sell the inventory to the parent. The group must still eliminate the full
internal sale, but the profit being removed was earned in the company partly owned by NCI.
Applying the minority percentage to that company’s unadjusted legal profit would allocate
profit that the group has not yet earned from an outside customer.
Keep the acquisition on 1 July 2025 unchanged. On 1 September, after control has begun, the
subsidiary buys inventory for 20 cash from an outside vendor. On 1 October it sells the lot
to the parent for 30 on credit. Buying the lot after control matters: inventory is not inserted
into the acquired net assets, and the original goodwill and acquisition NCI remain unchanged.
The subsidiary’s cash falls by 20. This external payment survives consolidation even if the
parent later settles the internal payable of 30. Settlement creates a payment by the parent and
a receipt by the subsidiary; both internal cash flows are eliminated in full.
Before any outside resale, the parent’s legal inventory is 30 and the subsidiary has legal trading
profit 10. Consolidation removes the reciprocal receivable and payable, eliminates internal
revenue 30, and reduces inventory by the unrealised margin 10. Group inventory is therefore 20.
Neither the inventory adjustment nor the reciprocal-balance elimination is multiplied by the
parent’s 75% ownership. Full elimination precedes the allocation of the subsidiary’s adjusted
earnings between parent owners and NCI (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026c,
paras. B86, B89–B94).
26.22.1
Tax recognition and attribution answer different questions
Continue with explicitly supplied separate tax bases and 25% rates. The subsidiary owes current
tax 5/2 on its legal margin. If the parent’s inventory tax base remains 30, the difference from
the group carrying amount 20 can support a deferred tax asset of 5/2 when the recognition
conditions are met. The comparison assumes those conditions in one branch and explicitly
withholds recognition in another. The tax base and recognition conditions remain distinct from
the inventory elimination (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2023, paras. 11, 24 and
illustrative example B11).
For this example, attribute the inventory elimination and its deferred tax effect to the subsidiary
seller’s earnings. This is a supplied attribution premise; the cited paragraphs do not themselves
determine the example’s tax sharing. With recognised deferred tax, removing profit 10 and
adding tax income 5/2 offsets the subsidiary’s after-tax legal margin 15/2. Its consolidated
profit returns to 63/2, the acquired-basis operating result already derived. NCI receives one
quarter, or 63/8, and parent owners receive 189/8.

The subsidiary contribution combines realised margin, its unchanged current tax, and the

Equal recognised-tax trading contributions happen to make several profit totals match the
downstream example. That numerical coincidence is not a reason to forget which company
earned the margin. The unrecognised-tax branch, the separate entity accounts and the carried
opening equity distinguish the two directions. Complete legal journals, account worksheets, cash
origins and attribution results are compared across the finite cases.7 The remaining question is
how the subsidiary-attributed margin and NCI move through retained profit when the outside
sale occurs in a later year.
