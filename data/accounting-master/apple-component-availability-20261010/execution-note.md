# Annual-06 integration decision and author source reading

10 October 2026. The original annual PDF page 11 (printed 8) was read at
normal resolution after the exact-source preparation. Both paragraphs and
the heading match the extraction; the predecessor outsourcing risk remains
context. Original-text and phrase checks are not independent semantic review.

The first paragraph retains current single/limited-source risk and shortages
even for multi-source components. It gives an historical semiconductor shortage
example, warns that disruption could recur, qualifies extension/renewal of
agreements, and allows supplier financial failure or industry consolidation.
The second distinguishes new/custom components, initial capacity and yield,
supplier priority for common components, historical component or completed-
product delays, and future consequences sensitive to restoring or replacing
the source. These are dated issuer assertions and conditional consequences,
not measured losses, universal quantifiers or an identified supplier history.

The original skeptical audit still passes. The distinct reader bridge is from
an existing supply agreement to feasible delivered output. Version 2.21 already
explains capacity, yield and component compatibility; it does not explain
renewal risk, supplier failure/consolidation, or why a named alternate source
needs quantity and timing evidence. Add a short section after the product-
transition discussion in a separate Version 2.22 tree. No new equation, numeric
illustration, supplier identity or probability is required. Keep all old prose,
displays and citations. The frozen baseline's clean build is already bound;
rebuilding it would change protected generated files without adding evidence.

Cold-reader questions: why can multiple sources still face shortage; what does
an existing agreement establish; why is common-component priority distinct
from a yield problem; what facts make switching feasible; and why do past
adverse effects not calibrate future losses? The preceding paragraph separates
product transition and recognition dates; the opening should now ask whether
the component agreement supports delivery on the required date. After this
section the reader can distinguish contractual commitment from operational
feasibility and can identify the missing facts before carrying either into a
journal. Defects and inventory then remain the next unresolved source question.
