# JPMorgan debt perimeter and maturity measurement

12 October 2026. E01/E07; MC01/02/03/05/08/15/16/21/28/30/33–35 and
G4/G5/G7/G11/G13. The complete retained 2025 Form 10-K Note 20, printed
281–282 / PDF 283–284, was read with Note 33's parent-only comparator.
The source ledger binds 14 table rows and eight qualifications; **50 source
and arithmetic checks pass**. Both pages were rendered and inspected.

Note 20 explicitly classifies its debt table by Parent company and
Subsidiaries and reports carrying values by remaining contractual-maturity
band. The parent subtotal is US$253,039m in 2025 and US$238,023m in 2024;
the consolidated long-term-debt total is US$435,206m in 2025. Note 33's
parent-only balance-sheet long-term debt is US$254,633m and US$239,626m.
The gaps of US$1,594m and US$1,603m exactly match Note 20's Subsidiaries
junior-subordinated-debt subtotals in the same years. This is an exact
displayed arithmetic bridge, **not** a verified legal-issuer or accounting-
classification explanation; Note 20 itself labels that row Subsidiaries.

The 2025 parent under-one-year carrying amount is US$14,298m, while Note 33
footnote (c) says US$14.3bn of parent long-term debt contractually matures in
2026. The 2027–2030 footnote numbers sum to US$102.7bn; the Note 20 parent
1–5-year carrying amount plus the junior row's 1–5-year amount is
US$102,677m. The 2m and 23m displayed gaps are within the stated 0.1bn
rounding of the footnote figures, but numerical closeness does not equate
carrying value with principal due. Note 20 expressly includes unamortized
premiums/discounts, issuance costs, valuation and fair-value adjustments;
its zero-coupon footnote separately reports US$18.1bn of carrying amount
versus US$57.6bn aggregate contractual maturity principal in 2025.

The five-year amounts in Note 20 footnote (h) are **consolidated carrying
values**: 42.6/55.8/105.4/31.4/39.8 billion for 2026–2030, a different
population and measure from Note 33's parent-only contractual maturities.
Page 282 separately reports US$47.6bn of subsidiary long-term debt and
structured notes guaranteed by the parent at 2025 year-end (US$41.2bn in
2024). Those amounts are neither parent-issued long-term debt nor the Note 28
credit-commitment table, and they do not state a guarantee fair value.

Post-run red team: the exact residual match across two years is tempting, but
it could reflect a presentation or parent-only accounting adjustment whose
legal and measurement mechanism has not been inspected. A direct instrument
and reconciliation source would settle that interpretation; the displayed
match alone cannot. The strongest alternative explanation for the annual
maturity differences is carrying-value adjustment rather than cash-principal
error. The source explicitly permits such adjustments. No statistical ranking
applies. This candidate failed no continuation veto; source-only work and
future manuscript integration remain justified.
