This document specifies a system that translates company disclosures into traceable evidence,
represents several compatible economic and accounting histories, and simulates their future
journals and financial statements under explicit economic scenarios.
The proposed data contracts, methods and acceptance cases specify the intended implementation.
Current source and domain limitations remain explicit; this candidate is not a completed product
or a claim of full proposal readiness. Proposed libraries can be replaced while preserving those
contracts. Sector rule packages require their own accounting review before they can be described
as compliant with a reporting standard.
Research materials, source qualifications and review records accompany this document in
docs/design/research and docs/design/review.

17.17
A date range can leave several information views possible
A filing marked as published on 2 January does not establish whether a manager had read it at
the beginning of that day. Nor does a correction received within a narrow interval around a
decision establish whether the original or corrected interpretation was available for that decision.
These are questions about the order of events. Assigning midnight or the interval’s midpoint
would settle them by assumption.
The selected successor retains that uncertainty explicitly. An exact timestamp is a point; a
calendar day in UTC is a half-open interval from its midnight to the next midnight. A supplied
time range has the same half-open convention. An unknown access time remains unknown.
Exact rational seconds preserve differences smaller than a microsecond and equivalent fixed-offset
spellings without passing through a floating-point or microsecond clock.
If the access time lies in [a, b), a decision at b is certainly after access. A decision at a can
coincide with access or precede it: the lower endpoint belongs to the interval. The distinction
disappears if an implementation replaces the interval with a single representative time. It also
disappears if a strict upper bound is inadvertently changed to a closed one.
17.17.1
Availability depends on the route and on joint chronology
A public-document retrieval and a private delivery impose different conditions. In the first
route the source must have been released publicly before the claimed access. In the second,
an actor-addressed delivery can establish access before any public release. The private route
neither invents a public timestamp nor makes the document available to other actors. Both
routes still require evidence about that actor’s access; publication alone cannot supply it.
The earlier whole-second reference did not distinguish these routes. One of its retained cases
places actor access before a later public release.
Under the new, explicit public-retrieval
premise, that chronology is inconsistent. It can be meaningful under a private-delivery premise.

Clocks are also dependent. Authorship precedes ingestion, ingestion precedes review, and

a replacement chain, while permitting retrospective effective dates. These are declared premises

An unknown clock need not make every conclusion unknown. If a private access time is supplied

can establish the relevant authorship inequality without identifying its timestamp. The result
must retain the unknown-time marker. Logical implication from other premises does not turn
an unobserved time into an observed fact.

Partition clock possibilities without enumerating timestamps
Let xe be the time of event e, measured in exact UTC seconds on the retained calendar axis.
Point and interval observations, chronology and cutoff conditions become difference inequalities.
A non-strict edge represents

with a strict variant when the right endpoint is excluded. A fixed origin converts an absolute

For example, publication before access contributes xpublication ≤xaccess, while access by the
decision contributes xaccess ≤tdecision. Keeping both inequalities in one system prevents the
calculation from accepting a combination that separate per-clock classifications would mistakenly

For each revision, eligibility is a conjunction of its required actor, system and effective-time
guards, g1, . . . , gm. The complementary cases can be written as a disjoint first-failure partition:
¬(g1 ∧· · · ∧gm) = ¬g1 ∨(g1 ∧¬g2) ∨· · · ∨(g1 ∧· · · ∧gm−1 ∧¬gm).
(17.27)
Each branch preserves the earlier satisfied guards. Repeating the construction for the relevant
revisions covers every clock assignment admitted by the original ranges and chronology. Eligibility
and the selected revision heads are constant within each surviving branch. The procedure
therefore represents families of timestamps symbolically; it does not need an explicit tree point
for every possible instant.
An empty branch requires a checked contradiction. Summing original difference inequalities
around a cycle cancels all clock variables. A negative right-hand sum is impossible; a zero sum
is also impossible if at least one inequality in the sum is strict. A nonempty branch instead
carries exact rational clock values satisfying every original inequality. The checker verifies those
values directly and reconstructs every complementary branch before accepting the partition.
The witness establishes nonemptiness, while the guards and complete partition establish the
scope of the conclusion. A few feasible timestamp examples would establish neither exhaustive
coverage nor the absence of an omitted information view. Conversely, reaching the computation
budget leaves an unfinished temporal family. The selected reference then reports incompleteness
and does not present the already examined views as the full set.

17.17.3
Different timing views can support different policies
Return to receipt 7 and the committed facility. Suppose a correction changes the available
observation scheme from coarse to refined, but the actor’s access interval straddles the first
decision. There are two remaining timing views. In the view where the correction has not yet
arrived, the coarse signal leaves incompatible first instructions in the same information cell
and no robust policy is feasible. In the view where it has arrived, the refined signal supports
the four previously derived policies. The complete financial row population is retained in both
calculations.
The timing evidence supplies no probability for either view. Selecting the four-policy answer
would discard the other possibility.
Reporting the two answers separately is therefore a
substantive conclusion.
Constructing one decision rule that is admissible when even the
observation mechanism is uncertain requires a common specification of what the actor sees and
how actions are represented across those mechanisms; a naive intersection of differently labelled
policy tables is insufficient.
The exact comparison verifies interval boundaries, equivalent offsets, unknown and untrusted
clocks, public and private access, correlated correction times, conflicting revisions and retractions.
The saved partitions retain unresolved source outcomes as well as selected ones. Their scope is
the supplied chronology and access model, not an empirical statement about how a real company
releases information. General revision practices, family-level dependency reuse, authenticated
evidence and durable recovery remain separate questions.7

26.17
A control history must fit the reporting method
A supplied history of control can answer the continuity question without yet supplying a method
for consolidated reporting. The existing selected group-period calculation supports a stable
perimeter and full ownership. A conditional history must therefore be checked against that
scope before the simulator uses the calculation. Matching opening and closing control edges
cannot replace the intervening history.
The checked connection carries the original history request, actor, exact interval and source
identities into the group-report request. It identifies the same parent and subsidiary in both. It
also requires an explicit relation between the history’s reporting basis and the book-composition
measurement basis. Similar labels do not establish that those two premises mean the same
thing.
Time precision matters here too. The book period uses calendar dates, whereas the control history
can use exact instants. The reference requires an explicit fixed-offset calendar interpretation
and compares the resulting midnight boundaries exactly. A partial-day acquisition cannot be
rounded into whole-period control merely because the annual report’s date fields contain no
time of day.
The outcome depends on the whole interval. Missing coverage, conflicting evidence or unresolved
actor availability leave the control-history requirement unresolved. Continuous absence of control
stays outside this parent–subsidiary reporting route, even if an ownership record says 100 per
cent. A gain or loss requires changing-perimeter methods; this includes a loss followed by
regain when the opening and closing assessments are identical. Continuous control with partial
ownership requires non-controlling-interest methods beyond the present full-ownership reference.
Only continuous supplied control together with a separate full-ownership premise fits the existing
method’s control and ownership scope. Even that result is a candidate for further report checks.
It supplies no missing legal-book report, acquisition measurement or accounting authority, and
the scope check itself produces no consolidated statement. The original report can still refuse a
request whose control scope fits but whose other inputs are absent or incompatible.
This separates two sources of failure. If a valid unchanged-period fixture is refused, the existing
report or its inputs need diagnosis. If a history contains a mid-period acquisition and the
stable-perimeter report refuses it, the refusal exposes a missing financial method. The next
work must specify acquisition or loss accounting, dated member and flow allocation, ownership
and non-controlling interests, and the required measurements. A source-history check cannot
manufacture those amounts or authorize a journal merely because its identities and dates
match.2
