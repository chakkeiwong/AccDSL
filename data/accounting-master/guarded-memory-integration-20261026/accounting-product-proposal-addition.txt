17.12.2
The relevant boundary now joins both rates
Following a first-date draw, ordinary second payment requires C2 −I2 ≥0. Substituting the
cash expression gives
I1 + I2 ≤5.25 + R.
(17.11)
For a receipt of 6, the boundary is I1 + I2 = 11.25. Within the interior floating-coupon pieces it
becomes a diagonal boundary in the two rates, 100r1 + 100r2 = 11.25. Separate bounds on the
two marginal rates do not describe which joint histories cross it. Equality permits payment;
larger coupon totals cause refusal.
For a receipt of 7, the corresponding bound is 12.25. Each coupon is at most 6, so their sum is
at most 12 and ordinary second payment is feasible throughout the first-draw region. After
ordinary first payment, I1 ≤5.5; even the receipt of 6 then supplies at least 6 before the next
coupon. The failure with the smaller receipt is thus concentrated in histories that needed an
earlier draw and then face a sufficiently large combined coupon.
The exact comparison first takes the products of the earlier point and open rate intervals. It
then splits those regions at the later linear cash conditions. Every resulting balance and journal
amount is affine in (r1, r2), while each action condition has a fixed truth value. The independent
financial calculation uses the closed-form cash expressions above. It checks that accepted regions
cover exactly the feasible domains, that refusals cover the remainder, and that both calculations
produce the same complete journal and financial state.
The geometric calculation also retains strict boundaries. On a bounded region described by
rational linear inequalities, its closed relaxation is generated by finitely many vertices. The
average of all those vertices satisfies a required strict inequality whenever at least one vertex
satisfies it strictly: every other vertex has nonpositive slack, so their average remains strictly
negative. If all vertices lie on its boundary, no point of the closed region can satisfy that inequality
strictly. Applying this reasoning to every strict constraint decides whether the original region
contains a point. Extremum attainment is checked by adding the optimizing equality and testing
the resulting face in the same way. These arguments apply to the supplied bounded linear
regions; a nonlinear or unbounded problem needs another method. The financial comparator
shares these geometric primitives, so its agreement does not constitute an independent solver
validation.

17.12.3
More cash does not reveal a hidden contract
With a committed facility and a receipt of 7, the refined first-date signal supports the same
initial rule as before: pay when the trigger is false and draw when it is true. On the second
date the manager observes whether the coupon exceeds 5.5 and whether available cash covers
it, together with the earlier signal and chosen order. Ordinary payment is feasible throughout
this family.
Some histories also permit a second-date draw: the first coupon was paid without drawing,
the facility remains unused and the second coupon exceeds 5.5. There are two such reachable
observation classes in this example, and either ordinary payment or drawing is feasible in each.
Consequently four policies satisfy the stipulated feasibility conditions. This count does not rank
their value. Optional borrowing incurs the fee and leaves debt beyond the current horizon.
Under the coarse first-date signal, the manager still cannot distinguish histories requiring
opposite first orders.
Raising the later receipt does not repair that information problem.
Retaining histories without a facility also leaves some first payments unfunded. A correct
continuation therefore preserves contractual memory, cash timing and the information available
to the actor together. Renewing the facility, redrawing the debt type or revealing an unobserved
trigger would change the problem being solved.2
17.13
Connecting the history to accounting execution
A financial path can be correct in a small mathematical model and still be translated incorrectly
into a company’s accounting system. The preceding example treats equity as a single amount
after income and expenses. An accounting book usually distinguishes capital, operating income,
interest expense and fees. Those distinctions matter when the simulator must produce an
income statement, cash-flow statement and retained claim history as well as a closing balance
sheet.
The checked connection uses the existing common accounting executor with explicit definitions
of the selected events. It does not put the earlier Python posting calculation inside that executor.
The comparison instead asks whether independently specified methods produce the same full
accounts, journals, cash-flow origins and contractual memory. Two different account charts test
whether a method depends on accounting roles rather than convenient account names.
17.13.1
Equity agrees only after the accounts are mapped

expenses then normally have positive balances, while liabilities, capital and income normally

corresponds to
E = −Bcapital −Bincome −Binterest expense −Bfee expense.
(17.12)
For example, an operating receipt of 7 gives the income account a credit balance of −7. Two

With no opening capital, the reported equity after profit is 7 −12 −0.25 = −5.25. This equals
the reduced model’s equity without hiding the income statement inside the capital account.

17.13.2
A checked path is not a checked continuous region
The common executor accepts concrete rational amounts. The selected comparison therefore
uses exact points at the coupon cap, at the first trigger, and at and immediately to either
side of the later joint cash boundary. Those executions exercise important failure mechanisms.
They do not by themselves establish that every rate in a continuous region produces the correct
accounting result.
Within a region where the guards are fixed, the amount relations above are affine. If the opening
accounts map correctly, each event has the stated affine effect, and the same guard, timing
and memory conditions govern both descriptions, induction over the accepted events gives the
same mapped financial quantities. The conditions carry the argument: a wrong guard or lost
drawing right can change which event occurs even when its monetary formula is correct. A
general checker must verify those conditions and every supported method, rather than replace
that obligation with a larger sample of rates.
The current evidence consequently has two parts. The exact continuous reference establishes
its financial formulas and feasible domains. The concrete common-engine comparison checks
complete accounting execution at declared points. Their connection is supported by the explicit
mapping and local event derivation, while general symbolic certification remains a separate
method problem. Source-backed terms and actual accounting applicability also remain distinct
from agreement between two synthetic representations.

Chapter 18
Reference computations that
distinguish a useful approximation
from a wrong answer
A method can be tested most clearly when a small version of its target is known exactly. This
chapter constructs such targets for cash bounds, breach probabilities, policy comparison and
residual minimization. The calculations are analytic reference cases. They are not a benchmark
ranking and do not supply empirical evidence about a real company. Their role is to expose
errors that a large simulation can conceal behind apparently stable averages.
18.1
An exact distribution from a declared prior
Return to cash C = 90−2α, and now deliberately assume α is uniform on [0, 1]. This assumption
is not inferred from the financial statements. It creates a transparent probabilistic reference.
Cash is uniform on [88, 90], with mean 89 and variance 4/12 = 1/3.
For a covenant requiring cash at least 89.4, breach occurs when α > 0.3. Its probability is 0.7.
The expected cash conditional on breach is the mean of the interval [88, 89.4), namely 88.7.
These quantities answer different questions: unconditional mean, breach frequency and severity
conditional on breach.
A simulation with the right mean but the wrong breach probability is not accurate for covenant
analysis. For example, a two-point approximation placing equal mass at 88 and 90 has mean 89
but breach probability one half. Matching the first moment does not preserve threshold events.
The exact distribution also gives quantiles. Under the usual left-continuous generalized-inverse
convention, the p-quantile is 88 + 2p for 0 < p < 1, with endpoint values defined by continuous
extension. The convention should be explicit when discrete mass points are introduced, because
quantile definitions can differ at jumps.
18.2
A discrete grid with known bias
Approximate α by equally weighted midpoints (i −1/2)/n, for i = 1, . . . , n. The mean of the
grid remains one half, so mean cash is exactly 89 for every n. Breach frequency is the fraction
of midpoints above 0.3. For n = 4, midpoints are 0.125, 0.375, 0.625 and 0.875, giving breach
probability 0.75.
