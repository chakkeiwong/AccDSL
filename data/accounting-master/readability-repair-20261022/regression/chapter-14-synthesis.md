# Chapter 14 — physical pages 355–368

The chapter asks what evidence the proposed design still needs. Engineering
conformance, accounting fidelity and scientific validity are separated before
the tests are listed. This order matters: the eighteen concrete cases then
read as discriminating checks rather than a universal certificate. Balanced
journals, preserved histories, correctly dated measurements and declared
probability mixtures answer different questions.

The later protocols turn the general evaluation principles into specific
comparisons. E1 preserves complete-package coverage and exact contexts; E2
uses finite independent references for soundness and completeness; E3 fixes
cash-flow targets, source vintages, eligible populations and a strong baseline
ladder. Its CRPS display supplies prerequisites, source anchors and an exact
two-point example. Fixed scaling, missing predictions and dependence-aware
uncertainty are explained as parts of the comparison, not optional reporting.

E4-E6 then distinguish sector predictions, numerical target validity and human
use. Bank allowances are not realized losses. Ultimate reserves and one-year
development are different targets. Exact normal and causal examples check
their supplied models without identifying real company interventions. A single
author using both interfaces remains development evidence. The budget ladder
and seed discussion make future work concrete without inventing a universal
sample size or a scientific default.

The joined-calculation discussion connects these distinctions to the whole
company: a group bridge can balance while its credit, actuarial or time inputs
are wrong. Package capabilities and P0-P6 milestones therefore remain
operation-specific and cumulative. Existing scripts check portions of the
proposal; the acquired report panel is not a complete semantic corpus or an
as-of-eligible forecast sample. The final resource caps describe bounded
failure, not measured capacity.
