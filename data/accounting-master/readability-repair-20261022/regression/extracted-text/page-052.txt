The interim risk preamble incorporates the annual risks and states that there have been no
material changes except those set out below (Apple Inc., 2025c, Part II, Item 1A, printed
p. 19). It preserves the annual dependency and an express exception; it does not assert identical
policies, claims, coverage or loss amounts at the later date. Keeping the original risk, the later
qualified statement and the separately reported updates allows a historical query to retain what
was actually disclosed. The resulting account connects direct proceedings, contract indemnities,
defense, settlement, operating restrictions and possible recovery without collapsing them into a
single loss balance. Calculating their consequences still requires the dated contracts, claim facts
and accounting treatment for each channel.
2.19
A regulation can change the feasible operating plan
A legal exposure need not begin with a claim or end with a payment. An import restriction can
prevent a product from reaching a customer, leaving the company to change its supply route,
offering or production plan. Apple’s annual and interim general-regulation risk both say that its
products could be banned, delayed or prohibited from importation despite procedures intended
to satisfy regulatory requirements. They also warn that measures may arrive with little or
no advance notice and that Apple may be unable to mitigate all adverse impacts (Apple Inc.,
2024a, Risk Factors, printed pp. 13–14) (Apple Inc., 2025c, Part II, Item 1A, printed p. 19).
This describes a possible constraint on operations, separate from the damages, defense and
insurance channels examined above.
The two reports preserve a broad list of regulated subjects: among them antitrust, privacy,
consumer protection, intellectual property, financial services, trade, foreign exchange controls,
cash repatriation, tax and employment. The interim adds national security to that list. It also
expands the discussion of new and changing laws and regulations to name executive orders,
directives and enforcement priorities. These additions appear in the cost-and-business-change,
interpretation and specialized-application passages. They identify the scope of Apple’s later
risk statement without identifying a particular new instrument, effective date, jurisdiction or
violation. A model cannot create a dated legal event merely because a subject is newly named
in this comparison.
Both reports describe how changing requirements can increase costs, restrict a product or
feature, alter its design, affect customer demand and change the business or supply chain. They
describe further risks as products enter specialized applications such as health and financial
services or expand the use of machine learning and artificial intelligence. The annual combines
these consequences and the compliance-policy discussion in one paragraph; the interim splits
and expands it. Paragraph count therefore cannot measure additional economic exposure.
Conversely, the notice-and-import paragraph repeats the same substantive wording. That
repetition establishes what Apple disclosed in each report, rather than identical underlying
laws, products or supply routes.
The policy qualification is essential to the mechanism. Apple says it has implemented policies
and procedures designed to ensure compliance, while giving no assurance that employees,
contractors or agents will not violate laws, regulations or company procedures. Its separate
import passage gives no assurance that programs will prevent a violation or a claim of one.
