Bank models demonstrate why journal generation needs economic state. Halaj (2016), Section
3, combines funding shocks, fire sales, loans, securities and risk constraints. The homogeneous
portfolios and specified funding process make the model tractable, but limit instrument-level
interpretation. Behn et al. (2019), Section 2, make managers’ choices over asset growth, debt,
risk and payouts depend on capital and liquidity constraints. These models motivate interfaces
for management policy and regulatory calculations. Their parameterisations must be calibrated
and evaluated for the target application; passing their accounting identities would not validate
an AccDSL forecast.
Two important neighbouring traditions remain explicit coverage qualifications. McCarthy’s
1982 REA model is a foundational candidate for the relation between resources, events and
agents; Caiani and co-authors’ stock-flow-consistent agent-based benchmark is a candidate for
simulation across interacting sectors. Verified primary PDFs could not be obtained in this
retrieval campaign after institutional and publisher attempts. Their records are retained in the
omission register, and no technical result from either is used to justify the design. AccDSL’s
event and counterparty records are specified and justified in Chapters 7 and 8. A later multi-
company equilibrium engine would require expansion of the stock-flow-consistent literature
before implementation.
4.2
Incomplete information and provenance
Financial disclosures are incomplete databases in a substantive sense. Multiple underlying
records can yield the same published observations.
Libkin (2014), Sections 2–6, explains
the semantics of incomplete databases, labelled nulls, conditional tables and certain answers.
Repeated occurrences of the same unknown must share a value. Conditional tables can represent
dependence between alternatives that independent missing cells cannot express. This is directly
relevant to a debt note and a cash-flow table that refer to the same unknown repayment.
AccDSL adopts shared symbolic unknowns and conditions.
It extends the database idea
with accounting constraints, continuous amounts, temporal transitions and economic event
models. A “certain” answer means a statement that holds for every history in a nonempty
declared feasible family. This is Boolean certainty; a table of individually certain rows can lose
existential information and must not imply that omitted rows are absent. The payable example
in Section 6.2 develops this distinction. The family itself may omit unknown mechanisms;
certainty is therefore always relative to a model scope. Imieliński and Lipski’s original 1984
paper is a foundational backward lead whose publisher copy was inaccessible in this campaign.
The inspected Libkin paper supplies the technical discussion actually used here.
Green et al. (2007), Definitions 3.1–3.2 and Section 4, treat provenance as annotations that
compose through relational operations. Combining sources and alternative derivations can
therefore have formal semantics, rather than being a list of unrelated citations. This is useful
for report aggregation and explanation queries.
