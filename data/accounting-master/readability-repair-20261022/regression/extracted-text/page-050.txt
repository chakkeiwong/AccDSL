2.17.1
Unchanged critical estimates do not mean constant amounts
Annual Critical Accounting Estimates says management makes judgments, assumptions and
estimates affecting reported amounts, using historical experience and other assumptions it
considers reasonable to judge carrying values. Its uncertain-tax discussion says reserves can
be adjusted as facts and circumstances change, and that final outcomes may differ from those
reserves. Its legal-contingency policy is the incurred-loss and estimability condition examined
above (Apple Inc., 2024a, Critical Accounting Estimates, printed p. 26). These are descriptions
of judgment and policy, not a disclosed numerical estimation model or an assurance that the
estimates will prove accurate.
The interim references both annual and interim Note 1 and reports no material changes to
critical accounting estimates since the annual filing (Apple Inc., 2025c, Critical Accounting
Estimates, printed p. 17). That statement preserves the annual dependency and its materiality
qualifier. It cannot make every carrying amount, reserve or underlying assumption exactly
constant, and it supplies no numerical tolerance for a comparison. A model should retain the
reported policy and qualified continuity assertion alongside the actual dated balances, then
request the missing estimate or transition facts when the query needs them. These estimate
passages supply policy and continuity assertions; they do not establish numerical stability,
current compliance or the tax and expense details still absent from these sources.
2.18
Claims against another party can still affect Apple
The estimate policy leaves a further question: whose claim can give rise to a cost for Apple? A
company can face exposure through an agreement even when the claim is against someone else.
Apple’s annual risk discussion says its agreements sometimes include indemnification provisions
that can subject it to costs and damages in the event of a claim against an indemnified third party
(Apple Inc., 2024a, Risk Factors, printed p. 13). The direction matters: the passage describes
