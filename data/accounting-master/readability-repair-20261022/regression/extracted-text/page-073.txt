The same rule protects committed accounting events. The connected insurance example commits
financing, service, incurrence and settlement as four separate events. After settlement, retrying
the exact original financing request returns its original receipt and reports no additional commit;
today’s evidence revision does not turn that retry into a new assessment. Changing the request
while retaining the business-event identity is a conflict. The request’s original bindings, historical
admission and required bytes must still validate before receipt lookup.
A new conclusion that the treaty came later does not itself authorize an entry reducing the
committed reinsurance asset. A correction proposal must identify its accounting treatment,
effective period, affected accounts and opening-balance consequences under the applicable
authority.
Without that support, the proposed assessment returns TREATMENT_UNSUPPORTED,
retains the revised conditional calculation and creates no journal. Versioning supplies the record
of a correction; it cannot supply the accounting rule that makes the correction appropriate.
