When the hedged purchase becomes inventory
A hedge reserve does not remain in equity indefinitely once the forecast purchase takes place.
When that purchase produces inventory, IFRS 9.6.5.11(d)(i) requires the accumulated reserve to
be included directly in the inventory’s initial carrying cost. This transfer is not a reclassification
through profit or loss. The carrying cost reaches earnings later, when the goods are sold, under
IAS 2.34 (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9, paragraph 6.5.11(d)(i); IAS 2, paragraphs
10–11 and 34).
Return to the three purchase layers: 100 tonnes at designation price 80, ten at 90 and twenty
at 95. Suppose all 130 tonnes are received when the supplier price is 97. The supplier is paid
130 × 97 = 12610. At that date the accepted cumulative derivative gain is 1900, reserve gain
1810 and ineffectiveness income 90. Assume the derivative is fully settled for its carrying value,
with no fees or further valuation movement. The terms permit that settlement; no such right is
inferred merely from the numbers.
Record the supplier purchase at 12610, then receive 1900 and remove the derivative asset. To
transfer the reserve, debit the cash-flow hedge reserve 1810 and credit inventory 1810. Inventory
therefore carries
K = 12610 −1810 = 10800.
The 90 of previously recognized ineffectiveness remains in profit. Starting with cash and equity
of 20000 before the hedge, cash after purchase and settlement is 20000 −12610 + 1900 = 9290.
Cash plus inventory, 9290 + 10800 = 20090, equals opening equity plus that profit of 90. The
reserve transfer creates no new income or cash.
The same direction rule handles a reserve loss. Take instead the later observation with supplier
price 80, derivative liability 500, reserve loss 400 and cumulative ineffectiveness loss 100. All 130
tonnes cost 10400; settling the derivative requires a further payment of 500. The reserve is a
debit balance, so transfer it by debiting inventory 400 and crediting the reserve 400. Inventory
again carries 10400+400 = 10800. Cash is 20000−10400−500 = 9100, and cash plus inventory
equals 19900, consistent with the loss of 100. These are two different terminal observations, not
two optional treatments of the same unchanged purchase.
In signed notation, let C be the supplier’s cost, R the cumulative reserve gain (negative for a
loss), D the signed derivative settlement receipt and I = D −R the cumulative ineffectiveness
income. Then
K = C −R,
∆Cash = −C + D.
The basis transfer is debit reserve R, credit inventory R, reversing both directions if R < 0. It
changes neither cash nor earnings. Suppose all the goods are subsequently sold for cash S, with
recognition conditions satisfied and no further costs. Cost of sales is K, so cumulative pre-tax
profit becomes
S −K + I = S −(C −R) + (D −R) = S −C + D.
For sale proceeds of 14500, the gain case ends with profit 3790 and cash 23790; the loss case
ends with profit 3600 and cash 23600. The purchase and sale are operating cash flows. IAS 7.16
classifies the cash flows of an identifiable hedge consistently with the hedged position, so the
derivative settlement here is also operating, while retaining its own gross receipt or payment
origin (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 7, paragraph 16).
