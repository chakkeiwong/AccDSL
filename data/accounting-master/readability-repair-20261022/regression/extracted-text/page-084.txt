6.1
Define what is observed before inverting it
Let Dk be the disclosures available under information cutoff k. Each numeric observation di has
a context: entity or group perimeter, concept, measurement basis, dimensions, currency, period
and precision. These contexts are part of the observation operator, not additional columns that
can be discarded after extraction.
A candidate history h contains an opening economic and accounting state, events through
the last historical date T, contract terms, accounting policies, consolidation relationships and
latent quantities. Let M denote the declared model scope: its event templates, admissible rule
versions and allowed unknowns. It excludes arbitrary events that the chosen model has no way
to represent. The reporting operator Oi(h) produces the quantity to which observation i refers,
including its basis and perimeter.
Reported numbers are often rounded. If a number vi is rounded to the nearest unit qi, an
illustrative admissible interval is [vi −qi/2, vi + qi/2) under the corresponding tie convention.
This half-open endpoint choice is used only when that tie rule is declared; Section 3.12 gives
the conservative closed enclosure when nearest rounding is assumed but the tie rule is unknown.
The actual rounding convention, scale and XBRL accuracy metadata govern the imported
constraint. A disclosure with unspecified precision does not acquire a convenient tolerance
silently. Write Ii for the justified observation interval, and CM(h) for the joint accounting,
contractual and semantic constraints. Then the historical compatibility set is
HM(Dk) = {h ∈HM : CM(h) ∧Oi(h) ∈Ii for every applicable i}.
(6.1)
Qualitative assertions enter as reviewed logical conditions, rather than numeric intervals.
Incompatible source contexts are never forced into the same equation.
Equation (6.1) distinguishes three outcomes. An empty set means that the observations and
declared model are inconsistent. A singleton means that this model identifies a history at its
chosen resolution. A larger set preserves unresolved alternatives. An empty set does not prove
that the company’s accounts are false: a wrong extraction, missing event type or incorrect
perimeter can cause the inconsistency. A singleton does not prove that the model contains the
real history.
