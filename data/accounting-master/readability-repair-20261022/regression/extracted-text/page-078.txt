FinQA (Chen et al., 2021), Sections 3–6, provides questions with executable reasoning programs
and supporting evidence. TAT-QA (Zhu et al., 2021), Sections 2–4, combines table cells and
text spans with aggregation operations, and explicitly treats numerical signs in evaluation.
DocFinQA (Reddy et al., 2024) extends financial questions to long documents and evaluates
retrieval/context choices. FinBen (Xie et al., 2024) broadens the collection of financial tasks
and model evaluations. These benchmarks can test retrieval and local numerical reasoning.
Their question-answer format does not require a complete multi-period company representation,
joint alternative histories or scenario-dependent journal generation.
A directly relevant recent competitor is the auditable RAG architecture of Cosentino et al. (2026).
Sections 3.1–3.2 describe persistent extraction, enrichment, retrieval and provenance records;
Section 4.6 checks citation resolvability and failure recovery. Section 5 limits the interpretation
of those results to the evaluated document-intelligence tasks. AccDSL should reuse the idea
of persistent evidence and recoverable processing, while adding executable accounting and
economic semantics. No performance comparison with that architecture has been run here.
4.6
Solvers and probabilistic execution
de Moura and Bjørner (2008), Sections 2–3, describe Z3’s interfaces and theory-solving architec-
ture. It supports choosing an SMT solver for logical choices and arithmetic constraints, but
does not make arbitrary nonlinear simulation tractable. The first solver fragment is deliberately
bounded: finite choices, exact linear arithmetic and selected calendar relations. Unsupported
formulas remain in the representation with a solver diagnostic.
van de Meent et al. (2018) develop probabilistic programs through sampling, observations
and inference; the inspected material includes the first-order language and weighted sampling
discussion. Stan (Carpenter et al., 2017), its model language and sampler-output discussion, and
Pyro (Bingham et al., 2019), Sections 2–3, show different ways to separate a probabilistic model
from inference machinery. They are candidates for optional inference over an already-defined
economic model. Exact accounting identities and discrete rule selection should not be turned
into nearly satisfied likelihood penalties merely to fit an inference library. Nor should successful
sampler execution be described as evidence that all interpretations were explored.
