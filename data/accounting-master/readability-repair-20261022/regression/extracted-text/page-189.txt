Now supply the fiscal treatment. The tax authority treats the initial 100 entirely as debt and
permits a deduction for the coupon when it accrues. It allows no separate deduction for the
accounting discount. The fiscal liability therefore rises to 104 before payment and falls to
100 when the coupon is paid. Assume sufficient taxable income to use the deduction and no
additional tax on either maturity redemption or original-terms conversion. These are explicit

The first row reduces conversion equity from 8 to 6. The next row has a different earnings
consequence: debit deferred tax liability 2 and credit deferred tax income 2. IAS 12.23 explicitly
places these subsequent changes in profit or loss. The coupon deduction also reduces current
tax expense by 4/4 = 1. Thus the financing cost after tax is
I + ∆Tcurrent + ∆Tdeferred = 12 −1 −2 = 9.
There is no second expense of 2 for the initial equity charge. Conversely, using only the coupon
would miss the discount accretion and its tax reversal (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 12,
paragraphs 23, 58 and 61A; IAS 33, paragraphs 33–34).
The relation extends to the specified one-period fiscal treatment. Let P denote both proceeds
and principal at maturity, L the initial debt, C the coupon and t the unchanged tax rate. Gross
conversion equity is E = P −L; effective interest is I = P + C −L = E + C. The initial
deferred liability tE reverses as book and tax amounts meet, while the coupon supplies a current
deduction of C. Therefore
I −tC −tE = (1 −t)I.
A zero coupon still leaves discount accretion and deferred tax income. If E = 0, the deferred
difference vanishes and only the coupon deduction remains. Different fiscal bases, changing
rates or restricted deductions require their own schedule before this identity can be used.
