These balances leave two practical financing questions open: how much cash can fund a payment,
and when the payment falls due. The annual cash and cash equivalents balance of 29,943 includes
2.6 billion held in escrow and restricted from general use for the State Aid tax obligation. Note
4 states the inclusion explicitly, and Note 7 connects the escrow to that obligation (Apple Inc.,
2024a, Notes 4 and 7, printed pp. 37 and 39). Subtracting the reported restricted component
gives 29,943 −2,600 = 27,343 million as an indicative remainder at the displayed amounts. The
restricted component is rounded to tenths of a billion, so this subtraction does not establish an
exact freely available balance. Interpreting the reported cash balance as excluding the escrow
is wrong relative to this footnote. Correcting that interpretation changes funding calculations
without changing the issuer’s reported number. It also requires revisiting any liquidity result
that relied on the exclusion.
The debt movement supplies a second reason to preserve a calculation’s missing terms. The
first-quarter cash flow statement reports repayments of term debt of 1,009 million, while carrying
amount falls by 96,662 −94,804 = 1,858 million. At the displayed amounts, the reconciliation is
94,804 = 96,662 −1,009 −849.
The 849 million is the residual after the reported cash repayment; it does not identify an
exchange loss, an income-statement charge or a particular journal. Explaining it requires the
missing movement information, including applicable currency, discount/premium and hedge-
accounting effects. The quarterly cash flow statement still reconciles independently: operating
inflow 29,935 plus investing inflow 9,792 less financing outflow 39,371 gives the reported cash
increase of 356, from 29,943 to 30,299 million (Apple Inc., 2025c, statement of cash flows and
Note 6). A balanced cash bridge therefore does not identify the debt history.

Apple’s disclosed 52-week fiscal 2025 ends on 27 September 2025 under its last-Saturday
convention. The November maturity therefore belongs to fiscal 2026 even though the bond’s
name says 2025. The annual Note 9 principal-payment schedule uses fiscal years, whereas the
instrument’s stated maturity is a calendar date. Joining those fields on their year number would
put a billion euros of scheduled principal into the wrong fiscal bucket. Neither the later holding
date nor the euro principal may be silently substituted for a September US-dollar consolidated
balance.
