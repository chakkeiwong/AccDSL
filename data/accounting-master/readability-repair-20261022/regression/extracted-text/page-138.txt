The invoice and the work need different clocks
The builder can earn the same construction and inspection revenue while being paid on a
different schedule. Retain the preceding allocations, costs and approved amendment, but place
all work and payments in calendar 2024. At inception and after the amendment, the expected
interval between each payment and its related service is at most one year, and the builder
elects the financing expedient. Receivables and contract assets have a separately assessed zero
impairment allowance in this example. These findings matter: AASB 15 requires the financing
assessment and AASB 9 treatment of the rights to consideration; eventual collection alone
establishes neither (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022e, paras. 63,105–109).
Suppose the customer initially advances only 30. Opening cash and capital of 100 become cash
130 and a contract liability of 30. In April the builder pays construction costs of 40 and visit
costs of 2, earning the same revenue of 55 as before. Cash falls to 88. The advance covers the
first 30 of that work; the remaining 25 is a contract asset because the right to payment still
depends on a billing milestone. Profit is 13 and equity is 113.
Now the milestone makes a gross invoice of 80 unconditional. The invoice credits the advance of
30 already received, leaving a receivable of 50. The contract asset disappears. But the builder
has earned only 55 of the 80 now paid or due, so a contract liability of 25 also appears. Figure
9.1 isolates this change. The receivable and contract liability coexist: one is an unconditional
payment right and the other is work still owed under the same contract.

The advance must therefore retain its identity after receipt. Crediting it reduces the new
amount collectible; it does not refund cash or recognise revenue again. Let G be cumulative
gross invoices, K the advances credited against those invoices, and C later collections of the
resulting receivables. The amount still collectible is
AR = G −K −C.
For the invoice just issued, this is 80 −30 −0 = 50. Collecting 20 changes cash to 108 and the
receivable to 30, with no change in revenue. Each credit names both its advance and invoice;
each collection names its invoice. A second credit cannot reuse an advance that has already
been consumed.
