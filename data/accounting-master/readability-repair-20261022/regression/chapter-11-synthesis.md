# Chapter 11 — credit losses and the bank balance sheet

Version 3.47, physical pages 244–277, read in order. This is author diagnosis;
independent reader, domain, implementability and human acceptance remain pending.

The bank chapter asks which facts turn a reported balance into a credit,
funding or capital consequence. Its source-based opening preserves parent and
consolidated populations, dated dividend capacity, guarantees and debt rights.
An exhibit relationship is not a complete control graph. A reported capacity
to distribute funds is not cash received. Default, notice, acceleration and
succession require their actors, periods and voting populations. Those details
give the later conditional examples a reason to retain more than totals.

Credit measurement starts with a limited exposure/probability/severity formula
and then adds dependence, cash-flow timing and classification. The capital
example separately derives its eligible allowance base before deductions.
Its arithmetic cannot supply missing leverage averages or turn a historical
scope into a current prudential conclusion. A deposit-payment failure can leave
capital ratios unchanged, so the chapter does not use capital as evidence of
liquidity. Classification likewise precedes the loan calculation: business
model, contractual terms and elections select distinct profit and OCI routes.

The loan sequence is cumulative. A commitment draw removes funding uncertainty
but retains original credit history. Survival conditioning differs from a new
forecast and from an admitted significant-risk increase. Credit impairment
changes the interest basis; a supported no-recovery finding permits write-off.
The ordinary example reconciles loss39.5 without an equity plug. The purchased-
impaired example then explains why its original expected loss belongs in the
credit-adjusted rate and why a later favourable change can require a negative
allowance. Interest unwind and forecast-revision income remain distinct.

Dated receipts and partial write-offs extend that argument beyond annual
terminal claims. The example supplies a day-count convention and a separately
identified unrecoverable portion. It does not treat a lower forecast as proof
that the whole claim should be written off. Fractional powers and irrational
original rates create numerical uncertainty about one history. Certified bounds
must therefore travel through the schedule, retain shared dependence and meet
the declared precision budget; separate endpoints cannot be assembled into
arbitrary journals. These numerical passages explain their assumptions before
the equations and use cash and accumulated origins as different checks.

Changed terms, credit cure and derecognition require different findings. The
paired cure example is particularly useful: equal final wealth conceals a
different interest presentation. Delayed collateral recovery includes timing
and enforcement costs, while accounting write-off leaves legal discharge
unassessed. Revolving repayment restores capacity without erasing credit
history. Fee purpose selects yield or service treatment. Partial transfers use
eligible rights and relative fair values; retained risks can turn the same cash
receipt into financing. Guarantees and options retain exposures through separate
asset and liability measurements. Call, put and collar equations explain the
economic difference; the amortised-cost case needs two interest schedules.

The final settlement example connects these methods to the earlier event and
information design. Capacity, request and confirmed receipt are different
facts. Three borrowing histories preserve unpaid deposits and the same equity,
while lost acknowledgements preserve uncertainty until reconciliation. Actual
unexpected cash remains recordable even when it violates an admission control.
Capital and behavioural policies then require their own dated inputs and
comparative evidence.
