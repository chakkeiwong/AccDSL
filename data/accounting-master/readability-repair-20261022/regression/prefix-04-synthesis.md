# Cumulative argument through Chapter 4

The ordered reading of physical pages 1–79 now supports a continuous argument
from the motivating financial ambiguity to the choices a system architecture
must make. The front matter and Chapter 1 establish the decision at stake:
identical historical journals can conceal fixed- and floating-rate exposures
that produce different future cash consequences. Balanced accounting is a
constraint on histories, not a unique reconstruction. Retaining an admissible
set also leaves its probability measure unspecified.

Chapter 2 makes that distinction inspectable across increasingly difficult
cases. The complete scoped Northstar example shows what can be reconstructed
when the relevant financial and economic assumptions are supplied. Corporate
and bank/insurer examples broaden the kinds of state and treatment needed.
The move to Apple then changes the observation problem. Statement arithmetic
constrains financial amounts while leaving transactions, ownership, operating
capacity, legal remedies and payment timing incompletely determined. Connected
conditional histories preserve settlement pairs and source versions; a few
feasible examples do not establish the complete wider family. This is a change
in what the reader can infer from the evidence, not merely a longer case list.

Chapter 3 supplies the reusable procedures those cases lacked. Assertions
retain their source, measurement, population and dates before a mapping or
solver uses them. Product and channel classifications demonstrate why matching
totals do not license double counting. Document coverage, assertion coverage
and query coverage answer different questions. A faithfully copied wrong
reference can be refuted as a navigation target without deleting the source.
Information views and selective invalidation then connect a correction to the
derived answers it changes. The final bank and treaty examples carry the
distinction into execution: revised knowledge cannot mutate an old committed
event or authorise a new correcting journal without an applicable treatment.

Chapter 4 now explains why those requirements lead to several technical
literatures. Conservation and stock-flow models support structure; incomplete
databases and provenance retain dependence and derivation; partial
identification distinguishes observed constraints from distributional choices;
rule and contract languages supply scoped execution semantics. Extraction and
solver results enter only after the meaning of their inputs and outputs has
been motivated. The fair typed-library/shared-IR competitor remains explicit.
No source family is presented as already solving the whole integration task.

The cumulative dependencies are therefore recoverable without reading ahead:
financial ambiguity motivates families; concrete disclosures expose the
information that families must preserve; representation and revision rules
give those distinctions operational meaning; the literature supplies candidate
methods and their limits. Repeated cautions about probability, completeness
and balanced journals have different consequences at these stages. They do
not yet contradict or replace the opening objective. Terms such as assertion,
history, economic event, journal and information view remain distinguishable.

The next architectural claim must show how components preserve those meanings
across their interfaces. It still owes construction procedures, exact formal
behaviour, compatible implementations and empirical comparison. The prefix
has not established originality, full disclosure semantics, general accounting
validity or practical superiority. No new confirmed defect is recorded through
page 79. This is a cumulative author assessment of the actual ordered reading;
independent comprehension, implementability, domain and human acceptance remain
pending, and the complete 401-page candidate remains not_ready.
