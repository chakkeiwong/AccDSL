A.54.6
Information cutoffs require time within the day
The assessment’s civil date cannot determine whether a source was available at the decision
time. Evidence arriving at 00:00:01 on 1 April is later than a cutoff at 00:00:00 on that date,
although the two civil dates agree. The retained bank assessment checks the full timestamp.
The common requirement versions, however, support ordered dates and rational numbers but
have no timestamp type. Passing a date comparison therefore cannot discharge that native
timing duty.
Precision matters even when a timestamp parser is available. The retained native schema
accepts both 2026-04-01T00:00:00.0000001Z and 2026-04-01T00:00:00.0000002Z. These instants
differ by one tenth of a microsecond, but its datetime-based conversion maps both to the same
value. That conversion gives the wrong ordering for a claim of exact timestamp comparison. The
retained bank examples themselves use whole-second timestamps; their checked comparisons
are unaffected. The defect concerns extending the same conversion to all precision accepted by
its schema.
The separate ExactInstantProjection/1 addresses this boundary without changing the original
requirement evaluators. First, the existing binder selects the timestamp strings from exact
supplied record bytes. The projection then converts each known value to rational seconds from
midnight UTC on 1 January of year 1, under an explicit proleptic Gregorian convention without
leap seconds. It counts whole days and seconds with integers, adds the exact decimal fraction,
and subtracts the supplied signed offset. No floating-point timestamp or microsecond truncation
enters this calculation. The resulting coordinate serves ordering within the declared convention;
physical elapsed time across leap seconds is a separate question.
For example, 01:00:00 with offset +01:00 and 00:00:00 with suffix Z have equal coordinates. Their
original strings and source identities remain distinct. The two fractional instants above retain
their different rational coordinates, so the later one fails an available-by comparison against the
earlier cutoff. The existing dimensional requirement evaluator performs that comparison. Each
value has a seconds dimension and the same explicit time-axis identity; the complete original
context is preserved separately from the time unit. A financial currency or scale in that context
cannot become a unit of time.
The accepted syntax requires a full date, hours, minutes and seconds, followed by Z or an
explicit signed hour-and-minute offset. An optional fraction has at most 18 decimal digits. That
precision bound is a declared reference limit: more digits are refused, never rounded. Leap
seconds, date-only values, missing offsets, the unknown-offset spelling -00:00, named timezones
and UTC normalization outside years 1–9999 are unsupported. Invalid dates and out-of-range
clock or offset fields are invalid. A date-only disclosure needs an explicit interval or availability
policy before it could support an instant comparison.
Unknown bindings keep their original reasons. Conversion preserves the selected role, pointer,
schema and raw-record identity together with the original string; it cannot upgrade a supplied
timestamp into an authenticated fact. Source-view membership, the truth of availability and
ingestion times, measurement dates and the current view still require separate evidence. A
capability to compare times grants none of those admissions.
