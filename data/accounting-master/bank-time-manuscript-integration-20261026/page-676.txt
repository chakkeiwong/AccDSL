The new profile binds the exact schema and semantic-description bytes. Eight record kinds
receive new schema identities because their typed references depend on the profile: the profile
itself, operation signature, request, result, transport observation, query receipt, coverage domain
and coverage region. Unchanged financial records retain their original schemas and, when
their contents are unchanged, their original identities. A conversion creates a new request
in a distinct workspace and new exact submission bytes. It publishes a new query receipt
while preserving the old request, receipt and query-index entry. Replacing a supplied evidence
timestamp additionally changes the identities of the records that depend on it.
Profile, schema, semantic-description and signature checks precede replay. An old request
consequently cannot acquire the successor’s meaning merely because it has a cached result.
Within the successor, submitting the same request bytes returns the same output, receipt,
statuses and coverage, even when the current view or accounting head has since advanced.
Different bytes under an already used request identity are refused. A new request still needs
the declared view, accounting head, execution environment and budget. A refusal has no result,
receipt or coverage and changes no world state. A successful or partial query changes the
immutable query records and index; it posts no journal and changes no accounting balance or
owner head.
The finite conversion is checked against all 33 retained positive publications: 25 regulatory
assessments, seven conditional-loss queries and one policy description. Expected outputs come
from the saved publications with declared reference substitutions. The comparisons preserve
entire outputs, including unknown amounts, undefined ratios, diagnostic codes, unresolved
obligations and coverage. They do not compare only a selected ratio. Each converted publication
coexists with its original query and receipt.
Thirty complete timing cases then exercise all three operations. Twenty-one outcomes inten-
tionally change relative to a comparator that retains the old time methods: later fractional
availability or ingestion, excessive precision in evidence or cutoff times, unknown offsets and
eighteenth-digit ordering are refused. Equal times and equivalent offsets remain accepted. Forty-
two exact replays and 28 additional controls check identity conflicts, stale locks, signatures,
decoder descriptions, heads, views and publication effects. All 63 saved conversion packages
reconstruct and pass their full response checks.
These examples establish a bounded conversion between two documentary profiles. They
share the retained financial methods and synthetic inputs; they do not independently validate
those methods against current banking rules or authenticate a real disclosure’s publication
time. Concurrent service execution, hardware-loss recovery, general profile migration and
empirical forecast accuracy require separate evidence. The original profiles and examples
remain preserved.

A.54.9
Writing the complete method behind an event
A command that selects a registered payment method does not yet explain how an author
writes that method. The payment needs a condition on available cash, a decrease in the cash
account, a decrease in outstanding principal, a financing cash-flow explanation and a check that
the resulting liability still agrees with the loan record. Leaving any of those parts behind a
method name would leave a material part of the financial algorithm outside the authored text.
