# New publisher lead leaves the zero-weight allocation unresolved

26 October 2026. MC17/19/24/26/29/30/35; P17-04.08.
The actual technical text recovered from EY International GAAP 2026 does not
select an allocation for the declared all-zero eligible carrying-weight case.
No method, allocation default or manuscript change follows from this source.

The publisher's IFRS resources page linked its International GAAP 2026 page,
which linked the complete PDF archive. The archive and 4,962-page PDF are retained
under sources. The cover identifies the 2026 edition and EY International
Corporate Reporting Group. This is the publisher's technical interpretation,
not a replacement for applicable standards or an entity-specific authority
assessment. Its edition label does not establish all current amendments.

Inspected Chapter 21 sections 11.3–11.4.2, physical/printed pages 1636–1639,
and the loss-allocation discussion in section 11.2 on page 1634. Pages 1638 and
1639 were rendered and visually inspected. Chapter 21 spans physical pages
1536–1661; its full extracted text is retained for targeted discovery, but it
was not read comprehensively. Keyword searches are discovery diagnostics only.

Section 11.4 distinguishes evidence of improved service potential from the
passage of time and describes the unimpaired carrying-amount limit. Illustration
11-5 concerns one independently cash-generating asset: positive opening carrying
amount, new expenditure and subsequent reversal. It is not a multi-asset CGU
with all eligible balances zero. Section 11.4.1, page 1639, describes pro rata
allocation excluding goodwill, individual limits and redistribution. It neither
supplies a zero-denominator alternative nor explains why the target case cannot
arise. The scope of this finding is those inspected passages.

The declared target is an authoritative allocation among at least two zero-
carrying non-goodwill assets with positive restoration ceilings. What was found
is ordinary pro rata/capped reversal guidance and a single-asset example. Those
are different. The existing 23 October limit proof already rejects a unique
epsilon continuation under its fixed-ceiling domain; it was not rerun or
reclassified as accounting authority.

The direct IAS Plus page returned HTTP 200 but only a script shell in the saved
HTML; no technical text was inspected there. Built-in search/open calls returned
no usable result content. Neither result establishes source absence. No paywall,
authentication restriction or publisher access control was bypassed; the EY
archive was a public publisher link. No outreach or fees were incurred.
