8.6
Equal totals are insufficient
Two states with cash 50 and debt 100 can differ in tomorrow’s maturity. Two states with equal
tomorrow payments can differ in a disclosure that changes the next decision. Neither pair can
automatically be merged for the corresponding query.
Givan et al. (2003, Section 3.1) shows why equality under fixed action sequences is too weak
for general policy equivalence in an MDP: policies react to intermediate states. Section 3.3
develops stochastic bisimulation through equal rewards and transition probabilities to equivalent
blocks for each action. The accounting application below adds observation and requested-output
preservation; this extension is a local sufficiency argument.
8.7
A sufficient probabilistic condition
Consider a finite horizon. Let x be full state, a an action and s a scenario control. Let
Kt(dx′, do, dy | x, a, s) be the joint law of next state, observation and requested output. Let
φt(x) be the compressed state. States sharing a compressed state must have equal feasible
actions and current outputs. For every allowed a, s and measurable set B, also require
Kt({(x′, o, y) : (φt+1(x′), o, y) ∈B} | x1, a, s)
= Kt({(x′, o, y) : (φt+1(x′), o, y) ∈B} | x2, a, s).
(8.2)
Initial distribution and information must be mapped consistently. Policies use only the retained
information history and permitted memory. Under these conditions, the compressed model
preserves the requested joint output and observation law. Equal means, or equal marginal
laws separately, are weaker. Dependence between observation and successor state matters for
learning.
8.8
Finite-horizon proof
At the final date, equality of current outputs establishes the result. Suppose the compressed
model reproduces continuation laws from date t + 1. At date t, an admissible policy selects
the same action distribution for identical retained information histories. Equal feasible actions
make this choice meaningful in either state.
Equation (8.2) gives the same joint law of next compressed state, observation and immediate
output.
At each successor, the induction hypothesis supplies the same continuation law.
Integrating equal continuation laws against the equal successor law gives equality of the
remaining path law. Backward induction reaches the initial date.
This condition is sufficient, not necessary. The finite helper described next checks explicitly
supplied finite models; it is not a general checker for arbitrary company-state representations. If
managers privately observe a hidden variable removed by φ, their policy may not be expressible
in the compressed model. The information condition must be strengthened or the state refined.
Incorrect kernels cannot be repaired by an otherwise correct aggregation proof.
