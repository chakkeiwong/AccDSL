IAS12.34–36 permits a loss DTA only to the extent of probable available taxable
profit; recent losses demand convincing other evidence or suitable taxable
temporary differences. The finding must identify the same taxable entity,
authority and usable interval. Paragraphs37/56 require reassessment. The finite
case supplies a supported amount expected in the next period, not a probability
estimated by this method. Negative evidence gives zero recognition; unknown
evidence leaves the dependent close unresolved.

IAS12.46–48 measures current tax at enacted/substantively enacted current rates
and deferred tax at the rate expected on recovery, supported by enacted law.
Paragraphs58/60/61A follow the underlying transaction's profit/OCI/equity origin.
Here all new operations and tax origins are profit; the parent's old compound
equity10 remains equity. The calculator cannot put a rate change into original
compound equity merely because that balance is present.

IAS12.71–76 separates current offset criteria and deferred offset criteria.
A deferred asset cannot be netted against a current liability. Parent and
subsidiary are different taxable entities; group consolidation alone grants
neither loss transfer nor offset rights. Standalone offset examples must supply
the applicable right/authority/settlement facts and retain gross amounts.
IAS12.79–82 requires major components, unused/expired losses, rate explanation
and evidence supporting recognised loss assets; the reference retains them.

IAS1.36 requires period/reason/noncomparability disclosure for the short stub;
37 permits calendars differing from the calendar year and38 retains prior
comparatives. IAS8.36–38 treats new estimates prospectively. Later recovery
evidence must not overwrite the stub's recognised asset or earnings. Known
prior errors belong to the existing correction method, not this estimate path.
