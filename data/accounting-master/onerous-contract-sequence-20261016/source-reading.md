Inspected IFRS15.99–104 (lines50611–50668) for systematic amortization,
recoverable cost/consideration, credit effects, other-asset-first/CGU-after
ordering and reversal ceiling. IAS37.14 (18914–18930) supplies present obligation,
probable outflow and reliable measurement. Paragraphs36–45 (19087–19167)
require best estimate, risk treatment and material discounting. Paragraphs59–62
(19242–19259) require current estimates and use only for the original expenditure.
Paragraphs63–69 (19264–19307) distinguish operating losses from a contract
obligation, least net exit cost, directly related allocated cost and prior
asset impairment. The text expressly includes depreciation among direct cost
allocations; this finite profile isolates cash allocations and refuses noncash
future cost rather than silently treating it as zero.

Let A0 be the original unamortized contract cost, A the currently carried amount,
B expected remaining consideration after credit effects, and D the complete
future direct cash cost excluding the existing asset's amortization. The
supported recoverable ceiling for the isolated cost asset is H=max(B−D,0).
After other-standard asset tests and before the separate no-additional-CGU-loss
finding, set Anew=min(A0,H). At a later review this permits reversal only up to
the original no-amortization amount, because no service has yet occurred.
Impairment expense at that event is A−Anew. This is a narrow application of
IFRS15.101–104; the current cost asset is not subtracted again inside D.

After these asset tests, total remaining fulfilment expense is D+Anew, while
remaining benefits are B. The net fulfilment loss is F=max(D+Anew−B,0).
An available enforceable cancellation route has net compensation K, with no
benefit, refund or other obligation on cancellation. The remaining provision
is P=min(F,K). This formula is a local derivation of IAS37.68 for these explicit
facts, not a universal rule for every customer or purchase contract. In
particular min(D,K)−B is wrong: it incorrectly retains the performance receipt
in the cancellation alternative. A0−Anew is already recognized as asset loss;
adding it to P again double counts the same carrying-value reduction.

Completion recognizes the supplied point-in-time performance, full cash
receipt B, future cash expenditure D and amortization Anew. Using P against
those same contract expenditures gives completion result B−D−Anew+P. When
fulfilment is the least net exit cost and the contract is onerous, that result
is zero. A cheaper cancellation can leave a further loss if management still
performs: minimum unavoidable loss is not a choice of action or a forecast of
management policy. Cash and accrual origins remain separate.

Cancellation pays the admitted K, extinguishes the contract and uses only the
provision for that same obligation. Any remaining contract-cost asset has no
future service and is expensed separately; the request needs an explicit no-
alternative-use finding. No customer performance revenue is recognized merely
because a provision is released. General administration was excluded from D
and cannot consume this provision; it is a separate period expense when paid.

The principal path has A0=20, B=100, D=130, K=50. It gives impairment20 and
provision30, not an additional provision50. With K=20, provision20 and later
cancellation yield a total loss40. A positive recoverable asset at D=90 leaves
asset10 and provision0. These cases distinguish the order and population of
costs; an aggregate trial balance alone cannot do so. Missing prerequisite
findings refuse this method rather than establish that no impairment or
provision exists in the real company.
