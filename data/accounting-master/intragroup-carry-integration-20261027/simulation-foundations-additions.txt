26.20.3
The dated journals make the closing report explainable
The comparison retains every legal entry, including the supplied opening trial balances, the
explicitly stipulated pre-control receipt and interim close, acquisition consideration, controlled-
period receipts and depreciation, internal sale and settlement, outside sale and current taxes.
Replaying these dated journals recovers the opening, acquisition and closing accounts. Their
origins distinguish an outside receipt from a reciprocal internal payment; the consolidation
entries remain in a separate worksheet. A set of closing balances could not establish that history
on its own.
No resale, half resale and full resale are compared under each selected NCI election and
consideration mix. Unknown tax preserves the missing dependency instead of completing
the report with zero. These results remain a one-lot, same-period downstream example with
supplied revenue, tax and ordinary-rights premises.5 Carrying unsold inventory into another
year creates a new question: the next report must release profit from opening group equity and
carry its tax basis, rather than repeat the original acquisition or eliminate a sale that occurred
in the prior year. That requires a separate two-year comparison.
26.21
The next year starts from closed group equity
Suppose the first year ends with the entire lot still inside the group. The next report cannot
eliminate that year’s internal sale as if it had happened again. The parent’s legal margin has
been closed into retained profit, and the group’s opening inventory already excludes it. The
first task is therefore to carry the two legal books and the consolidation adjustments into the
new year without changing the preceding report.

26.21.1
Releasing a carried margin changes current cost of sales
In the second year, let the subsidiary sell the full lot outside the group for 40 cash, under the
same supplied revenue-recognition premises. Its legal cost is 30, but the group opening inventory
is 20. The legal sale removes 30 from the subsidiary’s inventory; the opening group adjustment
still contains a reduction of 10. Releasing that carried adjustment debits inventory 10 and
credits current cost of sales 10. The group therefore reports cost 20 and zero closing inventory.
Debiting opening retained profit a second time would duplicate an adjustment already present
in the opening worksheet.
Under the supplied tax assumptions, the subsidiary’s current tax on its legal profit is 5/2, and
the carried inventory deferred tax asset of 5/2 reverses. The inventory transaction consequently
contributes after-tax group profit 15. The subsidiary contributes 15/2 after its current tax; the
remaining 15/2 is the parent-attributed release of the downstream margin, net of the deferred
tax reversal. These attribution premises remain explicit. An upstream seller would require a
different comparison.
26.21.2
Other acquired assets continue to affect the year’s result
The equipment did not disappear at the inventory sale. A second-year comparison that freezes
it would omit a known accounting dependency. Continue the synthetic example with separately
supplied full-year legal depreciation 14, additional acquired-basis depreciation 4 and a tax-
liability release of 1. The amounts extend the first year’s half-year pattern, but are declared
schedules, not inferred useful lives or tax deductions.
AASB 10 requires the subsidiary’s
consolidated expenses to retain the acquisition basis; it does not determine these particular
amounts (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026c, para. B88).
Equipment contributes a net expense of 17: legal depreciation 14 plus the additional 4, less
tax income 1. Combined with inventory profit 15, second-year group profit is therefore −2.
The subsidiary-attributed result is 15/2 −17 = −19/2, so ordinary NCI receives −19/8. Parent
owners receive three quarters of that result plus the parent-attributed margin release, giving
3/8. Their sum is −2. The negative NCI result is retained rather than clipped (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2026c, para. B94).
The closing group holds cash 310, equipment 63 and goodwill 135/4, with no remaining inventory
or inventory deferred tax asset. Assets total 1627/4. Liabilities are 57/2: the original external
payable 20, acquired deferred tax liability 7/2, and unpaid current taxes of 5.
