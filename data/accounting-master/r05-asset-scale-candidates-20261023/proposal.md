# Total-assets scaling requires a dated consolidated amount

Each paired forecast loss uses one identical positive total-assets amount,
known at the information cutoff and compatible with the outcome's reporting
currency, units and group scope. The proposed selection rule uses the most
recent reporting date among substantively admitted source versions. Neither
the latest file in the repository nor assets measured at the eventual outcome
date satisfy that rule automatically.

## Why no cross-company exchange rate is needed

For a positive fixed conversion factor c, convert both forecast laws, the
outcome and assets to the same new currency or unit. Positive homogeneity of
CRPS, derived in the completed target repair, gives

    [CRPS(c F_candidate,c Y)-CRPS(c F_comparator,c Y)]/(c A)
      = c [CRPS(F_candidate,Y)-CRPS(F_comparator,Y)]/(c A)
      = d.

Here c F means the law of c X when X has law F. Thus each company's
normalised difference is dimensionless. Summing these dimensionless
differences with the selected weights does not require conversion of every
company to USD or another common currency. Such scaling does not harmonise
accounting classifications or make the economic exposures identical.

The initial plan incorrectly suggested a common-currency prerequisite for
cross-company aggregation. That assumption was repaired before calculation.
Converting only the loss or only assets is wrong. Using a random future FX
rate or changing reporting currencies between the scale and target requires
a separately defined transformation and probability treatment; it is not the
fixed-factor identity above. Similarly, differing disclosed units within a
company must be reconciled explicitly. Exact display-unit multiplication does
not recover undisclosed precision.

## Availability and selection

At a strict pre-start cutoff, an eventual preceding-year annual balance sheet
may not yet exist or have been released. The source-timing inquiry already
identified that delay for several annual reports. A latest interim or older
annual balance sheet can be a candidate if its exact version, group scope,
reported date and availability are admitted. Prefer the newest eligible
reporting date under the locked rule, not the report with the latest download
timestamp. Preserve multiple unresolved values at the selected date instead
of breaking a tie by file order. A later revision cannot be substituted into
an earlier information set.

Seven exact reference cases demonstrate unit/currency cancellation, erroneous
one-sided conversion, selection of an earlier available interim over a late
annual, changes only at a later cutoff, unresolved version ties and missing
eligible scales. The fictional dates are exact supplied evidence; the helper
does not authenticate source timestamps or replace the previously specified
conservative time-bound predicates. It is not production admission code.

All nine annual values remain mapping candidates. No cutoff, interim scale,
exact historical byte time or actual forecast score is selected here. The
next smallest internal step is mapping the latest retained interim assets
alongside their existing timing-role evidence, then investigating exact
availability through distinct source leads. Future reporting transitions,
Q, refit/service and valid uncertainty remain separate open dependencies.
