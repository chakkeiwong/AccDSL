Chapter 7
The accounting language
7.1
What the current language can express
A company model needs to keep disclosures, possible histories and chosen methods distinct as
its accounts evolve. A reported balance constrains a history; it does not reveal every contract
or transaction that generated it. A collection policy supplies a behavioural assumption; it does
not establish what a customer will pay. An accounting method turns an eligible event into state
changes and postings; a balanced result alone does not establish that the method applies. The
language makes these distinctions explicit so that reports retain the meaning of the facts and
assumptions behind them.
The current design has several checked, bounded language profiles. Together they cover source-
constrained histories, conditional company paths, selected shared-book operations and complete
method authoring. Their interfaces are explicit, but they are not yet one complete whole-
company grammar. Section A.54.12 shows the present composition boundary: a single program
contains complete method text and registries, selects an inspectable state-invariant definition,
and retains full event intent beside the inputs used in a specialized calculation. Invariant
algorithms are still imported typed rules, and a selected native event contract retains its own
implementation and applicability limits.
To represent the historical record, company method selection first binds to a source-constrained
partial state (Section A.10). Statement observations have distinct relation roles rather than one
undifferentiated set of totals (Section A.11). Selected balances are tested jointly under explicit
rounding and identity hypotheses (Section A.12), with opening, movement and closing dates
kept separate across statements (Section A.13). These declarations can expose inconsistency or
missing information without inventing the underlying books.
Several histories can remain consistent with those observations. Section A.14 represents finite
conditional families with symbolic openings and explicit measurement attachment. Typed
economic quantities and constraints at each event give those histories economic content (Sec-
tion A.15); cash and quantity observations attach to the same history (Section A.15.2). Unknown
monetary amounts remain linked to selected method prerequisites without choosing a single
witness (Section A.16). The retained return example follows every branch through settlement
and sale, including funding and intermediate observations (Section A.17). Outcome queries
preserve which results are possible or necessary and their exact finite bounds (Section A.18).
Stored definitions and results can be recovered across restart, while the archive distinguishes
stored evidence from verified execution (Section A.19).
