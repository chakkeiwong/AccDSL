## What the body says

The filing groups political events, trade disputes, geopolitical tension,
conflict, terrorism, natural disasters, public-health issues, industrial
accidents and other interruptions as possible causes of material adverse
effects. It identifies Apple's global sales and manufacturing footprint, then
describes trade controls that can raise component and raw-material costs,
require supplier or relationship changes, restructure operations, or stop the
company from offering affected products and services. The text says such
restrictions may arrive with little notice and recalls earlier U.S.–China
tariffs and restrictions as examples; it does not identify a current operative
measure or quantify an effect.

The body then separates location and event classes: earthquake and weather
exposure, fire and power failure, industrial accidents, hostile acts,
ransomware, labor disputes and public-health measures. It says these events
can prevent manufacture or delivery, delay production, interrupt services,
raise costs and reduce demand. Supplier safety obligations do not prove that an
accident will not occur. The text also preserves past COVID-19 effects as a
reported example, not as a forecast parameter.

Finally, recovery is a separate stage. An interruption can require substantial
recovery time and expenditure, lose sales, and become worse when critical
components have only one or a few sources. Insurance may cover some losses but
may be insufficient for all losses or types of loss. No policy, limit,
deductible, claim, recovery timing or recognition rule is disclosed here.
