# I1 source investigation — interrupted policy-change reclassification

17 October 2026. Documentary investigation only; no numerical predecessor was
rerun and no new calculator or manuscript claim is promoted.

The checked historical IFRS17 text supports retaining each balance's original
88(b)/89(b) identity. It does not support relabeling every balance with the
current policy. The combined treatment of an unfinished B135 schedule and a
subsequent paragraph77 transfer remains **not established by this investigation**.
The existing finite methods correctly leave this case outside their admitted
scope. This is an unresolved interpretation, not evidence against retaining
origin histories or against the wider insurance design.

## What was checked

The retained official EU2023/1803 original was inspected at IFRS17.74–77,
87–92 and B128–B136. Paragraph74 defines derecognition; paragraph77 identifies
transfers and qualifying modifications and addresses CSM/new-contract effects.
Paragraph91(a) requires reclassification of remaining amounts previously in OCI
because of88(b);91(b) prohibits reclassification through profit of remaining
amounts previously in OCI because of89(b). B135(a) separately requires former
OCI to enter profit in the change and future periods as if the former approach
continued, using assumptions immediately before the holdings-driven change.
B136 prohibits recalculation as if the new approach always applied and freezes
those assumptions. B130's zero-over-duration condition is for systematic
allocation; it is not a general rule that every OCI origin must recycle.

The official AASB portal's December2022 compilation was retrieved and its91
and B134–B136 text inspected. It corroborates the historical wording; it does
not provide an additional ruling on this interaction. Neither edition has been
established here as current consolidated law for an actual company.

The official IFRS supporting-materials index and completed-project index were
retrieved. Two linked webcast PDFs were retained: recognition/derecognition
and participation features. Their extracted text and paragraph references were
screened. Some slide content is graphical; this screening does not establish
absence of a relevant statement in every figure. The recognition material
points to BC316–BC322 for modification/derecognition, not an inspected resolution
of the old-OCI interaction. Both materials disclose a May2017 basis supplemented
by2020 amendments. The legacy AASB BC URL and a guessed2024 IFRS PartC URL
returned HTTP404. These failures concern those exact URLs only; they do not
establish that the Basis for Conclusions is unavailable elsewhere.

## Competing readings and the precise remaining inference

**Proposed answer, conditional.** At termination, trace old and post-change
amounts separately. Apply91(a) to remaining original88(b) OCI and91(b) to
remaining original89(b) OCI. The phrase “as if ... continuing” in B135 could
include the termination consequences of the former approach. This would
accelerate the old88(b) remainder and stop recycling the old89(b) remainder,
without updating assumptions or releasing an amount twice. Its strongest
support is91's express reference to how amounts were previously recognized,
rather than the current policy label.

**Strongest counterargument.** B135(a) specifically requires reclassification
of the old amount in the change and future periods. B136 freezes assumptions.
Treating that obligation as a persistent old-policy schedule could leave a
post-termination reclassification stream, especially for old89(b) amounts.
The inspected text does not expressly say whether early termination ends that
stream or how to distinguish a termination adjustment from an amount already
due under the frozen schedule. Assuming that termination changes a frozen
schedule, or assuming that it can never do so, would both add an unexamined rule.

The remaining inference is therefore **whether “as if continuing” imports91's
termination treatment into the former-policy schedule and, if so, the ordering
of the last scheduled amount and termination-date remainder**. The second
switch direction is decisive: the delivered former89(b) balance−21 runs off
by expenses10 and11. After the first release, a remaining−11 is already known
from the frozen predecessor. Whether that remainder continues its old scheduled
profit release after transfer is the question, not a fresh valuation problem.
The former88(b) example similarly leaves−120/11 after its first scheduled
release. These are existing exact predecessor amounts, not new run results.
Reversing every sign leaves the same unresolved classification question.

Both readings can conserve total equity: a profit expense can have equal OCI
income. Thus aggregate balance, unchanged cash and a balanced journal cannot
select the supported reading. Nor does91 decide how to allocate an aggregate
balance to a particular contract when original contract-level attribution is
missing. Such attribution, transfer eligibility and liability/CSM measurement
must remain separate supplied or supported prerequisites.
