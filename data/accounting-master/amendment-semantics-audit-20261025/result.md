# Current due dates cannot preserve prior assessment meaning

25 October2026. The retained lifecycle prefix was checked on both alpha/beta
charts. Its February28,2027 assessment records remaining20 and overdue20 under
the January31 due date. Hypothetically advancing the book to March10 and
replacing due with April30 preserves balances but fails overdue_snapshot.
Changing old overdue to0 makes the current control accept while altering what
the February28 assessment says. Both counterexamples replay exactly; no frozen
engine, original state or source assertion was changed.

This is a missing amendment capability, not an error in the existing lifecycle's
declared immutable-terms scope. A valid prospective amendment must preserve the
assessment's original term basis. Source corrections continue to reconstruct
separate as-of histories and cannot be relabelled as contractual events.

| Decision | Primary criterion | Veto status | Main uncertainty | Next action | Not concluded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reject due-date overwrite as amendment semantics | Both charts expose inconsistent or rewritten historical assessment | Old meaning lost by overwrite/reset | Typed migration and term-selection contract | Retain dated term versions and bind each assessment to one version | Contract modification accounting, enforceability or actual event admission |

The smallest coherent repair is an immutable per-claim term history with a
current version reference and version-bound assessments. It can reuse typed
map insertion and existing exact state-only methods, while a new claim
schema/migration explicitly separates it from frozen lifecycle1. Latest
assessment observations must show their term identity and assessed date.
Zero journal does not by itself authorise real amendment accounting.
