This document specifies a system that translates company disclosures into traceable evidence,
represents several compatible economic and accounting histories, and simulates their future
journals and financial statements under explicit economic scenarios.
The proposed data contracts, methods and acceptance cases specify the intended implementation.
Current source and domain limitations remain explicit; this candidate is not a completed product
or a claim of full proposal readiness. Proposed libraries can be replaced while preserving those
contracts. Sector rule packages require their own accounting review before they can be described
as compliant with a reporting standard.
Research materials, source qualifications and review records accompany this document in
docs/design/research and docs/design/review.

26.23.1
The parent’s sale releases profit attributed to the subsidiary
In year two the parent sells the full lot outside the group for 40 cash. Its legal books recognise
cost 30 and current tax 5/2, leaving its own after-tax trading profit 15/2. The group opening
inventory adjustment still reduces the parent’s inventory by 10. Releasing it requires a debit
to the parent’s inventory and a credit to the subsidiary’s current cost of sales. The debit
removes the carried adjustment after the legal sale; the credit locates the realised margin in the
subsidiary seller’s earnings. A second charge to opening retained profit would duplicate the
opening bridge.
Where the inventory deferred tax asset was recognised, its 5/2 reversal is also attributed to the
subsidiary under the supplied premise. The subsidiary’s trading contribution is therefore 15/2.
Continue the explicit equipment schedules: legal depreciation 14, additional acquired-basis
depreciation 4, and acquired tax-liability release 1. They contribute a net subsidiary expense of
17, so its total second-year result is −19/2. NCI receives −19/8; parent owners receive 3/8 after
adding the parent’s own trading result. Group profit is −2. The negative NCI result remains
part of the allocation (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026c, paras. B88, B94).
Without a recognised inventory deferred tax asset, there is no asset to reverse. The subsidiary
instead contributes margin 10 less equipment expense 17, a loss of 7. NCI receives −7/4 and
parent owners receive 9/4; group profit is 1/2. This branch earned 5/2 less in the first year and
5/2 more in the second. After full realisation, the two branches therefore reach the same closing
equity under the stipulated tax assumptions. Their yearly earnings are different.
Both branches end with cash 290, equipment 63, goodwill 135/4, and no inventory or inventory
deferred tax asset. Assets are 1547/4. Liabilities of 57/2 include the original payable 20,
remaining acquired deferred tax liability 7/2, and unpaid current taxes 5. Equity is 1433/4,
split between owners 324 and NCI 137/4. The prior tax payable is carried; no settlement is
invented to obtain these balances.
Complete opening and closing accounts distinguish the attribution even where aggregate profits
coincide. Partial resale retains the appropriate margin and tax; an unresolved opening or
continuing tax method retains the prior result and proposed continuation without a second-year
final report.8 These acquisition examples all started with no prior interest in the subsidiary.
When an investor already holds an interest before obtaining control, the opening investment,
acquisition consideration and any remeasurement must be distinguished before the same
consolidation logic can be applied.
26.24
An investment already held before control
An investor may already own shares when it obtains control of a business. Suppose the old
holding is carried at 35, its acquisition-date fair value is 40, and the investor pays 80 for
additional shares. These are three different amounts. The old carrying value describes the
opening books; 40 measures the previously held equity at the acquisition date; 80 is the new
consideration. Treating 35 as new consideration would misstate the acquisition calculation.
Treating the increase from 35 to 40 as cash would invent a receipt.

For an acquisition achieved in stages, the previously held equity interest is remeasured at
acquisition-date fair value. The resulting gain or loss enters profit or loss or other comprehensive
income as appropriate; prior OCI follows the basis that would apply to a direct disposal of that
interest (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026h, paras. 41–42). That rule makes the
prior accounting classification consequential. It cannot be selected merely by observing that the
investor did not previously control the investee.
26.24.1
No control does not establish the prior accounting method
In the example, the old holding represents 20% of voting power. AASB 128 presumes significant
influence at 20% or more unless its absence can be clearly demonstrated. Board representation,
participation in policy decisions and other relationships can matter (Australian Accounting
Standards Board, 2025, paras. 5–8). Joint control is a separate question. The simulator therefore
needs explicit relationship assertions before it can use the restricted AASB 9 route below. A
percentage and a no-control assertion are insufficient.
For this synthetic case, stipulate both no significant influence and no joint control immediately
before acquisition, together with an AASB 9 fair-value- through-profit-or-loss classification.
These are supplied premises, including the rebuttal of the influence presumption, rather than
findings about an actual company. An unknown or positive influence or joint-control assertion
leaves the method unresolved in this finite example. The particular FVTPL election available to
specified venture-capital and similar investors is not presumed (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2025, para. 18).
Separate financial statements require their own accounting-method choice: AASB 127 identifies
cost, AASB 9 and the equity method as alternatives within its conditions (Australian Accounting
Standards Board, 2022c, para. 10). Here, explicitly supply an AASB 9/FVTPL separate-book
schedule and a fair value of 120 for the combined investment both at acquisition and at closing.
The equality 120 = 40 + 80 in this example is not a valuation rule for a controlling holding. The
combined measurement is independently supplied. Also stipulate zero current and deferred tax
on the prior-interest gain. This premise leaves the acquired equipment’s existing tax schedule
intact.

26.24.3
An equity-FVOCI election changes where the gain appears
The same difference between prior fair value and carrying value need not enter profit. For
an eligible equity investment with the irrevocable OCI election, AASB 9 sends the applicable
fair-value change to OCI. Amounts presented there are not subsequently recycled to profit
or loss; a transfer within equity is permitted (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026i,
para. 5.7.5 and B5.7.1). Applying the direct-disposal basis in the acquisition rule must preserve
that distinction.
Write C for the old carrying value, P for its acquisition-date fair value, and O for accumulated
prior OCI. The conditional current update is P −C. Under the eligible equity-FVOCI branch
it enters OCI, leaving the reserve O + P −C. An explicit transfer choice either retains that
reserve or transfers it within equity, with no recycling gain in profit. The FVTPL branch
instead puts P −C in parent pretax profit and requires zero prior OCI in this finite specification.
These symbolic presentation branches do not supply a complete FVOCI tax or legal-book
worksheet. Missing classification, relationship, measurement, tax or timely source premises
remain unresolved.9
A debt claim held before a conversion is not previously held equity simply because the conversion
produces shares. Its extinguishment or other settlement and any pre-existing relationship
require their own transaction analysis; AASB 3 separates qualifying acquisition consideration
from separate transactions (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026h, paras. 51–52 and
B50–B53). That distinction determines the next extension of the financing example.
