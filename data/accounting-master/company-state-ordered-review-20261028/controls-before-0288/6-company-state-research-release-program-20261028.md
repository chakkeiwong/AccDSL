## The question and the deliverable

Can AccDSL describe a company's economically relevant condition, its accounting
memory and different actors' information well enough to reconstruct alternative
histories, simulate decisions and shocks, and explain cash flows and claim
values without mistaking reported totals for a fully observed company?

The deliverable is an implementable, source-grounded scholarly specification
with connected reference cases and a reproducible release decision. A collection
of isolated examples, a large bibliography or a successful PDF build does not
meet that objective. The work has three inseparable strands:

1. Reconcile every original acceptance condition with present evidence and
   assign the remaining work to an executable dependency graph.
2. Compare the literature across accounting information systems, inverse
   problems, dynamic firms, valuation, reporting incentives, forensic evidence,
   filtering, control and computational approximation.
3. Develop a task-relative theory and construction procedure for company state,
   then connect observations, uncertain histories, actions, journals, reports,
   financing and claim valuation through existing typed interfaces.

The machine-readable task graph, original-condition crosswalk, source locks
and execution records live in data/accounting-master/unified-program-20261028/.
The program checker reports ready work and verifies dependencies and recorded
evidence; it does not launch agents, background campaigns, arbitrary commands
or professional engagements. Research judgments still require examination of
their evidence. A graph validator cannot confer scholarly acceptance.

## Evidence contract and assumptions

Baseline comparators are: reported-total state versus explicit resource/contract
and accounting memory; single selected history versus a declared history family;
independent branching versus preserved joint histories; finite exact enumeration
versus any proposed compression; and matched dividend, cash-flow and
residual-income valuations. Compare methods on identical information sets,
action opportunities, claims and computational budgets before making rankings.

The primary local criterion is correctness of the specified relation and
complete positive/adverse outcomes against independently derived expectations.
The final criterion is satisfaction of each original release condition and
resolution of independent review on one reproducible candidate. Test counts,
pages, solver speed, citation counts and predictive scores are explanatory
unless a later reviewed protocol explicitly makes them decision criteria.

## Survey questions and a teaching sequence

The intended chapters develop an argument, not a series of paper summaries:
(1) why financial statements are incomplete observations; (2) resources, rights,
histories and accounting memory; (3) reconstructing states from evidence;
(4) identification and sequential learning; (5) reporting quality and forensic
alternatives; (6) dynamic firms, finance and decisions; (7) cash flows, accounting
valuation and prices; (8) sectors, groups and changing structure; (9) representing
large families and approximating them safely; (10) connected cases and limits.
This revises the existing 150–300-page simulation/research-volume objective
without treating its current 244 body pages as substantive completion.

Every branch must answer: what state is assumed, what is observed and by whom,
what is identified, which decision/valuation quantity is computed, what empirical
or theoretical evidence supports it, and where its assumptions fail for AccDSL.
At least one direct alternative and one consequential failure must be examined
per branch. A numeric paper quota is not a sufficient stopping rule. A branch
can be ready only when its consequential claims, competitors and omission
risks have been dispositioned and the connected argument has been reviewed.
