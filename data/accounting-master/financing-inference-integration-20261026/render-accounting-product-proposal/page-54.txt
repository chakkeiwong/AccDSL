5.15
Equal balances after unequal payment histories
A borrower can recover from a cash shortage before the reporting date. Its closing statements
then need not reveal that it failed to pay on time. To isolate the timing, consider a synthetic
company with cash 60, inventory 100, debt carrying amount 80 and book equity 80. The
contractual promise is 100, payable on 30 June 2027.
Assume a zero coupon, no fees or
taxes, and one remaining measurement interval that accretes the discount of 20 at maturity.
This deliberately simple interval makes the cash and contract comparisons exact; a general
effective-interest schedule needs its own measurement rules.
The company issues 40 new shares for one unit of cash each. In the first history, the subscription
settles on 29 June. Cash rises from 60 to 100, the discount accretes on 30 June, and the borrower
pays the complete promise that day. In the second history, the shares settle on 2 July. The
borrower can pay only 60 at maturity. On 1 July an explicit observation records the unpaid 40;
the new cash arrives on 2 July and pays the remaining claim on 3 July. No overdraft, emergency
loan or retroactive receipt funds the earlier payment.

The zero in the first two historical-miss cells records that the observation has not yet been
made. It is not a finding that a payment was timely. The observation changes contract memory
without changing cash or posting another expense. The final payment clears the current claim;
it leaves the history of the earlier shortfall intact.
Both histories end with the same financial statements under the supplied treatment. Inventory
of 100 is the only remaining asset, the debt is zero, and equity is opening equity plus the
subscription less the discount expense:

Profit for the illustrated movements is −20. Net financing cash flow is 40 −100 = −60 in either
history. The late path divides the repayment into 60 + 40, which changes its timing but not its
total. Even agreement across the closing balance sheet, income and cash-flow statements does
not answer whether the maturity was paid on time. A simulation query about missed payments
must retain the distinguishing history.
