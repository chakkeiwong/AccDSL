7.12
Consolidation and scenario-dependent perimeter
An acquisition, disposal or change of control can alter the reporting perimeter. The group total
before and after the event may not be comparable without a bridge. A simulation that treats
acquired revenue as organic growth can distort future behavior and validation.
Perimeter state should identify included entities and the relevant control assessment over time.
Acquisition events introduce assets, liabilities and consideration under the selected accounting
treatment. Disposal events remove or reclassify them and may create gains, losses or continuing
interests. These are not ordinary sales receipts applied to an unchanged company.
Unknown deal terms can produce alternative histories. Earn-outs, guarantees and retained
interests may affect future cash after apparent disposal. A compact representation that keeps
only closing consolidated balances can lose these rights. The source-to-contract-to-event chain
should survive even when the initial reporting effect is aggregated.
7.13
Atomic posting and partial failure
A multi-leg event should either apply all admitted accounting movements consistently or remain
unapplied with a recorded failure. Posting cash but failing to create debt because a later
validation step failed corrupts the state. Similar risks arise when conversion removes debt but
does not issue shares.
Atomicity is an engineering property that supports accounting consistency. It does not certify
the event’s economic validity. Preconditions should be checked before mutation where possible,
and the event should retain an immutable input snapshot. Replay should reproduce the same
movements under the same rule version.
A failed accounting mapping should not erase a successfully observed economic event from the
evidence store. Instead, the system can retain the event and mark its accounting consequences
unresolved. This separation permits later repair without inventing or deleting transactions.
7.14
A finding can become stale within one day
Consider the late-funded borrower in Section 5.15. An observation records an unpaid principal
clause as satisfied on 2 July. The company then pays the remaining 40 on that same date.
A later command asks whether a continuing event permits a new acceleration. Matching the
observation’s date to the command’s date would accept the old unpaid finding, even though
the intervening payment changed its premise. Calendar equality does not establish that the
financial state is unchanged.
This is a concrete failure in the earlier selected adapter. Its original trace, with payment on the
following day, passed. Moving payment to the same day exposed the defect: the stale satisfied
finding remained usable. The successor retains the old observation as history but requires
reassessment before using it as a current condition. A fresh paid assessment then supports the
corresponding absence of a new trigger. It does not retroactively revoke an acceleration already
recorded under earlier valid facts.
