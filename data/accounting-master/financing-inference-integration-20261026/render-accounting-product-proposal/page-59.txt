6.5
The rights attached to a share
Shares can differ in voting rights, dividends, liquidation preference, conversion rights and
redemption terms.
An aggregate share count is sufficient only when the distinctions are
irrelevant to the question or explicitly normalized. A dollar of preferred funding is not generally
equivalent to a dollar of ordinary funding for future payouts.
A simple preference waterfall illustrates the point. Suppose new investors contribute 30 for a
nonparticipating preference claim entitled to 30 before ordinary holders, with the option to
convert into 25 per cent of ordinary equity. If distributable value is 80, the preference payment
30 exceeds conversion value 20. If value is 200, conversion value 50 exceeds the preference. The
exercise threshold is 120 under these assumptions.
This example requires a declared contract: no accrued preference dividend, no senior claims,
no participation after the preference and no other options. Adding any of these changes the
waterfall. A flat share-percentage allocation would misallocate value below the conversion
threshold. The model should retain class-specific rights and exercise policy.
6.6
Options, warrants and contingent issuance
An option can create future shares when exercised. Exercise can bring cash to the company,
transfer existing shares or settle in cash, depending on terms. Counting all potential shares as
already issued can distort current ownership; ignoring them can distort future dilution.
Let an option cover m shares at exercise price k. In a simplified physical exercise, the company
receives mk and issues m shares. The holder’s decision depends on share value, expiry, constraints
and possibly tax or employment conditions. A purely intrinsic-value rule is a modeling policy,
not a universal description of exercise behavior.
Contingent performance awards add dependence on future operating outcomes. A scenario
changing revenue can affect both the company’s value and the number of shares delivered.
Treating share count as independent of the operating shock loses that dependence. Historical
statement totals may not identify all award terms, so the family must preserve unresolved
conditions.
6.7
Conversion joins debt and equity
A convertible instrument links a liability schedule to ownership changes. At conversion, principal
or another contractual amount may be exchanged for shares according to a fixed ratio, a variable
price or a contingent formula. Cash settlement alternatives can change liquidity materially.
Suppose debt face 100 converts into ten ordinary shares. Physical conversion removes the
specified debt obligation and increases shares by ten under the assumed terms. It does not
also pay cash principal 100 unless the contract requires a separate payment. A simulator that
schedules both redemption and conversion double settles the same obligation.
Accounting derecognition and allocation can involve carrying amounts different from face and
separate components. Those postings need the applicable basis and facts. The economic
event should identify exactly which obligations and rights are extinguished or created before
the accounting module measures the consequences. Existing bounded conversion methods
in the repository can support this event discipline without claiming a universal convertible
classification.
