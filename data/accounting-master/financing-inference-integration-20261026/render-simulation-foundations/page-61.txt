Chapter 8
Keeping uncertainty without
enumerating every world
Twenty binary unknowns have more than a million assignments. A company can have thousands
of contracts, continuous unknown amounts and decades of possible events. Explicit enumeration
is therefore an unsuitable default. The alternative is to represent the family and compute the
answer demanded by a question. This can save enormous work, but cannot guarantee that
every problem has a small exact solution.
8.1
Representation size and answer size
A formula containing twenty Boolean variables can describe all 220 assignments without listing
them. A solver may answer whether any assignment violates a cash constraint without printing
every world. If the user asks for all distinct journals, the output itself can be enormous. No
representation can print an exponentially large answer in fixed small space.
Continuous uncertainty sharpens the distinction. The family 0 ≤α ≤1 has infinitely many
members, but interest 5 + 2α is a finite exact answer. Useful computations include symbolic
propagation, optimization over a region and classification of regions by behavior. Enumeration
is one method, not the definition of correctness.
Darwiche and Marquis (2002) compares knowledge-compilation languages by succinctness and
supported queries and transformations. Its definitions of directed acyclic representations, decom-
posability and determinism explain why sharing and structural restrictions help. Its tradeoffs
also warn against assuming that a compact formula makes every query cheap. Compilation can
itself be expensive.
8.2
Three different graphs
An evidence graph links assertions to sources and interpretations. A constraint graph links
variables through relations. An execution graph links states through dated events. Their edges
have different meanings. Source support is not causation; a constraint edge is not a probabilistic
independence statement.
An expression DAG can share a repeated discount factor or subtotal. An immutable history
graph can share common event prefixes.
