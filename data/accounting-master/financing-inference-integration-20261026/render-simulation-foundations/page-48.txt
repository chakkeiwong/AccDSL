Chapter 6
Equity funding, ownership, and
dilution
An equity issue can provide cash while changing ownership and future claims. A model that
records only an increase in cash and book equity captures one accounting consequence but misses
the allocation of control and value. A structural model that charges an issuance cost captures a
financing friction but does not automatically create a share register. Scenario simulation needs
quantities, prices, rights, holders and dates, together with an explanation of who is willing to
subscribe.
6.1
Book equity, market value and share capital
Book equity is the residual of recognized assets and liabilities under an accounting basis. Market
equity value is the value assigned to owners’ economic claims. Legal share capital is a legally
defined component of capital that may be related to nominal share values. These quantities
have different meanings. Dividing book equity by shares produces book value per share, not
automatically the market subscription price.
A company can have positive book equity and insufficient cash to pay a maturity. It can have
negative book equity and valuable future opportunities. Limited liability can bound the value of
a shareholder’s claim below by zero in a model without forcing the accounting residual to zero.
The simulator should retain these quantities separately and define any relation between them.
Let N be the number of existing ordinary shares, VE the total economic value assigned to their
claim, and p = VE/N a per-share value when shares are homogeneous and N > 0. This division
presumes that VE refers to exactly that class. If preferred shares, options or noncontrolling
interests are included in total equity value, allocating all of it to ordinary shares is wrong.
