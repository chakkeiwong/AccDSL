Those conditions have two roles. Snapshot conditions constrain current quantities: claims and
arrears are nonnegative, arrears cannot exceed the remaining claim, and under this matured
zero-coupon convention the carrying liability equals that claim. Transition conditions constrain
how an event changes them. Payment of p gives remaining claim q′ = q −p, reduces cash by p,
and leaves the historical missed amount unchanged. A share subscription must increase both
the monetary capital account and the separate share quantity under the supplied issue terms.
An internally plausible rewrite of historical miss from 40 to 50 can satisfy snapshot inequalities
but violates the transition condition. This is why both kinds of condition are needed.
Opening history creates another distinction. A supplied fiscal opening must satisfy the declared
period-start, temporary-balance and flow restrictions; an intermediate state containing this
year’s payments is not such an opening. Importing a consistent snapshot does not establish that
it includes every earlier missed payment. The selected opening method therefore compares it
with a separately supplied history premise and preserves unknown completeness. Actual source
evidence is still needed to establish that premise.
5.16
A missed payment and the available remedies
The amount unpaid does not by itself identify the creditor’s remedy. Apple’s 2024 annual
report provides a concrete historical description of why the distinction matters. Exhibit 4.1
expressly calls its description incomplete and qualifies it by the 2013 and 2018 indentures;
its issuer references concern Apple Inc., rather than automatically every subsidiary (Apple
Inc., 2024, Exhibit 4.1, printed p. 3). We use the summary to identify conditions a simulation
must represent. It does not establish an actual issuer default, complete operative terms, or the
applicability of those terms to the synthetic company.
The described interest-payment event requires default continuing for 30 days after the installment
becomes due. Principal and premium use a different condition: nonpayment when due and
payable, including maturity, redemption or declaration. Importing the interest grace period into
principal repayment would therefore apply the wrong described condition. The other-covenant
route has its own exclusions, a continuing breach and 90 days after qualifying written notice.
Holder notice uses at least 25% of outstanding principal under the 2013 indenture and at least
33% under the 2018 indenture; the trustee has a separately described notice route (Apple Inc.,
2024, Exhibit 4.1, printed p. 9).
The thresholds concern the relevant outstanding series, not a fraction of consolidated debt or a
count of creditors. Series mapping, denominator, delivery to the required recipients and validity
of notice are substantive inputs. No notice and no evidence about notice lead to different
answers. Likewise, day-level data at exactly the 30- or 90-day boundary may leave event order
unresolved. The conditional reference retains that uncertainty; it does not assert that the
complete contract has no answer at the boundary.
The summary next distinguishes declaration from automatic acceleration. Certain continuing
nonbankruptcy events permit a declaration by the specified trustee or holder route. Certain
bankruptcy events make the obligations due without that declaration. A negative equity balance
establishes neither the specified bankruptcy event nor the required category. Moreover, an event
for one series does not necessarily constitute an event for another. Neither automatic contagion
nor universal independence follows (Apple Inc., 2024, Exhibit 4.1, printed pp. 9–10).
