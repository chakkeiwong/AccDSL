IAS2.23–24 requires specific identification for noninterchangeable/project items
and explains why arbitrary selection of interchangeable units is inappropriate.
IAS2.25/27 permits FIFO or weighted average for the other inventories. The case
chooses weighted average for a single batch of identical interchangeable goods,
with no pre-existing inventory or later purchases. Its average unit cost is the
entire adjusted cost divided by units; original hedge designation layers do not
establish individually identifiable inventory costs. This is a deduction from
the specified acquisition population, not a rule for splitting a hedge reserve
among only partially realized forecast transactions.

IAS2.28–31 requires NRV based on current evidence and purpose; contracts and
uncontracted excess may need different estimates. The case supplies a common
per-unit NRV for identical goods with identical selling circumstances. It does
not aggregate unlike items or estimate NRV from a price alone. IAS2.33 caps a
recovery at original cost when supported changed circumstances justify it.
IAS2.34 expenses the carrying amount on sale and recognizes write-down/reversal
in the period of the corresponding event. Cumulative outputs here retain dated
journals; they are not one annual income statement across different years.

Let the adjusted total cost be K and initial units Q, so c=K/Q. For units q
remaining and an applicable common NRV v, gross cost is G=qc, allowance is
A=q*max(c-v,0), and carrying amount is G-A. A sale of x units removes xc of
gross cost and xA/q of allowance, so its cost of sales is x(c-A/q). The remaining
allowance becomes (q-x)A/q. A later valuation v' requires
A'=(q-x)*max(c-v',0); the expense change is A'-A_remaining. A negative change
reduces inventory expense and cannot reverse more than A_remaining because
A' is nonnegative. This derivation establishes the cap for units still held.
