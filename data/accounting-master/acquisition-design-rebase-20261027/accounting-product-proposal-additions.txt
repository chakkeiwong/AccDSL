This document specifies a system that translates company disclosures into traceable evidence,
represents several compatible economic and accounting histories, and simulates their future
journals and financial statements under explicit economic scenarios.
The proposed data contracts, methods and acceptance cases specify the intended implementation.
Current source and domain limitations remain explicit; this candidate is not a completed product
or a claim of full proposal readiness. Proposed libraries can be replaced while preserving those
contracts. Sector rule packages require their own accounting review before they can be described
as compliant with a reporting standard.
Research materials, source qualifications and review records accompany this document in
docs/design/research and docs/design/review.

26.17
A control history must fit the reporting method
A supplied history of control can answer the continuity question without yet supplying a method
for consolidated reporting. The existing selected group-period calculation supports a stable
perimeter and full ownership. A conditional history must therefore be checked against that
scope before the simulator uses the calculation. Matching opening and closing control edges
cannot replace the intervening history.
The checked connection carries the original history request, actor, exact interval and source
identities into the group-report request. It identifies the same parent and subsidiary in both. It
also requires an explicit relation between the history’s reporting basis and the book-composition
measurement basis. Similar labels do not establish that those two premises mean the same
thing.
Time precision matters here too. The book period uses calendar dates, whereas the control history
can use exact instants. The reference requires an explicit fixed-offset calendar interpretation
and compares the resulting midnight boundaries exactly. A partial-day acquisition cannot be
rounded into whole-period control merely because the annual report’s date fields contain no
time of day.
The outcome depends on the whole interval. Missing coverage, conflicting evidence or unresolved
actor availability leave the control-history requirement unresolved. Continuous absence of control
stays outside this parent–subsidiary reporting route, even if an ownership record says 100 per
cent. A gain or loss requires changing-perimeter methods; this includes a loss followed by
regain when the opening and closing assessments are identical. Continuous control with partial
ownership requires non-controlling-interest methods beyond the present full-ownership reference.
Only continuous supplied control together with a separate full-ownership premise fits the existing
method’s control and ownership scope. Even that result is a candidate for further report checks.
It supplies no missing legal-book report, acquisition measurement or accounting authority, and
the scope check itself produces no consolidated statement. The original report can still refuse a
request whose control scope fits but whose other inputs are absent or incompatible.
This separates two sources of failure. If a valid unchanged-period fixture is refused, the existing
report or its inputs need diagnosis. If a history contains a mid-period acquisition and the
stable-perimeter report refuses it, the refusal exposes a missing financial method. The next
work must specify acquisition or loss accounting, dated member and flow allocation, ownership
and non-controlling interests, and the required measurements. A source-history check cannot
manufacture those amounts or authorize a journal merely because its identities and dates
match.2

The smallest continuation considered here is one gain of control in an ordinary business
acquisition, with a single reporting currency, aligned reporting dates and no subsequent loss
of control or change in ownership rights during the selected interval. These are limits of the
first method. A purchase of assets that is not a business, a combination under common control,
an investment-entity exception and separate financial statements require their own treatments.
The business assessment and acquirer identification therefore precede the acquisition calculation.
The acquisition date follows the assessed date of obtaining control, rather than the share-issue
date by definition (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026h, paras. 2–9).
26.18.1
The acquisition balance needs its own measurements
Consider the consolidated acquisition worksheet at that date. Let A be the aggregate recognised
acquired assets and L the aggregate assumed liabilities, both expressed as positive magnitudes in
the same currency unit. Their amounts follow the applicable recognition and measurement rules,
including item-specific exceptions; an unchanged legal carrying amount is not automatically the
required acquisition amount. Let T denote measured consideration transferred, Q measured
non-controlling interests, and P the acquisition-date fair value of a qualifying previously held
equity interest. Under the ordinary business-acquisition branch, the excess described in AASB 3
paragraph 32 is
E = T + Q + P −(A −L).
(26.6)
The calculation compares the consideration and interests in the acquired business with its
recognised net assets. A positive excess can determine goodwill only when the route and
measurements support that recognition. A negative excess requires the reassessment specified
in paragraphs 34–36 before a bargain-purchase gain is recognised. Computing max(E, 0) and
putting the remainder into profit would skip that requirement (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2026h, paras. 10–21, 24–36).
The distinction between T and P matters particularly in the conversion example. A creditor
may hold debt immediately before obtaining shares. That debt is not, merely because it converts,
the previously held equity interest addressed by paragraph 42. Nor does the issuer’s transfer of
a carrying amount from debt to equity determine the holder’s acquisition consideration. The
contract and its financial-instrument treatment must establish what is transferred and which
effects belong to the business combination. Pre-existing relationships and separate transactions
require their own assessment (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026h, paras. 42, 51–53
and B50–B53). The face amount, an economic price and a carrying amount cannot silently fill
the same measurement slot.
Non-controlling interests introduce a further measurement choice. For qualifying present owner-
ship interests, a proportional-share election gives Q = q(A −L), where q is the non-controlling
fraction. A fair-value election instead requires a distinct measurement of Q (Australian Ac-
counting Standards Board, 2026h, para. 19). Substituting the proportional expression into
equation (26.6) gives
E = T + P −(1 −q)(A −L).
(26.7)
For a conditional 75 per cent ordinary interest, q = 1/4 and the acquired net-asset contribution
is 3(A −L)/4. This algebra does not establish that a particular preference share or other right
qualifies for the election. With full ownership, q = 0 and no non-controlling interest remains. An
unspecified prior interest does not become P = 0; that zero requires an explicit no-prior-equity
premise under the selected route.

26.18.2
Only the controlled interval contributes subsidiary results
After the acquisition worksheet is established, consolidation incorporates the controlled com-
pany’s income and expenses from the date control is obtained. Those subsequent amounts follow
the acquisition-date basis: for example, depreciation uses the acquired asset amount recognised
in the consolidated statements (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026c, para. 20 and
B86–B88). A legal-book expense may therefore need a group adjustment even when the legal
journal itself is correct. The simulator must preserve the legal books and put the adjustment in
the separate consolidation worksheet.
Write R for the subsidiary’s legal profit over the exact post-control interval, and D for the
signed additional expense required to express that interval on the group basis. Positive D
reduces profit; negative D increases it. Let O be the legal other comprehensive income over the
same interval and U its signed group adjustment. All four quantities have the same currency
unit. The adjusted subsidiary contribution is
π = R −D,
ω = O + U.
(26.8)
This is a period and measurement reconciliation, not a forecast or a rule for dividing an annual
total. A whole-year R cannot be prorated by elapsed months unless an independently supported
method justifies that approximation. A journal recorded only by calendar date also needs an
ordering premise when control changes within that date; rounding the control instant would
conceal the ambiguity.
Under the deliberately limited assumption of constant ordinary present ownership rights, the
owners’ and non-controlling shares of π are (1 −q)π and qπ, and similarly for ω. Actual
allocation must consider the relevant rights and the potential-voting-rights exception; the
fraction of issued shares is not a universal allocation rule (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2026c, B89–B95). Let V be distributions to non-controlling interests, expressed as a
positive reduction, and M the other signed, supported changes in those interests within this
limited branch. Their selected reconciliation is then
Nclose = Q + q(R −D) + q(O + U) −V + M.
(26.9)
Starting with measured acquisition-date NCI, the equation adds its share of adjusted profit
and OCI and then accounts for direct changes. A sufficiently large loss may make the closing
amount negative; clipping that deficit at zero would contradict the attribution requirement
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026c, B94). The equation cannot establish whether
the supplied M or adjustment population is complete. Ownership transactions with continued
control, and a later loss of control, need their separate methods rather than an unexplained
entry in M.
26.18.3
Cash and revisions retain their own dates
Acquisition also changes the cash included in the group. The conversion of debt to equity is
a noncash transaction, but the acquired company may already hold cash. Its cash enters the
consolidated perimeter even when the conversion itself creates no receipt or payment. The
method must retain cash consideration, acquired cash and noncash consideration separately,
with the applicable acquisition cash presentation net of acquired cash.
Acquisitions and
disposals are not netted against one another merely because both change control (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2026b, paras. 39–44B). Cash transfers before control are not
retrospectively intragroup flows; transfers afterwards require supported counterpart matching
before elimination.

The acquisition worksheet must also distinguish new information about conditions at the
acquisition date from later events. Provisional amounts and measurement-period adjustments
follow their own rules, including the one-year maximum, rather than a general permission to
rewrite the past (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026h, paras. 45–50). A revised
measurement can therefore have an acquisition-date effect and a later information-availability
date. The earlier actor view must remain reproducible, using the temporal distinctions developed
in Chapter 17.
26.18.4
A transaction date does not select an edition
The conditional AASB analysis preserves several dates because they answer different questions.
For example, the retained AASB 2026-2 compilation was prepared on 21 October 2026, has
a compilation date of 31 December 2027, and generally applies to annual reporting periods
beginning on or after 1 July 2029, with earlier application permitted under its terms. These
dates cannot be substituted for one another. Its amendments to AASB 3 and AASB 10 concern
application of the Conceptual Framework; entity classification and elections remain necessary
inputs (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026e, paras. 3, 18–19, 25; compilation details).
The inspected interaction with AASB 2026-4 also imposes a specific AASB 18 condition for
early use of that amendment (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026f, paras. 3, 25).
Other branches remain visible. AASB 20’s Appendix D adds a business-combination treatment
for regulatory assets and liabilities, with its own application timing and an additional dependency
when applied before AASB 18. The retained appendix alone does not establish the entity’s
eligibility or the complete transition treatment (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026g,
Appendix D, amendments to AASB 3).
AASB 1061’s Appendix C changes tier wording
relevant to AASB 10; it does not supply the full Tier 3 scope or measurement rules (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2026a, Appendix C, amendment to AG1). Similarly, the inspected
sale/contribution amendment chain affects a particular loss-of-control branch, rather than
replacing every acquisition method (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2024b, paras. 6–8).
The dated authority matrix retains these alternatives and exact paragraph anchors. A future
transaction’s policy must be checked against its entity, annual period, elections and then-current
applicable requirements.
26.18.5
What the selected reference establishes
The conditional expression reference binds the original control-history request and the exact
gain-of-control instant before constructing these relations. Each amount remains an explicitly
named unknown, with its role, currency, unit, timing, measurement basis and source identity.
Future or unknown source availability remains unresolved for the selected actor. Missing assets,
consideration, tax effects or adjustment methods cannot be supplied by the fact that an equation
balances.
Independent coefficient maps check both NCI elections, qualifying prior equity, the full-ownership
limit and the post-control reconciliations. Adverse requests test prior debt labelled as equity, pre-
control profit leakage, sub-day rounding, inconsistent currency or ownership, source alteration
and attempted insertion of an unmeasured face value. All source, accounting, posting and
report authorization remains withheld.3
