Exhibit 21 expressly selects significant legal entities, parents of significant
subsidiaries and certain material entities for resolution planning. It excludes
subsidiaries that would not be significant in aggregate under the stated SEC
definition. The names are the same across these four tables apart from the
literal PLC/plc capitalization change; this does not establish an unchanged
complete group. Display nesting is retained as presentation evidence, with
direct ownership and percentages unconfirmed. The jurisdiction column means
“organized under the laws of,” not tax residence or functional currency.

Exhibit 22.2 identifies **JPMorgan Chase Financial Company LLC** as securities
issuer and **JPMorgan Chase & Co.** as guarantor. The financial company appears
in none of the four Exhibit 21 lists. The two exhibits therefore give a direct
example of why absence from a qualified subsidiary list cannot mean absence
of a relevant company or financial exposure. Financial Company LLC is not
JPMorgan Chase Holdings LLC, the IHC in the earlier funding passage.

Each description says the relevant securities are fully and unconditionally
guaranteed by the parent. These are the issuer's descriptions, not an
independent reading of the underlying instruments. The later wording does
not establish that warrants were issued, that earlier debt was redeemed, or
that the guarantee first arose in 2024. The dates above describe referenced
registration effectiveness as reported; they are not independently verified
registration events or availability dates for the retained PDF bytes.

The source fixes the entity and security roles, but supplies no outstanding
principal, valuation or accounting recognition. The parent/IHC funding
restrictions from the preceding supplement remain applicable as reported
conditions. A parent guarantee does not create unrestricted access to bank
cash, and the exhibit list does not locate the financial company's immediate
owner. Those missing facts must remain explicit in a simulation.
