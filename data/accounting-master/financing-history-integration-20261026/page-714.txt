The retained conditional reference distinguishes seven kinds of question: clause satisfaction,
authorized notice, new acceleration, remaining acceleration after rescission, protected-change
consent, majority waiver and a finding about another series. Explicit typed predicates reproduce
its 58 hypothetical classifications, including unknown facts and unresolved boundary-day order.
Only three of these routes are composed into state updates here: a clause observation, a new
acceleration assessment and a rescission assessment. The classification table is not a complete
contract execution system.
In the composed example, the funded but still unpaid company first receives a supplied
conditional principal-clause finding. That observation changes no cash and does not itself
accelerate the obligation. A separate, supported declaration establishes acceleration. The
existing typed settlement then pays the 40. A fresh paid-clause observation becomes unsatisfied,
but a lack of a new acceleration trigger leaves the earlier acceleration finding intact. The selected
rescission assessment changes that state only when all of its supplied conditions are resolved
as required. The historical missed amount remains 40 throughout. These operations record
conditional contractual status; priority, penalties, accelerated cash amounts and insolvency
consequences need their own economic and accounting methods.
The initial contract-status record and its schema extension are explicit synthetic premises. They
do not establish that the retained issuer description governs this promise. The source-dependent
conditions retain their original scope and unresolved operative-instrument dependencies. Known
automatic and declaration routes also keep their different information requirements: the
automatic branch does not require a notice finding that it does not use.
A matching date does not establish that a finding is current.
The first composed
candidate required a clause finding from the same date as the proposed acceleration. Its original
trace passed, but a further comparison found a failure: record the unpaid clause, pay the full
claim later in the same dated sequence, then request acceleration from the old finding. The
date still matches although the economic state has changed. That candidate incorrectly accepts
the stale finding relative to its own current-clause requirement.
The successor records a digest of a fixed economic-state projection with each resolved clause
observation.
The projection contains the book’s identity and period, account definitions,
balances, cash account, cash-flow history, non-contract memory and its types. Conditional-
status memory and seen-event bookkeeping are excluded, so recording a remedy does not
invalidate itself. The selected schema and types are checked separately. A new acceleration
requires both the date and economic-state digest to match the stored observation.
The same-day payment now makes the stale request unresolved before any accepted write. A
new observation of the paid claim supports the consistent assessment that there is no new
trigger. Balanced account changes, changes to unrelated economic memory and revisions to
cash-flow evidence also invalidate the old observation. This conservative rule avoids guessing
which economic changes matter. Matching digests establish equality of the supplied record, not
truth of the evidence or persistence of external legal facts. The relevant external findings still
have to be supplied for the event.
The selected financing contract reproduces four complete financing paths on two account
charts, including their statements. The repaired remedy composition reproduces eight complete
transitions, with separate same-day failure and repair evidence. These checks support the
stated finite declarations and preserve the rejected predecessor. Wider financing decisions, carry,
changed rights, actual series admission and independent review remain necessary for the full
design.
