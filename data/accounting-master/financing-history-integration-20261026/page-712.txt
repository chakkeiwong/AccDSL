A.54.23
Preserve payment history when financing and remedies change
A company that pays late can end with the same cash, debt and equity as a company that pays
on time. The ending balance sheet does not distinguish the two histories. A simulator must
retain what was due, what remained unpaid when payment was missed, what was subsequently
paid, and which contractual consequences were actually established. Otherwise a successful
funding decision can silently make a past failure disappear.
Consider the supplied zero-coupon promise used in the financing comparison. The opening
book contains cash of 60, inventory of 100, debt with a carrying amount of 80 and equity of 80.
Contractual face is 100, due on 30 June 2027. A single explicit measurement interval increases
carrying debt and interest expense by 20 at maturity. Forty new shares are subscribed for 40 in
cash. There are no fees, taxes, penalties or modification effects in this example; the price and
funding date are supplied scenario assumptions. These choices make payment timing visible
without requiring a financing equilibrium.
If the subscription settles on 29 June, the company pays all 100 at maturity. If it settles on 2
July, the company first pays its available 60 at maturity, records the unpaid 40 on 1 July, and
pays the remaining 40 on 3 July. Both paths finish with cash and debt equal to zero, inventory
of 100 and equity of 100 after the interest expense. Only the second path has a missed-payment
history. The signed journal convention records debt as a credit balance; the following table
displays its positive carrying amount.
