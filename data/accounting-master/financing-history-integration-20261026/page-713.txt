A recorded miss requires a matured promise, an observation after its due date and a positive
historical missed amount. The recorded arrears equal the remaining claim in this selected

explicit missed-payment observation.
These conditions are useful but insufficient. After full payment, a snapshot with a historical
missed amount of 50 could satisfy the inequalities even though the recorded amount was 40.
Likewise, a nonnegative share count can remain unchanged when a subscription should have
increased it. The transition declaration therefore binds the old state, typed event inputs and
new state. For a late payment of amount p, let U be the remaining claim, A the recorded
arrears and H the historical missed amount. The selected change requires
Uafter = Ubefore −p,
Aafter = Abefore −p,
Hafter = Hbefore.
Cash falls by p, the signed debt balance increases by p, and the missed flag remains true.
Original face, due date and old shares are preserved. A subscription has a different relation:
new shares increase by the supplied share quantity, while cash and capital change by quantity
times issue price. Money and shares have different dimensions, so a money-typed value cannot
be used as a share count merely because both are exact rationals.
The five declared transitions cover maturity, subscription, due-date payment, missed-payment
observation and late payment. They also preserve unrelated memory, account balances and
prior cash-flow records. Deliberately removing the share-memory or historical-miss write can
still yield a balanced proposal from the generic engine. The selected transition relation rejects
both. The failed proposal remains available for diagnosis, while the accepted current state and
unexecuted command suffix retain the last valid prefix.
An internally consistent history still needs an opening premise.
Opening assessment
binds the selected method registry, schemas, reporting map, account roles and typed financial
state. A separate supplied history record names the exact opening book and its debt-term
fields, with nonblank provenance. Missing or partial history is unresolved. A paid opening that
erases the missed flag and amount can be internally consistent, but it conflicts with the retained
history record and is refused. A caller who supplies the same false account in both records can
still satisfy this consistency test; source evidence must establish the historical facts.
A valid intermediate snapshot is also not automatically a fiscal opening. The existing statement
engine requires the opening date to equal the fiscal period start, an empty current-period cash-
flow sequence and zero temporary account balances. The first verification attempt incorrectly
expected an intermediate funded state to qualify as an opening. Its refusal exposed the test
error. The corrected comparison checks snapshot and supplied-history consistency separately
while preserving the fiscal-opening restrictions. Closing and carrying the financing history
require an explicit further step.
Keep current contractual findings apart from economic history.
The conditional
remedy examples introduce a separate record for an identified hypothetical series. It contains the
selected clause finding, its observation date, the acceleration finding and the evidence identifier.
The applicability of the selected clause is a supplied premise. A known unpaid principal amount
must agree with the outstanding claim in the economic book; an unknown applicability finding
remains unresolved rather than becoming false.
