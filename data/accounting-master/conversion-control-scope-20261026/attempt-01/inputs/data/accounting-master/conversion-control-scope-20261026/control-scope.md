# Conversion changes more than the holder's investment balance

26 October 2026. Conditional AASB design analysis, not selection of the user's
reporting basis or actual accounting admission. Physical page numbers refer to
retained PDFs; acquisition metadata and hashes are in source-attempt-02/.

The synthetic conversion gives the creditor120 of160 votes. That arithmetic is
75%, but it does not finish the accounting analysis. The relevant question is
who has the current ability to direct the activities that significantly affect
the investee's returns, whether that party receives variable returns, and
whether it can use its power to affect those returns. AASB10 §§6–8 states these
three elements and requires reassessment when relevant circumstances change
(physical6–7). The simulation must preserve the evidence for each element.

## Rights and the date control begins

Majority votes support power under AASB10 B35 when they direct the relevant
activities or appoint the directing governing body. B36–B37 require substantive
rights and retain contrary cases, including relevant activities directed by an
administrator or regulator (physical17). B22–B25 examines practical ability,
barriers, timing and substantive rights held by other parties (physical14–16).
Protective rights alone do not confer power; exceptional or contingent rights
are not automatically protective (B26–B28,physical16). Principal versus agent
must also be assessed under §18 and B58–B72; this unit inspects the general
principal/agent requirement and B58–B60, not every delegated-management case.

A conversion date is not automatically the first control date. B47–B50 requires
consideration of substantive potential voting rights, including convertible
instruments (physical19–20). B24 relates exercise timing to when relevant
activity decisions must be made. B53 states that power over activities arising
only in particular circumstances can exist before the event occurs (physical20).
The local consequence is to require separate before/after control assessments.
The current contract supplies neither all rights nor the decisions/calendar
needed to settle them. AASB10 §20 and AASB3 §§8–9 tie consolidation/acquisition
to obtaining control; closing and acquisition dates can differ (physical7 in
each standard). Control is therefore not generated from the share issue event.

## Separate investment accounts and consolidated reports

AASB127 §10 permits cost, AASB9 or the equity method in separate statements,
subject to the standard's requirements and consistent accounting for each
investment category (physical6). Its §3 does not mandate which entities must
prepare separate statements (physical5). §11A requires the corresponding fair-
value treatment when AASB10 §31 requires that treatment (physical6). Selecting
cost as a policy does not establish the investment's initial cost or authorize
carrying the earlier convertible-asset amount into it without analysis.

AASB10 §4 normally requires a parent to present consolidated statements. The
§4(a) conditions, Australian modifications, §4A and §4B require separate scope
assessment (physical5–6). An investment entity satisfying §27 generally measures
its subsidiary investment at fair value through profit or loss under §31;
§32 retains consolidation of a non-investment-entity subsidiary whose main
purpose and activities provide services related to the parent's investment
activities (physical8–9). Under §33, a non-investment-entity parent consolidates
entities controlled through an investment-entity subsidiary. The entity class,
service relationship and exemptions are missing facts here, not numerical
settings to fill from the75% fraction.

Consolidation is also more than adding the holder's investment to the issuer's
assets. AASB10 B86 combines like items, eliminates the parent's investment
against its portion of subsidiary equity, and eliminates intragroup balances,
transactions and cash flows (physical31). B87–B93 addresses policy consistency, post-control income,
potential-rights allocation and reporting dates. B89–B90 distinguishes existing
ownership interests and the special current-return-access case from merely
possible future conversion. A control assessment cannot silently set NCI profit
allocation to a prospective cap-table percentage.

## A controlled entity need not be an acquired business

AASB3 §3 requires a business assessment. Its §§2(b),2(c),2A exclude asset groups,
common-control combinations and specified investment-entity acquisitions from
the ordinary business-combination route (physical6–7). B7A–B7B allows an optional
concentration test: passing establishes that the acquired set is not a business;
failing the test does not establish that it is one (physical22). B8 and
B12–B12D requires an input and substantive process, with different tests when
outputs are absent or present (physical23–24). A cash-generating project is not
classified from its name or forecast terminal payoff.

For an in-scope business combination, §§4–10 requires identifying the acquirer,
control date, identifiable assets/liabilities and NCI; §§32–33 sets out goodwill
measurement components (physical7,11–12). The fixture does not provide the
required acquisition-date measurements. Under §2(b), an asset-group acquisition
uses the applicable asset/liability recognition and allocation requirements and
does not generate goodwill. Neither route can be selected or valued by making
the four-party funding journal balance.

## Proposed implementation boundary

The dependency reference accepts dated, source-labelled assessments, not raw
votes as a substitute for control. It distinguishes unresolved/negative/positive
control, earlier control, requested separate reports, consolidation exemptions,
investment-entity/service-subsidiary scope and business/asset acquisition. Missing
measurements stay as named prerequisites. Every result keeps source admission,
accounting admission and posting authorization false. The existing group report
accepts already supplied synthetic control edges and requires consistent opening/
closing membership; it does not resolve this new acquisition-period boundary.
Thus a mid-period acquisition is a separate integration problem, not an
unnoticed change to its current same-perimeter contract.
