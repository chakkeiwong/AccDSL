14.18
Funding the project does not yet determine its accounts
The price calculation leaves two different questions. Can the parties deliver the promised
cash in the required order? If they can, which amounts should their financial statements
recognise between issue and conversion? The first question has a complete answer under the
supplied synthetic cash balances. The second still depends on the contract, reporting basis and
measurement evidence. Keeping the questions separate prevents a feasible transaction from
silently becoming an unsupported accounting history.
At the financing date the new holder pays 17000/441 to the company and the old owner
contributes 9460/441. Their receipts sum exactly to the project price 60. The company then
pays 60 to the seller. The transfer records identify both parties, the date and the same amount
in the two legal books. In this example the company starts with no available cash for the
purchase, so both receipts must precede payment to the seller. There are six possible orders of
these three transfers. The two orders with the seller paid last succeed; the other four fail at the
first payment that exceeds available company cash. Positive final net cash is not a substitute
for an affordable cash prefix.
The four-party reference retains the company, old owner, new holder and seller books separately.
It checks two account charts and both owner cash 22 and cash exactly equal to the required
contribution. A refused later transfer preserves the accepted prefix. Counterparty cost-basis
entries are explicit synthetic premises. They demonstrate reciprocal transfers and funding

14.19
Issuer and holder classify the same contract differently
The same contract is a claim issued by the company and an investment held by the creditor.
Those perspectives need not have matching classification components. The source research
therefore begins with the complete settlement terms: who can affect the conversion signal,
whether either party can elect cash settlement, the currency and share terms, any redemption
provisions, and agreements involving other members of a group. The example supplies none of
these facts merely by stating that 120 shares will be issued.
Under the inspected AASB 132 provisions, fixed-for-fixed analysis is subject to the definitions
and exceptions for qualifying own-equity instruments. A genuine contingent liability settlement
beyond both parties’ control raises the separate conditions in paragraph 25. A derivative with
settlement choices has its own rule in paragraph 26. The non-derivative compound-instrument
assessment in paragraphs 28–32 cannot be inferred from a fixed share count alone(Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022d, paragraphs 15–16, 22–26 and 28–32). These passages
identify the questions to answer; they do not classify the underspecified fixture.
The holder’s analysis is different. For an in-scope financial-asset host, AASB 9 assesses the
hybrid as a whole. Its fixed-number convertible-bond example links the return to issuer equity
and fails the solely-principal-and- interest condition(Australian Accounting Standards Board,
2026e, paragraphs 4.3.2 and B4.1.14, Instrument F). Issuer liability/equity components therefore
cannot simply be copied into the holder’s asset book. Initial fair value, transaction costs,
subsequent measurement and relevant exceptions remain separate dependencies. Likewise,
AASB 132 AG32 expressly addresses conversion at maturity; it alone does not justify the
fixture’s 2028 conversion before its 2029 terminal date.

of control before legal conversion. Its repaired dependency analysis preserves uncertain control

14.20
Evidence over an interval is stronger than two snapshots
To support uninterrupted control, an analyst needs evidence covering the intervening period. An
empty event list supplies no such evidence. The selected interval reference requires an explicit
assertion that a control status holds throughout a specified half-open interval, together with its
subject, reporting basis, source identity and availability to each actor. This is a representation
of an asserted coverage premise. It does not certify that the assertion is true or that the source
collection is complete.
For example, let one source assert no control from 30 December to 31 December, and another
assert control from 31 December to 1 January. A half-open interval includes its starting instant
and excludes its ending instant, so the shared boundary is assigned once. If both assertions are
eligible for the selected actor view, they conditionally support a gain at that boundary. If the
second source is missing, the second interval remains unresolved. If it conflicts with another
eligible assertion, the conflict remains visible rather than being resolved by whichever record
was loaded last.
Effective time and information time do different jobs. A correction can describe control during
December yet become available to an analyst in January and to a manager only in February.
The analyst’s January view can use it; the manager’s January view cannot. A later available
revision replaces the corresponding earlier assertion only within its declared subject and interval.
Unknown actor availability remains unresolved. The selected profile refuses partial-interval
replacement, whose meaning would require an additional rule.
The implementation reuses the exact fixed-offset timestamp parser and partitions the query at
every eligible interval boundary. It retains every covered, uncovered, unknown or conflicting
piece. Equivalent timestamp offsets denote the same instant, and adding a future correction
leaves an earlier view’s control conclusions unchanged. The checked cases include all two-cell
combinations of control, no control and unknown control, as well as revision conflicts, coverage
gaps, missing ancestry and corrupted source identities.
This gives the simulator a precise way to carry unresolved control history alongside otherwise
balanced company books. It does not yet connect an admitted real history to acquisition
measurement, changing group membership or complete financial statements. The next financial
composition must supply those methods and their dated authority while preserving the source
and actor-view distinctions. Neither a successful funding transfer nor a complete conditional
interval partition can supply the missing accounting evidence.
