# Exact-URL archive inquiry: one byte-matched version lead

The bounded Internet Archive inquiry used five requests: exact CDX queries for Apple and JPMorgan, two JPMorgan replay attempts, and the Wayback availability endpoint for Apple. Request URLs, bodies, headers and transport metadata are retained under `requests/`. Apple returned no record from these routes. That is a search result for the exact URL and scope, not evidence that no archive exists.

JPMorgan's exact-URL CDX response listed eight captures. The 19 November replay returned a 5,242,880-byte payload whose SHA-1 base32 matches the CDX digest. It is an exact 5 MiB prefix of the later complete PDF and lacks an EOF trailer; it is rejected as an intact PDF. CDX length and replay content-length fields differ, and their semantics are unresolved. The 26 December payload also has a CDX length field that differs from the returned byte count; its CDX digest and direct SHA-256 both match the complete retained PDF. The 26 December 2025 08:17:31 UTC replay returned a 10,427,256-byte, 358-page PDF. Its SHA-256 exactly matches `data/financial-report-benchmark/documents/jpmorgan/jpmorgan-2025-q3/original.pdf` (`db4947d675ef76327306f7cfece3bcd6f51476450fe72d397d30041cfdedfc54`). The archive response also reports an origin Last-Modified time of 4 November 2025. This is a strong exact-version archive lead under the archive timestamp/source assumption, not a signed timestamp certificate.

The eight annual years remain a data inventory rather than a sufficient nested forecasting panel. This archive inquiry does not fix historical development sample size, define permitted information at each origin, justify future-history probabilities, or resolve expected-performance inference. All 192 historical vintage admissions remain false; no empirical forecast or requirement closure follows.

**Plan audit and defaults.** Exact-URL retrieval was selected because it tests the retained source identity directly. The two issuers were access diagnostics, not a representative sample. The 2025-01-01–2026-10-22 CDX interval and 50-row cap were search limits, not study cutoffs. Archive timestamps and metadata are not cryptographic timestamp attestations; the byte match establishes content identity, while the date claim remains conditional on archive provenance. A later admissible cutoff must be strictly after the archive observation under the adopted timing policy.

**Red-team note.** The strongest alternative explanation is that an archive replay can serve bytes through a later capture endpoint without establishing when an eligible forecaster could have obtained the file. The lead is overturned as a historical timing signal if WARC/source details show the timestamp is unreliable or the replay was not the corresponding capture. The weakest evidence is independent authentication of the archive clock and capture chain.
