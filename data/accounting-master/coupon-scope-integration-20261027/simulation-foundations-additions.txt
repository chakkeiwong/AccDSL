The zero-payment prefix satisfies these identities. If they hold after i instructions, subtracting

Because the unpaid claim always exceeds cash by 1/16, any positive affordable payment q ≤Ci is
strictly below Ui. For a finite schedule of positive payments, affordability is therefore equivalent

the economic amounts. Dates, identities and runtime support impose separate conditions. At
cash exhaustion the company still owes 1/16; further cash-only payment cannot remove that
claim. A separate cash inflow or a supported change to the claim would be needed.
The selected method’s actual expressions, account bindings, cumulative memory write and
preserved fields match this recurrence on both charts. Twenty complete runtime comparisons
include nonuniform and same-day payments, unequal rational fractions, a tiny first payment,
and a schedule leaving exactly 1/264 in cash. The last residual is retained rather than rounded
to zero. These finite checks support the selected implementation while the induction establishes
the algebraic result.28
17.30.1
A date or an event identity can distinguish equal balances
Now compare a payment of 1 on 2 January with payments of 1/2 on 2 and 3 January. Both
leave cash 69/16, unpaid coupon 35/8 and cumulative payment 1. Their contract memory
agrees. Their current dates, accepted event identities and dated gross flows do not.
A fresh instruction dated 2 January can follow the first history. It cannot follow the second,
whose last accepted event is already dated 3 January. Similarly, an instruction using the
second instalment’s event identity can be fresh in the one-payment history and a replay in the
split-payment history. The runtime rejects the replay without changing balances. Thus equal
financial totals do not preserve the full set of permissible instructions.
28data/accounting-master/coupon-schedule-invariant-20261027/ retains four method inspections, 517 assertions
and eight controls. The runtime permits at most 64 commands per program, bounds its accumulated trace at 8
MiB, and imposes record-size and exact-rational limits. A 65-command schedule whose total equals available
cash is refused by the command limit. Such a resource refusal is not economic infeasibility; satisfying one limit
alone does not guarantee execution.

17.30.2
Resume from the current book and report from the period opening
Execution and reporting need different dates here. The original period opening supplies the
starting cash and empty current-period flow history for a cumulative report. The current
book supplies the balances, last event date, used identities and contract memory for the next
instruction. Replacing one with the other would either forget accepted events or misstate the
reporting interval.
The present declared-program runner validates a fiscal opening at the period start with no flows.
It therefore refuses a midperiod current book as its opening. The selected continuation uses the
same typed event engine directly for one instruction, retains the actual prefix unchanged, and
reports the resulting book against the original period opening. This is a checked single-step
interface. A general program-resumption interface still needs explicit bindings for both books,
the verified prefix and the remaining commands; it cannot be implemented by resetting a date
or discarding prior flows.

bypass the independent comparison.
