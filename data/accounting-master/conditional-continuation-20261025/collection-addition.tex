\paragraph{Keep the selected history attached when the book changes.}
The conditional opening can support a further explicitly assumed transaction,
provided that the method's premises are supplied and no target obligation
remains unresolved in this bounded composition. The
\texttt{ConditionalCompanyContinuation/1} wrapper recomputes the complete
opening result and compares it with the supplied result before invoking the
ordinary book transition. A stale state, altered witness record or different
company context refuses. Missing opening premises or remaining target
obligations return an unresolved result. The wrapper retains the complete
opening evidence alongside the financial result; a copied provenance label
alone would not establish that connection.

For the two preceding histories, assume a cash distribution of USD~1 on
2~January 2025. This synthetic method checks the explicitly selected
applicability premise, a positive amount and sufficient cash. It credits cash
and debits the declared equity account by USD~1, leaving cash of USD~14 and
an equity credit of USD~14. The financing cash flow is minus USD~1 and profit
is zero. In the statement reconciliation, the direct movement in reported
equity is minus USD~1, although the ledger debit is positive. The common
report rejects an explanation with the opposite sign. Neither history's
sales memory changes: USD~5 and USD~7 still distinguish their pasts.

The checked wrapper agrees exactly with the unchanged transition and report
under both account charts. Unknown event inputs retain their reasons, false
applicability remains unsupported, and insufficient cash refuses without
changing the supplied book. This is a declared financial continuation of a
selected conditional history. It does not establish lawful distributions,
distributable reserves, accounting compliance or admitted execution. A future
method that depends on unresolved invoice terms needs those terms explicitly;
the successful distribution example cannot supply them.
