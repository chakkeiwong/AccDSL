Different designation origins require their own gain histories
A reduction of five tonnes does not identify a reserve when the tonnes entered at different prices.
Consider two forecast purchase origins, A and B, each of ten tonnes. A was designated at a
price of 8 and B later at a price of 9. Each has its own documented match to an external
linear instrument from the date of designation. The instruments entered at zero value and
cost. Under the supplied model there is no discounting, carry, credit or basis difference, and
each instrument’s gain exactly offsets its named item’s risk change. Prices are currency units
per tonne; gains and all balances below are currency units. These are synthetic measurement
assumptions, rather than a procedure for deriving actual forward values from spot prices.
At a subsequent measurement price of 11, A’s instrument has gained 10(11 −8) = 30, while
B’s has gained 10(11 −9) = 20. The relationship’s reserve is 50. If five tonnes from A cease
to qualify, their recorded effective gain is 5(11 −8) = 15. If five from B cease to qualify, it is
5(11 −9) = 10. In both cases the stopped quantity is one quarter of the total. Multiplying
50 by that nominal fraction gives 12.5, which is wrong for either identified history. Quantity
without the designation origin has lost information needed for this calculation.
The accounting basis remains the historical IFRS 9 text in EU 2023/1803 (European Commission,
2023a). Paragraph B6.5.17 preserves an added volume’s own designation date and reference
price; paragraph B6.5.20 leaves the measurement of continuing quantities unaffected by removal.
Paragraphs 6.5.6 and B6.5.22–28 make cessation prospective when qualification fails. The
example supplies the required documentation, individual eligibility, management as a group
and proportional variability under paragraphs 6.4.1 and 6.6.1–3. It also supplies the finding
that the stopped forecast volume is no longer highly probable and that the rest continues to
qualify. Choosing a quantity in a report does not establish those facts.
The retained gain histories support a narrower deduction than a general allocation of a hetero-
geneous reserve. Let origin j have quantity qj, designation date dj and original price fj. At a
supplied measurement price S, its cumulative instrument gain and the opposite change in its
hedged purchase cash flows are
