Give 100 employees ten equity instruments each, with grant-date fair value 3 per instrument
and three years of equal service. At the first year-end, 90 employees are expected to complete
service; at the second, 80; at vesting, 75 have completed it. The cumulative expenses are
90(10)(3)/3 = 900,
80(10)(3)(2/3) = 1600,
75(10)(3) = 2250.
The annual charges are therefore 900, 700 and 650, credited to award equity. If an additional
non-market performance condition ultimately fails for all awards, cumulative expense becomes
zero and previously recognised expense reverses. In a separate award whose supplied grant-date
fair value of 3 already incorporates a market hurdle, failure of that hurdle does not reverse
the 2250 for the 75 employees who satisfy the other conditions. Equal supplied fair values in
these examples do not imply that different contracts have the same economic value. Once an
equity award has vested, later non-exercise does not reverse total equity under paragraph 23; a
transfer within equity remains possible.
Cancellation also differs from forfeiture. Paragraphs 27–28 (European Commission, 2023a,
IFRS 2.27–28A) preserve the minimum original grant-date expense, subject to failure of
the specified non-market vesting conditions, and accelerate unrecognised service expense on
cancellation during vesting. For one grant with total grant-date value 300, one of three equal
service years gives expense 100. An entity cancellation that is not a forfeiture accelerates the
remaining 200. If it pays 330 when the repurchased award’s fair value is 320, debit equity 320
and expense 10, and credit cash 330. Total service/cancellation expense is 310. Starting from
cash and capital 1000 leaves cash and equity 670 after all entries. Calling the event a service
forfeiture would wrongly reverse the first 100.
Non-vesting conditions enter fair value under paragraph 21A. However, when the entity or
counterparty can choose whether to meet such a condition, failure to do so during vesting is a
cancellation under paragraph 28A. Consequently the condition’s classification and the party’s
control of it must be preserved together. Beneficial modifications require their incremental
value analysis under paragraph 27 and Appendix B; this cancellation example does not replace
that calculation.
10.13.2
Cash obligations change as the award’s value changes
For a cash-settled award, IFRS 2.30–33D (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 2.30–33D)
requires remeasurement at each reporting date and settlement. Service and non-market vesting
conditions affect quantity; market and non-vesting conditions enter the current valuation. Apply
the service fraction to the current eligible quantity and fair value, then charge the difference
from the previously recognised liability. The final cumulative expense equals cash paid, unlike
the fixed grant-date measurement of an equity award.
