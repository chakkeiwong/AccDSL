Following the margin through the year
Why can the segment release €819 million of CSM during 2023 when newly recognised contracts
add only €154 million? The opening margin carries profit from earlier business. Munich Re’s
measurement-component reconciliation for ERGO Life and Health Germany begins with CSM of
−10,449 million euros and ends with −9,569 million. Its signs present the margin as a negative
balance: new business makes it more negative, while service release moves it towards zero. Keep
that convention while reading the movements (Munich Re, 2024, printed p. 246).
The nonzero CSM movements are service provided during the year, contracts initially recognised,
changes in estimates that adjust CSM, and derecognition or changes in the consolidated group.
In that order, the displayed amounts give the complete bridge
−10,449 + 819 −154 + 214 + 1 = −9,569.
In positive margin amounts, opening CSM of 10,449 receives 154 from new business, releases 819
for service, and falls by another 214 and 1 through the other two movements. The net reduction
is 880. The table’s CSM service subtotal of 879 already combines 819 −154 + 214; adding that
subtotal to the individual movements would count them twice. The estimate movement here is
the CSM column’s 214, not a claim that every change in the underlying estimates is absorbed
by CSM (Munich Re, 2024, printed p. 246).
The numbers connect three distinct disclosures. New-business CSM of 154 matches the initial-
recognition table; the 819 release matches the segment’s service revenue; and closing CSM of
9,569 is the balance used in the issuer’s schedule of expected future releases. The latter match
verifies which balance that schedule starts from, rather than the accuracy of the future releases.
The comparative-year column also reconciles: −9,412 + 948 −221 −1,764 = −10,449, which
becomes the 2023 opening amount (Munich Re, 2024, printed pp. 247, 253–254 and 264).
The larger liability totals require a different comparison. The measurement reconciliation
includes the full GMM/VFA balances and the incurred-claims liabilities of PAA contracts. It
excludes PAA remaining coverage. It would therefore be wrong to describe the whole table
as GMM/VFA only. The footnote continued after its comparative table explicitly gives the
omitted PAA remaining-coverage balance: −62 million euros in the comparative period and −1
million at the end of 2023 (Munich Re, 2024, printed pp. 241 and 247).
This scope difference accounts for the displayed opening and closing differences. The complete
liability reconciliation starts at −109,556, against −109,494 in the measurement table: the
difference is −62. Their closing amounts are −112,808 and −112,807, a difference of −1. Thus
an apparently small missing balance is a disclosed excluded population, rather than permission
to omit it or absorb it into rounding (Munich Re, 2024, printed pp. 230–231 and 246–247).
