12.24.1
Reconstructing the margin from cash and service history
The transition route must eventually produce a measured opening balance. Consider a separate
nonparticipating group with three equal service periods, transition after the first, and supported
zero discount rates. This example uses the retained EU 2023/1803 provisions; it does not infer
EU applicability from the neighboring AASB or ASBE discussion. Assume no tax, foreign
exchange, OCI, incurred unpaid claims or changes in remaining expectations. The zero rates
and equal service units isolate reconstruction; they are supplied conditions, not defaults for an
insurer’s portfolio (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.C3–C8,44,B119).
The insurer starts with capital and cash 100. Before recognition it pays acquisition costs of
10. A supported allocation assigns 6 to the recognized group and 4 to a future group, whose
acquisition asset remains recoverable. Premium is 120, initial expected claims are 90 and initial
risk adjustment is 6. The recognized group’s initial margin is consequently
120 −90 −6 −6 = 18.
That amount is CSM, not immediate profit. The acquisition payment appears once in cash;
the allocated 6 reduces the margin, while the future group’s 4 stays as a separate asset.
These different destinations implement the acquisition and initial-measurement requirements
(European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.28A–28F,38,C4).
Under full retrospective application, contemporaneous records show expected first-period claims
of 30 and risk release of 2. Actual claims of 30 are paid. The one completed service unit releases
18/3 = 6 of CSM, leaving 12. At transition, remaining claims 60 and risk adjustment 4 give FCF
64 and liability 64 + 12 = 76. Cash is 100 −10 + 120 −30 = 180; adding the future acquisition
asset of 4 and subtracting liability gives equity 108. Equivalently, service releases and CSM
revenue, less actual claims, give 30 + 2 + 6 −30 = 8, the increase from opening capital 100.
Suppose the prior accounting basis instead reports liability 80 and acquisition asset 10 at this
same date, with the same cash 180. Prior equity is 110. Replacing those balances gives a
transition equity adjustment of
(80 −76) + (4 −10) = −2.
There is no transition cash receipt or payment. C4(c) places the resulting net difference in
equity; it does not supply a new cash flow to make the books balance (European Commission,
2023a, IFRS 17.C4).
Now change the available information, keeping the baseline cash history. Assume full retro-
spective application is impracticable, and that the evidence conditions permit the specified
modified-retrospective calculations. C12 reconstructs initial expected claims as remaining claims
60 plus known past payments 30, giving 90. C14 reconstructs initial risk adjustment as the
current 4 plus expected past release 2 supported by similar contracts issued at transition. C15(b)
compares one past coverage unit with two remaining units. With the supported acquisition allo-
cation, these inputs again yield initial CSM 18 and transition CSM 12 (European Commission,
2023a, IFRS 17.C6–C8,C12,C14–C15). The agreement follows from the supplied evidence and
numbers; it is not a property of the route names.
A different actual claim payment makes the distinction visible. Suppose actual first-period
claims were 35, while the full-history initial expectation remained 90 and the remaining
expectation remained 60. Full retrospection retains CSM 12: the extra claim cost is past-service
experience. Cash becomes 175 and equity 103. In the separately admitted modified information
view, C12 instead reconstructs initial expected claims as 60 + 35 = 95. Initial CSM is then
120 −95 −6 −6 = 13, of which 26/3 remains. Liability is 64 + 26/3 = 218/3, and equity is
