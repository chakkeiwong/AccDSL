The policyholder’s investment payment is max(0.9S, 90). Its top-up above the ordinary share
is (90 −0.9S)+, where x+ = max(x, 0). That top-up pays zero in the up state and 18 in the
down state. The matching portfolio has δ = −0.45 and b = 54: −0.45(120) + 54 = 0 and
−0.45(80) + 54 = 18. Its cost is −0.45(100) + 54 = 9. Adding the ordinary share’s value of 90
gives a financial-benefit value of 99. This derives the option value; no option price has been
supplied as an unexplained input.
For a separate physical forecast, suppose the up state has probability 3/4. The expected top-up
is then 18/4 = 4.5, although its market-consistent price remains 9. Suppose also that a covered
death triggers an additional payment of 20, with physical probability 1/10, independently of the
market state. Its expected cost is 2. Nonfinancial risk adjustment of 1 is a separately supplied
measurement assumption, with its entire release in the service result. The financial state prices,
physical joint law and compensation for nonfinancial risk therefore occupy different records
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 33, 37, 81 and B42–B49).
12.9.1
The same measurement through service and payment
Use the retained historical Australian provisions for a conditional calendar-2024/25 example.
Assume the contract has significant insurance risk, an enforceable link to the identified pool
and the required direct-participation characteristics. The component rights terminate together;
neither can be retained separately after the other matures. This supplies the specified interre-
lationship under B32(b), together with an assessment that no embedded derivative must be
separated. These admission findings need actual terms before real use; neither q nor the 90%
share proves them (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 10–13, B17–B23,
B31–B32 and B101–B108).
The investment payment is due whether the insured lives or dies. There are no surrender,
lapse, acquisition or tax cash flows in this example, and all figures are pretax. The insurer
holds one unit of the underlying asset at fair value through profit or loss and makes no risk-
mitigation election. The portfolio used to calculate the guarantee price is not its actual hedge.
Insurance finance also enters profit or loss under paragraph 89(a). The investment component
remains within the insurance measurement but is excluded from service revenue and expense
under paragraph 85 (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 85, 89 and
B115–B118).
Start with owner capital and cash of 50, and receive premium 110. Before receipt, fulfilment
