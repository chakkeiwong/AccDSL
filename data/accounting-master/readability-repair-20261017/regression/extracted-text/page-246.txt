A bank’s event history must therefore identify the instrument, declaration, obligation and

A hypothetical bank in the first quarter of a year illustrates the income distinction. Suppose
each of the preceding four quarters has Call Report net income of US$10m. Assume its only
distributions in that preceding calendar year were US$38m of declared dividends, with no
associated tax adjustment. Part 5 retained net income for that year is then US$2m: US$40m
less US$38m. Part 3 eligible retained income is US$10m, the greater of that US$2m remainder
and the US$10m quarterly average. These are two calculations for the same hypothetical history,
not interchangeable names for one balance.
Now suppose the bank falls within the amended rule’s covered population and applies that
rule. Take its previous-quarter capital conservation buffer as 1.5%, its applicable countercyclical
amount as zero, its leverage buffer standard as 1%, and its measured leverage buffer as 0.2%.
These are supplied example inputs, rather than estimates from a public balance sheet. The rule’s
Table 1 gives a 40% payout ratio, which alone would produce US$4m from the US$10m eligible
income. Table 2 gives zero because the leverage buffer is at most one quarter of its standard.
The required lower ratio therefore gives a zero table-based maximum, absent the specified
OCC permission. Conversely, a table entry saying that no payout-ratio limitation applies does
not mean a 100% ratio or permission under every other rule (Office of the Comptroller of the
Currency, 2026d, 12 CFR 3.11(a)(4), (c), Tables 1–2).
11.1.4
Systemic indicators and the applicable surcharge
Replacing the example’s supplied leverage-buffer standard with an observed requirement needs
a specific surcharge. For a bank within the amended rule’s covered population, the standard is
the lesser of 1% and, if applicable, half of the controlling global systemically important holding
company’s most recent required Method 1 surcharge, subject to the specified effective-date
provisions. The holding company’s overall surcharge instead takes the greater of its Method 1
and Method 2 surcharges. The overall figure alone does not identify the input this bank rule
requires. Annual determination and the date an adjusted surcharge takes effect also remain
separate (Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, 2026d, 12 CFR 3.11(a)(1)(v)) (Board of
Governors of the Federal Reserve System, 2026c, 12 CFR 217.403(a)–(d)).
Method 1 compares twelve reported systemic indicators with published global amounts, applies
their specified weights and caps the substitutability category’s contribution at 100 basis points
(Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, 2026b, 12 CFR 217.404). The denominators
have their own measurement and publication dates. The Federal Reserve’s notice published on
16 December 2025 supplies the 2025 denominators using year-end 2024 global data, converted
from euros into dollars at the stated 31 December 2024 rate. The preceding notice, published
on 18 December 2024, uses year-end 2023 data (Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve
System, 2025, pp. 58245–58246) (Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, 2024,
pp. 102908–102909). The release-year label therefore does not identify the underlying observation
year or the date a bank’s capital requirement changes. The published amounts are in dollars;
reported indicator values must be brought to the same unit before division.
