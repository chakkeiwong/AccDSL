The contract record must therefore retain the two promises, the original allocation and its
price evidence, delivery identity, coverage, progress measure, cumulative service revenue and
actual costs. Each service update posts the difference between newly supported cumulative
revenue and revenue already recognised. Replaying an accepted delivery cannot release the
product allocation twice. Unknown progress is not zero performance: paragraphs 44–45 require
a separate assessment of reasonable measurement and, where applicable, cost recovery. This
percentage method cannot invent those findings.
The classification also has a substantive boundary. A separately purchasable warranty is a
distinct service under B29. Without that option, B30 sends an assurance-only warranty to AASB
137 unless it provides additional service. Law, duration and promised tasks help distinguish the
cases under B31; there is no universal number of months that makes a warranty a service. If
assurance and additional service cannot reasonably be accounted for separately, B32 treats the
two warranties together as one performance obligation. Harm or infringement compensation
under B33 is not such a service. The historical AASB 137 population-measurement case in
Section 9.4.13 supplies the separate assurance method. Actual coverage, claims, timing and
reimbursement evidence remain required; missing facts cannot become a zero provision or
convert assurance into this service example.
9.4.4
Changed promises can affect future units or past progress
A contract can change after some performance has already been recognised. The accounting
consequence depends on what remains to be supplied. Consider an original prepaid order for
ten distinct units at 10 each. Four have been delivered, earning 40. The parties approve an
enforceable amendment adding six units for 48. Comparable standalone prices remain 10 each,
and no appropriate contract-specific adjustment makes the 48 addition reflect those prices. The
addition therefore fails the separate-contract condition in paragraph 20. Because the remaining
units are distinct from those already delivered, paragraph 21(a) reallocates the consideration
for the remaining performance (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022e).
The undelivered original units carry 60 of consideration. Adding the new 48 gives 108 for twelve
remaining units, whose standalone prices are equal. Thus their new unit allocation is
unrecognised original consideration + additional consideration
