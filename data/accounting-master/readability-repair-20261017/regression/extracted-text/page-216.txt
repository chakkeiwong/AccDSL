Adding the other origins gives total actual derivative value D = Rc + F0 + U.
To follow later expectation or loss findings, let L be cumulative signed reserve released to profit.
A released gain makes L positive; a released loss makes it negative. Remaining stopped reserve
F and cumulative profit P then satisfy
F = F0 −L,
P = U + L,
D = Rc + F + P.
The distinction between no longer highly probable and no longer expected remains the one in
paragraph 6.5.12. Expected cash flows retain F, subject to the immediate unrecoverable-loss
requirement in paragraph 6.5.11(d)(iii). Cancellation releases the entire remaining F once. If
a loss was released earlier, only the balance remains available for cancellation or an eventual
inventory transfer.
Follow the two origins to a terminal price of 12. A’s actual instrument settles for 40 and B’s for
30 in every history; neither instrument was sold when hedge accounting stopped. If five tonnes
from A were stopped and then canceled, the continuing reserve is 5(12 −8) + 10(12 −9) = 50.
The stopped instrument’s post-cessation gain is 5(12 −11) = 5, and releasing its frozen gain of
15 gives profit 20. If instead five tonnes from B were stopped and canceled, continuing reserve
is 10(12 −8) + 5(12 −9) = 55. Releasing its frozen gain of 10 gives profit 15. For example,
canceling A debits the stopped reserve by 15 and credits profit by 15; the later gain of 5 is a
separate instrument remeasurement.
