For an admitted group, the source separates the obligation equal to the underlying items’ fair
value from the variable fee deducted for future service. Changes in the former do not adjust CSM.
Changes in the insurer’s share and in eligible future-service costs do, subject to loss and risk
mitigation conditions. The latter adjustments use current rates; a GMM locked-rate calculation
cannot be copied into this model (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.45,B104,B110–114).
Suppose a qualifying insurance group receives premium 100, invested in its identified pool.
Policyholders are entitled to 80% of the pool’s value regardless of whether an insured event
occurs, together with separate insured benefits. Supply remaining nonfinancial claims of 10
and risk adjustment 2, two equal current/future service units, and zero discount, tax and FX
effects. The 80% amount is an investment component under the stated terms. All eligibility and
service-quantity findings are explicit case premises. With pool value 100, FCF is 80+10+2 = 92,
leaving CSM 8 and LRC 100. Opening cash and equity 200 become cash 200, pool asset 100
and liability 100 after receiving and investing the premium.
Before the first service allocation, the pool rises to 120. The policyholder’s share rises by 16 to
96. The insurer’s 20% share rises by 4, increasing CSM to 12. FCF becomes 96 + 10 + 2 = 108,
and total LRC becomes 120. The financial increase in the liability is therefore 20, not merely
the policyholder’s increase of 16. With all pool gains and insurance finance in profit, asset gain
20 offsets insurance finance expense 20; the extra fee 4 remains unearned within CSM.
Now supply expected and actual first-period insured claims 5 and risk adjustment release
1. Allocate the CSM 12 equally across the current and remaining service units, releasing 6.
Revenue is 5 + 1 + 6 = 12, claims expense 5 and service profit 7. Paying the claims leaves cash
195, pool asset 120 and LRC 96 + 5 + 1 + 6 = 108, giving equity 207. In the second period, with
unchanged pool value and the same claims and release, profit is another 7. Sell the pool for
120 and repay the investment component 96. Final cash and equity are 214. The repayment
reduces the liability and cash; it is excluded from both insurance revenue and service expense
(European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.81,83–85,B119).
12.10.1
Losses and risk mitigation change the CSM adjustment
Consider a separate adverse branch from the initial FCF 92 and CSM 8, before any service.
Future nonfinancial costs rise by 15. Use the CSM 8 first, then recognise service loss 7 and a
tracked loss component 7 within LRC 107. Equity falls from 200 to 193. A subsequent cost
reduction of 6 reverses loss 6 and leaves loss component 1, CSM zero and LRC 101. A further
reduction of 4 reverses the last loss 1 and restores CSM 3; FCF 97 plus CSM 3 brings LRC
back to 100. Those loss reversals remain service results under the specified rules even when
a qualifying source of the original loss was a decline in the insurer’s underlying-item share
(European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.45,48–50,87(c)).
A financial guarantee change needs a different cause record. From the same initial position,
suppose its supplied future-service value increases by 6 while a derivative gains 6. Without
applying the risk-mitigation choice, FCF rises to 98 and CSM falls to 2, leaving LRC 100. The
derivative gain enters profit, increasing equity to 206. If B115–116’s conditions are met and
the insurer applies the choice to this change, CSM remains 8 and LRC rises to 106. Insurance
finance expense 6 then offsets the derivative gain, leaving equity 200. The choice changes
recognition timing, not the derivative’s value or the obligation.
This treatment requires a previously documented risk-management objective and strategy,
an economic offset and credit risk that does not dominate that offset. B115 specifies which
derivatives, FVTPL non-derivative instruments or held reinsurance can mitigate each exposure.
