12.11
Partial reinsurance and changes in insurance estimates
The following methods use IFRS 17 in the retained September 2023 EU compilation. They
extend the preceding nonparticipating general-measurement examples. Contract rights, group
assignments, recovery percentages, risk adjustments and discount rates are explicit conditional
inputs. A change in one of these inputs must retain its cause: changed service expectations,
financial risk, counterparty performance and a new legal agreement do not have interchangeable
accounting consequences.
12.11.1
Only the covered contracts support loss recovery
IFRS 17.62–66B (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.62–68 and B119C–F) keeps a held
treaty separate from the issued contracts, with its own entry, coverage and recognition dates.
Proportionate coverage can delay recognition until an underlying contract is recognised. B119C
permits the initial onerous-loss recovery only if the treaty was entered into before or at the
same time as the underlying onerous contracts were recognised. The record must distinguish
these dates; the treaty’s later cash payment does not identify when it was entered into.
When a treaty covers only some contracts within an onerous issued group, B119E requires a
systematic and rational allocation of the group’s loss to the covered contracts. B119D then
multiplies that covered loss by the percentage of their claims expected to be recovered. A
treaty’s headline quota percentage must not be applied to uncovered losses. Preserve the
contract-to-issued-group and contract-to-treaty joins, covered risks and periods, attachment
points, limits and exclusions, the loss-allocation method, expected claims/recoveries and the
separate transferred-risk adjustment. For excess-of-loss or capped cover, expected recoveries
require the admitted claim distribution and treaty payoff; a constant quota share cannot be
used without support.
Take an issued group with premium 100, expected claims 118 and risk adjustment 2, all measured
with zero discounting at recognition. Initial loss is 20 and CSM is zero. A source-supported
allocation in this synthetic contract set identifies a covered portion with premium 41, claims 48
and risk adjustment 1, giving loss 8; the uncovered portion has premium 59, claims 70 and risk
adjustment 1, giving loss 12. These amounts sum to the group; they are not separate groups
created to improve the result.
A treaty entered into on time covers half of the covered contracts’ claims. Expected recovery is
24, transferred-risk adjustment is a separately supplied 0.5, and premium paid is 26.5. After
payment, the ordinary fulfilment asset is 24.5 and cost CSM is 2. Loss recovery is
8 × 50% = 4,
so cost CSM rises to 6 and the measured held asset to 30.5. The loss-recovery component of 4
sits inside that asset; it is not another receivable. Starting from cash and equity 50, receive
issued premiums 100 and pay treaty premium 26.5. Cash 123.5 plus held asset 30.5 equals issued
liability 120 plus equity 34. Applying 50% to the entire loss would recognise 10 and overstate
equity by 6. If this same treaty had been entered into only after the underlying recognition, the
initial loss-recovery adjustment would not apply.
