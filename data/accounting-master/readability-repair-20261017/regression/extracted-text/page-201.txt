10.10.1
An offer, an amendment and delivery can occur on different dates
The immediate conversion above combines several events that need not coincide. An issuer
can announce an offer before it changes any enforceable right; the holder can accept a binding
amendment before shares are delivered. Retain the announcement, approval or acceptance
conditions, legally effective amendment, discharge of the old debt, and delivery separately. The
following conditional examples use IAS 32 and IFRS 9 in the retained September 2023 EU
compilation. Their dates and terms are synthetic. IAS 32.AG35 measures the inducement at
the amendment date, while IFRS 9.3.3.1 and B3.3.1 remove the debt when it is discharged,
cancelled or expires, including a qualifying legal release by the creditor. Administrative delay
alone does not keep a discharged debt outstanding (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 32.13,
AG35; IFRS 9.3.3.1–3, B3.3.1–6).
Suppose an offer is announced on 1 January 2024 but, under its supplied legal terms, changes
no rights until approval and the holder’s binding acceptance on 5 January. Acceptance releases
the issuer from the old cash obligation in return for 50 fixed ordinary shares; delivery occurs on
20 January. There is no cash alternative, further condition or variable-share obligation. The
ordinary shares satisfy the equity definition. The old terms would have delivered 40 shares.
Share quotations are 30, 40 and 50 on the three dates, respectively. The incremental ten shares
give an amendment-date loss of 10(40) = 400. Using the announcement quote would give 300;
using the delivery quote would give 500. Both would measure the wrong date.
With debt carrying amount 960 and original conversion equity 60, debit debt 960 and inducement
expense 400, and credit equity to be issued 1,360 on 5 January. Original conversion equity
remains within equity. On 20 January, transfer 1,360 from equity to be issued to issued equity;
the new quotation does not remeasure that equity-classified obligation (European Commission,
2023a, IAS 32.15–16, 21–22, AG32–35). Starting with cash 1,020, debt 960 and equity 60
gives closing cash and total equity 1,020: original equity 60 plus new equity 1,360 less loss 400.
Neither the announcement nor administrative delivery creates another expense or cash flow.
Recognition within equity does not settle the separate IAS 33 question of when shares enter the
weighted denominator; retain the actual participation and issue terms for that assessment.
Change the accepted consideration to the original 40 fixed ordinary shares plus a fixed cash
bonus of 100 payable later. Assume zero discount and no intervening interest in this short
illustration, with the old debt legally released at acceptance. The incremental consideration
and loss are 100. Debit debt 960 and expense 100; credit equity to be issued 960 and bonus
payable 100. Equity is then 60 + 960 −100 = 920, with cash 1,020 and the payable 100. Later
payment debits the payable and credits cash 100, leaving cash and equity 920 without a second
loss. The payment buys the debt holder’s early conversion and is a financing outflow under
the stated facts; the share exchange is disclosed as noncash financing (European Commission,
2023a, IAS 32.AG35; IAS 7.6, 17, 43–44).
These entries require the supplied legal and classification findings. A binding instrument with a
contingent cash clause can already be a liability under IAS 32.25; it is not equivalent to the
expressly nonbinding offer above. An obligation to deliver shares worth a fixed cash amount
uses a variable quantity and is also a liability. A derivative’s cash settlement alternative fails
equity classification unless all alternatives qualify as equity (European Commission, 2023a,
IAS 32.19, 21, 25–27). The procedure first determines whether rights changed, then classifies
each new obligation, measures the inducement at the effective amendment, and separately tests
debt discharge. If the debt has not been released, retain and measure that debt and assess the
actual modification under IFRS 9; do not copy the 960 debit from this case. Unknown approval
effects, contingent consideration values or discharge terms remain unresolved inputs to that
procedure, with the original history intact.
