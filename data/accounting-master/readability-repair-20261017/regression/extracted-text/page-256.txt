11.3
Classification comes before the loan calculation
A loan’s contractual principal is not enough to determine its carrying amount. The dated
amortised-cost examples that follow require both an eligible contractual cash-flow pattern
and a business model for collecting those flows. These are findings about the instrument
and the way assets are managed, not labels chosen to obtain a preferred profit figure. The
following procedure uses the retained historical IFRS 9 clauses in EU Regulation 2023/1803;
the surrounding Australian and Chinese examples retain their own stated jurisdiction and date
boundaries.
First establish the financial asset’s terms and the level at which the bank actually manages it.
Evidence includes how performance is reported to key management, how risks are managed and
how managers are compensated. An intention to retain one asset is insufficient. Sales must
be explained by their reasons, timing, volume and place in that management objective; the
source supplies no universal sale-count threshold (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.4.1.1–3,
B4.1.1–4B).
Next ask whether contractual payments are solely principal and interest on the outstanding
principal. Principal here begins with fair value at initial recognition. Interest compensates for
time value, credit risk, other basic lending risks and costs, and a profit margin. Equity-linked
returns and leverage introduce different exposures. For a hybrid whose host is a financial asset,
assess the entire contract under IFRS 9.4.3.2; the issuer’s separate liability and equity components
do not automatically become the holder’s measurement units (European Commission, 2023a,
IFRS 9.4.1.3, 4.3.2, B4.1.7A–9, B4.1.14).
If that cash-flow test passes, a hold-to-collect business model leads to amortised cost, while a
model achieved by both collection and sales leads to fair value through other comprehensive
income (FVOCI) for debt. Other financial assets generally use fair value through profit or loss
(FVTPL), subject to the separate equity election below. At initial recognition, an otherwise
eligible asset may be irrevocably designated FVTPL when the specified accounting-mismatch
condition is met. Preserve that designation and its reason. A later change in one asset’s
intended sale date is not by itself a business-model change authorising reclassification (European
Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.4.1.2–5, 4.4.1, B4.1.2A–3).
