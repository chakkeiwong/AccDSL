At a later spot of 115, actual intrinsic value is 115 −100 = 15, while the hedged item’s aligned
intrinsic value is 115 −110 = 5. Suppose actual and aligned time values are both 3. The actual
option is worth 18 and the hypothetical option is worth 8. The effective intrinsic gain is 5; the
remaining actual intrinsic gain of 10 enters profit. Because initial actual time value exceeded
aligned time value, B6.5.33(a) gives a cost reserve change of 3−4 = −1 and time-value mismatch
profit of (3 −6) −(−1) = −2. Thus total reserve is 4 and profit is 8, together accounting for
the actual option’s gain of 18 −6 = 12.
The distinction can be expressed directly from the contractual price levels. Let x be the observed
price, q the common quantity, KA the actual strike and KH the hedged price level. The two
intrinsic values are
IA = q max(x −KA, 0),
IH = q max(x −KH, 0).
With zero initial intrinsic values, these are also the cumulative intrinsic gains. In the supplied
qualifying relationship, the intrinsic reserve is RI = min(IA, IH), and intrinsic mismatch profit
is IA −RI. Writing actual and aligned time values as TA, TH, with designation values TA0, TH0,
the admitted cumulative-loss cases give

Here RT is the separate cost reserve. Both time values remain between zero and their own initial
values in this profile; the second branch compares cumulative losses. It is not a signed-value
rule for every collar or opposed change. Matching initial values are admitted here only with
fully matched terms and matching subsequent component values. The intrinsic payoff identity
does not price either time value.
At expiry spot 130, actual intrinsic value is 30 and aligned intrinsic value is 20, while both time
values are zero. The intrinsic reserve is 20, the cost reserve is −4, and cumulative profit remains
10 −2 = 8. Start with cash and capital 200: pay premium 6, pay the inventory supplier 130,
and receive actual option settlement 30. Cash closes at 94. The two basis transfers give
Inventory cost = 130 −20 −(−4) = 114,
94 + 114 = 200 + 8.
The intrinsic reserve reduces the inventory cost, while the negative cost reserve adds the related
protection cost. Both transfers are separate from cash under IFRS 9.6.5.11(d)(i) and 6.5.15(b)(i)
(European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.6.5.11, 6.5.15). If actual intrinsic value 30 were copied
into the hedged-item calculation, inventory would become 104 and profit −2. Cash 94 plus
inventory 104 would still equal capital 200 less the loss 2. The book would balance while
measuring the wrong hedged risk.
For the lower-actual-time-value branch, reverse the strikes: actual strike 110, hedged price 100,
and initial actual/aligned time values 2 and 3. At spot 105, actual intrinsic is zero and aligned
intrinsic is 5. With time values 1 and 5/2, the smaller cumulative loss is 1/2; the cost reserve
and mismatch profit are each −1/2. At spot 115, time values 1/2 and 1 give cumulative losses
3/2 and 2. The cost reserve is now −3/2 and cumulative time mismatch profit is zero. The
intervening profit gain of 1/2 reverses the earlier mismatch. Applying a new lower-of comparison
only to each period would leave a reserve of −1 and miss that reversal.
