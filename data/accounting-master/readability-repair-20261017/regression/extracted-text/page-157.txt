9.5.3
Local alternatives need different company histories
The two asset books expose a constraint on combining explanations. Both fit the selected
PP&E movements and capital payments. Can they therefore be inserted interchangeably into
one otherwise unchanged history of Apple? The journals above answer no. Relative to History
1, History 2 retires an additional 1,000 of gross cost without removing additional accumulated
depreciation, so it recognises an additional 1,000 loss. It acquires an additional 1,000 of gross
cost on credit. Those changes cancel in net PP&E and leave cash unchanged, but they add
1,000 to liabilities and subtract 1,000 from profit and equity. Amounts remain millions of dollars,
under the same conditional asset-book assumptions, including exact illustrative depreciation of
8,200 and no tax effect within that subsystem.
Let Pi denote the vector of statement effects generated by asset History i, and let Ri denote
the effects of the rest of the company’s history. For this comparison, order each vector as asset
change, liability change, profit, equity change and cash change. Both full histories must explain
the same reported vector Y . If an admissible remainder exists for each branch, their necessary
relation is
P1 + R1 = Y = P2 + R2,
R2 −R1 = −(P2 −P1) = (0, −1,000, +1,000, +1,000, 0).
Thus the rest of History 2 must contribute 1,000 less liability growth and 1,000 more profit
and equity growth, with no additional asset or cash change. Holding the remainder fixed is
wrong relative to the common-statement target. The balance-sheet identity alone fails to detect
the error: adding 1,000 to liabilities and subtracting 1,000 from equity still leaves their sum
unchanged.
The reported annual totals make the required differences concrete. Apple’s asset growth is
12,397, liability growth is 17,593, profit is 93,736 and equity change is −5,196 (Apple Inc., 2024a,
printed pp. 29–32). Since History 1 contributes liability growth of 1,082, profit of −8,564 and
equity change of −8,564, its hypothetical remainder must contribute 16,511, 102,300 and 3,368
respectively. History 2 instead requires 15,511, 103,300 and 4,368. In both cases the remaining
asset change must be 19,879. These are necessary subtraction results. They do not supply the
transactions, expense classifications, taxes or liability assignments that would make a remainder
admissible.
The distinction matters for cash as well. In the isolated asset book, depreciation and the
retirement loss are noncash expenses.

to the noncash loss and 1,000 to the corresponding addback, leaving subsystem cash unchanged.
