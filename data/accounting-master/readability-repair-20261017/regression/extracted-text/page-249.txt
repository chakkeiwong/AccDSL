The filings then describe general Series A terms that may apply to securities including the
Callable Notes. In their summary, unpaid interest must continue for 30 days to become an event
of default; principal and other amounts default when due. An issuer covenant breach has a
90-day period after the specified written notice. For listed continuing payment, issuer-covenant
or guarantee events, the trustee or holders of at least 25% of the aggregate principal then
outstanding for the relevant series may, by written notice to the issuer and guarantor, declare
principal and accrued interest due. Specified continuing issuer insolvency events accelerate the
debt automatically without a declaration. Guarantor insolvency alone is excluded and does
not authorize acceleration under this description, while a payment default by the guarantor
remains a separate possibility (JPMorgan Chase & Co., 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). A history must
therefore keep the failure, notice, elapsed time, actor, declaration and changed due schedule
distinct, rather than record one group-wide default flag.
Collective remedy control also differs from an individual’s right to payment. The summaries
condition an ordinary holder remedy action on prior trustee notice, a request by holders meeting
the stated 25% threshold, 60 days of trustee inaction, indemnity and no inconsistent majority
direction. They separately protect each holder’s right to receive and sue for payment when due
against impairment without that holder’s consent. The summary permits certain amendments
without holder consent under Section 8.01. For consent-based modifications under Section 8.02,
it specifies a majority in aggregate principal amount of each affected outstanding series, whereas
listed changes to maturity, amounts, currency, payment method, redemption, payment suit or
specified holder repurchase rights, voting threshold or adverse guarantee terms require each
affected holder’s consent. These thresholds attach to different actions and voting populations,
not a generic creditor vote. The 2022 default-and-waiver subsection omits the trustee-conflict
paragraph that the 2023–2025 versions place there.
The 2022 filing discusses a potential
default, trustee conflict and possible resignation later under its trustee heading. This change in

supplements remain uninspected (JPMorgan Chase & Co., 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026).
