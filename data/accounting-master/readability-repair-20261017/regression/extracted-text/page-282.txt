Financing moves the loss component as well
B’s loss component must be allocated across claim releases, risk release and finance under
paragraphs 50–52. For this unchanged group, use the initial proportion α = 30/130 = 3/13 for
all three. If F is the period’s financing of B’s future claim value and S is its expected claims
plus risk-adjustment release, its loss component K follows
Kclose = Kopen + αF −αS.
This is a declared systematic allocation for these facts. It preserves K = α times B’s remaining-
coverage liability and reaches zero at coverage end. New estimates, coverage or risk patterns
would require their own reassessment of the allocation; the example does not prescribe this ratio
for every insurer (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 49–52 and B124).
In year one, B’s claim value accrues finance of 12. Its loss component therefore increases by 36/13,
before service releases claims 66 and risk adjustment 5. Of that release of 71, 213/13 reverses the
loss and 710/13 is insurance revenue. The closing liability is 130+12−71 = 71, comprising future
claim value 66 and risk adjustment 5. The loss component is 30 + 36/13 −213/13 = 213/13,
within that liability. Debit finance expense 12 and credit B’s liability; then debit the liability
71, credit revenue 710/13 and credit loss reversal 213/13. Separately incur and pay claims 66
through the incurred-claims liability.
R’s claim value accrues 6 and its CSM accrues 2. The asset gains finance income 8. Its CSM of
22 is then allocated across two equal current and remaining service units, releasing 11. Recovery
33, risk release 2.5 and CSM release 11 reduce remaining coverage by 46.5; the closing asset is
85 + 8 −46.5 = 46.5, comprising 33, 2.5 and 11. Its loss-recovery component tracks half B’s loss
component: finance adds 18/13, service reverses 213/26, and the closing component is 213/26.
No component is added on top of the measured asset. In this unchanged, fully covered case, the
component reversal is represented by the scheduled cash-flow and risk release; paragraph 66(bb)
must not produce a second CSM reversal for it.
The service entry debits reinsurance premium-allocation expense 46.5 −213/26 = 498/13
and loss-recovery reversal 213/26, and credits R’s remaining-coverage asset 46.5. Separately
recognise recovery income 33 and an incurred-recovery asset 33, then collect the cash. Thus R’s
service result is 33 −46.5 = −13.5, with finance income 8 shown separately. The component
determines presentation within that service result; it does not change its total. Paragraph
86 also prevents the reinsurance premium allocation from reducing issued insurance revenue
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 66–67, 80–88, B72, B119 and
B119F).
