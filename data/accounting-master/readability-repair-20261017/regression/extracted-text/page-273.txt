11.11
A transferred asset can still contain an option exposure
The guarantee example retains a possible credit loss.
A call instead retains the right to
buy an asset back; a written put obliges the bank to buy it if the counterparty exercises.
Neither name determines whether a sale has occurred for accounting purposes. The transfer
assessment first identifies the cash flows being tested and whether substantially all risks and
rewards were retained or transferred. Only the intermediate case proceeds to control and,
where control remains, continuing involvement. A transferee’s practical ability to sell the whole
asset unilaterally, without additional restrictions, matters. In particular, a call over an asset
readily obtainable in the market need not prevent derecognition. Deeply in-the-money and
out-of-the-money options can also lead to different risk-transfer findings (European Commission,
2023a, IFRS 9.3.2.2–16, B3.2.6–9, B3.2.16).
The following fair-value cases assume that this assessment requires continuing involvement and
that the underlying asset is measured through profit or loss. They use the historical IFRS 9
text retained in EU Regulation 2023/1803. Amounts are in one currency, with no tax, coupons
or transaction costs. Fair values and option time values are supplied measurement inputs.
Applying the cases to an actual transfer requires its rights, market access, applicable rules and
valuation evidence.
11.11.1
The asset less the liability represents the retained call
Suppose an asset worth 80 is transferred for 75 while the bank retains a call with exercise price
95 and value 5. The call is out of the money: exercise would currently cost more than buying the
asset. Its entire value of 5 is therefore time value. Under the admitted continuing-involvement
branch, the bank keeps an asset of 80 and recognises an associated liability of 75. Their difference
is precisely the call’s value. Opening cash 200, asset 80 and equity 280 become cash 275, asset
80, liability 75 and unchanged equity. The receipt debits cash and credits the associated liability;
it creates no sale gain in this constructed example.
Let S denote the asset’s current fair value, K the call exercise price and Tc its supplied time
value, all in the same currency. The call’s intrinsic value is max(S −K, 0). The source’s two
measurement branches can consequently be written together as
A = S,
L = min(S, K) −Tc,
A −L = max(S −K, 0) + Tc.
Here A and L are the recognised asset and associated liability. When the call moves into the
money, the liability uses the exercise price instead of the higher asset value. This preserves the
same net call exposure (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.B3.2.13(c)).
At a later measurement, take S = 100 and Tc = 4. The asset becomes 100, the liability 91
and the net exposure 9. Asset income 20 and liability expense 16 produce net profit 4. At
expiry, take S = 110 and zero time value. The asset becomes 110 and the liability 95, producing
further net profit 10 −4 = 6. Physical exercise pays 95 and removes the liability; the already
recognised asset remains. Closing cash 180 plus asset 110 equals equity 290, the original 280 plus
profit 10. Recognising a separate call of 5 at transfer would have counted the same right twice.
IFRS 9.B3.2.14 prohibits that duplication, while 3.2.22 preserves separate asset/liability and
income/expense presentation (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.3.2.19, 3.2.22, B3.2.14).
