10.12.4
A zero-cost collar can have a signed interim cost reserve
Equal option premiums can conceal a changing net time value. A purchaser may pay for a call
that limits price increases and finance it by writing a put that gives up price decreases below a
lower strike. Even when those premiums cancel, the two options can have different time values
before expiry. IFRS 9.B6.5.31 expressly includes a purchased/written option combination with
net nil time value at designation: its related time-value changes enter other comprehensive
income, although the cumulative change over the full relationship is zero (European Commission,
2023a, IFRS 9.B6.5.31–32).
Consider a highly probable purchase of ten units. A purchased call has strike 11 and a written
put has strike 9, both expiring on the purchase date. Spot is initially 10, so both intrinsic
values are zero. The call premium paid and put premium received are each 6. They are
two gross cash flows and two actual contract balances, even though their net cash effect is
zero. Assume external contracts, a qualifying combined designation that is not a net written
option, documented matching risk components, complete values and continuing qualification.
IFRS 9.6.2.5–6 permits joint designation subject to those eligibility restrictions; equal premiums
alone do not establish them (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.6.2.1–6, 6.3.7, 6.4.1).
Let q be the common quantity, L < U the lower put and upper call strikes, and S the spot
price at a measurement date. Denote the positive call asset by C and positive put liability by
P. Their supplied nonnegative time values are TC and TP . The contract payoffs give
IC = q max(S −U, 0),
C = IC + TC,
IP = q max(L −S, 0),
P = IP + TP .
The put enters the purchaser’s net position with a negative sign. In this finite measurement
profile, zero discount and the supplied matching item-risk measure make the hedged-item change
exactly opposite to IC −IP . No carry, credit or basis difference remains. The lower-of test
therefore gives the signed intrinsic reserve RI = IC −IP , with no ineffectiveness, while the fully
aligned cost reserve is RT = TC −TP . Consequently
C −P = RI + RT .
The reserves retain different origins. A zero sum cannot justify dropping either one. This use of
the matching item measure follows the declared designation; a hypothetical derivative cannot
import instrument features absent from the item (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.6.5.11,
B6.5.5).
One intermediate valuation has spot 11.7, call asset 15 and put liability 3. Call intrinsic value
is 7, leaving call time value 8; the put has no intrinsic value and time value 3. Thus RI = 7
and RT = 5. At a later valuation, spot is 8.3, the call asset is 3 and the put liability is 15.
Put intrinsic value is now 7 and its time value is 8, giving RI = −7 and RT = −5. The earlier
common-loss restriction for purchased-call time values cannot describe this signed reversal.
