Both histories satisfy the selected note totals, yet their new deferrals differ by one billion. They
are witnesses within this aggregate model, not reconstructed Apple contracts or a fit to every
financial-statement constraint. Additional contract, cohort, return and movement information
can rule out or refine them. No probability attaches to either merely because two examples
were written down.
The corresponding recognition entry debits deferred revenue and credits revenue when service is
provided. Receipt or billing of consideration that creates the deferral instead credits deferred
revenue and debits cash or, where an unconditional right to payment exists, a receivable.
Collection of that receivable changes cash and receivables without creating revenue a second
time. For example, hold History 1’s other revenue cash-settled and omit costs, taxes, refunds and
other movements solely to isolate this subsystem. Receiving its 4.1 billion of new consideration
gives revenue-side cash inflow 124.7 billion. If that new consideration is receivable at quarter
end, cash inflow is 120.6 billion and receivables increase 4.1 billion. In both cases the asset
increase is 124.7, matched by a 0.4 increase in deferred revenue and 124.3 of revenue before
omitted expenses. These conditional entries explain the identification problem; neither inflow is
an estimate of Apple’s actual cash collections.
2.11.2
A dated recognition expectation is not a cash forecast
At both September and December, Apple expects 64% of the then-reported total deferred
revenue to be realised in less than a year, 25% within one to two years, 9% within two to three
years and 2% after three years (Apple Inc., 2024a, Note 2, printed p. 36) (Apple Inc., 2025c,
Note 2, printed p. 6). These are two dated expectations about two balances. Multiplication
at the displayed December values gives 13.2 × 0.64 = 8.448 billion in the first bucket. The
corresponding September calculation gives 8.192 billion. Their difference does not measure
revenue recognised during the intervening quarter: the dates, remaining horizons and contract
populations have changed.
The percentages divide an expected amount by recognition horizon.
They do not assign
probabilities to alternative histories, fix collection dates or forecast all future sales. The 8.448
billion calculation also cannot replace the reported 8.461 billion current liability. Rounded
amount and percentage disclosures do not justify an exact equality between those figures, and
the query must retain the source’s distinct description of each quantity. A future calculation
that requires monthly service or receipts needs finer timing and payment evidence.
The note leaves another part of future service unquantified. For certain long-term arrangements,
Apple has not yet delivered the service, has no right to bill for it and attributes the unbilled
consideration entirely to that undelivered service. It reports neither revenue nor an amount
for those obligations in this passage (Apple Inc., 2024a, Note 2, printed p. 35). A query must
retain an unknown amount; setting it to zero or equating it with recorded deferred revenue
would erase the distinction the company makes.
