11.1.3
Dividend declarations and payout restrictions
For a future cash dividend, the model needs the bank’s declaration as well as the eventual
payment. The OCC’s national-bank dividend rules, retrieved for 1 January 2026, use the
declaration date to determine compliance. They define retained net income as net income
less all dividends declared in the period. Absent OCC approval under the earnings provision,
current-year declarations, including the proposal, are compared with current-year net income to
date plus the previous two years’ retained net income, less specified required transfers. The
current year is the calendar year of declaration. A cash-flow statement of dividends paid
therefore cannot supply the declaration history by itself (Office of the Comptroller of the
Currency, 2026f, 12 CFR 5.61–5.64).
The preceding two years are not always enough. Section 5.64(c)(2) provides conditional offsets
for retained losses caused by dividends exceeding a single year’s net income. Those offsets can
require the third and fourth preceding years and follow a specified order; other earnings deficits
do not qualify for that adjustment. An implementer must preserve the cause of the loss and
the allocation of offsets before computing the earnings restriction. Capital, undivided-profit
and supervisory conditions remain separate: an earnings calculation alone cannot establish a
permissible transfer (Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, 2026f, 12 CFR 5.63–5.65).
Selecting the applicable capital rule also requires more than a calendar lookup. The final
leverage rule published on 1 December 2025 is effective 1 April 2026 but permits early adoption
from 1 January (OCC, Federal Reserve Board, and FDIC, 2025, p. 55248). JPMorgan’s 2025
Form 10-K reports that the Firm elected early adoption effective 1 January 2026 (JPMorgan
Chase & Co., 2026, p. 91). The rule’s publication, general effective date, permitted election date
and the issuer’s reported election answer different questions. The later filing provides evidence
about the election; it does not establish that the election was publicly known on 1 January. A
historical information query must retain that distinction even when reconstructing an earlier
regulatory state.
The amendment removes the additional 6% supplementary-leverage condition from the affected
national banks’ well-capitalized classification and adds separate leverage-buffer payout provisions.
That classification change does not remove the distinct undercapitalization test or establish
unrestricted distributions (OCC, Federal Reserve Board, and FDIC, 2025, pp. 55287–55288). The
reported Firm election also does not supply every bank-specific application fact. A permissible
dividend calculation still needs the relevant capital definitions, distribution limits, supervisory
conditions and bank-level inputs. The US$30.1bn remains the issuer’s dated capacity disclosure,
rather than a recomputed legal limit.
One of those inputs, eligible retained income, differs from the retained net income used in the
national-bank earnings rule. Part 3 takes the greater of two amounts: Call Report net income
for the four preceding calendar quarters, net of distributions and associated tax effects not
already reflected in that income; and the average quarterly net income over those four quarters.
Its maximum payout amount for the current quarter multiplies this eligible income by the
applicable payout ratio. The relevant capital buffers use the previous quarter’s closing ratios,
so the declaration, income window and capital-measurement date must remain distinct (Office
of the Comptroller of the Currency, 2026d, 12 CFR 3.11(a)(2)–(4)).
