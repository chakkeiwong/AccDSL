Stopping hedge accounting also requires a reason. If the future cash flows remain expected, the
accumulated reserve remains until the flows occur, subject to the unrecoverable-loss rule. If
they are no longer expected, the reserve is reclassified immediately to profit or loss. No longer
highly probable is not the same as no longer expected (Australian Accounting Standards Board,
2022h, 6.5.11(d)(iii) and 6.5.12). The worked alternatives keep a gain reserve of 10 in the first
case and release it in the second. A loss reserve of 10 with an unrecoverable portion of 4 instead
releases expense 4 and leaves a loss reserve of 6. Each decision changes a named exposure and
reserve; it cannot be inferred from the derivative’s cash settlement alone.
10.11.1
Fair-value adjustments and benchmark changes
An admitted fair-value hedge of amortised-cost debt uses a different route. Both the derivative
change and the risk-attributable debt adjustment enter profit or loss; the latter changes the
debt carrying amount (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, 6.5.8). For debt initially
carried at 100, a derivative gain of 8 and debt-value increase of 10 give a derivative asset of 8,
debt of 110 and net loss of 2. No cash-flow hedge reserve is created.
Once the debt ceases receiving hedge adjustments, the retained adjustment must be amortized
using a recalculated effective interest rate (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h,
6.5.10). Suppose contractual principal of 100 and coupon of 21 are payable together one year
later. From carrying value 110, the recalculated rate is 121/110 −1 = 10%, giving effective
interest expense 11 before settlement. Cash interest remains 21 and principal remains 100; the
difference between cash interest and effective expense amortizes the 10 adjustment. Reusing
the old 21% rate would over-accrete the liability. Missing contractual dates or payments leave
that calculation unresolved.
Benchmark reform is not a general exemption from this analysis. The retained standard’s rate-
update expedient applies to necessary reform changes whose new cash-flow basis is economically
equivalent to the old basis. Additional changes then receive the ordinary modification or
derecognition analysis (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, 5.4.5–9). Comparing
one 4% coupon with 3.8% plus a 0.2% spread verifies one number, not equivalence of all future
terms. The amendment record must retain the legal change, rates, spreads, reset terms, dates
and the supporting equivalence analysis.
A qualifying change to formal hedge designation is neither automatic discontinuation nor
a new relationship. Existing cash-flow reserves follow the alternative benchmark under the
specified conditions rather than being reset to zero. Other qualifying requirements continue to
apply; additional changes and mixed benchmark groups need their own assessment (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, 6.9.1–10). The rate-specific 24-month exception for a
separately identifiable risk component likewise requires a supported expectation and ends on
the stated adverse reassessment (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, 6.9.11–13).
