Forward conditional moments do not determine backward conditional moments. Let X be an
earlier cumulative claim and Y the next cumulative claim. Suppose X = 1 or 2 with equal
probability. Given X = 1, let Y = 1 or 3, equally likely. Given X = 2, let Y = 3 or 6 with
probabilities 2/3 and 1/3. These positive claims satisfy E[Y | X] = 2X and Var(Y | X) = X.
Thus the forward factor Y /X has conditional mean 2 and variance 1/X, the form of a one-step
Mack moment relation.
Now observe Y = 3. The joint masses for X = 1 and X = 2 are 1/4 and 1/3, respectively. Their
sum is 7/12, so Bayes’ rule gives Pr(X = 1 | Y = 3) = 3/7 and Pr(X = 2 | Y = 3) = 4/7. The
backward conditional mean is 11/7, and its variance is 12/49. Dividing the observed cumulative
amount by the forward mean factor would instead give 3/2. Choosing a backward factor with
mean 1/2 and variance 1/3 would imply a variance of 3 for X, also wrong for this joint law.
The example establishes the missing implication; it does not assert that this finite distribution
satisfies every assumption of an infinite-triangle theorem.
In the published paper, Section 5.1 proposes reciprocal-factor backward moments, while Remark
5.1 and Assumption 6.2 separately require adequate backward conditional behavior. The
proposal therefore keeps such a generator as a hypothesis requiring its own justification. Given
a joint model, the backward distribution must be derived from that model or approximated
with a checked error criterion. Choosing a gamma family and matching reciprocal moments
alone cannot establish the required conditional limit properties (Steinmetz and Jentsch, 2024,
Section 5.1, Remark 5.1 and Assumption 6.2).
