A distinct, ordered credit review at that same close changes the supplied full-payment probability
to 9/10. Expected 99 has present value 90; allowance falls 20 to 10, adding an impairment
reversal 10 and leaving equity 10. Compare two histories at this point. In one, reinstatement of
the borrower’s operating licence and verified repayment capacity support a finding that the
asset is no longer credit-impaired. The improvement is objectively related to an event after
credit impairment. A significant increase in risk relative to origination nevertheless remains.
In the other history, the supplied credit assessment still finds impairment despite the same
improved cash expectation. A forecast mean alone does not decide between these findings.
The distinction changes the next year’s income presentation. For the cured asset, paragraph 5.4.2
returns subsequent interest to gross 100: debit gross loan 10 and credit interest 10. At maturity,
the expected shortfall 11 is no longer discounted, so debit impairment expense 1 and credit
allowance 1.
For the still-impaired asset, paragraph 5.4.1(b) applies the original rate to
net 90: debit gross loan 10, credit interest 9 and credit allowance 1. Both histories reach
gross 110, allowance 11 and net 99. Interest is 10 in one history and 9 in the other, with the
compensating difference in impairment expense (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h,
paragraphs 5.4.1–2).
The borrower then pays 110, an outcome admitted by both supplied laws. Debit cash 110 and
allowance 11, credit gross 110 and impairment reversal 11. Cash 210 equals deposits 180 plus
equity 30. Profit since the opening is 55, equal to 110 received less the opening net loan 55.
Equal closing wealth therefore cannot detect the wrong interest basis. Returning from lifetime
to twelve-month expected credit losses would require a separate reversal of the significant-risk
finding under paragraphs 5.5.3–12.
Ending contractual rights requires another decision. Suppose a different loan has gross 100
and allowance 20, and an enforceable agreement releases all remaining rights for an actual
payment 85. There is no retained interest or replacement instrument. Debit cash 85 and
allowance 20, credit gross 100 and gain 5: consideration 85 exceeds net carrying amount 80 by 5.
Without the release, the same payment cannot by itself establish full derecognition (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, paragraphs 3.2.3 and 3.2.12).
