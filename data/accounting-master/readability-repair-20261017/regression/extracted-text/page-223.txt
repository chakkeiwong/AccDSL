10.13.3
Who chooses settlement matters
If the employee can demand cash or shares, paragraphs 35–40 (European Commission, 2023a,
IFRS 2.35–43) treat the award as a compound instrument. Measure the debt component first,
then the equity component taking account of the cash right that must be surrendered for shares.
Do not add the standalone values of both alternatives. Over the service period, remeasure the
debt component under the cash-settled method and recognise the grant-date equity component
under the equity-settled method.
For an immediately vested award, admit debt value 100 and incremental equity value 20.
Recognise expense 120, liability 100 and equity 20. At settlement, remeasure the liability to 130
and charge expense 30. If the employee takes cash, pay 130 and retain the original 20 within
equity. If the employee takes shares, transfer the liability 130 directly to equity, leaving total
award equity 150. Both branches have cumulative expense 150; only the cash branch has a
cash outflow. The values are an admitted compound valuation, not a claim that standalone
alternative values uniquely determine the equity part.
If the entity chooses, paragraphs 41–43 first ask whether it nevertheless has a present obligation
to settle in cash. Commercially insubstantial share settlement, a past practice or stated policy of
paying cash, or normally paying cash whenever requested can create that obligation. A written
share option therefore does not by itself establish an equity award. If no cash obligation exists,
use equity-settled accounting. At settlement, cash is an equity repurchase, except that choosing
the higher-valued alternative creates expense for the excess. For an immediately vested grant
with admitted value 100, settlement cash 130 and alternative shares worth 120 require a debit
to equity of 120, additional expense 10 and cash credit 130. A 130 expense would count the
repurchase as remuneration again.
These cash choices are distinct from statutory withholding. Under paragraphs 33E–33H, an
otherwise equity award can retain equity classification when shares are withheld to satisfy a
legally imposed employee-tax withholding obligation. Preserve the legal obligation and required
quantity. The exception does not cover an arrangement without that obligation or excess shares
paid out in cash. It cannot justify classifying every net-settled award as equity.
10.13.4
A beneficial amendment adds a second service-cost clock
Cancellation accelerates an award’s remaining cost. A beneficial amendment instead normally
preserves the original grant-date cost and adds value measured at the modification date.
IFRS 2.B42–B44 requires the original minimum, subject to failure of the relevant original
non-market vesting conditions, and recognizes the beneficial change. The comparison uses the
award’s value immediately before and after modification at the same date (European Commission,
2023a, IFRS 2.27, B42–B44). A higher current value is not a replacement grant-date value.
