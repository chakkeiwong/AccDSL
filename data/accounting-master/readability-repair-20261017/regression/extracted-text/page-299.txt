When a holding change moves the insurer between 88(b) and 89(b), B135–136 requires pre-
viously accumulated OCI to be reclassified using the former approach and the assumptions
immediately before the change. Those assumptions are frozen, and comparatives are unchanged
(European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.B135–136). The new policy governs new-period finance.
Recalculating the old balance as if the new policy had always applied would erase the required
history.
Use the terminal-payment arithmetic from the preceding variable-benefit example in a separate,
supplied direct-participation case with no remaining service, CSM or nonfinancial risk adjustment.
At the holding change, payment 144 is due in two years. Its frozen current value at 10% is
14400/121, while the old systematic carrying amount is 100 with allocation rate 20%. Old
insurance OCI is therefore 100 −14400/121 = −2300/121. Qualification and actual holdings
are supplied findings; these values cannot establish either on their own.
Under the old assumptions, after one year the current-value calculation would be 1440/11 and
the systematic amount would be 120. Old OCI would be −120/11, reaching zero at payment.

To see both histories together, suppose the insurer now holds a matching underlying asset
initially measured at 14400/121, with post-acquisition cash of 200. The acquisition cash is part
of this supplied opening snapshot. In the first year, actual asset and liability both become 130.
The asset reports profit income 1440/121 and OCI expense 10/11, which together reconcile its
increase. New insurance profit finance exactly offsets that asset profit income; new insurance
OCI is income 10/11, offsetting the asset OCI. The old 980/121 reclassification remains a
separate profit expense and equal OCI income. Total equity is unchanged, although profit and
OCI each contain that old history.
If the actual first-year asset and liability instead become 132, with the same asset profit income,
asset OCI becomes income 12/11 and new insurance OCI becomes expense 12/11. The old
reclassification is still 980/121. A method that changes this old amount in response to the new
valuation has failed the frozen-assumption requirement. At terminal receipt and payment of
144, the asset, liability and both OCI schedules are exhausted; cash and equity remain 200.
The reverse direction also needs a former-policy calculation. Suppose a held underlying FVOCI
asset has fair value 121, amortised cost 100 and original effective rate 10%, with a terminal
payment 121 in two years. Under the supplied frozen zero-current-rate assumptions, its fair
value remains 121 while amortised cost grows to 110 and then 121. A matching insurance
history therefore has old OCI −21, −11, 0. After the holdings change to 88(b), the old balance
reclassifies as expenses 10 and 11. The new systematic allocation begins at 121 with rate zero.
If the actual liability becomes 130 and then returns to 121, new OCI is expense 9 and then
income 9; it does not replace the old 10 and 11 schedule. Post-change cash 200 and liability
121 give equity 79, which returns to cash and equity 79 after settlement. The actual holdings
change and asset accounting remain independently supplied.
