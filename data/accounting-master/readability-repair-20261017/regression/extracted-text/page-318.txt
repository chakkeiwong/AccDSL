The transition also changes how later growth is calculated. Ping An reports assets of 11,583,417
at the end of 2023. Subtracting its restated 2022 comparative gives a reported-balance increase
of 11, 583, 417 −11, 009, 940 = 573, 477. Subtracting the original 2022 amount instead gives
446,249, mixing the old and new bases and understating that comparable movement by 127,228
(Ping An Insurance (Group) Company of China, Ltd., 2024, p. 141). The first difference is a
movement in reported asset balances; it is not a cash-flow measure or an explanation of every
economic cause of growth.
An original-source query for assets at 31 December 2022, explicitly selecting the 2022 report,
therefore returns 11,137,168. A query selecting the 2023 report’s restated comparison for
that date returns 11,009,940. Each assertion retains its report identity, consolidated scope,
RMB-million scale, accounting basis and economic date. A link records that the later assertion
restates the comparison; it does not delete the original. A request for a comparable annual
movement must reject the mixed-basis pair unless an explicit reconciliation has first aligned
it. Because the basis and disclosure version differ, these two amounts are not a same-context
source conflict.
The information date remains a separate question. The 2023 report says its board authorised
the statements on 21 March 2024 (Ping An Insurance (Group) Company of China, Ltd., 2024,
Note I, p. 154). Neither that authorisation nor the 31 December reporting date establishes when
the exact retained document became public. The corpus has archive-date leads but no verified
publication date or historical byte vintage for either selected report. A query for information
available to investors at 31 December 2023 must therefore retain an unresolved availability
result for this source selection; it cannot silently import the later restated balance. The corpus’s
October 2026 revalidation timestamps describe local evidence handling, not investor knowledge
in 2023.
Finally, Note IV.34 reports financial-asset reclassification, including use of the classification
overlay for relevant assets derecognised during 2022. It also states that some insurance groups
used modified-retrospective or fair-value transition because retrospective application was imprac-
ticable. The headline bridge consequently cannot be reconstructed by applying one percentage
to every insurance liability. The example establishes how to retain and compare disclosed
histories; reconstructing the transition itself still requires the affected groups, asset classifications
and underlying measurement inputs (Ping An Insurance (Group) Company of China, Ltd.,
2024, Note IV.34, p. 192).
