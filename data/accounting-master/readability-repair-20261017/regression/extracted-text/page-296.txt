To isolate the nonfinancial rule, assume no passage of time, zero rates, no new contracts and
no service release. A group has fulfilment liability 80, CSM 20 and total liability 100. An
adverse future-service estimate of 15 raises fulfilment liability to 95 and reduces CSM to 5, with
no immediate profit effect. A further adverse change of 10 raises fulfilment liability to 105,
exhausts CSM and creates loss 5 and a loss component of 5. A subsequent favourable change
of 8 first reverses that loss 5; only the remaining 3 restores CSM. Closing fulfilment liability
97 plus CSM 3 is 100. Crediting all 8 to profit would overstate the reversal by 3. The loss
component is an internal allocation of the liability, never an additional liability of 5.
12.11.6
Finance presentation preserves total income and expense
For the nonparticipating case in paragraphs 87–90 (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.87–91
and B128–132), a portfolio-level policy can present insurance finance entirely in profit or
disaggregate it using the required systematic allocation. Under B129–B131, the choice and the
contract characteristics determine the method. If financial assumptions do not substantially
affect the nominal amounts paid, the systematic allocation uses the initial-recognition rates
specified by B72(e)(i). OCI is the difference between that allocation and total finance, not a
discretionary smoothing reserve. Over the group’s duration it totals zero under B130.
Consider a GMM group with a now-incurred, fixed claim of 121 payable in two years, no
remaining coverage or CSM, and zero risk adjustment because no nonfinancial uncertainty
remains in this illustrative obligation. Initial and current discount rates are 10%, so liability
is 121/1.12 = 100. The next year’s current rate falls to zero; one year remains, and current
liability becomes 121. Total first-year finance expense is 21. Under the admitted OCI policy, the
initial-rate allocation is 10 in profit and the difference 11 in OCI. In the second year, with rate
zero unchanged, current liability stays 121, so total finance is zero. The initial-rate allocation
accretes the alternative carrying amount 110 to 121, giving profit expense 11 and OCI income
11. Paying 121 extinguishes the liability. Cumulative profit finance is 21 and OCI is zero.
With opening cash 150, liability 100 and equity 50, first-year equity falls to 29 and stays 29 in
the second year because profit expense and OCI income offset. Final cash and equity are 29.
Presenting all finance in profit gives expenses 21 and zero instead, with the same total equity and
cash. The OCI choice changes presentation, not the contractual claim or current measurement.
This is the B131 fixed-payment case; variable-payment B132, VFA/underlying-item B134–B136,
PAA and actual curve/RA/process valuation retain their separate methods. A successful
fixed-claim example does not establish those models.
