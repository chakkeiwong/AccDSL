10.14
Control and an elimination book
The consolidation procedure selects the control graph at each date, aligns supported accounting
policies and presentation currencies, combines controlled entity books, and posts acquisition,
elimination and non-controlling-interest adjustments in a separate book. A control conclusion
needs evidence and a rule version. Ownership percentage alone is insufficient. Unknown purchase
allocation, currency translation or unmatched intragroup differences remain named unresolved
adjustments rather than unexplained balancing entries.
For a simplified acquisition of a different subsidiary from the goodwill case that follows, assume
control is established, all net assets are already at the relevant acquisition measurements, no tax
or goodwill arises, and non-controlling interest is measured proportionately. A parent has cash
60, investment 40 and equity 100; an 80%-owned subsidiary has cash 30, plant 20 and equity 50.
Consolidation debits subsidiary equity 50, credits investment 40 and credits non-controlling
interest 10. Group assets are cash 90 plus plant 20, and group equity is 100 attributable to the
parent plus 10 to other owners.
A subsequent intragroup loan of 10 shifts cash from parent to subsidiary, creates a receivable and
payable, and is eliminated by debiting payable and crediting receivable 10. Group totals remain
110. An unmatched loan currency or settlement date would block that exact elimination. If the
parent instead sells its entire interest for 44 immediately after the initial state and loses control,
the group removes subsidiary cash 30, plant 20 and non-controlling interest 10, recognises cash
consideration 44 and gain 4; remaining cash/equity is 104. More complex acquisitions and
retained interests require additional measurement operations; this example fixes the structure
and arithmetic without certifying a complete consolidation standard.
10.15
Perimeter and consolidation state
The acquisition, control and currency methods below use the inspected IFRS 3, IFRS 10 and
IAS 21 provisions in the retained 2023 EU text (European Commission, 2023a). The acquisition
with goodwill is a new example; it does not change the preceding zero-goodwill case.
ControlAssessment records investor/investee, relevant activities, rights and their substantive/pro-
tective character, variable returns, ability to use power, principal/agent analysis, effective interval
and evidence. IFRS 10.5–9 requires power, returns and their connection, with reassessment
on changed facts. An 80% share count is evidence to analyze, not an automatic substitute for
control. Unknown control yields separate conditional perimeters and unresolved group results;
no entity disappears merely to make reconciliation easy.
PerimeterVersion binds control intervals, ownership classes, report cutoffs, functional/pre-
sentation currency, policy-alignment adjustments and the exact source entity books. Con-
solidationAdjustment is a distinct group book with matched counterparty/event references;
legal-entity journals remain intact. The procedure is: select time-compatible entity books;
apply supported policy alignment; translate them; add like balances/movements; eliminate
investment against acquisition equity; eliminate intragroup balances, income/cost and unrealised
gains; allocate profit/OCI to owner/NCI; reconcile all statements and movement notes. IFRS
10.19–23/B86–B96 support these steps. Reporting-date differences need B92–B93’s conditions
and intervening adjustments; a generic nearest-date join is invalid.
