9.11.5
A tax benefit retains the origin of the underlying item
Acquisition-related recognition depends on information as well as dates. Under IAS 12.68
and IFRS 3.45–46, a newly recognised acquired deferred tax benefit from acquisition-date
information within the open measurement period reduces goodwill, with any excess recognised
in profit. A benefit of 20 against goodwill 5 therefore reduces goodwill by 5 and gives tax income
15. The measurement period cannot exceed one year; elapsed time alone does not establish that
the new information concerns acquisition-date facts.
Other benefits retain their independently identified origin. In particular, IAS 12.67 excludes an
acquirer’s own deferred tax benefit from acquisition accounting; it does not place every such
benefit in profit. IAS 12.61A–63 still routes tax associated with OCI or direct-equity items to
that origin. An acquirer-owned benefit of 20 with an OCI origin therefore adds DTA 20 and
OCI 20, leaving goodwill and profit unchanged. Missing origin remains unresolved.
Mixed tax schedules retain one lot per entity, authority, temporary difference, recovery-period
rate, recognition finding and origin. For a signed difference Di, positive for deductible and
negative for taxable, admitted rate ri and supported deductible recognition fraction fi, the
signed deferred amount is Dirifi; taxable lots use fi = 1. Changes go to the traced profit, OCI
or equity account. A supplied schedule can thus move gross DTA from 30 to 19 and DTL from
20 to 30 while allocating expense 5 to profit, 10 to OCI and 6 to direct equity.
Gross asset and liability amounts stay available even when presentation is net. The finite offset
route requires the same taxable entity and authority and an enforceable right to set off current
tax under IAS 12.74. It does not implement the separate cross-entity simultaneous-settlement
route. Neither a blended rate nor an assumed right of setoff can substitute for those findings.
9.12
Rule selection
The proposed selection procedure distinguishes operative authority from historical source
support. The retained sources include official AASB compilations, the original EU incorporating
regulation and official ASBE lease and income-tax texts (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2022h,e, 2023c,b; European Commission, 2023a; Ministry of Finance of the People’s
Republic of China, 2019, 2018, 2006b, 2022). Historical US lease additions are separately
identified in the retained amendment source (Financial Accounting Standards Board, 2016b).
