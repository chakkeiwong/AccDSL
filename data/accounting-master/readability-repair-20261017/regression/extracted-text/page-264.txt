This terminal-claim example supplies credit forecasts to isolate their accounting consequences.
It does not estimate their probabilities or accuracy. Intermediate payments, altered contractual
terms, fees and earlier write-offs need their own dated transitions. Positive expected recovery
also cannot justify postponing a required partial write-off. The original credit-adjusted rate and
the distinction between interest and subsequent credit changes remain the starting conditions
for those methods.
Receipts and write-offs between annual dates
The terminal claim isolates how an initial credit loss differs from a later change in that loss. A
bank also needs to know what happens when cash arrives in instalments and a portion must
be written off before the next year end. Consider a separate purchased credit-impaired asset
acquired on 1 January 2025. The price is 98 and an integral transaction cost is 2, giving initial
carrying amount 100. Contractual receipts are 66 on 1 January 2026 and 72.6 on 1 January 2027.
Initial expected receipts are 55 and 60.5. Assume the same separately established POCI and
amortised-cost classifications as before, and no modification, foreign exchange or tax. Opening
cash and capital are 300.
