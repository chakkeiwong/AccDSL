10.16
Acquisition and ownership case
Assume a business combination, no common-control transaction, parent obtaining control and
80% of ordinary residual interests for cash 96. Acquiree identifiable assets fair value 160 and
liabilities 60 give net 100. There is no prior interest, contingent consideration, tax difference,
separately unrecognized intangible, transaction cost or impairment; those absences are synthetic
facts. Select proportionate-share NCI under IFRS 3.19: NCI 20 and goodwill 96+20−100=16
under .32. In parent books debit investment 96/credit cash 96. In the consolidation overlay,
debit acquiree acquisition equity 100 and goodwill 16, credit investment 96 and NCI 20. Only
post-acquisition income enters group performance. Asset measurement exceptions, deferred
tax and a bargain gain reassessment under .36 would change the calculation; goodwill cannot
absorb an unexplained missing balance.
If post-acquisition net profit is 20, all accrued in retained earnings with no dividend/OCI, NCI
increases 4 to 24 and parent’s attributable profit is 16. Parent buys another 10% for 15 while
retaining control: transfer 12 from NCI (10% of current net 120), credit cash 15 and debit
parent equity 3. Remaining NCI 12; goodwill 16 unchanged; no new profit. IFRS 10.23/B96
makes this an equity transaction. This example has equal ordinary rights; preference rights
need their actual allocation and cannot use a simple ownership fraction.
In a separate alternative starting before that additional purchase, sell all 80% for 130
when net assets 120, goodwill 16, NCI 24 and OCI zero.
On loss of control, group
gain=130+24−(120+16)=18. Derecognize individual assets/liabilities and goodwill, remove
NCI, recognize consideration and gain.
The separate parent book’s investment gain is
not added again.
If 20% is retained and 60% sold for 100 with retained fair value 35,
group gain=100+35+24−136=23; recognize the retained investment 35.
Its subsequent
associate/financial-asset method needs separately established significant-influence/classification
facts. IFRS 10.B98–B99 also requires the appropriate prior-OCI treatment; this case assumes
none.
10.17
Intercompany inventory and tax
Assume parent’s external inventory cost 60 is sold to its controlled subsidiary for 100 on credit,
all still held at report date, tax ignored in the base case. Parent records AR 100/revenue
100 and COGS 60/inventory 60; subsidiary inventory 100/ AP 100. Consolidation debits AP
100/credits AR 100 and debits revenue 100/credits COGS 60 and inventory 40. Group inventory
60, revenue and COGS zero, and no group profit arises. When subsidiary later sells externally
for 120, group COGS is 60 and profit 60, not 20; release the previously eliminated 40 in the
continuing adjustment roll-forward. Reversing a closing overlay and recreating a new one must
not recognize the profit twice.
If the purchaser’s independently supported tax base is 100, group carrying amount 60 creates
deductible difference 40. At assumed applicable 25% and with all IAS 12 DTA conditions satisfied,
recognize group DTA 10/deferred-tax benefit 10; reverse on outside sale. The original tax
expense and cash obligations in the seller’s tax jurisdiction remain. Different rates, recoverability
or tax-law transfer rules change the answer; tax is not simply eliminated with group profit. For
an upstream subsidiary sale, attribute elimination and related tax effects to the appropriate
subsidiary/NCI performance; downstream parent’s profit elimination does not mechanically
reduce NCI by the same fraction.
