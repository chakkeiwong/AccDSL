# E3 now concerns future forecast dates for the named companies

The user selected expected forecast performance at future dates for the same
named companies. The active panel identifies Apple, Caterpillar, Henkel, HSBC,
JPMorgan, Midea, Munich Re, Ping An and SAIC Motor. Their inclusion defines the
scope of the question; it does not turn them into a random sample of issuers or
admit their accounting/perimeter mappings. The older three-sheet record remains
immutable evidence of the decision before it was made.

The substantive comparison remains whether preserving alternative histories
adds predictive information beyond one fixed interpretation under the same
economics. The primary target remains operating cash flow, evaluated against
its first release using negatively oriented CRPS when a complete predictive
law with finite first moment is admitted. The secondary cash/debt vector
retains the separate joint-law and fixed-scale requirements. Keep the full
baseline ladder, matched information and tuning opportunities. Neither the
question's scope nor this note supplies probabilities for an unweighted set.

The selected estimand has the form

    Delta_Q = E_Q[sum_i w_i d_(i,O)],

where the company identities are fixed, O is a declared future-origin rule,
d is candidate loss minus the locked comparator's loss, and Q includes the
future information/outcome process and the specified fitting policy. The
weights remain unselected. So do the origin law, horizons, regime, meaningful
improvement margin, confidence level, sample size and inference procedure.
Company names settle only one part of the old deployment_population field.
That field cannot be marked fully admitted while Q remains undefined.

Origin and target definition come before inference. Three useful candidate
tasks have materially different meanings: predicting the next complete fiscal
year using only an earlier information cutoff; predicting the remainder of a
partly elapsed fiscal year after a release; and predicting the next specified
quarter or half-year. The first preserves a full future-year outcome but may
have a long gap before its start or require a less recent information set.
The second must disclose what portion has elapsed and how cumulative flows
are reconciled. The third must respect different issuer release frequencies
and interval lengths. An annual/interim file count cannot choose among them.

For a first common task, a complete future fiscal-year operating-cash-flow
target is the most direct candidate for the existing company-wide E3 question.
Its proposed cutoff would be a named release event before that target period
starts. The latest usable release and its exact timestamp/bytes must be checked
issuer by issuer. This is a proposal for the user's substantive forecasting
use case, not a selected calendar, horizon or empirical admission.

The weighting objective also needs a substantive choice. Equal company weights
give each named company the same contribution to the expected effect after
the retained as-of asset scaling. Weighting by eligible forecast count would
favor issuers or tasks with more releases. Economic-exposure weights answer a
portfolio or business-value question and need declared exposures. The earlier
exact sign-reversal example shows that these are different objectives. Equal
company weights are a reasonable proposed first objective for this fixed-panel
design; they stay unselected until the objective is settled.

Fixing company identities changes the sampling argument. A whole panel vector
at a declared origin can be reduced to g_t=sum_i w_i d_it. Its time-series
covariance still includes every cross-company and cross-origin term. Sampling
companies anew by default changes the selected target. Treating all company-
date cells as independent ignores common shocks. Neither independent nor
perfectly correlated company losses are justified by the membership choice.
The accompanying conditional example shows this distinction without estimating
dependence from the source panel.

Reducing to g_t does not solve forecasting inference. A historical average
estimates an average of historical-origin expectations under its assumed law.
The desired future expectation is equal to that target only with a defended
stability, stationarity, regime or transport argument. An exact covariance
identity alone supplies none of those assumptions, and finite CRPS first
moments alone do not establish finite loss variance. The earlier ForeComp
reading remains an optional source, with its scalar/two-sided/zero-fill limits.
No bandwidth, block length, cluster unit or confidence transformation is selected.

The current 192 reports retain their exposure and vintage statuses. None has
admitted exact-byte historical availability in the completed reconciliation.
Some have filing dates or attributed declarations, but those are not the same
as selected-byte availability. Retrospective development can proceed only with
its own evidence and exposure labels; it cannot be relabelled untouched
prospective evaluation. New future tests require forecast/model locks before
outcome release and an explicit collection/execution plan. This direction
authorizes internal design, not an autonomous future campaign.

Keep every eligible issuer-origin cell, including failed forecasts, unresolved
mass, missing targets and absent scales. A common-success score is conditional
on that coverage. It does not identify full-service expected performance.
Failure policy, fallback and eligibility must precede outcome inspection.
Different fiscal calendars, currencies, accounting bases and perimeter changes
need explicit target records, not automatic pooling by a year label.

The prerequisite ledger separates internal work from choices of objective.
First settle target interval/cutoff and weighting purpose. Then write Q and
the refit/coverage protocol, reconcile feasible as-of inputs and outcomes, and
inspect primary inference theory matching that design. A material margin must
express a justified decision value; a default zero, five percent or confidence
level is not supplied by this user choice. Sample and resource needs follow
the intended uncertainty claim, rather than the available report count.
