The description checks the newly added decision against its exact request, result and receipt.
Previously published records and decisions cannot disappear or be overwritten. New records
must equal the publication’s dependency closure; an appended journal must equal the bound
native result’s journal. The closed world shape prevents an unfamiliar ownership field from
being silently omitted. These checks establish the stated documentary effects. Recognition,
measurement, applicable authority and actual source eligibility still require their own evidence.
Native submission encoding also matters. InsuranceComponents hashes the request body,
whereas the other three profiles hash the complete immutable request record. Group and bank
submissions bind a decoder and wire blob rather than containing a request-reference field. The
comparison preserves these bindings instead of rewriting all four submissions into an invented
common encoding.
39.7.2
Identity, replay and missing acknowledgement
Request identity is a pair within an exact profile namespace: a client or workspace identifier
and a request identifier. The retained InsuranceComponents/1 index incorrectly joins its two
allowed strings with a slash. The distinct pairs ("a/b","c") and ("a","b/c") both become a/b/c.
Both pairs satisfy the native field schema, so that index is not faithful to its declared identity.
The defect can falsely conflict distinct requests; it does not establish that a retained financial
amount is wrong or that a duplicate posting occurred.
A separate metadata successor encodes the ordered pair as a canonical JSON array and binds
the index to the exact profile. Decoding recovers both strings, so equality of the encoded
arrays implies equality of the pair. Explicit conversion retains the old request, result and
receipt references and refuses conflicting, missing or ambiguous decision history. It cannot
reconstruct a decision already erased by an old collision without independent retained evidence.
A missing lookup result therefore grants no permission to post. The execution-policy successor
in Section 39.7.3 adopts this identity repair in complete financial requests while preserving their
original bytes and the existing financial profile.
A replay describes the effects of the present invocation. It returns the original receipt and
preserves the original diagnostics, coverage and result, while creating no additional publication
or financial change. Treating the earlier receipt’s journal as a new journal would double count
the transaction. A refusal likewise changes no world and supplies no success receipt.
