## What the risk body says

The annual text connects international sales and a globally distributed supplier
network to regional and global economic conditions. It names macroeconomic
demand channels—recession, unemployment, inflation, tighter credit, higher
rates, currency movements, fiscal or monetary policy, market volatility and
changes in income or asset values. It then extends the same uncertainty to
suppliers, contract manufacturers, logistics providers, distributors, carriers,
channel partners and developers, including possible financial instability,
inability to obtain credit and insolvency. Finally it names four linked
financial channels: trade-credit and collectibility risk, derivative or
financial-institution failure, limits on new debt, reduced liquidity and
fair-value declines.

These are issuer assertions about possible mechanisms. They do not state which
product, entity, supplier, counterparty or instrument will be affected, when
the state begins, how large a response is, or which recognition rule follows.
The source also does not establish that all channels move together in every
adverse state.

## Conditional mechanism and decision status

A model may carry an assumed macroeconomic state into several branches at once:
demand can change for a named product and region; a supplier or carrier can
lose financing; a receivable can become harder to collect; a counterparty or
instrument can change in value; and management can face a different funding or
liquidity choice. Those branches share the state but need separate facts and
recognition rules. A branch that changes demand does not automatically change a
receivable, debt balance or fair value.
