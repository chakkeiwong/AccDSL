CHAPTER 12.
INSURANCE SERVICE, FINANCE AND CLAIMS
277
A one-year policy can leave claims unpaid for several years. The distinction between the period
of insurance service and the period until settlement is therefore essential even when PAA is
available. The official ASBE 25 text separates three timing tests. Article 50 permits PAA when
each contract’s coverage is no longer than one year, or when the stated no-material-difference
condition holds. Article 54 concerns a different interval: the time between each portion of
service and the related premium due date. If that interval does not exceed a year, the remaining-
coverage liability need not be adjusted for financing. Article 56 instead measures from claim
incurrence to expected payment or receipt. Its option to omit the time-value and financial-risk
adjustment requires settlement within a year of incurrence (Ministry of Finance of the People’s
Republic of China, 2020, Articles 50, 54, 56). The retained AASB 17 compilation makes the
corresponding distinctions in paragraphs 53, 56 and 59(b).
Consider a separate group of issued policies with exactly one year of coverage. All amounts
are illustrative currency units. Premium 120 is due and received when coverage starts, with no
earlier onerous trigger; group recognition is therefore at that date under Article 16. Assume
valid insurance grouping, uniform risk release, no acquisition costs, investment component,
reinsurance, tax, other expenses or other movements, and no facts indicating onerous remaining
coverage.
Elect the permitted remaining-coverage financing exception.
After half a year,
recognise revenue 60 and retain remaining coverage of 60. Articles 53 and 57 support this
roll-forward and time allocation; Article 57 requires a different allocation when risk release is
not predominantly time-based (Ministry of Finance of the People’s Republic of China, 2020).
These assumptions isolate the claim measurement from other changes in the group’s result.
At that reporting instant a claim is first incurred, with an expected payment of 60.5 exactly
two years later. This is a mean estimate, not a certain settlement. Assume an appropriate flat
annual effective discount rate of 10% and a separately estimated non-financial risk adjustment
of 5 in current measurement units. The inputs are authored for the example; applying them
to an insurer would require market and actuarial evidence. Articles 21, 23, 25 and 26 require
current probability-weighted cash flows, appropriate discounting and a separate non-financial
risk adjustment (Ministry of Finance of the People’s Republic of China, 2020). The claim’s
cash-flow present value is 60.5/1.12 = 50, so its liability is 50 + 5 = 55. The risk adjustment is
not discounted a second time. The claim has only just been incurred, so no intervening finance
accrual enters this measurement.
Record debit cash 120/credit remaining coverage 120 on premium receipt, then debit remaining
coverage 60/credit insurance revenue 60 for the service provided. Recognising the claim gives
debit insurance service expense 55/credit incurred claims 55, consistent with Article 31. The
group then has cash 120, remaining coverage 60, incurred claims 55 and a cumulative result
of 5: 120 −60 −55 = 60 −55 = 5. These are balances attributable to the group at mid-year,
not a complete insurer forecast. Using the undiscounted 60.5 + 5 = 65.5 for this two-year claim
would overstate the required liability by 10.5 and understate the result by the same amount.
The shortcut is wrong under the stated rules and assumptions. A zero rate would remove that
discounting difference while leaving the separate risk adjustment intact. Articles 86(3) and
97 also require disclosure of the distinct claim components and the PAA/financing choices
(Ministry of Finance of the People’s Republic of China, 2020).
