The discovery entry joins the books to the restatement
The system discovers the mistake in April; it does not rewrite the time at which it learned the
fact. Its correction records 1 April as the posting and knowledge date while attributing the
prior-period amount to opening equity. Debit retained earnings 15, debit DTL 5 and credit
accumulated depreciation 20. AASB 112.61A(b) and 62A(a) place the associated tax correction
directly in equity with the prior error, rather than crediting current tax expense (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2023b).
There is also a current-quarter error. Before discovery the books have already charged deprecia-
tion 2.5 and deferred tax expense 0.625 for January–March. Correct depreciation for those three
months is 5. Debit current depreciation expense 2.5 and credit accumulated depreciation 2.5;
debit DTL 0.625 and credit current deferred tax expense 0.625. That second tax entry reverses
the current quarter’s erroneous DTL increase. It has a different origin from the prior-period DTL
correction of 5. After both corrections, April PPE is 75 and DTL is zero. Depreciation 15 over
the remaining nine months leaves PPE 60; full-year current depreciation is 2.5 + 2.5 + 15 = 20,
not 40.
The comparative is a presentation of the corrected earlier periods, not an instruction to post
the correction a second time. A correction identity binds the entity, affected periods, original
close, accepted treatment and target restatement. An exact retry returns the original receipt
before testing later staleness. A changed payload under that identity is a conflict; a new request
identifier cannot post the same target correction again. Original publications remain retrievable
and explicitly identified as containing the error. These are proposed semantic compositions
of the existing time, dependency and commit rules, not new portable request encodings or
evidence of a running accounting engine.
The cash bridge provides a useful independent check. Cash after acquisition is 80; 2023 income
increases it to 130. Subsequent annual income of 50, less payment of the previous year’s tax
7.5, gives 172.5 and 215. Under the case’s operating-cash convention, corrected 2025 profit 22.5
plus noncash depreciation 20 gives 42.5. The estimate history gives the same cash increase
from profit 21 plus depreciation 22 less the deferred-tax benefit 0.5. Neither the opening-equity
correction nor the comparative restatement creates cash.
When the classification cannot yet be made
Missing original-life documentation is not proof that management made an error, and finding a
later shorter-life estimate is not proof that the old estimate was reasonable. The unresolved
case retains both questions until evidence distinguishes them. A change of measurement basis
requires the policy-change procedure. Paragraph 35’s instruction for difficulty distinguishing a
policy change from an estimate change does not resolve an error-versus-estimate ambiguity.
Nor does a missing local file establish impracticability. Paragraphs 43–45 distinguish inability to
determine period-specific effects from inability to determine the cumulative effect; paragraphs
50–53 require the relevant effort and prohibit hindsight. Such a case needs its own supported
starting date and correction route. The fully practicable example above cannot supply them.
Unknown materiality also leaves the material-restatement route unestablished; it does not
authorize ignoring a known error.
