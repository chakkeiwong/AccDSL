Contribution reductions require a different calculation. Use employer service costs, excluding
employee-funded amounts, over the shorter of expected plan life and entity life. Assumptions
must be consistent with the benefit obligation and the situation at the reporting date. In a
two-period example with zero discounting solely for illustration, future employer service costs
are 20 and 20. Without a prepayment, required future-service funding would be 12 and 25.
Their net difference is 8 −5 = 3. Suppose a prior prepayment separately reduces future required
contributions by 7. The available reduction benefit is then
7 + max{0, (20 −12) + (20 −25)} = 10.
The floor applies to the combined paragraph 20(b) amount, not separately to each period:
ignoring the second period’s excess would incorrectly produce 15. With no minimum fund-
ing requirement, use the available future employer service costs under paragraph 16 instead
(European Commission, 2023a, IFRIC 14.16–22).
The refund and contribution routes may be mutually exclusive. In that case the available benefit
is the greater of 25 and 10, not their sum 35; the recognized asset is also capped by the actual
surplus. If the refund right fails but the contribution route remains supported, the asset is 10
and the ceiling adjustment is 20. Compatible combinations require their actual feasible terms
and cannot consume the same surplus twice.
Past-service funding can require a liability before any payment. Suppose plan assets are 120
and the IAS 19 obligation is 100, but none of the surplus can ever be refunded or reduce
contributions. The recognized pension asset is zero. A separate funding-basis assessment now
requires a past-service contribution of 10 that will also be wholly unavailable after payment.
IFRIC 14.23–24 requires the resulting liability when that obligation arises. With no intervening
interest or other pension changes, recognize an OCI ceiling loss of 10 and a net pension liability
of 10. On payment, debit that liability and credit cash 10. Plan assets become 130, but the
recognized asset remains zero and payment causes no second loss or gain. Starting company
cash and equity of 100 become cash 90 and equity 90. The obligation’s recognition and the
payment are distinct events (European Commission, 2023a, IFRIC 14.23–24; IAS 19.120(c),
126–127).
