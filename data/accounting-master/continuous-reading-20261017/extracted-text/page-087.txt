6.4
The accounting state and its invariants
For one entity book with na accounts in one functional currency, let bt ∈Qna be signed account
balances, with debit amounts positive and credit amounts negative. Exact decimal amounts
can be represented as rationals with a power of ten denominator. A journal batch jt contains
the signed functional-currency amounts posted by one atomic event or explicitly grouped event
batch. Its balance condition is
1Tjt = 0,
bt = bt−+ jt.
(6.2)
If 1Tb0 = 0, substitution gives 1Tbt = 1Tbt−+ 1Tjt = 0. Induction establishes conservation
through every committed batch. Opening balances must therefore be supplied as a balanced,
explained entry or a balanced imported state with unresolved composition explicitly identified.
This proof establishes a bookkeeping invariant. It does not establish that an amount belongs
in the chosen account, that a contract exists, or that an accounting standard permits a
treatment. Posting validation additionally checks account roles, ownership, currency conversions,
applicability of rules and event preconditions.
The balance vector includes temporary revenue and expense accounts during the period. Period
closing transfers their net balance to equity through balanced entries. A reporting function can
include current profit in equity before closing, or use the closed book afterwards, but must not
include it twice. Income and cash-flow reports use classified movements and exclude mechanical
closing transfers from operating flows. Consequently statements are functions of balances,
journals, contract state and reporting rules:
Y[u,v) = R(bv−, J[u,v), Xv−, ρ, [u, v)).
(6.3)
Here ρ specifies the reporting basis and perimeter, and X contains economic state beyond ledger
balances. Cash-flow classification is part of R, not recoverable from the closing cash balance
alone.
Each book has its own functional currency. Quantities such as shares, tonnes and foreign-currency
units are typed separately and never added to this balance equation. A foreign-currency posting
stores its original units and the functional-currency valuation with its rate source. Translation
into a group’s presentation currency and consolidation adjustments occur in a separate book
under explicit rules.
