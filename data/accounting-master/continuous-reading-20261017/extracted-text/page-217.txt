The same signs work for losses. With the original prices still 8 and 9, stop five tonnes from A
at price 7. Their frozen reserve is −5. A supplied finding that 2 will not be recovered releases
−2 to profit and leaves −3 attached. If the purchase remains expected and the terminal price
is 6, continuing reserve is 5(6 −8) + 10(6 −9) = −40, the stopped instrument’s later gain is
5(6 −7) = −5, and profit is −5 −2 = −7. Purchasing twenty tonnes for 120 and attaching
reserve −43 gives inventory 163. Actual instruments settle at −20 −30 = −50, leaving cash
130 and net assets 293, equal to capital less the loss of 7. If cancellation follows the partial loss
release, only the remaining loss of 3 moves to profit. A subsequent rise in instrument value does
not restore the 2 already released from the stopped reserve.
More generally, let Q be continuing purchase quantity plus z only if the stopped purchase still
occurs. Immediately before purchase, the attached reserve is B = Rc + F. For opening cash
and capital C0,
I = QS −B,
C = C0 −QS + D,
C + I = C0 + (D −B) = C0 + P.
Thus the inventory basis I, final cash C and profit reconcile without using net settlement to
conceal financing. The finite example pays the supplier before settling each instrument in origin
order, so every intermediate cash balance must also be nonnegative. Capital of 240 exactly
funds the retained twenty-tonne purchase at 12; 239 does not, even though the later instrument
receipts would make final cash positive. This payment order is a supplied convention, rather
than a universal legal requirement.
The associated read-only method, OriginMatchedCessation/1, retains the item and instrument
identities, original dates and prices, each supplied measurement, signed releases and the final
basis transfer. It proposes balanced journals without posting them. Missing qualification
or matching facts leave the report unresolved; contradictory values are rejected. Historical
ineffectiveness, opposed active-origin gains, acquired nonzero origins, additional designations,
repeated stops and a failure of continuing loss recoverability lie outside this finite method. A
negative resulting inventory basis is also refused: the accounting for that extension has not been
established here. General heterogeneous attribution remains open. Paragraph B6.6.14 addresses
allocation across income-statement lines and does not supply the missing stopped-reserve
measurement rule.

When the hedged purchase becomes inventory
A hedge reserve does not remain in equity indefinitely once the forecast purchase takes place.
When that purchase produces inventory, IFRS 9.6.5.11(d)(i) requires the accumulated reserve to
be included directly in the inventory’s initial carrying cost. This transfer is not a reclassification
through profit or loss. The carrying cost reaches earnings later, when the goods are sold, under
IAS 2.34 (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9, paragraph 6.5.11(d)(i); IAS 2, paragraphs
10–11 and 34).
Return to the three purchase layers: 100 tonnes at designation price 80, ten at 90 and twenty
at 95. Suppose all 130 tonnes are received when the supplier price is 97. The supplier is paid
130 × 97 = 12610. At that date the accepted cumulative derivative gain is 1900, reserve gain
1810 and ineffectiveness income 90. Assume the derivative is fully settled for its carrying value,
with no fees or further valuation movement. The terms permit that settlement; no such right is
inferred merely from the numbers.
