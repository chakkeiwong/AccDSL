2.13.1
Restriction changes what can be spent
At September 2024, cash includes 2.6 billion dollars held in escrow, while marketable securities
include a further 13.2 billion. Both are restricted from general use and designated for the
State Aid tax obligation discussed earlier. Subtracting their displayed centres from the total
gives 156,650 −2,600 −13,200 = 140,850 million. MD&A separately reports unrestricted cash,
equivalents and securities of 140.8 billion (Apple Inc., 2024a, printed pp. 25–26 and Note 4).
The apparent difference does not establish conflicting disclosures: the restrictions and the
MD&A total are rounded to tenths of a billion, whereas the table is in millions. If each amount
is rounded to its nearest displayed quantum, the subtraction has possible bounds of 140,749.5
and 140,950.5 million. Those bounds overlap the 140,750 to 140,850 million rounding range
for the MD&A total. This is a conditional compatibility check, not an issuer-stated rounding
convention or a confidence interval.
The December footnote makes the distinction between maturity and restriction especially
clear: current marketable securities include 3.3 billion dollars of restricted escrow funds, and
cash includes 126 million. At the displayed centres, total resources less this disclosed escrow
are 141,368 −3,300 −126 = 137,942 million. Cash plus current securities is 53,775 million;
removing the same escrow gives 50,349 million. These are indicative subtractions, not exact
freely spendable balances. Under the same nearest-rounding hypothesis the first subtraction
ranges from 137,891 to 137,993 million. Interim MD&A supplies no numerical unrestricted total
(Apple Inc., 2025c, Note 4 and printed p. 16). A current classification therefore cannot serve as
permission to spend an asset, and excluding escrow does not establish immediate liquidation at
the reported price.
Maturity supplies a different kind of timing information. Note 1 tests the three-month cash-
equivalent maturity at purchase, while annual Note 4 classifies marketable debt as available-
for-sale and assigns current or non-current status using each instrument’s underlying maturity
(Apple Inc., 2024a, printed pp. 34 and 37). Among non-current debt excluding mortgage- and
asset-backed securities, the September maturity shares are 86%, 10% and 4% for 1–5, 5–10 and
more than 10 years. The separate mortgage- and asset-backed population has shares of 14%,
9% and 77%. At December these become 85/11/4% and 13/10/77%, respectively (Apple Inc.,
2025c, Note 4, printed p. 8). Each triple divides its own population; the two triples cannot
be added. Nor do contractual maturity bands establish a sale date, sale price or cash receipt
schedule. Management’s stated belief in sufficient resources also relies on ongoing operating
cash and continued access to debt markets. It is a dated funding assessment, rather than a
solvency guarantee derived from the table.
