The acquisition-cost row appears within future cash outflows. Its reported total of 243+25 = 268
therefore cannot simply be entered as the previous example’s asset A, which arose from costs
already paid before the related groups were recognised. Nor do the table’s acquired-contract

qualification. The selected disclosure does not identify separate prior acquisition assets, their
impairment history or their allocation to individual groups (Munich Re, 2024, printed pp.
252–253).
A third quantity appears in the segment’s service revenue. For 2023, Munich Re reports €202
million as the portion of premium relating to recovery of acquisition costs. Its policy explains
that acquisition costs enter insurance service expense in the same amount as the recovery
included in revenue, allocated over the coverage period. This is a period service amount; the
€268 million above is an estimate of future acquisition outflows for newly recognised contracts.
Subtracting one from the other would not produce a remaining acquisition asset. The relevant
populations, dates and movements must first be linked (Munich Re, 2024, printed pp. 177, 253,
264).
The segment’s liability reconciliation makes that caution concrete: it shows €637 million in the
service-expense row labelled “Recovery of acquisition costs”, and €978 million of acquisition
costs within cash flows. Thus the €202 million recovery in revenue does not describe the entire
expense row. The difference of €435 million is an arithmetic residual; assigning its cause requires
the relevant populations and expense treatments. Neither that residual nor cash less expense
identifies a separate acquisition asset (Munich Re, 2024, printed pp. 230–231).
The same timing distinction applies to profit. The report gives €819 million of CSM for services
provided during 2023 in this segment. That amount concerns service during the year, whereas
€154 million is the margin attached to the newly recognised contracts in the selected table.
It is not a release from that new-business amount alone. A reconciliation needs the opening
CSM and every intervening movement. The segment’s total insurance revenue also combines
measurement approaches: €7,966 million under GMM/VFA and €1,976 million under PAA
make the reported €9,942 million (Munich Re, 2024, printed pp. 253, 264).
Even within one insurer, an acquisition-cost policy needs its applicability conditions. Munich
Re reports using the PAA option to expense acquisition costs directly for a substantial part
of its property-casualty primary insurance business. The earlier general-measurement asset
example therefore requires the relevant treatment and group facts before it can be applied to
a real portfolio (Munich Re, 2024, printed p. 166). These are readings of the retained 2023
English translation, whose German original is binding. They identify reported aggregates and
necessary further facts; the underlying group histories and valuations remain unresolved.
