When the acquisition asset enters the insurance group
Continue from the preceding reversal: cash is 40, the two acquisition assets are 35 and 15,
and equity is 90. The next event is recognition of the related insurance groups. Assume that
coverage begins and the first premiums become due at this date, with no earlier recognition
trigger. All contemplated contracts are admitted together to their correctly identified groups.
No service has yet elapsed and no claims have been incurred. Paragraphs 25–28 determine
recognition; the later cash receipt is a separate event (Australian Accounting Standards Board,
2022f, paragraphs 25–28).
Keep the measured group net inflows of 45 and 30 from the previous example. To expose their
components, suppose premiums of 100 and 60 are due immediately, the present values of future
claims and expenses are 50 and 25, and each group has a nonfinancial risk adjustment of 5. Thus
fulfilment cash flows before premium receipt are 50 + 5 −100 = −45 and 25 + 5 −60 = −30,
using positive amounts for net outflows. These are supplied conditional measurements; present
values do not imply a zero discount rate. Previously paid acquisition costs are not another
future payment. Assume general measurement and no other assets, liabilities, taxes, reinsurance
or intervening measurement changes.
Paragraph 38 requires initial CSM to prevent income or expense from the FCF, any initial cash
flows and the derecognition of the acquisition asset, subject to its stated exceptions. The paid
cost therefore affects initial CSM even though it is no longer a future cash flow. Let A denote
the acquisition asset’s remaining carrying amount, F the FCF and C the CSM. Removing the
old asset without a gain or loss requires the new group balance to replace it. With positive L
denoting a liability, the relation in this case is
L = F + C = −A,
C = −F −A.
G1 consequently has CSM 45 −35 = 10 and a net group asset of 35; G2 has CSM 30 −15 = 15
and a net group asset of 15. Both margins are positive, so the onerous-group exception does not
arise. There are no other previously recognised cash-flow assets to which paragraph B123A’s
revenue-and-expense exception would apply (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f,
paragraphs 28C, 32, 38, 47 and B123A).
An illustrative recognition entry debits the two group assets by 35 and 15 and credits the
corresponding acquisition assets by those amounts. Cash is still 40 and equity is still 90. The
separate acquisition balances are now zero. The amounts transferred are the carrying values
after impairment and reversal, rather than the original allocations of 40 and 20. The earlier
loss history remains available even though those assets have entered the group measurements
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 28C–28D and B35C).
Now receive the premiums in full, immediately after that measurement. Debit cash 160 and
credit the group carrying balances by 100 and 60. Because the premiums are no longer future
inflows, FCF becomes 55 for G1 and 30 for G2. CSM stays 10 and 15: no service or time has
passed. The groups therefore carry liabilities of 55 + 10 = 65 and 30 + 15 = 45. Cash has
increased to 200, and 200 −65 −45 = 90 confirms unchanged equity. Paragraph 83 ties revenue
to service, while B123(a)(i) expressly distinguishes premium cash inflows from the liability
reductions that generate revenue. Later acquisition-recovery revenue and the equal service
expense are allocated over time under B125; neither is created by this instantaneous receipt
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 40–41, 83–85 and B123–B125).
