A net hedge still needs its gross transactions
A company expecting dollar sales and dollar expenses may manage only their net currency
exposure. Hedge accounting still needs both gross amounts and the periods in which they affect
earnings. IFRS 9.6.6.1 and B6.6.1–8 require individually eligible items and actual group risk
management. For offsetting cash-flow positions, the designated risk must be foreign currency risk,
and the designation must specify nature, volume and the relevant reporting periods. An abstract
label such as “net dollars 20” is insufficient (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.6.6.1–3,
B6.6.1–8).
Use a euro-functional company with highly probable external cash sales of USD 100 and
payments of USD 80 for services consumed immediately. The designation identifies those
transactions, their earnings periods and a forward to sell USD 20 for EUR 20. Assume actual
net risk management, an eligible external derivative, contemporaneous documentation and all
effectiveness conditions. Interest, discount, forward points and credit effects are zero in this
illustration; the whole forward is designated. At inception the rate is EUR 1 per dollar and
the derivative is worth zero. At execution the rate is EUR 1.10, so sales are EUR 110, service
expense EUR 88 and the forward loses EUR 2. No inventory asset is created in this example.
The effectiveness comparison retains the gross changes: the expense’s currency effect of minus 8
and the forward loss of 2 offset the sales effect of plus 10. IFRS 9.B6.6.5 and B6.6.9 require
the offsetting item to enter that comparison alongside the instrument, without recognizing a
forecast transaction before it occurs. When both transactions affect the same period, recognize
the effective forward loss of 2 in OCI and subsequently release it as a separate hedge expense of
2. Revenue remains 110 and service expense 88; profit is 110 −88 −2 = 20. The hedge result
is presented separately because the offsetting risks affect different statement lines (European
Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.6.5.11, 6.6.4, B6.6.5, B6.6.9, B6.6.15). Showing revenue 108 would
preserve profit but misstate the required presentation. Opening cash and equity 200 become
220 after gross operating receipts 110, service payments 88 and forward settlement 2. The
reserve and derivative liability both close at zero. The designated forward follows the operating
classification of the hedged flows (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 7.16).
Move only the service expense into the next period, retaining the same EUR 1.10 rate and
a forward that settles with that expense. The designation already identifies these different
earnings periods. At the first close the company has cash revenue 110 and a forward liability
of 2. The revenue line must still use its transaction-date spot rate. To reflect the net hedge’s
timing, recognize a separate hedge expense of 10 when the sale occurs, with the corresponding
credit to the cash-flow hedge reserve. Together with the forward’s OCI loss of 2, the reserve
is a credit of 8. First-period profit is 100 and OCI is 8; cash 310 less the derivative liability 2
