At each observation post only ∆Dt, −∆Rt and −∆It to derivative, reserve and gains, using
debit-positive signs. Their sum is zero because ∆Dt = ∆Rt + ∆It. Adding a layer at its current
price gives that layer an initial change of zero, so it creates no immediate journal and does not
reset earlier amounts. The same argument applies at each subsequent addition.
For example, add another 20 tonnes at 95 after the preceding measurement. At price 97 the
cumulative item loss is 100(97−80)+10(97−90)+20(97−95) = 1810. A cumulative derivative
gain of 1900 then gives reserve 1810 and profit 90. Later price and derivative reversals are
evaluated from these same origins. They can reverse previously recognized ineffectiveness rather
than merely add another positive amount. Fewer intermediate observations leave the final
cumulative amounts unchanged when the designation history and final valuations are the same.
The documentary RepeatedItemRebalancing/1 report applies this add-only item method to a
finite dated event list. Each addition requires an immediately preceding same-date measurement,
a new layer identifier, the current designation price and updated eligibility/documentation
findings. Missing findings are unresolved; different risk or valuation methods and false eligibility
findings are unsupported. Changes in units, dates or layer origins reject the whole proposed
report without posting. The offset arithmetic does not itself establish an admissible hedge
ratio. Removed item volume, changed instrument volume and the reserve’s treatment when the
purchase occurs require the separate rules described above.
10.12.8
An added derivative has its own starting value
Increasing the instrument volume introduces a second measurement origin. IFRS 9.B6.5.19
retains the existing instrument’s history and measures the added instrument’s change from the
date of rebalancing. If the company pays for an instrument with a positive fair value, that
payment establishes an asset under IFRS 9.5.1.1. Only changes after designation contribute to
its hedge gain (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9, paragraphs 5.1.1 and B6.5.19).
Return to the original 100-unit exposure designated at price 80, measured at price 90 with
derivative gain 1200, reserve 1000 and cumulative profit 200. Here the item volume stays at 100.
Suppose changed circumstances require adding an eligible external derivative A, acquired and
designated together for its fair value of 200. The risk-management objective is unchanged, the
revised ratio is eligible and the documentation is updated. Debit derivative A 200 and credit
cash 200. Total derivative carrying value is now 1400, but the cumulative hedge gain remains
1200: the company acquired the additional 200 of value with cash. Reserve and profit remain
1000 and 200.
At price 95, suppose the original instrument is worth 1400 and A is worth 220. The original
instrument has gained 1400 since its zero-value designation; A has gained 220 −200 = 20.
The combined gain is therefore 1420, while total carrying value is 1620. The item loss is
100(95 −80) = 1500, giving reserve 1420 and cumulative profit zero. Since the preceding
