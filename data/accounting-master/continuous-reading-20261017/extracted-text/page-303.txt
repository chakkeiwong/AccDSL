Every draw records its model, source version, data vintage, residual pool, fitted parameters,
process law and random stream. Nonpositive degrees of freedom, an unsupported negative mean,
a zero projection denominator or non-finite arithmetic receives an explicit failure. Structurally
absent triangle cells have a separate mask. A failed payment is not converted to zero or silently
replaced by another draw. In the authored example, if the M = 8 case cannot be evaluated, its
probability 1/2 remains unresolved: the known contribution to the full mean is 1

complete mean is withheld. In a finite bootstrap, attempted failure frequency is reported as
such; it does not identify population probability.
The retained source itself needs a qualification. Its negative-mean suggestion draws X with mean
|m| and then subtracts 2m. Read literally for m < 0, the resulting mean is |m| −2m = −3m,
not m; at m = −4 it is 12. That prescription is wrong relative to its stated mean target in
the inspected working version. A translation or reflection can preserve a negative mean, but
changes the support and requires a different variance model. AccDSL leaves that extension
unsupported pending an explicit signed-claims model; this local finding does not reject the
positive-mean two-stage procedure (England, 2001, Section 3, final paragraph).
Software names also conceal consequential choices. In the inspected ChainLadder 0.2.17 source
snapshot, od.pois calls a negative-binomial generator for dispersion greater than one. The
paper’s scaled Poisson and that negative binomial can share a mean and variance while assigning
different probabilities to possible payments. The same snapshot uses signed gamma draws and
replaces missing process values by zero. An adapter must distinguish structural absence from
failed computation, identify the exact law and its parameter domain, and preserve separate
resampling and process streams before its output can satisfy this proposal. These are source-code
observations, not completed runtime validation (Gesmann and contributors, 2023).
The paper compares 1,000 simulations with analytic and earlier bootstrap summaries. That
provides a worked implementation comparison under its assumptions. It does not establish
future tail coverage, select an insurer’s risk adjustment or justify 1,000 draws as a universal
budget. A later empirical protocol must address calendar effects, tail development, pooling,
heldout diagonals, simulation uncertainty and consequences for measured insurance groups
(England, 2001, Section 4, Tables 1–3 and Figure 1).
12.15.3
The measurement date changes the question
A lifetime payment distribution differs from a one-year change in estimated liabilities. The 2019
accepted manuscript by England, Verrall and Wüthrich defines the claims development result
as opening reserves less payments during the year and less the revised closing reserve. The
closing estimate uses the information that has emerged during that year (England et al., 2019,
equations 5–7 and Section 3.2). If the opening reserve is 100, payments are 30 and the revised
closing reserve is 80, the result is 100 −30 −80 = −10. Subtracting cash alone would yield 70
and answer a different question. The scenario must carry both realized next-year payments and
the prescribed re-estimation rule, with the correct information cutoff. A lifetime draw cannot
be relabeled as a one-year result. Neither statistic by itself supplies the discounting, group
allocation or risk compensation required for insurance measurement.
