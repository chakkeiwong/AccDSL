9.7.3
Uncertain tax positions do not supply a payment distribution
The last part of Note 7 preserves another population: gross unrecognised tax benefits. Its 2024
movement is 19,454 + 1,727 −386 + 2,542 −1,070 −229 = 22,038 million. The increases concern
prior- and current-year positions; the decreases concern changed prior-year positions, settlements
and expired limitation periods. Interest and penalties are excluded from this rollforward (Apple
Inc., 2024a, Note 7, printed p. 41). The 1,070 settlement-related decrease is a movement in that
gross balance, not an independently identified total of cash paid to tax authorities.
Of the rounded 22.0 billion gross amount, the issuer says that 10.8 billion, if recognised, would
affect its effective tax rate. It also describes a reasonably possible decrease of approximately
5–13 billion over the next twelve months, primarily concerning transfer pricing and deemed
repatriation tax, while noting uncertainty about examination timing. These are different con-
ditional statements. The range is neither a probability law nor a promised cash payment or
expense release. A forecast still needs jurisdictional outcomes, recognition, interest, penalties
and settlement dates. Retaining those unknowns keeps the tax note connected to the com-
pany’s funding needs without turning management’s dated assessment into an invented future
transaction.
9.8
Returning to the company forecast
The Northstar case remains the broader ordinary-company teaching baseline: collections,
purchases/payments, inventory, payroll, PPE, financing, tax, statements, annual/interim and re-
vision views are already specified. Its 2024 collection/purchase/payment solution is 245/155/145.
The 2025 forecast in the Northstar reference fixture opens with cash 74.6 and equity 90.6,
spends 20 on capital expenditure at period end, pays dividend 8 and uses a base floating rate
7%. Its simplified tax is settled in the year earned. The running case uses fixed-rate share α;
here floating-rate share x = 1 −α describes the same uncertainty. For 2025 the same unknown
floating fraction x ∈[0, 1] determines every statement amount:
I(x) = 5 + 2x,
pretax profit(x) = 82 −2x,
tax(x) = 16.4 −0.4x,
profit(x) = 65.6 −1.6x,
operating cash(x) = 48.6 −1.6x,
closing cash(x) = 95.2 −1.6x,
equity(x) = 148.2 −1.6x,
assets(x) = 288.2 −1.6x.
Receivables 55, inventory 40, net PPE 98, payables 40 and debt 100 complete the balance
sheet. Liabilities are 140 for every x, so the common cash/equity slope preserves the accounting
identity. There is no default distribution on x. At a 9% floating rate the all-floating history has
interest 9, profit 62.4, cash 92, equity 145 and assets 285. The annual cash bounds still cannot
establish daily payment solvency without event-date cash flows.
The lease/tax example extends the interaction evidence without changing the original Northstar
fixture or pretending that its teaching 20% tax formula is an IAS 12 method. In a real-company
package, each required method is admitted separately and unsupported operations remain
visible. The extended obligations for pensions, impairment, provisions, valuation and share-
based payment are in the additional corporate methods that follow; omission from a finite
company case never proves the actual company has none.
