The current charge is therefore 32,780 and the deferred benefit 3,031. The tax payable has its
own scope. Note 6 reports current income taxes payable of 26,601 and noncurrent income taxes
payable of 9,254 at 28 September 2024, against 8,819 and 15,457 a year earlier. Combining
both maturity classes gives 35,855 against 24,276, an increase of 11,579 (Apple Inc., 2024a,
Note 6, printed p. 39). Current expense less net cash taxes is only 32,780 −26,102 = 6,678.
Reclassifying an amount between current and noncurrent cannot explain the difference in their
combined total.
To expose the missing movements, let T denote that combined tax payable and N the net
deferred tax asset reported in Note 7. Let C be current tax expense, D deferred tax expense
(negative for a benefit), and P net cash taxes paid, all for the same annual period and in millions
of dollars. Define U and V as the residuals needed to connect the reported amounts:
∆T = C −P + U,
∆N = −D + V.
(9.2)
These are diagnostic definitions, not assertions that every tax balance has the same recognition
or settlement scope. The first relation gives U = 11,579 −6,678 = 4,901. Setting that residual
to zero would produce the wrong closing payable. Assigning it to currency translation, uncertain
tax positions or another journal would require the missing transaction and classification evidence.
The deferred balance illustrates the second relation. At September 2024, gross deferred tax
assets of 34,873 less a valuation allowance of 8,866 leave 26,007. Deducting deferred tax liabilities
of 6,805 gives net deferred tax assets of 19,202, compared with 17,251 a year earlier (Apple
Inc., 2024a, Note 7, printed p. 41). The increase is 1,951, so V = 1,951 −3,031 = −1,080. The
deferred benefit in profit does not explain the whole net-balance movement. Its allocation
among currency, equity, other comprehensive income and other causes remains unresolved here.
Note 6’s 19,499 deferred-tax-asset presentation is also different from Note 7’s 19,202 net position.
Their difference of 297 is an arithmetic consequence, not a separately disclosed liability line
or authority to offset jurisdictions. The reported temporary differences—including research
expenditure, deferred revenue, leases and depreciation—need their own tax bases and recovery
conditions. The note reports indefinitely available Irish foreign-tax and California R&D credit
carryforwards, but also a valuation allowance for those credits and some other differences. An
indefinite term does not establish that an asset is recoverable (Apple Inc., 2024a, Notes 6–7,
printed pp. 39–41).
