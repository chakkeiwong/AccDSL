10.1.2
Net borrowing can conceal refinancing
Commercial paper exposes a second limitation of aggregate financing data. Apple describes
unsecured short-term promissory notes used for general corporate purposes, including dividends
and repurchases. The annual note says their maturities are generally less than nine months;
that description does not identify each note or trace individual borrowed dollars to a use. At
the annual statement amounts, commercial paper increases from 5,985 to 9,967 million, a rise of
3,982, while net cash proceeds are 3,960. The resulting 22 million difference remains unallocated.
During the following quarter the balance falls from 9,967 to 1,995, while net cash repayments
are 7,944; the corresponding unallocated difference is −28 million (Apple Inc., 2024a, Note 9
and balance and cash-flow statements) (Apple Inc., 2025c, Note 6 and balance and cash-flow
statements).
Even a complete explanation of those differences would leave refinancing activity unresolved.
Annual Note 9 reports net cash for paper with maturities of at most 90 days, but separate
proceeds and repayments for longer maturities. In fiscal 2022, for example, the longer-maturity
proceeds of 5,948 and repayments of 7,257 give net repayments of 1,309 million. Adding
short-maturity net proceeds of 5,264 gives total net proceeds of 3,955. For fiscal 2024 the
longer-maturity cash rows are zero and the short-maturity net inflow is 3,960. Neither that
net amount nor a closing balance reveals how many issues and redemptions occurred between
reporting dates.
Consider a hypothetical incremental borrowing of 1,000 million US dollars, issued at par and
repaid after 60 days. Stipulate simple interest of 5% using actual days over a 360-day year,
with no fees, taxes, hedges or early settlement. The rate is an assumption of this example;
Apple’s disclosed 5.00% weighted rate at September 2024 does not establish the rate on such a
transaction. Issuing and repaying the principal adds 1,000 −1,000 = 0 to net principal cash
flow and leaves zero principal outstanding, yet supplies 1,000 million between those dates. The

This example isolates what a net principal-flow observation fails to reveal. It is not a recon-
structed Apple history: interest expense, interest cash and other disclosures could distinguish or
exclude the added borrowing. Within that scope, it shows why a financing model needs dated
issuance, accrual and redemption events. Matching a net financing row does not establish either
intraperiod liquidity or funding cost.
10.1.3
A rate scenario needs an exposure definition
The contract’s coupon is also insufficient to describe exposure after hedging. Apple’s annual
debt note states that interest-rate swaps effectively convert some fixed-rate US-dollar notes to
floating rates, while cross-currency swaps effectively convert some foreign-currency notes to
US-dollar notes. The effective-rate ranges include interest, discount or premium amortisation
and applicable hedge adjustments. These are relationships between separate contracts and
measurements, not a single rate that can be applied to total principal (Apple Inc., 2024a, Note
9, printed p. 43).
