The year is the same; the report is different
An analyst asking for 2024 profit can legitimately encounter both 30 and 22.5. The first is what
the company originally published; the second is the corrected comparative in its later report.
The useful-life error explains the difference, but does not decide which number belongs in an
analysis made at a particular date. Each answer needs the identity of the report and the time
at which that report was available.
Keep the same synthetic asset, income and tax assumptions. To make the information dates
explicit, suppose the original 2024 report was authorized, published and entered into the system
on 31 March 2025. Management discovers the error on 1 April and accepts its correction on
2 April. The 2025 annual report is authorized on 31 March 2026, published on 2 April and
entered into the system on 3 April. These publication and ingestion dates are authored for the
example. They are not observations about a real issuer.
A latest-available query imposes two cutoffs. The public cutoff limits the reports an outside
analyst could obtain; the system cutoff limits the reports actually recorded in the system. With
a public cutoff of 1 April 2026, the answer for 2024 still comes from the original report, even if
the system is queried much later. With a public cutoff of 2 April and a system cutoff of 2 April,
the new report is public but has not yet entered the system, so the same original report remains
the answer. Once both cutoffs reach its respective dates, the 2025 edition supplies corrected
2024 profit of 22.5. An explicit request for the original edition can still retrieve 30 afterward.
Discovery of the error does not backdate publication of its correction.
Now reconstruct the positions shown in that later report. For this separate historical source
comparison, IAS 8.42–49 and IAS 1.40A–D in the retained EU 2023/1803 text support the
particular retrospective steps described here (European Commission, 2023a). The corresponding
IAS 12, IAS 16 and IAS 33 provisions were checked for this example’s tax, depreciation and
private-entity EPS assumptions. That comparison does not establish equivalence of entire
standards or the law applicable to a later actual filing.
