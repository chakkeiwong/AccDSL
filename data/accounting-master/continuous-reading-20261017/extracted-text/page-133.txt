The component record carries asset identity, inspection cohort, original cost allocation and
evidence, availability date, residual value, consumption pattern, useful life until the next
inspection, accumulated depreciation/impairment and the replacement event. Allocate the initial
asset’s existing total cost among its components; do not increase that total while recognizing
the old inspection. Advance depreciation to the replacement date, remove the old carrying
amount, add eligible new cost and begin the new component’s depreciation when available.
IAS 16.43–55 requires separate significant-component depreciation and retains depreciation
during idle periods unless the applicable consumption method gives zero. Ordinary servicing is
expense under IAS 16.12, not a new inspection asset. Other production-cost rules may place
depreciation in inventory; these examples assume a non-production use, so charges enter profit.
Suppose original equipment cost 120 includes an inspection component 20. The remaining
physical part costs 100, has ten-year straight-line life and zero residual; the inspection has
four-year life and zero residual. After two full years the physical part carries 80 and the old
inspection 10. A qualifying inspection performed early costs 24 and is available immediately for
four years. Derecognize the old 10 as loss, capitalize 24, and retain physical carrying amount 80.
The equipment now carries 104. One subsequent full year charges 10 physical and 6 inspection
depreciation, leaving 88. Expensing all 24, retaining both inspection cohorts, or depreciating
104 uniformly over the physical life would compute different targets.
With opening cash 100 and equipment 90, equity 190, replacement leaves cash 76, equipment 104
and equity 180. Subsequent depreciation 16 leaves equity 164 and equipment 88. An otherwise
separate routine service costing 3 reduces cash and profit by 3 and leaves equipment unchanged.
An unavailable old-cost allocation does not justify writing off zero: reconstruct a supported
component estimate or retain the unresolved derecognition amount. Revaluation/impairment
histories must first update the component carrying amounts under their own methods.
Reducing a lease and paying usage-dependent rent
IFRS 16.44 (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 16.38–46) first tests whether an added right
and commensurate consideration make a separate lease. A scope reduction cannot meet that
increase-in-scope test. For a modification that is not separate, paragraph 45 requires revised
contract allocation, term and discount rate at the effective date. Paragraph 46(a) requires
right-of-use derecognition and the gain/loss for a partial or full termination; the remaining
liability remeasurement adjusts the surviving right-of-use asset. A mere decrease in price with
unchanged scope belongs to paragraph 46(b), not automatic derecognition.
Represent the terminated rights explicitly, including the physical/use/time units and their
carrying-cost allocation. In a homogeneous space lease with an unchanged remaining term and
proportional rights, let fraction q of the rights be surrendered. The illustrative proportional
allocation removes qA of the right-of-use carrying amount A and qL of the pre-modification
liability L; the termination gain is q(L −A). This allocation requires the homogeneous-rights
facts. Do not use the payment reduction as q or apply floor-area proportions to heterogeneous
equipment or a pure term change without a supported allocation.
