12.3
Group state and general measurement
The following cases apply selected general-measurement and service-allocation provisions from
the retained AASB 17 compilation (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f). They
extend the premium-allocation example without presuming actuarial calibration or a complete
insurer model.
The general method distinguishes fulfilment cash flows (FCF), including an explicit risk ad-
justment (RA), from the contractual service margin (CSM), which holds unearned profit. The
liability for remaining coverage (LRC) concerns future service; the liability for incurred claims
(LIC) concerns service and insured events already experienced. Keeping these components
separate makes a change in future estimates different from an unpaid claim.
Contract records retain insured risks, premiums/benefits, service and settlement dates, op-
tions/guarantees, cancellation/repricing rights and acquisition costs. Group admission evaluates
portfolio/similar-risk management, onerous status and required issuance cohorts under AASB
17.14–24, recognition under 25–28 and boundary under 34. Cash outside the boundary be-
longs to future contracts, not this group’s profit. GroupState retains expected dated cash
flows, current discount curves, inception rates, explicit risk adjustment, CSM, loss component,
LRC/LIC, coverage units, service experience and assumption vintages. Same premium totals
are not sufficient state for merging different guarantees or claim tails.
General measurement follows 32–38: fulfilment cash flows are probability-weighted future net
outflows, adjusted for timing/financial risk without double counting, plus explicit non-financial
risk adjustment. CSM removes initial unearned gain under its conditions; an initial net loss is
recognized under 47, not negative CSM. Paragraphs 40–44 separate remaining coverage and
incurred claims. For a nonparticipating group, accrete CSM at the specified locked-in rate,
apply future service changes at their required rate to CSM up to its available amount, record
any excess loss and allocate current service over current plus future coverage units under B119.
B96–B97 distinguish future service from incurred-claim changes, financial changes and current
experience; not every revision is a CSM adjustment.
A MeasurementInput must resolve cash-flow/law, discount, risk-adjustment method, service
grouping and coverage-unit evidence. Missing probabilities or RA support permit conditional sen-
sitivity cases but block a source-supported measurement. Risk adjustment is the compensation
the entity requires under 37; a standard deviation, confidence quantile or arbitrary percentage
is only a candidate method until justified for that entity. Disclosures and the confidence-level
equivalent, where applicable, are separate obligations. Market-consistent financial inputs and
entity risk compensation must not be conflated.
12.4
Complete two-year general-measurement case
Take a two-year nonparticipating group with one premium 120 received at inception, claims
expected/actually incurred and paid 40 each year, zero discount rate, RA 10 initially released 5
with each equal service year, no acquisition costs, investment component, lapses, taxes, finance
changes or reinsurance. Coverage units are 1 in each year because equal benefits and coverage
are assumed. Before premium payment, expected net cash outflow is 80−120=−40, RA 10,
FCF −30 and CSM 30, with net initial carrying amount zero. After the premium debit cash
120/ credit LRC 120. Its components are remaining expected claims 80, RA 10 and CSM 30.
Each year releases expected service claims 40, RA 5 and CSM 15: debit LRC 60/ credit
insurance revenue 60. Incur claims 40: debit service expense 40/credit LIC 40; payment debits
LIC 40/credits cash 40. End of year one: cash 80, LRC 60 (claims 40, RA 5, CSM 15), LIC 0
