2.12
Apple: commitments and the cash needed to fulfil them
The same distinction between recognition and cash arises when Apple buys capacity and
components. A lease liability measures one part of an existing obligation; a purchase commitment
describes goods or services still to be acquired. A funding projection needs the payments arising
from both, together with new orders and operating decisions. Adding every disclosed amount
would mix measurements and could count the same requirement twice.

Thus 14,790−2,360 = 12,430 reconciles the maturity and liability measurements. The recognised
liability is already included in the balance sheet; it is not an extra cash requirement to add
to 14,790. Its current components total 1,488 + 144 = 1,632 million, whereas the fiscal-2025
maturity bucket is 1,991 million. A current carrying amount and a gross payment bucket answer
different questions. The right-of-use asset likewise need not equal the remaining liability; these
reported balances do not disclose the complete amortisation, prepayment and modification
history.
Apple also reports 849 million of fixed payments under additional leases that had not commenced.
It expects them to commence in 2025–2026, with terms from less than one year to twenty-one
years. Adding this separately disclosed amount to the maturity total gives 14,790+849 = 15,639
million. That sum is consistent, under a nearest-0.1-billion rounding assumption, with the 15.6
billion of fixed lease obligations in management’s cash-requirements discussion (Apple Inc.,
2024a, printed pp. 25 and 42). Numerical compatibility does not establish a contract-by-contract
reconciliation. It neither adds 849 to the recognised liability nor identifies its commencement
and payment dates.
The remaining lease information cannot supply those dates mechanically. The note describes
original terms typically no longer than ten years, with renewal options, some reasonably certain
to be exercised. Its weighted-average remaining term of 10.3 years describes a different quantity.
