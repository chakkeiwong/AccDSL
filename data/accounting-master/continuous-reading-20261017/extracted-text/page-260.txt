For a direct payment outside the bank, debit gross loan 100 and credit cash 100. In the same
draw event, debit the existing provision 2, debit additional impairment expense 2 and credit the
loan allowance 4. The first 2 moves an existing loss estimate from a liability into a deduction
from the funded asset; the other 2 reflects the information that funding has occurred. AASB
7.B8E distinguishes commitment provisions from financial-asset allowances, while 35H–35I
and B8D require an explanation of their movements (Australian Accounting Standards Board,
2022g). Funding and its impairment transition are committed together under the existing event
procedure, even though the entries are explained separately here.
There is no remaining undrawn capacity. Cash 100 plus gross loan 100 less allowance 4 gives
net assets 196, equal to deposits 180 plus equity 16.

The route through the borrower’s bank account has a different intermediate state. Debit gross
loan 100 and credit the borrower’s deposit 100, with the same provision-to-allowance transition.
Cash stays 200, deposits become 280, and equity is 16 after allowance 4. If the borrower then
transfers 100 to another bank, debit deposits and credit settlement cash by 100. The final state
matches direct disbursement. Creating the deposit did not itself consume settlement funds; the
later transfer did.
