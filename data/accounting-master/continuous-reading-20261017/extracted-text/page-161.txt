9.9
Employee benefits
The additional methods below use the selected IAS 19, IAS 36, IAS 37, IFRS 2, IFRS 13 and
IAS 23 provisions in the original 2023 EU incorporating regulation (European Commission,
2023a). This is historical mechanism support; later amendments and actual entity facts remain
separate applicability inputs.
DefinedContributionState retains service periods, promised contribution formula, contributions
due/paid and applicable legal terms. For earned service requiring contribution 10, debit employee
expense 10/credit payable 10; payment settles it. Calling a plan defined contribution needs the
limited-obligation facts; a guaranteed benefit or additional funding obligation cannot disappear
by name.
The defined-benefit obligation (DBO) measures accrued employee benefits under the stated
actuarial assumptions. DefinedBenefitState retains employee/cohort service, benefit formula,
projected salary, retirement/turnover/mortality assumptions, discount-curve currency/term,
obligation, plan-asset fair value, asset ceiling, contributions, benefits and remeasurements.
IAS 19.67–74 requires projected unit credit and the stated service attribution; .83 supplies
discount-rate requirements. Assumptions are separately admitted measurements, not constants
provided by the accounting rule. A one-employee toy plan promises 100 for each completed
service year, paid after year two, with certain completion, zero salary growth and supported
10% discount. At year-one end accrued benefit 100 due in one year has PV 1000/11. In year
two that opening obligation accretes 100/11 and the second earned benefit adds 100; obligation
200 then settles 200. Merely using current salary times years without timing would give the
wrong year-one obligation.
A separate funded-plan roll-forward supplies an independent statement example: opening DBO
100, plan assets 80 and net liability 20; current service 12; interest 5 on DBO, interest 4 on assets
at 5%; contributions 10 at year end; benefits 8 paid from plan at year end; actuarial obligation
loss 7; actual asset return 6. DBO closes 100+12+5−8+7=116, assets 80+6+10−8=88, net
liability 28. Expense is 12+1=13, OCI loss is 7−(6−4)=5. Debit service expense 12/credit net
pension liability 12; debit interest expense 1/credit liability 1; debit OCI 5/credit liability 5;
debit liability 10/credit cash 10. End liability 20+13+5−10=28. Plan-paid benefits reduce
both DBO and plan assets and do not cause a second employer cash payment. IAS 19.113,
.120–127 supports these components; remeasurement OCI is not recycled to profit under 122.
A surplus requires the asset ceiling and applicable minimum funding analysis, not recognition
of any calculated negative liability.
Unknown actuarial inputs give conditional obligations or measurement_unresolved. Curtailment,
settlement, amendment and multi-employer/insured arrangements need their own fact conditions
and measurement-date remeasurement.
A buyout is not settlement if obligations remain.
Sensitivities vary one assumption only when that conditional meaning is explicit; correlated
risks require joint scenarios.
