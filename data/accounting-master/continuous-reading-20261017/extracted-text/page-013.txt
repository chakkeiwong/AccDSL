2.1
What a balance must remember
Northstar Components is a synthetic seller of one homogeneous good. Its disclosures, transactions
and contract terms are specified together, so we can check whether reconstruction preserves a
known possible history. All amounts in this example are currency units, not thousands. The
company has no foreign currency, subsidiaries, leases, returns or other comprehensive income.
These are declared features of the example, not assumptions to impose on an imported company.
Its source package and expected results are supplied in fixtures/northstar.json.
At the beginning of 2024, Northstar holds cash of 60, receivables of 20, inventory of 30 and
plant costing 100 with accumulated depreciation of 20. It owes suppliers 20 and lenders 100.
Share capital is 50 and retained earnings are 20. Thus assets of 190 equal liabilities of 120 plus
equity of 70. Inventory consists of six units costing 5 each. The existing plant depreciates by 10
a year, with no residual value. The loan remains outstanding throughout 2024; both possible
contract types charge 5% during that year.
The six months ending June and December provide two successive observation periods. Here
the chosen accrual convention assigns one half-year to each. Cash-flow dates within a half-year
are not recovered from a period aggregate; the synthetic history supplies a feasible ordering in
which inventory is purchased before delivery and customer receipts precede the payments they
fund. More detailed liquidity questions require more detailed dates.
