Curtailment, settlement and the asset ceiling
The benefits record separates obligation D, fair-valued plan assets P, asset- ceiling adjustment,
recognized net liability/asset, actuarial assumptions, remaining-year service cost, discount rate,
legal discharge and actual payments. For a liability D −P is positive. If there is a surplus, the
recognized asset is limited to the admissible economic benefit C; the added ceiling adjustment is
max(P −D−C, 0). This simple representation assumes no further minimum-funding adjustment;
any applicable IFRIC 14 obligation remains a separate admission dependency. The example
supplies C, not a method for inferring legal recoverability.
IAS 19.99 (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 19.99–112,120–126) requires current assumptions
and asset fair values immediately before and after amendment, curtailment or settlement. First
recognize pre-event remeasurements, then the change in benefits/covered employees, then any
later settlement. Past service cost arises from an amendment or curtailment and is recognized
at the earlier date specified by paragraph 103; a related restructuring or termination-benefit
recognition can therefore precede the formal change. The examples have no such earlier trigger.
A settlement discharges obligations; ordinary scheduled benefits included in assumptions are not
a settlement gain event. Buying insurance while retaining an obligation is also not settlement
under paragraphs 111–112.
Measure past service and settlement gain/loss without the asset ceiling, then remeasure the ceiling
under paragraph 101A. Service cost and settlement enter profit unless eligible capitalization
applies; actuarial and ceiling remeasurements enter OCI under paragraph 120 and are not later
recycled under paragraph 122. For the rest of the year, use updated service assumptions and
the post-event net position/discount rate, allowing for subsequent contributions and benefits,
under paragraphs 122A–126. An unchanged beginning-of-year interest base would miss this
required reset.
Consider opening D = 1,000, P = 900, net liability 100 and company cash 500/equity 400. At
the event date, current measurement gives D = 1,100, P = 920: actuarial loss 100 less asset
gain 20 creates OCI loss 80 and liability 180. A curtailment reduces the freshly measured
obligation by 150 to 950. Recognize past-service gain 150, leaving net liability 30. A later
transaction fully discharges a measured obligation 200 for plan assets 170 plus company cash
20. Settlement price 190 gives gain 10; D falls to 750, P to 750, liability to zero and cash to 480.
Profit 160 and OCI−80 reconcile equity from 400 to 480.
