10.20
Keeping the group calculation attached to its books
The disposal example gives the parent a cash receipt while removing cash from the consolidated
balance sheet. A software implementation must know whose cash each entry describes. If
it posts the removal of the subsidiary’s 156 to the parent’s legal book, it records a payment
the parent never made. If it leaves the subsidiary’s cash in the group after control ends, it
overstates the group’s assets. The consolidation calculation therefore needs its own book and
exact references to the legal books it reads.
Call the legal entities Parent-P and Subsidiary-S, and the reporting entity Group-P. Parent-P’s
functional currency is USD; Subsidiary-S’s is EUR. Their identifiers remain distinct even if two
imported records have the same display name. Each legal-book snapshot identifies its entity,
book, currency, relative date and signed balances. At acquisition the parent’s investment 115.2
and cash payment stay in its own USD book. The subsidiary’s opening cash 20, inventory 140
and payable 60 stay in its EUR book. Group-P’s consolidation entries refer to both snapshots
without altering either. This implements the separation between combining balances and
eliminating the parent’s investment against subsidiary equity (European Commission, 2023a,
IFRS 10.B86–88).
The selected perimeter has equally precise dependencies. Its control assessment records the
investor, investee, substantive rights, variable returns and ability to use power, together with
the interval for which those findings hold. Ordinary ownership of 80% and proportionate NCI
measurement are separate inputs. The currency record distinguishes the subsidiary’s functional
currency from the group’s presentation currency. A rate of 1.30 is usable here only when its
units are USD per EUR and its date is the closing date R. An inverse quote or a later rate is a
different input, even when its label looks familiar. The relative A, O, R and D calendar defines
this synthetic history; it supplies no evidence of when an actual investor could have known its
facts.
The group needs more state than the initial corporate example. Goodwill, the parent’s translation
reserve and NCI have distinct balances, while profit, OCI and cash movements retain their
period histories. The proposed GroupForeignOperation/1 profile supplies those fields under
its own schema lock. It preserves the common request and result fields rather than silently
adding accounts to the closed corporate profile. Loading this extension would still require an
implementation to declare support for it; there is no automatic conversion of a corporate state
into a group state.
