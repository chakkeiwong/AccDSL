Timing requires a different measure. Note 20 places US$14,298m of parent carrying value in
its under-one-year bucket; Note 33 reports US$14.3bn of parent long-term debt contractually
maturing in 2026. They align at the footnote’s displayed precision, but carrying value includes
premiums, discounts, costs and valuation adjustments rather than simply principal payable.
For the separately reported consolidated zero-coupon population, Note 20 gives US$18.1bn of
carrying value against US$57.6bn of aggregate principal at the notes’ respective maturities. The
firm’s US$435,206m consolidated long-term-debt total and US$47.6bn of subsidiary debt and
structured notes guaranteed by the parent are also separate from the parent’s own debt balance;
the latter guarantee amount supplies neither a liability valuation nor a payment forecast. A
dated parent debt-service path still needs instrument terms, cash dates and permissible funding
transfers (JPMorgan Chase & Co., 2026, pp. 281–282, 310–311).
