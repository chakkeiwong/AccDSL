The recoverable part of a transition loss
Group B’s loss component of 20 describes the issued contracts.
If the insurer also holds
reinsurance, how much of that loss is expected to be recovered? Extend the preceding example
by adding a separately recognised, qualifying group of reinsurance contracts held. Assume it
covers all contracts in B and only B, with remaining coverage and no incurred claims, and
uses general measurement. This changes the earlier no-reinsurance assumption. The issued
obligation to policyholders remains 120, with its loss component of 20; purchasing reinsurance
does not extinguish it (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 60–64 and
75).
The transition calculation has a specific timing exception. Ordinarily, paragraph B119C permits
the recovery-income treatment in paragraph 66A only if the reinsurance contract was entered
into before or when the onerous underlying contracts were recognised. Suppose the underlying
contracts’ recognition date, applying these rules, is 1 July 2020, the treaty was entered into on
1 October 2020 and both coverages remain in force at transition on 1 January 2022. The treaty
does not meet that ordinary condition for the original underlying recognition. Paragraph C20A
nevertheless expressly removes B119C’s condition when determining the loss-recovery component
at transition. The exception does not remove the treaty’s other recognition, grouping, boundary
or measurement requirements (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs
61–66B, B119C and C20A).
Let q be the percentage of claims on the underlying contracts that the insurer expects to recover
from this reinsurance group, expressed as a fraction. Assume q = 0.60 for this conditional
illustration. A nominal treaty quota alone does not establish that expectation: coverage, limits,
exclusions and collectability matter. The full reinsurance measurement also includes issuer
non-performance risk and the nonfinancial risk transferred, under paragraphs 63–64. With
K = 20 still denoting B’s loss component, C20A gives the reinsurance loss-recovery component
R:
R = Kq = 20 × 0.60 = 12.
This 12 belongs to the reinsurance asset for remaining coverage. It is not the issued liability,
the amount of expected claims, or a valuation of the whole reinsurance asset. If only some
contracts in the underlying group were covered, the required allocation of the losses would also
have to be established; the all-covered assumption avoids that extra calculation here (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 63–64, 66B, B119E and C20A).
The complete asset still needs the treaty cash flows, discounting, transferred risk and net cost
or gain of purchasing reinsurance. The reinsurance CSM reflects that net cost or gain; held
reinsurance is not classified as onerous under paragraph 68. Applying the issued-group CSM
and loss formulas to the held contract would therefore be wrong. Presentation likewise keeps
the issued and held-reinsurance portfolios separate (Australian Accounting Standards Board,
2022f, paragraphs 65–68 and 78–79).
