9.4.3
A warranty can promise another year of service
A returned product and a warranty claim need different histories. The return example unwinds
a sale when a customer exercises a refund right. A warranty may instead promise to keep
a product working, or provide a service after the product has been delivered. The company
must identify which promise it made before deciding how much of a customer’s payment it
has earned. The following cases use the same retained AASB 15 compilation and historical
Australian for-profit scope as the return example.
Suppose a customer buys a product and a separately purchasable service warranty for a combined
108. Comparable standalone selling prices at contract inception are 100 for the product and
20 for the warranty. Both are distinct promises; the product works independently of the
additional service. The contract has neither a discount attributable to just one promise nor
variable consideration. Under paragraphs 73–86, the 12 bundle discount is therefore allocated
in proportion to the standalone prices. The product receives 108 × 100/120 = 90 and the
service receives 108 × 20/120 = 18. An invoice’s labels would not by themselves establish those
standalone prices or justify a different discount allocation (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2022e).
On 1 July 2024 the company receives 108 and transfers control of the product, whose inventory
cost is 60. First, receipt debits cash and credits the contract liability by 108. Delivery then
debits that liability and credits product revenue by 90, while debiting cost of sales and crediting
inventory by 60. Profit is 30 and a liability of 18 remains. Recognising all 108 on delivery would
credit revenue for service the company still owes.
The service warranty runs for twelve monthly service periods to 30 June 2025. The company
stands ready uniformly; the customer receives and consumes the benefits as service is supplied.
The admitted measure gives each completed service month one twelfth of the allocation and
faithfully depicts performance. These facts support over-time recognition under 35(a), 39–45
and B14–B19; the existence of a twelve-month date range alone would not suffice. At 31
December 2024, half the service has been provided, so half of 18, or 9, is earned. Debit the
