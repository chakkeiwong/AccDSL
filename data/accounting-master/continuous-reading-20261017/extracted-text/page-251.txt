CHAPTER 11.
CREDIT LOSSES AND THE BANK BALANCE SHEET
242
An allowance calculation is a dated measurement based on forecast information. For a single
horizon, let EAD be a fixed exposure at default, PD the probability of default, and LGD the
mean loss fraction conditional on default. Ignoring discounting, the illustrative expected loss in
currency units is
L = EAD × PD × LGD.
With exposure 100, default probability 0.02 and loss given default 0.4, it produces 0.8. Changing
those inputs to 0.04 and 0.5 produces 2, so the allowance increase is 1.2 if there are no other
movements. This calculation is a demonstration model. A standard-specific expected-credit-loss
implementation must determine horizon, staging or other classification, discounting, forward-
looking information, write-offs and purchased-credit-impaired or other special cases. The simple
formula is not an IFRS 9 or US GAAP compliance implementation.

Notes 13 and 28 define these populations and measurements separately (JPMorgan Chase &
Co., 2026, pp. 258–262, 295–297). The contractual total contains consumer and wholesale
commitments; the allowance table excludes specified populations. The commitment allowance
is reported in accounts payable and other liabilities. Carrying value can also contain guarantee
liabilities, fair-value measurements and deferred fees or purchase adjustments, as applicable.
For standby letters of credit and other financial guarantees, the report explicitly reconciles a
US$175m allowance and a US$441m guarantee liability to US$616m of carrying value. Treating
either the contractual total or carrying value as the allowance would compute the wrong quantity.
The report also distinguishes contractual capacity from expected funding. Customers may
leave facilities unused, refinance them or let them expire; some commitments can be reduced or
cancelled under their contracts or law. The reported policy records no allowance for future draws
on unconditionally cancellable commitments. That exclusion concerns the loss measurement; a
liquidity scenario still needs the conditions under which a customer can draw, the bank can
cancel, and a payment requires settlement funds. Those conditions and their dependence on
borrower distress cannot be recovered from a commitment total. Comparisons also need the
same reporting basis: specified wholesale amounts are net of risk participations here but gross in
Federal Reserve filings. Parent guarantees of subsidiary debt are expressly outside the Note 28
table (JPMorgan Chase & Co., 2026, pp. 258, 295–296, 300).
The historical FASB amendment ASU 2016-13 helps explain these distinctions. Its paragraph
326-20-30-11 measures off-balance expected credit losses over the contractual period of a present
obligation to extend credit, except for an obligation the issuer may cancel unconditionally. For a
covered commitment, the estimate considers both whether funding will occur and the expected
loss on commitments that do fund. Example 10 keeps funded credit-card loans within loss
measurement while excluding an allowance on unconditionally cancellable undrawn credit, even
though the bank has sometimes advanced funds before detecting default. Paragraph 460-10-30-5
separately requires a contingent expected-loss liability and a noncontingent fair-value guarantee
liability for covered financial guarantees. These are historical amendment provisions, not verified
current Codification; applying them to the 2025 report requires the actual contracts and later
rule history (Financial Accounting Standards Board, 2016a, PDF pp. 2, 108, 118, 144, 173–176).
