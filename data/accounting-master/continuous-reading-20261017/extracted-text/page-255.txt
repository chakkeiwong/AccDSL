Several plausible substitutions give the wrong quantity. Net loans 117.6 are the accounting
amount, not this regulatory exposure. Deducting the full allowance from risk-weighted assets
loses the rule’s distinction between admitted and excess allowance. Deducting it again from
CET1 counts an expense twice. Including goodwill in the exposure base reverses its required
exclusion. Finally, using D to calculate its own allowance cap gives the circular equation
T = (117.6 + T)/80, hence T = 117.6/79, rather than the required 1.5. Solving that equation
accurately cannot make it the specified calculation.
A capital ratio does not settle the next cash payment
Now suppose 25 of deposits runs off. The supplied funding policy draws 4 within an available
facility limit of 10, then pays 25. Cash becomes zero, deposits 92 and borrowing 4. Net assets
119.6 still equal liabilities 96 plus equity 23.6. Capital and risk-weighted assets are unchanged:
the disappearing central-bank cash has zero weight, and no additional income or expense is
assumed. The facility is borrowing by this bank, not an off-balance extension of credit by it to
a customer.
If instead 40 is demanded, the same facility supplies at most 10. The bank pays 31 and leaves
9 unpaid, retained within its deposit liability of 86. Borrowing is 10 and cash zero. The
capital ratios remain the same, although the payment obligation has failed. No capital or cash
adjustment may be invented to remove that default. The two runoff outcomes are alternative
histories of this bank, not balances to be combined.
The ordinary leverage ratio asks for another denominator: average total consolidated assets
as reported for the relevant window, less specified Tier 1 deductions. Neither closing assets
140.6 before runoff nor 119.6 after it determines that average. If a separate, properly matched
input supplies average assets 145, the denominator is 145 −2 = 143 and the ratio is 21.6/143 =
108/715, about 15.10%. Without that input, the leverage ratio remains unresolved while the
supported risk-based ratios remain available (Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, 2026e,
12 CFR 3.10(b)(4)).
