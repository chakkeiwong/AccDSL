9.4.15
Replacing a component removes an old carrying amount
An asset can remain in use while one of its components is replaced. The company then owns
a new component and has given up the remaining benefits of the old one. Recording only
the new purchase would preserve an asset that no longer exists. The register therefore needs
component identity, original cost, accumulated depreciation, remaining life and the dates of
replacement and use. Paragraphs 13 and 70 of the retained AASB 116 require the replaced
part’s carrying amount to be removed when an eligible replacement is recognised (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022b).
Consider a synthetic company with cash and contributed capital of 200 on 1 January 2024. It
pays 100 for an administrative asset: a shell costing 80 with an eight-year useful life, and a
significant component costing 20 with a two-year life. Both are available immediately, have zero
residual values and consume benefits evenly. The company admits the cost model. These are
authored facts, including recognition eligibility and the absence of any additional impairment;
real assets require evidence for those assessments. Administrative use makes depreciation an
expense. A production asset might instead contribute to inventory cost under paragraph 49.
Acquisition debits the two asset costs by 80 and 20 and credits cash by 100. Paragraphs 43–44
require separate depreciation of significant components; the full year’s charge is 80/8+20/2 = 20.
Debit depreciation expense 20 and credit each component’s accumulated depreciation by 10. At
31 December 2024, cash is 100, net PPE is 80 and equity is 180 after the loss of 20. No revenue
or other operating activity is included in this asset-only example.
On 1 January 2025 the company replaces the old component. Its remaining carrying amount
is 20 −10 = 10. Debit accumulated depreciation 10 and disposal loss 10, and credit the old
component’s cost 20. The replacement costs 30, is paid in cash and is available immediately
for three years of even use, again with zero residual. Its entry debits new PPE 30 and credits
