Receiving 160 has changed liquidity and the insurance balances without creating profit. Omitting
the acquisition assets from the initial CSM calculation would instead give margins of 45 and
30, overstating total CSM by 50. Retaining the separate acquisition assets after the correct
transfer would overstate assets and equity by 50.
Even a balanced statement can conceal both mistakes together. If the implementation leaves
acquisition assets of 50 and uses the overstated margins, its post-receipt liability becomes 160.
Cash 200 plus the retained asset 50 less liability 160 still equals equity 90. That identity passes,
but the asset, liability and CSM are wrong relative to the stated measurement. Checking the
components and the derecognition event is therefore necessary in addition to checking that the
statement balances.
As a boundary case, if no acquisition asset exists, A = 0 gives C = −F and a zero group
carrying amount before premium receipt, under the same positive-margin assumptions. A
general implementation must also handle onerous groups, other initial cash-flow balances and
contracts added over time. The present calculation establishes the two-event bridge for the
specified groups, rather than those additional cases or an insurer’s actual valuation.
