The two service periods need not end together. In a separate modification, leave the original
100 instruments unchanged and add 20 instruments worth 4 each that require only the second
year’s service. IFRS 2.B43(b) measures the additional 80 at the modification date over their
own service period. Annual charges are now 100, 180 and 100, totaling 380. Spreading the
additional 80 over both remaining original years would understate year 2 by 40. This example
does not shorten the original award’s service condition: a beneficial change to that condition
must itself be reflected under B43(c). An adverse value-only change generally leaves the original
300 cost, while reducing the number of instruments invokes the cancellation rule for the affected
portion (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 2.B43(b)–(c), B44).
A change from cash settlement to equity settlement follows a distinct rule. Suppose two of
three equal service years have passed. The cash award liability, remeasured at the modification
date, is 180. Replacement equity has a total modification-date fair value of 300 and retains
the third year’s service requirement. IFRS 2.B44A recognizes 300(2/3) = 200 in equity for
services already received, removes the liability 180 and charges the difference 20 immediately.
Debit liability 180 and expense 20; credit award equity 200. The remaining year charges 100 to
expense and equity. Prior cash-award expense 180 plus the modification charge 20 plus later
service 100 gives lifetime expense 300, with no cash payment and no remaining liability. If
the correctly remeasured liability had instead been 220, the immediate difference would be an
expense credit of 20. It is not automatically an extra positive cost (European Commission,
2023a, IFRS 2.B44A–B44C).
Keep the original grant, every modification’s before-and-after valuation, eligible quantity, original
and modified service conditions, settlement classification, cumulative expense and component
balances together. Apply each event once and recognize the change from the previously recorded
cumulative amounts. B44B requires the modified vesting period when applying the cash-to-
equity rule; B44C extends it to an identified replacement for a cancelled cash award. Supplied
fair values in these examples do not provide an option-pricing method. Missing valuation or
legal terms prevent application without erasing the original award or assuming that it costs
zero.
10.13.5
Participation allocates the numerator before division
IAS 33.A13–A14 (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 33.A13–A16) allocates earnings according
to the actual dividend and undistributed-earnings rights of the classes. First deduct the declared
and contractually required dividends from the period’s allocable result. Allocate the remainder
as if it were distributed, using the participation formula and any caps. Add each class’s dividends
back to its allocation, then divide by the instruments to which that allocation belongs. A class’s
right to profits does not establish a right or obligation to share losses; preserve the actual terms.
Unknown participation rights block the allocation.
Suppose parent-attributable profit is 120, 100 ordinary shares participate with weight one, and
ten nonconvertible participating shares have weight two. Assume all are outstanding throughout
the period and there are no senior claims, caps or other instruments. Declared dividends are
20 and 4. Remaining profit 96 allocates over total weight 100 + 2(10) = 120: ordinary shares
receive 80 and participating shares receive 16. Adding dividends gives class earnings 100 and
20, so per-share earnings are 1 and 2. Allocating all 120 to the ordinary shares would overstate
their EPS as 1.2. Dividends are an allocation of this profit, not a second expense. A capped
