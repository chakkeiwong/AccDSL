A claim process needs joint probabilities
The preceding calculations take cash-flow and risk-adjustment values as inputs. Obtaining them
requires a law for the dated obligations, including their dependence. Means alone can support a
linear expected-claim total while giving the wrong answer for a treaty with a deductible. IFRS 17
requires probability-weighted discounted cash flows, with supportable non-market assumptions
and consistency with market variables. It does not make independent claim draws a default
when their dependence is unknown (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.B37–41,B49–55).
Let claims X1 and X2 at two specified dates each be either 0 or 40, with probability one half
for either amount. Assume zero discount and no counterparty loss solely to isolate dependence.
Total expected claims are always 40. A treaty pays max(X1 + X2 −60, 0). If both claims
always occur together, total claims are either 0 or 80 and expected recovery is 10. If exactly one
claim occurs, total claims are always 40 and recovery is zero. The two models have identical
single-date distributions.
All joint laws on this declared binary support can be described by a = Pr(X1 = 40, X2 = 40).

The same law supplies a sequential event procedure. Draw the first claim with probability one
half. After observing 40, the conditional probability that the second claim is 40 is 2a; after
observing zero it is 1 −2a. These transitions reproduce the joint table and provide a conditional
recovery forecast after the first event. This is an exact finite-law specification, not a simulated
result. If the evidence leaves a unresolved, retain its admissible interval and separate conditional
results; do not assign a probability distribution over possible values of a without additional
evidence.
