Suppose two eligible external derivatives jointly hedge a highly probable purchase of 100 units,
originally designated at price 10. Derivative A has nominal quantity 60 and B has quantity 40.
Both initially cost zero and have zero value. At the first observation, the purchase price is 11,
A is worth 60 and B is worth 80. These complete values and the qualifying relationship are
supplied facts. The total derivative gain is 140, the hedged purchase change is −100, and the
lower-of calculation gives reserve 100 and cumulative profit 40.
Now suppose a documented change in the relationship requires reducing A’s designated quantity
by 30 while the risk-management objective remains unchanged. Half of A remains designated,
all of B remains designated, and the purchase stays at 100 units. The company still owns
both derivatives. The removed half of A is worth 30 at the reduction date. Taking 30% of
the aggregate value 140 would give 42, which is wrong: A and B have different values per
nominal unit. IFRS 9.6.2.4(c) permits a proportion of an entire instrument, and 6.2.5 permits
joint designation of combinations (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.6.2.4–6.2.5). Those
permissions do not make every instrument unit financially interchangeable.
The reduction itself changes no value, reserve or profit. A’s earlier gain of 60 remains in the
history, including the 30 attributable to the portion now removed. Only subsequent changes
on that portion are outside the hedge and enter profit under B6.5.16. At a later price of 12,
suppose A is worth 120 and B is worth 100. The gains accumulated while designated are
G = 140 + 1
2(120 −60) + (100 −80) = 190.
The removed half of A contributes a further gain U = 1
2(120 −60) = 30 outside the hedge.
Together, G + U = 220, the full value of the two derivatives. The purchase change is still
measured on all 100 units from price 10, giving H = −200. The reserve is 190 and cumulative
profit is (190 −190) + 30 = 30. Profit has fallen from 40 to 30 since the previous observation:
reversal of earlier ineffectiveness exceeds the new undesignated gain by 10.
