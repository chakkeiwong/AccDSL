The request fixes the entity, book and dated head, evidence view, reporting population and
period, ordinary class, share basis, currency and scale, and historical rule version. Basic and
instrument records repeat a hash of that context. Every premise also points to exact source
bytes and a source anchor. Matching hashes establish which evidence was used; interpretation,
completeness and operative authority still require their own findings. Potential-share windows
must lie within the period and must not overlap. A converted series also identifies its subsequent
actual-share window and confirms that those actual shares already enter basic EPS. Only the
earlier potential-share window is added by the diluted calculation. The date checks prevent
overlap; instrument methods must still establish the amount and weighting of each increment.
A completed conditional report returns basic and diluted continuing, total and discontinued
EPS, their numerators and common denominators, and the ordered comparison for every series.
Each comparison retains its increments, the amounts before the test, the amounts if admitted,
and the admission decision. Missing attribution can prevent both basic and diluted EPS.
Missing instrument consequences or an incomplete inventory preserve an independently ready
basic result but leave diluted EPS unresolved. Known dependent effects or participation terms
produce an unsupported result; both kinds of obstacle are reported together when present.
Invalid bindings, malformed fractions, duplicate or omitted inventoried series, and overlapping
windows reject the request. None of these outcomes returns a partial selection as complete.
The exact fields and result states are specified in Section A.28. This is a read-only proposed
report: it creates no journal or share and changes no book. Its finite reference checks cover
the supplied arithmetic, ordering and refusal behavior. Applying it to a company still requires
instrument-specific preprocessing, complete facts and applicable authority; joining it to a
company’s dated earnings, tax and share records is a further implementation obligation.
10.2.6
Actual conversion, expiry and incomplete terms
Actual events divide the year differently. Suppose the debt pays its coupon and then converts
under its original terms on 1 October; interest ceases that day. Before conversion, recognized
interest is 400 with a tax benefit of 100. Paying the coupon reduces the liability to 9,200,
which transfers to ordinary equity; the original conversion-equity component remains in equity.
The transfer of carrying amount in this early-conversion illustration is a supplied accounting
treatment for the EPS example. IAS 32.AG32 expressly addresses conversion at maturity; a
general earlier-conversion measurement rule requires separate source and terms analysis. The
maturity-conversion company case in Section 10.2.7 uses that explicit rule. After conversion,
600 actual shares enter basic EPS for 92 days. The diluted calculation adds only the preceding
92 days of potential shares and the preconversion net expense of 300. There are therefore
600(92 + 92)/365 additional share-years in the diluted comparison, with no overlap. Under
the otherwise identical supplied earnings assumptions, basic ordinary earnings rise by the 300
of avoided later expense, and the remaining addback of 300 restores the same fully diluted
numerator as before. Treating the potential shares as outstanding for all 184 days in addition
to the actual shares would double count (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 33, paragraphs
21(c), 36–38).
