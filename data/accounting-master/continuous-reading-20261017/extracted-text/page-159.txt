9.7.2
A recognised charge, restricted assets and a later payment
Apple attributes its large 2024 State Aid charge to the September 2024 court decision confirming
the European Commission’s recovery decision. This is the issuer’s account of the dispute and
its accounting consequence. Note 7 describes a 15.8 billion dollar payable to Ireland, partly
offset by a 4.8 billion US foreign tax credit and an 823 million decrease in unrecognised tax
benefits (Apple Inc., 2024a, Note 7, printed pp. 39–40).
The narrative calls the net charge 10.2 billion; the statutory-rate reconciliation gives the more
precise amount 10,246 million. Subtracting the displayed narrative centres gives 15,800−4,800−
823 = 10,177, which is 69 million lower. That is compatible with the coarser inputs: if the two
billion-dollar amounts are rounded to the nearest 0.1 billion and 823 to the nearest million, the
component calculation lies in the closed outer interval [10,076.5, 10,277.5] million, containing
10,246. The 10.2 billion charge is compatible with it as well. This is a conditional rounding
check, with the issuer’s precise rule and tie convention unverified; the centre difference does not
establish a conflicting source assertion.
The tax charge does not make the settlement funds disappear at recognition. At 28 September
2024 the escrow comprised 2.6 billion dollars of cash and cash equivalents and 13.2 billion of
current marketable securities, restricted from general use (Apple Inc., 2024a, Notes 4 and 7,
printed pp. 37 and 39). The annual liquidity discussion separately identifies the obligation and
the funds held to settle it. The following quarter reports both a payment and the remaining
obligation (Apple Inc., 2024a, printed p. 26) (Apple Inc., 2025c, printed p. 16).

a common exchange rate or an exact exchange-gain entry. The interim tax discussion mentions
foreign-currency revaluation effects related to the decision, but does not allocate this 500 million
dollar residual (Apple Inc., 2025c, printed p. 16).
The remaining funds also retain their asset classifications. At December 2024, restricted cash and
cash equivalents were 126 million dollars and restricted current marketable securities 3.3 billion
(Apple Inc., 2025c, Note 4, printed p. 8). Their displayed sum, 3,426 million, is compatible
with the rounded 3.4 billion obligation. It is not all cash available for ordinary operations. The
opening 2.6 billion of restricted cash alone could not have funded the reported 11.9 billion
payment; securities sales, maturities, transfers and valuations need their own histories.
For an isolated settlement illustration, suppose 11,900 million of restricted cash is available and
the amount paid equals the liability’s carrying value at settlement. Debit tax payable and credit
restricted cash by 11,900. Assets and liabilities each fall by 11,900; equity and current-period
tax expense do not change in this posting. This assumes the required cash conversion has
already occurred and omits any associated gain, loss or fee. It explains settlement of an existing
obligation without pretending to reconstruct Apple’s funding and currency entries. The quarter
