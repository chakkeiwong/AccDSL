2.7.6
Carrying the company into its next fiscal periods
The next period begins with the company’s existing assets, obligations and owners. The parent
closes its 2025 profit of 49.5 into retained earnings, raising that balance from 12 to 61.5; the
subsidiary closes its profit of 52.5 into retained earnings of 52.5. The parent still holds cash
491.5, plant 80 and investment 100, financed by equity 671.5. The subsidiary has cash and
equity of 152.5. Eliminating the investment gives group equity 724, of which 708.75 belongs to
the parent and 15.25 to non-controlling interests. Closing the income accounts does not earn
the 2025 profit a second time.
Consider a hypothetical continuation under the same historical accounting principles and a
supplied tax law. The companies change their reporting year-end to June to align with their
operating cycle. A six-month period from 1 January to 30 June 2026 is followed by two July–
June years. The source requires the reason for the short period and a warning that its amounts
are not entirely comparable with a full year (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 1.36–38). These
scenario dates do not establish the standards or tax law that will actually govern a future
reporting period. Control, the parent’s 90% interest, common policies and the outside-basis tax
exception are expressly renewed for each period. The earlier control finding ended at the start
of this continuation.
In the short period the parent receives revenue of 20, pays operating costs of 110 and depreciates
plant by 10. Book and tax depreciation are equal, so both its accounting loss and its tax loss
are 100. Cash is 401.5 and plant is 70. The supplied law permits the parent to carry this loss
forward until 30 June 2028, but provides no carryback or transfer to the subsidiary. A deduction
that may be used later is not automatically an asset today. IAS 12 requires probable taxable
profit against which the loss can be used; recent losses require convincing other evidence or
suitable taxable temporary differences (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 12.34–37).
Suppose convincing evidence supports 40 of taxable profit in the next period, and the enacted
rate expected on recovery is 25%. Recognise a deferred tax asset of 40 × 25% = 10, leaving 60 of
the loss without a recognised asset. The parent’s after-tax loss is 90. The subsidiary separately
earns 20 in cash and incurs tax of 5, which remains unpaid. Its equity rises to 167.5. Group
cash is 574, plant 70 and deferred tax assets 10, against a current tax liability of 5:
574 + 70 + 10 −5 = 649 = 724 −75.
