After the ordinary issue entry, debit conversion equity 2 and credit deferred tax liability 2. Cash

More generally, let issue proceeds be P, supplied debt value L, and residual equity E = P −L.
Under this specific fiscal treatment the liability’s tax base is P, so at rate t,
T0 = t(P −L) = tE,
Enet = (1 −t)E,
P = L + T0 + Enet.
With the alternative matching tax base L, this initial difference is zero. A zero tax rate also
produces zero deferred tax; neither the 25% example nor the debt value 92 is a universal validity
condition. Other fiscal bases need their own supported analysis instead of interpolation between
these two examples.
This issue-date calculation leaves later earnings to be derived. IAS 12.23 states that subsequent
changes in this deferred tax liability are recognized in profit or loss under paragraph 58. The tax
bases, deduction timing and settlement consequences determine those changes. In particular,
the initial tax charge to equity cannot simply be substituted for the earnings tax effect used
in diluted EPS. The finite CompoundTaxBasis/1 reference calculation checks the two initial-base
profiles, their equity origin and refusal of missing or unsupported inputs; it does not supply
that later tax schedule or alter the preceding company’s reported results.
10.2.9
Follow the tax difference into earnings
The initial charge to equity does not tell us the later cost of financing. That cost depends on
how the debt grows and what the tax authority allows the issuer to deduct. To expose the
distinction, consider a separate July issue whose principal at October maturity is 100, with a
coupon of 4. Its proceeds are again 100 and its initial debt value is 92. The supplied comparable
rate for the 92-day period is now 3/23, so
L0 = 100 + 4
1 + 3/23 = 92,
I = 92 3
23 = 12,
Lafter coupon = 92 + 12 −4 = 100.
The accounting interest of 12 includes both the coupon of 4 and the debt discount of 8. These
are different terms from the preceding principal-92 instrument. At maturity the holder may
instead convert the principal into six ordinary shares after receiving the coupon; the original
conversion equity remains equity under IAS 32.AG32.
Now supply the fiscal treatment. The tax authority treats the initial 100 entirely as debt and
permits a deduction for the coupon when it accrues. It allows no separate deduction for the
accounting discount. The fiscal liability therefore rises to 104 before payment and falls to
100 when the coupon is paid. Assume sufficient taxable income to use the deduction and no
additional tax on either maturity redemption or original-terms conversion. These are explicit
illustrative fiscal facts, not consequences of a known tax rate. At the supplied rate of 25%, they
give the following sequence.
