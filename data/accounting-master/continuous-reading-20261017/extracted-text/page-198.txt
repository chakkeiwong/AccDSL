10.7
Schedules and initial classification
The following liability and ownership methods use selected dated AASB 9 and AASB 132
provisions (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h,c). Issuer, holder, contractual cash
flow and accounting measurement remain separate throughout the schedule.
DebtContract contains parties, currency, principal, issue/settlement dates, payment calendar,
fixed/index/spread/reset terms, compounding/day-count, business-day adjustment, principal
changes, fees, options, security, priority and legal default/remedy terms.
Each term has
evidence/admission provenance. The economic schedule emits due cash independently of the
recognition method.
A market-value adapter may value that schedule but cannot choose
amortised cost, FVOCI, FVTPL or liability/equity classification.
An investor’s classification evaluates business model and contractual cash-flow features; a
debt label alone cannot establish the AASB 9.4.1.2/4.1.2A conditions. At initial recognition
the selected 5.1.1 rule measures fair value and qualifying costs as appropriate. An unknown
day-one observable-value condition yields unresolved under 5.1.1A/B5.1.2A. The retained
acquisition cases distinguish FVTPL expensed fees, amortised-cost fees, original EIR and POCI
credit-adjusted EIR. No rate method inferred from market value can overwrite the contract
rate.
For an issuer with a plain amortised-cost debt, net proceeds 100 and one legally due payment 121
at the end of two annual periods, the exact EIR solves 100=121/(1+r)^2, hence r=10%. Issue
debit cash 100/credit debt 100; year-one debit interest 10/credit debt 10; year-two debit interest
11/credit debt 11; redemption debit debt 121/credit cash 121. Outstanding contractual payment
stays 121 until settled even while carrying value is 100 or 110. Total interest 21 equals cash
outflow 121 minus proceeds 100. An immediate transaction fee 2 changes net proceeds to 98 and
the EIR root; retaining 10% is wrong. The numerical root method is in the numerical-method
discussion; an irrational rate is not silently an exact rational.
A fractional period uses the declared convention. Annual-effective 21% over half a year produces
factor 1.1 and interest 10 on 100; simple proration gives 10.5 and is wrong for that contract. A
floating coupon resets from the admitted fixing available at the reset date, not a later observed
rate. Actual day-count and holiday-calendar versions are immutable inputs. Unknown fixings
preserve conditional paths, rather than defaulting to the latest value.
10.8
Modification, extinguishment and default
At a modification, retain original and replacement terms, counterparty, fees, effective date,
legal-release evidence and original EIR. Determine derecognition before measurement. AASB
9.3.3.1–3.3.3 and B3.3.1–B3.3.6 govern the selected issuer-liability cases. The quantitative
ten-percent test compares the PV of new terms, including specified borrower/lender fees, at
original EIR against remaining original PV. It is not a universal asset test, nor proof that every
change below ten percent is immaterial; changed currency, rights and other qualitative terms
need the applicable analysis. Insufficient evidence remains unresolved. Third-party payment
without legal release does not extinguish debt.
