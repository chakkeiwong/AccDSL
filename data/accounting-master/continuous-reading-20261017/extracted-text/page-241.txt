The constant-exposure formula does not apply to arbitrary turnover. For a piecewise constant
nominal exposure, split the history at every monetary change. If Mj is the net monetary
amount held between index observations Ij−1 and Ij, its loss expressed at closing index IT is
Mj(Ij/Ij−1 −1)IT /Ij; sum those interval losses and separately handle contractually indexed
balances. Recognition times and index observations determine those intervals. IAS 29.27 also
permits a weighted-average estimate; its approximation must be identified. Reconcile the result
with restated nonmonetary amounts, equity and income rather than inventing a residual profit.
For example, external cash income 30 at index 125 changes the preceding net monetary exposure
from 20 to 50. The two losses are 6 and 10, totaling 16; restated income 36 and monetary loss
16 reconcile closing net assets 140 to restated opening capital 120. Cash-flow items likewise use
closing purchasing-power units. In the no-flow case, opening cash expressed in closing units
is 150 and ending cash 100; the purchasing-power reconciliation of minus 50 is not a payment
(European Commission, 2023a, IAS 29.26–34).
First application requires earlier acquisition history. IFRIC 7.3–5 requires restatement as
though the economy had always been hyperinflationary and a particular ordering for opening
deferred tax. In a separate inventory example, cost 40 at index 80 becomes 50 at the opening
index 100 and 75 at the closing index 150. Assume a fixed nominal tax base 40, tax rate
20 percent, profit-origin differences and no recognition exception. Opening deferred tax is
(50 −40) × 0.2 = 2, then restated to 3. Closing deferred tax is (75 −40) × 0.2 = 7; the period
expense is 4. Indexing a nominal opening deferred-tax balance of zero would miss this sequence
(European Commission, 2023a, IFRIC 7.3–5; IAS 29.32).
