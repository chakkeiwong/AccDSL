A complete hypothetical calculation still needs the applicable dilution rule. For example, change
only initial profit before the bonus to 80. Basic earnings are 64 and EPS is 16/25. Either

The ConsequentialEPSWorlds/1 method therefore reports the complete four worlds and leaves

The official illustrative ordering example provides a useful comparison. AASB 133 Example 9
orders options, bonds and preference shares by fixed incremental earnings per share of 0, 1.5 and
4. Options and bonds reduce the continuing-operations ratio to 13,000,000/4,020,000 = 650/201;
adding preferences would raise it to 970/281, so they are excluded (Australian Accounting
Standards Board, 2021b, Example 9, pp. 28–30). The earnings increments there do not depend
on another assumed conversion. Examples 7 and 12 explain contingencies, actual conversion
dates and the continuing-operations control number, but do not add a rule for the shared
threshold expense above.
Fixed standalone ranking followed by complete recalculation, and ranking by the next marginal
effect, both reject the first conversion in the 80-profit example. With only two series, these
calculations do not distinguish those ranking policies in general. The inspected examples
therefore do not settle when changing increments should be reordered or a rejected issue
reconsidered. The unresolved selection is kept separate from the correct conditional world
amounts; lower combined EPS alone does not establish an admissible selection.
There is a separate, explicit dependency for contingently issuable potential shares. IAS 33.57 first
tests whether the instruments may be assumed issuable under paragraphs 52–56, then applies
their instrument-specific dilution rules. Its final sentence also requires assumed exercise or
conversion of similar outstanding potential shares that are not contingently issuable (European
Commission, 2023a, IAS 33.52–57). A satisfied contingency alone is insufficient.
Consider profit 100 and 100 ordinary shares. A similar outstanding option series contains 20
options at strike 6; a contingent option series contains 20 at strike 5. Average market price is
10. For this conditional example, similarity has been separately admitted; different exercise
prices neither establish nor automatically disprove that finding. Both agreements cover the full
reporting period, with no future-service proceeds, earnings effects, participation or additional
dependencies. The contingent option is excluded from basic shares. Its nonfinancial condition
requires five stores, assessed at the reporting-period end while the contingency period remains
open.
Under paragraphs 52 and 56, use the period-end condition status. Five observed stores qualify; a
forecast of a fifth opening next year does not. The outstanding option supplies 20−20(6)/10 = 8
incremental ordinary shares. Its exercise is dilutive, reducing EPS to 100/108 = 25/27. With
the condition satisfied, the contingent option supplies another 20 −20(5)/10 = 10 shares, giving
diluted EPS 100/118 = 50/59. Both have zero earnings increments, so testing the prerequisite
first respects their tied incremental-earnings ratios while enforcing paragraph 57.
Now give the similar outstanding option strike 12, retaining the example’s explicit similarity
finding. It is out of the money at average price 10 and its exercise is not assumed. The contingent
option would reduce EPS to 100/110 = 10/11 in isolation, but paragraph 57’s prerequisite is
unsatisfied. Both are excluded and EPS remains 1. If instead only four stores are observed, the
original outstanding option remains dilutive but the contingent option is excluded, leaving EPS
25/27. At zero or negative earnings neither zero-earnings-effect option strictly reduces EPS or
increases loss per share.
