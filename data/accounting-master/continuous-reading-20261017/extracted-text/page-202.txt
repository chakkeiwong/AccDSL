A hedge reserve is neither cash nor a second gain
Suppose a company hedges a highly probable inventory purchase.
A derivative gain can
arrive before the inventory affects earnings. Cash-flow hedge accounting preserves that timing
through a separate equity reserve, but only after the relationship qualifies. The designation
must identify an eligible instrument and item, the hedged risk, the risk-management objective
and the assessment method at inception. An economic relationship, the absence of credit-
risk dominance and an appropriate actual hedge ratio are separate requirements (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, 6.2–6.4 and B6.4.1–19). Favorable price correlation alone
does not establish them. Unknown designation or valuation information remains unresolved; it
cannot be repaired by assigning zero or placing a gain in other comprehensive income.
Take one documented, offsetting exposure and a derivative worth zero at designation. The
illustration includes the whole derivative, no excluded option or forward component and no
intermediate reserve release. Let Dt be its cumulative gain, including settled gains, and Ht the
cumulative change in the hedged item’s present value attributable to the designated risk. Their
signs refer to their economic values. With admitted offsetting changes, the cumulative effective
gain Et and ineffectiveness It are
Et = sgn(Dt) min{|Dt|, |Ht|},
It = Dt −Et.
(10.3)
The reserve follows the lower absolute cumulative amount; effective changes enter other com-
prehensive income and the remainder enters profit or loss (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2022h, 6.5.11). This formula handles the stated offsetting case; a complex exposure
outside it needs its own measurement method. In particular, one same-direction period is not
itself proof that no economic relationship exists. Item valuation must include time value and
only the item’s relevant features; a hypothetical derivative cannot import features that exist
solely in the actual hedging instrument (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, B6.4.5
and B6.5.4–6).
Initially, supplied values D1 = 12 and H1 = −10 produce effective gain 10 and profit 2. At the
next measurement, D2 = 15 and H2 = −14 produce effective gain 14 and cumulative profit 1.
The second period therefore records a derivative increase of 3, other comprehensive income of 4
and a loss of 1. Applying the minimum only to that period’s changes would incorrectly give
effective gain 3. The retained cumulative values are essential economic memory.
Now buy the inventory for 114 and settle the derivative for cash of 15. Settlement removes the
derivative asset; it does not by itself release the 14 reserve. Because the forecast transaction
has produced a nonfinancial asset, debit the reserve 14 and credit inventory 14. Inventory cost
becomes 100. This direct basis adjustment is not an other-comprehensive-income reclassification
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, 6.5.11(d)(i)). Selling the inventory for 130
subsequently produces revenue 130 and cost of sales 100.
With opening cash and equity of 200 and no other movements, the full sequence has cumulative
pretax profit 31, other comprehensive income 14 and a separate equity-to-inventory basis transfer
of −14. Closing cash and pretax equity are both 231. The equity reconciliation is
200 + 31 + 14 −14 = 231,
(10.4)
where omitting the direct basis transfer would overstate equity.
Gross operating cash is
−114+15+130 = 31: the identified hedge’s cash flows follow the inventory position’s classification
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2023a, 16). A matching terminal cash total does not
excuse a wrong interim reserve, profit or basis.
