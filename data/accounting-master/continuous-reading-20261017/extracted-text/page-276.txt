A RegulatoryAssessment reads an accounting view and an independent jurisdiction, date,
perimeter, eligibility, deductions and exposure-weight policy. It outputs capital components,
RWA/exposure measures, liquidity measures and reconciliations with their distinct denominators.
Illustratively, equity 20 minus an assumed regulatory deduction 2 yields capital 18; assumed
RWA 125 gives 14.4%. Those inputs are synthetic, not a Basel rule, national capital approval
or default threshold. Selected dated US national capital rules are inspected in the surrounding
bank inquiry. A connected accounting-to-capital reconciliation for the same bank still requires
its applicable perimeter, eligibility, deductions and exposure facts; the illustrative inputs above
do not supply those facts.
Behavior under capital/liquidity constraints is a separate economic model. Specify feasible
actions, objectives, information set and ties before solving; failure to find an action returns
policy_failure or default under the stated contract. Halaj/Behn sources can motivate candidate
behavior but cannot calibrate it for an individual bank. A forecast experiment must compare
simple fixed-book, contractual runoff and explicitly estimated policy baselines with time-
safe data, before claiming a predictive improvement. CECL and other bases retain separate
authority/method branches in the dated-authority discussion.
