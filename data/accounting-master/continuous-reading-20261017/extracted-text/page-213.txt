10.12.10
A stopped hedge portion leaves a reserve behind
Return to the original forecast purchase of 100 tonnes at an initial forward price of 80. At the
first observation, price is 90 and the derivative has gained 1200: the cash flow hedge reserve is
1000 and cumulative ineffectiveness is 200. Now management finds that 20 tonnes are no longer
highly probable, while the remaining 80 tonnes continue to satisfy the hedge conditions. The
derivative is still owned. Stopping hedge accounting for a portion therefore changes where its
later gains go, even if no contract is sold and no cash changes hands.
IFRS 9.B6.5.27(b) requires partial discontinuation for the forecast volume that is no longer
highly probable; paragraphs 6.5.6 and B6.5.22 make the change prospective. Paragraph B6.5.23
prevents voluntary discontinuation of a relationship that continues to qualify. These are the
inspected historical EU 2023/1803 provisions (European Commission, 2023a). The example
supplies the qualification and documentation findings; a percentage reduction alone cannot
establish them.
For this case, the 100 tonnes have identical per-unit exposures, one original measurement date
and one price origin. The identified 20 tonnes correspond to 20% of the entire derivative,
whose proportional designation is permitted by paragraph 6.2.4(c). This homogeneity matters.
Multiplying both sides of the lower-of calculation by a nonnegative fraction a gives
min{a|D|, a|H|} = a min{|D|, |H|},
where D is cumulative derivative gain and H the cumulative change in the hedged cash flows.
