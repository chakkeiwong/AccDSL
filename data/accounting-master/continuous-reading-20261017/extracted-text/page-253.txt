11.2
A bank’s cash, allowance and capital in one path
The bank reference state contains settlement cash 30, gross loans 100, allowance 0.8, deposits
110 and equity 19.2. Originating a loan of 20 first debits loans and credits the borrower’s
deposit. A subsequent transfer of 15 to another bank debits deposits and credits settlement cash.
Loan origination and external cash use are consequently distinct events. Receive loan interest 6
in cash and credit deposit interest 2 to customer balances. Remeasure the allowance to 2.4,
posting an additional loss of 1.6. The resulting state is cash 21, gross loans 120, allowance 2.4,
deposits 117 and equity 21.6. Net assets are 21 + 120 −2.4 = 138.6 = 117 + 21.6.
Now 25 of deposits runs off. The declared funding policy uses cash first, then a committed
facility with limit 10. It draws 4 and settles 25, leaving cash zero, deposits 92 and wholesale
debt 4. A runoff of 40 instead leaves 9 unpaid even after the maximum facility draw. The
simulator records default and retains the unpaid deposit obligation. It does not assume unlimited
central-bank or shareholder funding.
The prudential calculation is a separate, illustrative policy. Deduct 2 from accounting equity
to obtain capital 19.6; apply a 75% risk weight to gross loans for risk-weighted assets 90. The
resulting ratio is 19.6/90. Neither the deduction nor the weight is a Basel default. Actual
prudential packages require jurisdiction, exposure class, eligibility, buffers and dated rules, with
a reconciliation from the accounting book. The structure follows the need to model capital and
liquidity jointly in Behn et al. (2019, Section 2), without transferring that paper’s estimated
preferences or shock parameters to this bank.
11.2.1
An allowance affects capital and exposure in different ways
The illustrative ratio above shows where a prudential calculation belongs. Applying a particular
rule requires a further step: reconcile its numerator and denominator to the same bank’s
accounting records. An allowance already reduces accounting equity through expense, yet
a specified portion may enter Tier 2 capital. The regulatory loan exposure can meanwhile
remain gross of that allowance. Using the net loan balance for every purpose would erase these
distinctions.
Consider a separate synthetic US national bank, with amounts in millions of US dollars. Its
conditional reporting window is January–March 2026. For this calculation, freeze the retained
1 January 2026 national-bank rules; this assumption does not establish the rules actually
applicable to a 31 March filing. The bank has no subsidiaries, minority interests, AOCI, deferred
taxes, other capital instruments or deductions apart from goodwill. It is outside the advanced-
approaches, Category III, market-risk and community-bank leverage-ratio regimes, and has
no transition or special supervisory adjustment. Its common stock is supplied as satisfying all
thirteen conditions in section 3.20(b)(1). These are explicit case facts, not conclusions drawn
from a security’s name (Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, 2026c, 12 CFR 3.20(b)(1)–(2)).
