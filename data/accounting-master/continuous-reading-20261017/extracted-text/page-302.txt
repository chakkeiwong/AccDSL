12.15.1
What the two stages require
The addendum starts from a particular model for incremental payments. For an observed
incremental payment Xij in origin period i and development period j, its fitted mean mij is
strictly positive. The model assumes E[Xij] = mij, Var(Xij) = φmij and log mij = c + αi + βj,
with reference levels fixed to identify the parameters. This incremental model and Mack’s
conditional cumulative model have separate assumptions, even where their fitted reserve amounts
agree. A common point estimate does not make their uncertainty models interchangeable
(England, 2001, equations 2.1–2.3).
The first stage measures each observed increment’s departure from its fitted mean using the
Pearson residual rij = (Xij −mij)/√mij. With N observed cells and p fitted parameters,
the paper estimates bφ = P r2

Start from the observed latest cumulative amounts, work backwards through fitted development
factors, and difference the fitted cumulative amounts into increments. Calculate the residuals
and dispersion from those increments. For each bootstrap draw, resample adjusted residuals
with replacement and invert the residual relation to form pseudo-increments. Accumulate them,
refit the development factors, project future cumulative claims and difference them into future
means. Retain the same fitted draw across all cells. This produces a joint array of conditional
means, not yet a joint array of payments (England, 2001, Section 2 and Appendix A, Triangles
1–9).
The second stage chooses a process law. For a positive future mean m and positive dispersion φ,
the paper proposes φZ, where Z has a Poisson law with mean m/φ. Its mean is m and variance
is φm. A gamma law with shape m/φ and scale φ has the same two moments but different
support and tail. That is a modeling choice, not a change of random-number syntax. Each
future payment is drawn conditionally, and aligned payments are summed by origin, payment
period and total (England, 2001, Section 3 and Appendix A, Triangles 9–10).
Dependence must survive aggregation. In the first small example, suppose two cells share the
same M but are independent conditional on it. Their conditional total variance is 4M, with
expectation 20, while their conditional total mean is 2M, with variance 36. Their total variance
is therefore 56. Independently redrawing each cell from its marginal payment law would give
19 + 19 = 38, losing the dependence created by the shared fit. Marginal distributions are
insufficient instructions for a joint simulation.
12.15.2
Sparse data, failed draws and implementation choices
The six observed increments in the earlier triangle fit the incremental model exactly. It has
p = 2(3) −1 = 5 parameters and only N −p = 1 residual degree of freedom, giving zero
fitted dispersion. This arithmetic does not establish that future payments are deterministic.
