10.12
Excluded hedge components and rebalancing
The following conditional methods use the retained AASB 9 compilation for calendar-2024/2025
Australian for-profit Tier 1 illustrations, without optional later amendments. Actual authority
admission and component valuation remain separate requirements.
10.12.1
The option premium pays for protection
A purchased option can protect an inventory purchase even if it expires with no intrinsic value.
Its time value is the price of that protection. When only intrinsic value is designated, applying
the earlier whole-derivative method would mix the price of protection with the payoff that
offsets the purchase. AASB 9.6.5.15 (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, 6.5.15 and
B6.5.29–33) separates them. The designated intrinsic-value movement follows the applicable
hedge method; the related time-value movement accumulates in a different equity component.
The nature of the hedged item determines when that cost affects earnings. A transaction
such as an inventory purchase uses a cost attached to that transaction. Protection over a
period uses systematic, rational amortisation over the period in which the intrinsic-value hedge
adjustment can affect earnings. The life of the option alone does not decide which route applies
(B6.5.29–30).
The event record must identify the instrument, designated component, underlying item and
risk, critical terms, actual and aligned time values at designation and every measurement date,
intrinsic-value changes, separate reserve balances, the transaction or time-period classification,
amortisation policy, and each basis transfer or reclassification. Existing intrinsic-value effective-
ness requirements remain necessary. Unknown component valuations cannot be replaced with a
total derivative value and a zero time value.
Suppose a qualifying inventory hedge begins with cash and equity of 200. Buy an option for
6, entirely time value. Cash is 194 and the option asset is 6; there is no initial expense. The
first supplied valuation is intrinsic value 12 and aligned time value 4. The option is worth 16.
Its increase of 10 comprises an effective intrinsic gain of 12 and a time-value loss of 2, both in
OCI but in separate reserves. At purchase and expiry, intrinsic value is 20 and time value zero.
The option is worth 20, the intrinsic reserve is 20 and the cost reserve is negative 6. The last
period’s change of 4 comprises intrinsic gain 8 and time-value loss 4.
Buy the inventory for 120 and settle the option for 20. Settlement removes the option asset and
leaves cash 94. The intrinsic reserve reduces inventory cost by 20. Removing the negative cost
reserve adds 6 to inventory cost:
120 −20 + 6 = 106.
Both direct transfers affect the asset and equity; neither is a second cash flow or an OCI
reclassification. Cash 94 plus inventory 106 equals equity 200 after the transfers. Selling for 130
gives profit 24 and cash 224. The cash bridge is 200 −6 −120 + 20 + 130 = 224. The equity
bridge is opening 200, profit 24, cumulative OCI 14, and a direct basis transfer of negative 14.
Omitting the premium would report cost 100 and overstate profit by 6. For a forecast sale
instead, the accumulated cost enters earnings when the sale does. An unrecoverable portion
enters earnings immediately under 6.5.15(b)(iii), rather than waiting for a transaction that
cannot recover it.
