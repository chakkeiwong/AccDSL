In year 3 the ordinary dividend is 60 + 180 = 240. Its remaining base allocation is −50, and
the tax adjustment on that residual is −150, giving 240 −50 −150 = 40. Setting the negative
adjustment to zero would give 190 and count part of a benefit already attributed to earlier
earnings again. The −150 is an EPS-allocation reversal; it is neither a negative cash dividend
nor evidence that every tax loss creates a recognised asset.
The dependence on payout timing cancels under these fixed rights. Substituting H = taD and
R = P −D into the class allocations yields the local identities
NO = bD + taD + (b + ta)(P −D) = (b + ta)P,
NP = aD + a(P −D) = aP,
NO + NP = P + taP = G + taR.
Thus current ordinary earnings depend on current profit and its associated hypothetical benefit,
even when the declared dividend distributes older profit. At zero tax the formula reduces to
the ordinary base share bP; with no participating entitlement it reduces to P. Total class
allocations can exceed reported profit because they include the hypothetical tax consequence.
For example, year 1 allocates 120 + 300 = 420 while reported profit is 330. Those two quantities
serve different purposes and must not be forced to match by posting the hypothetical difference.
ParticipatingTaxEPS/1 specifies this basic-EPS calculation with exact rational amounts and a
supplied denominator. It checks actual class dividends, the benefit amount and recognition
location, and an opening base-profit balance sufficient to fund any prior-period distribution. It
permits a negative post-dividend residual while restricting current base profit to nonnegative
amounts. Missing rights or tax findings leave the result unresolved; inconsistent payouts, future
information and unsupported findings produce no partial numerator. Its output contains no
dividend, tax asset or journal entry.
The interpretation also says that a tax benefit shared by both classes must follow the applicable
rights (IFRS Interpretations Committee, 2017b, p. 2, footnote (a)). That is a different allocation
from the ordinary-only benefit used here. Retained actual benefits, changing or capped rights,
loss-tax recognition and nonadditive diluted selection require their own supported treatment.
In particular, a fixed after-tax earnings input to the earlier capped-participation method cannot
silently stand in for the hypothetical benefit calculated here.
