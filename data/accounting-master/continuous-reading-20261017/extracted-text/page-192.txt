Finally, grant-date value, vesting-date value, compensation expense and future unrecognised
cost answer different questions. Apple’s stated policy recognises expense on a straight-line basis
for its estimate of awards that ultimately vest (Apple Inc., 2024a, Note 1, printed p. 34). Its
unrecognised RSU cost increases from $19.4 billion over a weighted-average 2.4 years to $29.2
billion over 2.9 years at the quarter end. Dividing either amount by its weighted period does not
disclose a constant annual expense schedule or a cash liability: different cohorts have different
remaining recognition periods, and future vesting estimates can matter even under the stated
straight-line policy. The ownership model therefore needs linked award cohorts, recognition
periods, share events and cash settlements. Keeping those dates and measurements distinct
allows an actual later grant or settlement disclosure to narrow the histories without overwriting
the reported EPS or inventing a missing event.
10.3
A plan’s capacity is different from an employee’s award
The share register has already separated awards, vesting and common shares issued. The
documents governing an award explain why those quantities can move differently. Apple’s 4
March 2022 Form 8-K supplies the 2022 Employee Stock Plan, a time-based restricted-stock-unit
form and a performance-award form as Exhibits 10.1–10.3. These are the counterparts of three
references in the annual report. The filing reports shareholder approval and effectiveness on 4
March 2022. The plan also records Board adoption on 9 November 2021 and uses that earlier
date as its defined Effective Date when setting its term. The two dates perform different jobs;
replacing them with one undifferentiated date would lose information needed to interpret the
plan (Apple Inc., 2022, Items 5.02 and 5.07; Exhibit 10.1, Sections 1 and 15(a)).
