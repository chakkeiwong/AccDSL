borrowing or owner movements in this slice. Tax effects are uncomputed: the last row is a
pretax net-assets reconciliation, not after-tax equity, distributable profit or a complete statutory

Preserve both the cost origin and the cash movement
The low-output journal path starts with 66 kits costing 132. Debit raw materials and credit cash
132. The accident debits abnormal-loss expense 12 and credits raw materials 12. Transferring
the 60 good kits to production moves raw-material cost 120 into finished goods; direct labor 60
and variable overhead 60 enter the same goods account against cash. No raw materials remain,
and the goods initially carry 240.
The fixed costs first enter a production clearing account: debit it 120 against cash and 60
against accumulated depreciation. Then debit finished goods 108 and unallocated-overhead
expense 72, crediting the clearing account 180. Its balance is now zero, and finished goods cost
348. The separate administration and selling entry debits expense 18 against cash. On delivery,
debit cash and credit revenue 400, then debit sold-stock expense and credit finished goods 232.
The final 116 is the cost of 20 unsold goods, not a residual chosen to make the books balance.
Cash receipts of 400 less payments of 132 + 60 + 60 + 120 + 18 = 390 increase cash by 10.
The whole factory’s depreciation is 60 even though part remains in inventory. To reconcile
pretax profit with operating cash, add that noncash consumption and subtract the full inventory
increase:
∆cash = 66 + 60 −116 = 10.
