9.13.4
Contract costs survive more than one service period
IFRS 15.91–104 first distinguishes eligible contract costs from amounts governed by another
standard. Acquisition costs must be incremental and recoverable. Fulfilment costs outside
other standards must relate directly to the contract, create or enhance resources for future
performance and be recoverable. Waste, past service and the specified administrative costs are
expensed. The event records these findings before calculating the posting.
For example, expense waste 5 and capitalise eligible fulfilment cost 100 for five equal service
units. Delivery of one unit amortises 20, leaving 80. After other applicable asset impairments
have been recognised, credit-adjusted remaining consideration 70 less direct future cost 20
supports a contract-cost asset of 50: recognise impairment 30. Delivery of another unit amortises
50/4 = 12.5, leaving 37.5. Without the impairment, the unamortised balance would now be 60.
If circumstances improve enough to support 60, reversal is limited to 60 −37.5 = 22.5; the final
three units amortise 60.
That ceiling needs a separate no-impairment balance, not merely the present asset. Subsequent
CGU assessment under IFRS 15.103 remains required after the component test. A successful
component calculation does not establish that the wider CGU has no impairment.
The lost asset and the obligation still to be met
Writing down a contract-cost asset does not necessarily recognize the whole loss on the contract.
The asset represents expenditure already incurred. The company may still have to spend more to
perform than the customer will pay. IAS 37.66–69 requires a provision for that remaining onerous
obligation, after recognizing impairment of assets used in fulfilling the contract (European
Commission, 2023a).
Suppose a company has cash 200, an eligible unamortized contract-cost asset 20 and capital 220.
One service remains to be delivered at completion. The customer will pay 100; incremental
future cash costs are 100, and an allocation of other directly related contract cash costs adds 30.
The existing asset’s amortization is separate from those future payments. There are no advances,
taxes, foreign exchange or reimbursements, and the time value of money is immaterial. Assume
the required other-asset impairment tests have been completed and the later CGU assessment
finds no additional loss. These are necessary supplied facts, not consequences of the calculation.
