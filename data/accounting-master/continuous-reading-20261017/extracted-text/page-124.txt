Use a synthetic manufacturer in calendar 2024 and the retained AASB 102 compilation dated 31
December 2021. Its documented application interval includes that year; subsequent amendment
and actual-entity admission remain separate. The company makes one ordinary manufactured
product, with no opening stock, work in progress, joint products or normal production loss. One
material kit produces one good unit. A kit costs 2; direct labor costs 1 and variable production
overhead costs 1 per good unit, all paid in cash. These goods are outside the agricultural,
financial-instrument and broker-trader exceptions. Periodic weighted average is used for this
homogeneous batch under paragraphs 25–27.
The fixed 180 consists of cash factory management and maintenance of 120 and depreciation of
60. Opening factory PPE is 600, with ten remaining years, zero residual value and admitted
uniform time-based consumption. There is one component and recoverability has been separately
established. The depreciation method therefore remains unchanged across the output scenarios.
Paragraph 12 includes factory depreciation and production administration among production
overheads; AASB 116.48–49 allows depreciation to enter another asset’s carrying amount when
appropriate (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022b). Recording it in inventory does
not make it a second cash payment.
Keep excluded costs visible. A separately documented accident destroys six unused kits costing
12 before labor or conversion. They are additional to the kits used for good output and have
no salvage value. Unrelated head-office administration of 10 and period selling services of 8
are also paid in cash. The abnormal loss and these unrelated expenses do not bring the good
units to their present location and condition, so paragraphs 10–16 exclude them from inventory.
Factory management and head-office administration cannot be classified solely from the word
administration.
All completed goods have supported net realisable value of 10 per unit, with no further completion
or sale costs. The example therefore still applies the lower-of-cost-and-NRV requirement rather
than suspending it. Delivered units sell for cash of 10 each under an approved, enforceable
single-product contract: rights and payment terms are identified, collection is probable, the
contract has commercial substance, and delivery, acceptance, title and control transfer coincide.
There are no remaining promises, returns or concessions. These facts admit the existing AASB
15.9 and 31–38 delivery method (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022e); cash receipt
by itself would not do so.
Allocate the cost that production can carry
Let Q denote good completed units and S the units sold, with 0 ≤S ≤Q. For this single cost
pool, normal capacity is 100 and fixed overhead is 180. Each good unit first receives its direct
and variable cost of 2 + 1 + 1 = 4. When output is low, the fixed allocation is 1.8 per unit;
when output is abnormally high, the total allocation is capped at the 180 actually incurred.
For Q > 0, this gives

expensed now.
This compact formula implements the stated homogeneous, constant-cost
example. Multi-product pools, work in progress or changed production resources require their
own allocation. With no output, there is no unit cost to divide by: stock is zero and all 180 is
current expense.
