Tax presentation preserves the same legal distinctions. The parent’s deferred asset cannot be
netted against the subsidiary’s current liability. Current tax offset requires an enforceable right
and an intention to settle net or simultaneously. Deferred tax offset also requires the same
authority and either the same taxable entity or the specified settlement intention across the
relevant future periods (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 12.71–76). Consolidation alone
supplies none of these conditions. Each close therefore carries the loss’s owner, authority, expiry,
supported recovery and rate evidence alongside the financial balances. Multiple loss vintages,
different recovery-rate schedules and tax arising in other comprehensive income or equity require
those additional origins before this successor can represent them.
2.8
A bank and insurer share a group close
A bank’s loan receipts, an insurer’s claims and a parent’s guarantee can all affect the same
group’s cash without belonging to the same legal taxpayer or the same shareholders. Consider
a parent P that owns 80% of bank B and insurer I. Control and equal ordinary ownership
rights are supplied facts. P reports in USD; both subsidiaries keep their books in a foreign
currency, FCU. They apply the same retained historical EU accounting profile. Their opening is
1 January 2025; the two intervals close on 1 January 2026 and 1 January 2027. Events described
as first- or second-period events occur at those respective closing dates. This explicit calendar
also fixes which ownership fraction receives each period’s profit.
P begins with cash 500, investments 80 in each subsidiary and an external financial-guarantee
liability 6, so its equity is 654. B has cash 300, customer deposits 200 and equity 100. I has
cash 200, insurance liability 100 and equity 100. Of I’s cash,20 is a demand deposit at B; the
same deposit is included in B’s liability 200. The deposit is identified by its creditor I, debtor
B, currency, amount and matching closing dates. No interest or movement occurs in it during
these two periods. Opening FX is 1 USD per FCU.
The group therefore starts with cash 500+300+200−20 = 980, liabilities 200+100+6−20 = 286,
and equity 694. Eliminating the parent’s investments leaves NCI 20 + 20 = 40, alongside the
parent’s equity 654. The two component calculators’ notional funding amounts do not become
new legal capital. Only their identified loan and insurance movements enter B’s and I’s books.
Adding the isolated component balance sheets would answer a different question.
