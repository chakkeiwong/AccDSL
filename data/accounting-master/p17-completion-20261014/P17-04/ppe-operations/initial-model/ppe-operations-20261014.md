# PPE component replacement, disposal and revaluation

14 October 2026. P17-04; MC02/04/07/11/13–16/19/20/28/30.
This extends the existing cost/depreciation method with complete synthetic
operations. The two cases are separate companies and are expressed in exact
currency units before tax. They do not specify an asset valuation engine.

## Authority and admission

Retained AASB116 compilation 6, 31 December 2022, F2023C00192, supports the
Australian for-profit rules at 7/12–15/29–55/60–62/67–73. Its official retained
HTML gives annual starts from 1 January 2023 to before 1 January 2027. The
cover, comparison with IAS16, operative text and compilation/amendment tables
are inspected. The Australian not-for-profit class-offset exceptions at
Aus39.1/Aus40.1–2 are not the for-profit rules at 39–40.

AASB107 compilation 5, 31 December 2023, F2024C00050, supports cash-flow
presentation at 16(a)–(b), 18(b), 20 and 43. Despite the local `jun2023`
filename, its retained HTML interval is annual starts 1 January 2024 to before
1 January 2026. Accordingly cash-flow reports here cover calendar 2024/2025.
The revaluation case's 2023 history derives a 2024 opening under AASB116;
it is not a 2023 AASB107 presentation comparison. Selected source bytes,
passages and application metadata are retained in the case source manifest.

Admission requires the entity/book/context, relevant annual period, owned PPE
scope, class policy and membership, recognition assessment, available-use date,
reliable acquisition costs, consumption pattern, useful life and residual
estimate. Replacement also requires the old and new component identities and
the old carrying amount. Disposal requires evidence of control passing and
the amount/terms of consideration. Here consideration is cash with no selling
costs. Routine rental sales, sale-and-leaseback, held-for-sale measurement,
investment property, grants and special sector assets need their own methods.
Missing required facts produce a named unresolved input; they are never zero.

The examples assume administrative use, so depreciation is expense under 48;
manufacturing costs may instead enter inventory under 49. Each case assumes
no additional impairment after the required assessment. Actual impairment
inputs remain a separate AASB136 obligation. Income tax is uncomputed, not
assumed zero: 116.42 points revaluation tax effects to AASB112, including their
profit/OCI origins. These pretax slices do not close that obligation.

## Owned state and operation ordering

An asset register retains item and component ID, class, recognition and use
dates, gross carrying amount, accumulated depreciation/impairment, original
cost and cost-model history, remaining life, residual, consumption method,
active/disposal status, and the evidence for every estimate. Revaluation also
retains each asset's surplus credit, outstanding prior revaluation decrease
recognized in profit or loss, valuation date/value/source and class membership.
Class totals cannot replace that individual history for these for-profit cases.

| Operation | Required transition | Failure or unresolved result |
|---|---|---|
| Depreciate to event date | Consume the admitted portion of remaining benefits, preserving gross cost and accumulated charge | Missing date/life/method, negative remaining depreciable amount or unsupported production destination |
| Replace component | Derecognize the old remaining amount, recognize eligible new cost, activate the new component on its use date | Missing old identity/value, reuse of a disposed identity, or cost ineligible under 7/12–14 |
| Revalue a class | Validate all members and admitted values; update each asset's gross/accumulated amounts and individual profit/OCI history | Partial member set, unreliable/missing valuation, wrong entity regime or unsupported date |
| Dispose | Depreciate through control transfer, remove gross/accumulated amounts, recognize net proceeds less carrying amount | Control unestablished, unsupported consideration/scope, or duplicate disposal |
| Transfer surplus | On the elected occasion debit the asset's surplus and credit retained earnings, with no profit or cash effect | Transfer larger than retained surplus or attempted recycling through profit |

The normal authorization/EventBasis/journal/EconomicDelta procedure owns the
whole transition. Reject partial removal/replacement and stale or replayed
state changes under the common contract. This method supplies documentary
postings and state, not another runtime implementation or complete serialized
profile. A zero valuation adjustment still updates the class valuation record;
the state-only rule prevents a consumer from dropping that economic event.

## Cost-model company

At 1 January 2024, cash and contributed capital are 200. A cash purchase of
100 buys an administrative asset whose shell costs 80 and lasts eight years,
and whose separately identified old component costs 20 and lasts two years.
Both are immediately available for use, have zero residual value and consume
benefits evenly. Debit the two PPE costs 80/20 and credit cash 100. The full
year's depreciation is 80/8+20/2=20. Credit the two accumulated-depreciation
accounts 10 each. At 31 December 2024, cash is 100, gross PPE 100, accumulated
depreciation 20, net PPE 80 and equity 180 after expense 20.

On 1 January 2025 the old component is replaced before further use. Its
carrying amount is 20−10=10. Debit accumulated depreciation 10 and disposal
loss 10; credit old-component cost 20. The replacement costs 30, is paid in
cash and is available immediately for three years, with zero residual and
even use. Debit new PPE 30 and credit cash 30. Paragraphs 13 and 70 require
removal of the old component even if it was not originally separately
depreciated. Here the original component is known exactly. If it were unknown,
70 permits replacement cost as an indication of its original cost; it does
not equate today's replacement invoice to the old remaining carrying amount.
An estimate needs its own supported acquisition-date/consumption assumptions.

The 2025 charge is shell 80/8 plus replacement 30/3 =20. Before disposal on
31 December 2025, gross active PPE is 80+30=110, accumulated depreciation is
20+10=30, and net PPE is 80. Cash is 70 and equity is 150. The buyer then
obtains control and pays 90. Debit cash 90 and accumulated depreciation 30;
credit the two gross PPE accounts 110 and disposal gain 10. Under 68/71 this
is a gain, not ordinary revenue. All three component identities remain in the
register, with the old component and sold shell/replacement marked disposed.

| Pretax report | 2024 | 2025 including replacement and sale |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation expense | 20 | 20 |
| Replacement loss / sale gain | 0 / 0 | 10 / 10 |
| Profit / OCI | −20 / 0 | −20 / 0 |
| Closing cash / net PPE | 100 / 80 | 160 / 0 |
| Closing liabilities / equity | 0 / 180 | 0 / 160 |
| Operating cash, direct and indirect | 0 | 0 |
| Investing cash | −100 | −30+90=60 |

Indirect operating cash is −20+20=0 in 2024 and −20+20+10−10=0 in 2025.
The gain is removed from profit while the entire sale proceeds are investing
cash. Lifetime investing cash −40 matches the reduction of equity 40 once
the assets are gone. Retaining the old component after replacement overstates
PPE/equity by 10 immediately, even though each purchase entry still balances.
A separate ordinary servicing payment 4 is expense under 12: debit maintenance
4/credit cash 4 and classify its cash as operating. Capitalizing the same
service would balance but violate the admitted facts and overstate PPE/profit.

## Revaluation company and asset-specific history

Two administrative machines A and B are the entire class for which the
revaluation model is elected. Both are available on 1 January 2023; original
costs are 100/40, lives five/four years, residuals zero, and use is even.
Historical cash/capital before their purchase is 200; after purchase cash is
60. For every revaluation below, reliable fair values are supplied as explicit
synthetic assumptions. Paragraphs 29/31/36–38 require the whole class and
sufficiently current valuations; they do not supply the missing valuations.

At 31 December 2023, depreciation 20/10 leaves carrying amounts 80/30. Values
120/24 then increase A by 40 and decrease B by 6. Under 35(b), first eliminate
accumulated depreciation against each gross amount. Increase A's adjusted
cost by 40 and credit its surplus through OCI; reduce B by 6 and debit
revaluation loss. Because B has no own surplus, A's surplus cannot absorb
B's loss. The 2024 opening is cash 60, PPE 144, capital 200, retained earnings
−36 and surplus 40: equity 204. The 2023 net class increase 34 is not all OCI.

For asset i let u be a positive revaluation increase, d a positive decrease,
L its outstanding earlier revaluation decrease in profit or loss, and S its
surplus credit. The routing under 39–40 is:

```
increase: profit gain = min(u,L); OCI increase = u−min(u,L)
          L' = L−min(u,L); S' = S+u−min(u,L)
decrease: OCI decrease = min(d,S); profit loss = d−min(d,S)
          S' = S−min(d,S); L' = L+d−min(d,S)
```

These formulas concern revaluation history, not the separate AASB136 impairment
reversal ceiling. Here no surplus is transferred during use. If such a transfer
were elected, the surplus available for later decreases would change. Keep
each transfer and original-cost depreciation history rather than reconstructing
the available surplus from fair values alone.

In 2024 depreciation is 120/4=30 for A and 24/3=8 for B, leaving 90/16.
Revalue the entire class to 45/22. A's decrease 45 uses its surplus 40 through
OCI and recognizes the remaining loss 5 in profit. B's increase 6 reverses
its own previous loss 6 through profit. Gross balances become 45/22 with
accumulated depreciation eliminated. Surplus is zero; A now carries a
revaluation-loss history 5, and B none. Profit is −38−5+6=−37 and OCI −40.
Cash 60 plus PPE 67 equals equity 127, down 77 from the opening 204.

In 2025 depreciation is 45/3=15 and 22/2=11, leaving 30/11. Values 50/11
increase A by 20: reverse its profit loss 5 and put the remaining 15 in OCI.
B has no monetary change but its valuation is still included. Before sale,
PPE is 61, cash 60 and equity 121. Immediately afterward on 31 December 2025,
the buyer obtains both machines and pays 55/12. Remove PPE 50/11 and recognize
gains 5/1. Elect the permitted transfer of A's surplus 15 directly to retained
earnings. The transfer is not income, is not a new OCI item and creates no cash.

| Pretax report | 2024 | 2025 including sale |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation | 38 | 26 |
| Revaluation profit loss / gain | 5 / 6 | 0 / 5 |
| Disposal gain | 0 | 6 |
| Profit / OCI | −37 / −40 | −15 / 15 |
| Closing cash / PPE | 60 / 67 | 127 / 0 |
| Closing retained earnings / surplus | −73 / 0 | −73 / 0 |
| Closing equity | 127 | 127 |
| Operating cash | −37+38+5−6=0 | −15+26−5−6=0 |
| Investing cash | 0 | 67 |

In 2025 profit reduces retained earnings by 15 and the direct surplus transfer
increases it by 15, leaving −73. OCI 15 offsets the year's loss in total
comprehensive income; the transfer itself leaves total equity unchanged.
Recycling the surplus through income would falsely raise profit from −15 to 0.
Reporting only net class movements, guessing a missing valuation, or silently
switching to not-for-profit class offsets invalidates the method despite a
balanced trial balance. The exact checker retains the complete journals,
individual asset histories, statement totals and adverse dispositions.

## Remaining work and strongest competing interpretation

The plausible wrong alternatives are to expense every replacement, capitalize
every repair, net class revaluations through one reserve, or report disposal
cash as revenue. Distinguishing facts are the recognition/service nature,
old component's remaining amount, for-profit regime and same-asset history,
control transfer and ordinary/rental disposal scope. Those facts are explicit
here; missing actual facts remain unresolved in a real company.

Further initial-cost components, pre-use proceeds, major-inspection estimates,
disposal groups, asset reclassifications and restoration/impairment/tax
interactions retain their own residuals. The current unit does not close all
P17-04 operations. Full company assembly, reusable serialized profiles,
whole-manuscript review and independent/human acceptance remain later work.
