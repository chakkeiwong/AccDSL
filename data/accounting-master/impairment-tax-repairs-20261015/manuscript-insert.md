## Impairment across a group and the origin of tax

The following methods use the retained historical EU 2023/1803 text and the
separately retained November 2023 IAS 12 amendment. Each example is conditional
on its declared authority, perimeter, allocation and tax assumptions. These
texts do not establish current issuer compliance or cross-GAAP equivalence.

### Testing assets that serve more than one business

IAS 36.100–102 identifies corporate assets by their contribution to multiple
cash-generating units without independent cash inflows. Do not invent a separate
recoverable amount for a headquarters just to make every row independently
testable. When a reasonable consistent allocation to a unit exists, include
that share with the unit. When it does not, first test the individual unit
excluding the corporate asset, recognize that loss, then test the smallest
larger group to which an allocation is reasonable and consistent. The second
test uses the already adjusted carrying amounts.

The impairment record retains the hierarchy of units, legal/group/perimeter
identities, corporate asset links, allocation basis, carrying and recoverable
amount dates, goodwill allocation, individual floors and prior loss origins.
The calculation is postorder: individual tests precede wider group tests.
IAS 36.104 applies loss first to goodwill, then pro rata to other asset carrying
amounts. Under paragraph 105, no asset falls below its measurable fair value less
costs of disposal, determinable value in use, or zero, whichever is highest.
Allocate a capped asset's unabsorbed loss again over the eligible remainder.
Do not create a balancing liability for an unallocated remainder unless another
standard actually requires it. Revised depreciation follows the adjusted amounts.

For a conditional example, unit A has two inseparable assets of 40 each, unit B
two of 60 each, and headquarters 40. Headquarters cannot be reasonably allocated
to either unit alone, but it serves their combined group. Unit A's recoverable
amount 70 first causes loss 10, allocated 5 to each A asset. B's recoverable amount 130
causes no loss. The group's adjusted carrying amount is then 70+120+40=230.
Given group recoverable amount 207, additional loss 23 allocates 3.5 to each A
asset,6 to each B asset and 4 to headquarters. Closing amounts 31.5,31.5,54,54,36
sum to 207. No individual asset's value in use or fair value less disposal costs
is determinable in this example, so only the zero floor binds. The unit-level
recoverable amount 70 is not an individual A-asset floor.

Using the original group carrying amount 240 in the second test would repeat
the first loss 10. Treating headquarters as costless would omit its exposure.
These are different errors even if both versions produce balanced journals.
The calculation supplies an allocation method, not a valuation method for the
assumed recoverable amounts.

### Partial goodwill and the non-controlling interest

IAS 36 AppendixC distinguishes the consolidated carrying amount from the amount
used for an impairment test. When acquisition-date NCI was measured as its
proportionate share of identifiable net assets, the reported goodwill omits NCI
goodwill, while the unit's recoverable amount includes its contribution. C 4
therefore requires a notional gross-up before the test. C 8 prevents recognizing
an impairment of goodwill that was never recognized. Neither the notional
goodwill nor its notional impairment is posted as an asset or expense.

Retain the acquisition's NCI measurement election, ownership and profit-allocation
history, unit allocation and evidence for the notional amount. Division of parent
goodwill by ownership is a conditional illustration when proportional goodwill
is justified; it must not silently assume away a control premium or differing
synergies. For a subsidiary that is itself a unit, C 6 allocates loss on the same
basis as profit. C 7 separately allocates a larger unit's goodwill losses by the
parts' relative goodwill and identifiable-asset losses by their relative net
identifiable carrying amounts, then allocates within each part. Preserve the
NCI-bearing and parent-only parts rather than applying one ownership fraction
to the entire group.

Assume the parent owns 80%, acquired proportionate-share NCI, has recognized
goodwill 40 and supports proportional notional goodwill 50. Identifiable unit
assets carry 200. If recoverable amount 220, test 250 against 220 and allocate loss 30
entirely to goodwill. The recognized goodwill loss is 24; the NCI notional loss 6
is not recorded. Reported goodwill falls 40→16 and reported assets total 216;
adding the remaining notional NCI goodwill 4 gives test carrying amount 220.
Demanding the reported amount equal 220 would improperly capitalize that 4.

If recoverable amount instead falls to 180 in a separate branch, test loss 70
removes notional goodwill 50 then identifiable assets 20. Recognized loss is 60:
parent goodwill 40 plus asset loss 20. Of the recognized loss,56 belongs to the
parent and 4 to NCI. The unrecognized NCI-goodwill loss 10 never enters either
profit or NCI equity. Closing identifiable assets 180 and goodwill 0 reconcile
the reported decrease 240→180. Goodwill impairment cannot subsequently reverse
under IAS 36.124; a rising valuation is not a new recognized goodwill asset.

A larger-unit branch adds a wholly owned part with goodwill 20 and identifiable
assets 100. Total test carrying amount 370 includes subsidiary notional goodwill 50
and parent-only goodwill 20. At recoverable amount 270, loss 100 first removes
all goodwill 70, then allocates 30 to identifiable assets in their 200:100 ratio.
Recognized loss 90 comprises goodwill 60 and assets 30; attribution is parent 86
and NCI 4. The notional 10 remains unrecognized. Asset floors and allocation of
remaining goodwill must be respected when a branch does not exhaust goodwill.

### Revalued impairment and its related deferred tax

IAS 36.60–64 treats a revalued asset's impairment as a revaluation decrease:
charge the same asset's existing surplus through OCI before charging profit.
Afterward, determine deferred tax from the revised carrying amount and tax base.
IAS 12.61 A attaches current and deferred tax to the related item even when the
item was recognized in a different period. Preserve the origin of each temporary
difference so a later tax-rate change does not move all tax to profit by default.
Where exact attribution is exceptionally impracticable, paragraph 63 permits a
reasonable allocation or a more appropriate method; document that circumstance.

Take an asset carrying 140, tax base 100, gross same-asset revaluation surplus 40
and related deferred tax liability 10 at a supplied 25% rate. There is no passage
of time or tax-base movement. Recoverable amount 90 causes loss 50: OCI 40 and
profit 10. The old DTL 10 reverses in OCI. A deductible difference 10 gives DTA 2.5
in profit only if recovery is probable; that condition is supplied here. Net
OCI loss 30 plus net profit loss 7.5 equals the net-asset decrease 37.5, from
asset 140 less DTL 10=130 to asset 90 plus DTA 2.5=92.5. Offset presentation is
not assumed: DTA and DTL eligibility must follow the applicable jurisdictional
and legal-offset rules.

In the same no-time-passage illustration, recoverable amount later recovers to 130.
IAS 36.117–120 limits reversal to the no-impairment amount and reverses the prior
profit loss 10 first, with the remaining 30 through OCI. Reversing DTA 2.5 charges
profit; establishing DTL 7.5 charges OCI. Net profit gain 7.5 and OCI gain 22.5
sum to 30, the increase 92.5→122.5. A revaluation increase beyond the no-impairment
amount is a separate IAS 16 event, not unlimited reversal; future depreciation
must update both the reversal cap and carrying amount.

Separately, if the original carrying 140/tax base 100 asset's admitted future tax
rate changes 25%→30% before any impairment, DTL rises 10→12. The additional tax 2
relates to the earlier revaluation and is charged to OCI, not profit. The example
assumes enacted/substantively enacted rates and the same manner of recovery;
it does not infer tax law or settlement method from the accounting carrying value.

### Acquisition and investment tax are separate questions

IAS 12.66 requires acquisition-date temporary differences on identifiable assets
and liabilities to enter acquisition accounting, subject to recognition rules.
For a simple full acquisition paying 150, identifiable fair-valued assets 150 with
tax base 100 and an admitted 25% rate create DTL 12.5. Net identifiable assets 137.5
give goodwill 12.5. There is no recursive DTL on the initial recognition of that
goodwill under paragraph 15(a). This exception is not permission to omit the DTL
on the identifiable asset. The treatment of an acquirer's own previously
unrecognized DTA, and acquiree benefits recognized later, follows paragraphs 67–68;
the former does not automatically change acquisition goodwill.

Outside-basis differences on investments are tracked separately from temporary
differences inside the subsidiary's asset book. Under IAS 12.39, a taxable
difference avoids DTL only to the extent the investor controls reversal timing
and reversal is probably absent in the foreseeable future. Both conditions are
required. For a taxable difference 60 and supplied 20% tax on the expected reversal,
DTL is 12 when the exception does not hold; it is not recognized where both
conditions demonstrably hold. Unknown control or distribution intent is unresolved,
not evidence for the exception. An associate investor usually cannot infer control
from a minority shareholding. Under paragraph 44, a deductible difference requires
probable foreseeable reversal and available taxable profits to recognize a DTA.
The source gives asymmetric tests; copying the liability exception to assets is
wrong. Rates and recovery paths follow paragraphs 46–51.

### Pillar Two is not another deferred-tax rate

The original September 2023 compilation predates the specific amendment adopted
by Commission Regulation(EU)2023/2468 of 8 November 2023, published 9 November 2023.
Its Article 3 enters into force the day after publication. Article 2 and the annex
state the application and retrospective rules. The retained amendment adds
IAS 12.4 A/88 A–88 D/98 M. For legislation within 4 A, an entity neither recognizes nor
discloses information about related deferred-tax assets/liabilities, and discloses
that it applied this exception. It separately discloses current Pillar Two tax
expense/income. Enacted or substantively enacted legislation not yet effective
requires known or reasonably estimable exposure information; unavailable
information requires the specified explanation and assessment progress.

The record therefore separates ordinary current/deferred tax from Pillar Two
current tax, exception status, applicability authority and exposure disclosures.
A supplied in-scope current top-up tax 7 creates current expense/payable 7 and
later payment 7. A hypothetical Pillar Two temporary difference 40 does not create
a DTL 6 by multiplying by 15%. That is the wrong target under the exception.
Ordinary IAS 12 temporary differences continue under their own rules. The current
top-up amount 7 is an illustrative input, not a calculation of an actual GloBE
liability. Jurisdictional enactment, group thresholds, covered taxes, allocation,
safe harbours and current-law measurement remain separate required facts/methods
before actual application. The inspected amendment supplies the accounting
boundary; it does not itself calculate tax due for a company.
