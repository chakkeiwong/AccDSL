A net hedge still needs its gross transactions
A company expecting dollar sales and dollar expenses may manage only their net currency
exposure. Hedge accounting still needs both gross amounts and the periods in which they affect
earnings. IFRS 9.6.6.1 and B6.6.1–8 require individually eligible items and actual group risk
management. For offsetting cash-flow positions, the designated risk must be foreign currency risk,
and the designation must specify nature, volume and the relevant reporting periods. An abstract
label such as “net dollars 20” is insufficient (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.6.6.1–3,
B6.6.1–8).
Use a euro-functional company with highly probable external cash sales of USD 100 and
payments of USD 80 for services consumed immediately. The designation identifies those
transactions, their earnings periods and a forward to sell USD 20 for EUR 20. Assume actual
net risk management, an eligible external derivative, contemporaneous documentation and all
effectiveness conditions. Interest, discount, forward points and credit effects are zero in this
illustration; the whole forward is designated. At inception the rate is EUR 1 per dollar and
the derivative is worth zero. At execution the rate is EUR 1.10, so sales are EUR 110, service
expense EUR 88 and the forward loses EUR 2. No inventory asset is created in this example.
