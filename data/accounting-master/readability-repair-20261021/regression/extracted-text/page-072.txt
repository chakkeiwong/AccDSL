A treaty correction separates knowledge from measurement
In Section 12.6, treaty R entered before group B’s recognition produces an initial reinsurance
asset of 85, initial equity of 85 and year-one equity of 83. If R instead enters immediately
afterward, with all other stated facts unchanged, the initial asset is 70, initial equity is 70
and year-one equity is 74.75. The loss-recovery recognition condition causes the difference
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraph 66A and B119C). Both histories
eventually reach cash of 88.3 on the same supplied claim and recovery path. Agreement on that
terminal amount therefore cannot establish correct recognition timing.
Now give the two order assertions different information dates. A constructed January disclosure
says the treaty preceded recognition; a February correction says it followed recognition and
explicitly replaces that order assertion. A latest-available query cut off in January retains
the January assertion. The February record is known to be too late and cannot suppress it.
A February query may select the correction and produce the later-entry measurement. The
correction concerns the order assertion for B and R; it does not replace every other assertion in
the company’s report.
A competing record with unknown public availability has a different effect from a record known
to be published after the cutoff. The known late record is excluded. The unknown record
leaves an unresolved dependency if it might affect the requested conclusion, even while an older
eligible assertion is displayed. Similarly, an asserted publication date without evidence linking
the retained bytes to that date does not establish historical admission. If only the common
calendar date of treaty entry and group recognition is known, their order remains unresolved;
the model cannot choose 85 merely because that treatment is more favorable.
An extraction correction is different again. A source may consistently say that R followed
recognition while its first accepted extraction mistakenly records the opposite. Correcting the
reading creates a new reading and review decision linked to the same source bytes. The old
result remains retrievable as a historical error; reproducing it does not make it correct. An
issuer’s correction instead changes the source assertion and usually its bytes. The system records
which change occurred before deciding which derived results require reassessment.
Invalidate the selection, preserve the committed event
A result depends on the candidates considered by its selector as well as the candidate ultimately
chosen. Adding a possible competing treaty record changes that candidate population. Reusing
a cached selection solely because its chosen assertion identifier is unchanged would miss the
new obligation. The proposed dependency graph therefore includes a version for candidate-set
membership. The change propagates from membership through selection and interpretation
to measurement and the resulting statement. A fresh selection may exclude a known late
competitor and calculate the same amount, but it records that decision under a new view.
Newly created descendants must check their complete ancestry too. Otherwise a report created
after an invalidation scan could inherit a stale measurement without appearing on the scan’s
earlier list. In contrast, a bank result whose complete dependency set contains no changed
insurance inputs can remain reusable. Neither a shared company label nor an unchanged
displayed value establishes independence. Missing ancestors leave reuse unestablished; a cycle
or missing required reference rejects the affected dependency chain.
