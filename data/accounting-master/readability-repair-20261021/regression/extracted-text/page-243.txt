The following method uses that amendment, rather than extending the older paragraph 26
rule about temporary lack of exchangeability to later periods (European Commission, 2024,
Article 2; IAS 21.8–8B, 19A, 26, 60L–M).
Record the entity, both currencies, measurement date, purpose, amount needed, amount
obtainable, permitted mechanisms and their enforceability, normal administrative delay and
evidence for the finding. The assessment concerns ability, including indirect exchange, rather than
management’s intention. For individual transactions, the purpose is realization or settlement of
those transactions; for presentation-currency translation it is realization or settlement of net
assets or liabilities; for a foreign operation it is realization or settlement of the net investment.
Assess the relevant direction and purpose separately. Obtaining only an insignificant amount
fails the exchangeability test. The ratio 5 available to 1000 needed is evidence for a judgment,
not a threshold supplied by this design (European Commission, 2024, IAS 21.A2–A10).
When exchangeability fails, IAS 21.19A requires an estimate reflecting an orderly exchange
between market participants at the measurement date under prevailing economic conditions.
A rate observable for another purpose, or the first subsequent exchange rate, can be used
without adjustment only if it meets that objective. Examine incentives or penalties, restricted
purposes, interventions, update frequency, elapsed time and inflation. Another estimation
technique may adjust observable rates, including those from mechanisms that would not
establish exchangeability. It must preserve the inputs, adjustments and reasoning supporting
the measurement-date objective. Absence of that support leaves the rate unresolved; the design
does not manufacture a universal estimator (European Commission, 2024, IAS 21.A11–A17).
For a conditional arithmetic illustration, assume an admitted estimate of USD 0.08 per local unit
for a foreign operation’s net investment. Local net assets 1000 translate to 80. A preferential
import rate 0.10 would produce 100; a later rate 0.09 following changed conditions would
produce 90. Neither alternative is interchangeable with the admitted estimate merely because it
is observable. This calculation exposes the consequences of the choice; the numerical differences
do not prove that 0.08 meets paragraph 19A.
Initial application has its own journal origin. Suppose an affected monetary payable of 100
foreign units moves from 10 to 12 functional units per foreign unit at initial application. The
liability increase 200 adjusts opening retained earnings under paragraph 60M(a). In a separate
nonhyperinflationary foreign-operation translation example, changing the rate for net assets
1000 from 0.10 to 0.08 reduces translated equity 20; paragraph 60M(b) assigns the initial effect
to accumulated translation differences. Hyperinflationary functional-currency equity items also
use the estimated initial rate. Comparatives are not restated for this amendment. Keep these
transition adjustments distinct from subsequent-period profit or OCI (European Commission,
2024, IAS 21.60M).
The accompanying disclosures identify affected currencies and restrictions, transactions and
carrying amounts, rates, estimation methods and assumptions, and exposure to risk. A foreign
operation also needs the additional information in paragraph A20, including relevant support
arrangements (European Commission, 2024, IAS 21.57A–B, A18–A20). These methods extend
the documentary group design. They do not silently add fields or supported events to the closed
GroupForeignOperation/1 exchange profile; its successor must explicitly bind the additional
histories and assessments before an implementation can claim these operations.
