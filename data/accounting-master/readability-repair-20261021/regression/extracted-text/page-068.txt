The page numbering does not explain it. Printed page 156 is physical PDF page 158 and
contains management’s discussion and analysis. Printed page 163 is physical page 165 and
begins the report of the independent registered public accounting firm. The two-page offset
applies to both locations. Item 15’s explicit reference to the material listed in Item 8 also rules
out treating it as a direction to a broader discussion beginning in MD&A (JPMorgan Chase &
Co., 2024, printed pp. 156 and 163). For this document and target, the page-156 direction is
wrong. The supported navigation correction is page 163; it is a separate interpretation of the
retained document, not a replacement for what Item 15 actually says.
The review must retain those two meanings of correctness. The source record keeps the PDF,
both located sentences, their unchanged extractions and images of the referenced pages. A
proposed extraction decision can accept 156 as the faithfully transcribed number. A translation
can likewise record that Item 15 directs the reader there. Neither decision accepts the proposition
that the auditor’s report actually starts there. That target proposition is refuted by the inspected
page. A separate navigation reading points to 163, supported by Item 8 and the report heading.
This reading permits locating the report; accounting admission still requires the relevant financial
assertions, contexts and methods.
Consider the resulting review sequence as a specification example. Propose both literal read-
ings and retain their common target and document identity. Accept their transcription and
attribution at the stated layers. Reject 156 as the verified start location, keeping the rejected
proposal and its reason, and propose 163 for navigation admission. The source disagreement
remains visible after that repair. It does not require keeping 156 and 163 as equally admissible
start locations: an AcceptAlternative action for 156 at the target-location layer would fail the
evidence check. Uncertainty is preserved when evidence leaves alternatives open; a refuted
reading is preserved as review history.
Different queries now have different exact answers. Asking what Item 15 says still returns 156,
with its locator and the conflict annotation. Asking for the supported start of the auditor’s
report returns printed 163, physical 165, with both supporting passages. Asking whether a
particular accounting treatment is valid remains outside this navigation decision. Nor can a
search confined to page 156 conclude that the entire filing contains no auditor’s report. Its
proper negative result is only that the report was not found on the selected page.
Suppose an earlier analysis had nevertheless accepted 156 as the correct start. This is a
counterfactual mistaken decision, not an observed human review in this project or a permitted
new admission after the target check. Repair creates a new reading and an explicit Supersede
decision in a new evidence view. The old accepted decision, its reason and its results remain
available under the old view. The corrected view cannot reuse a cached result whose justification
still depends on that old navigation choice.
That dependency includes the pages selected for a search. A cached “not found on the selected
page” answer must be recomputed when the admitted location changes, even if its output
contains no extracted assertion from the new page. A coverage conclusion derived from that
answer changes with it. By contrast, the literal Item 15 quotation remains unchanged. A
separately located statement value whose provenance does not depend on the repaired reference
may also be reused after its other dependencies are checked. An annotated display of the
unchanged quotation still needs to refresh when its conflict or review status changes; preserving
the text does not preserve every display derived from it. The correction therefore invalidates
dependent navigation, search and coverage results; it does not mechanically invalidate every
financial amount in the filing.
