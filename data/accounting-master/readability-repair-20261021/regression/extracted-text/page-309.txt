12.17.3
Tomorrow’s information changes tomorrow’s risk
A cost-of-capital calculation needs a sequence of future capital amounts. It is tempting to
obtain each amount by taking today’s simulated payments, removing the payments made before
that future date, and measuring the spread of what remains. But the insurer will then know
more than it knows today. Some of today’s uncertainty will have become information, even
when no cash has yet been paid.
Consider an authored two-period example with one payment at final settlement. The ultimate
payment U is 0, 2, 8 or 14, each with probability 1/4. After the first period the insurer learns
whether the payment belongs to the lower pair or the upper pair. Within either pair the two
possibilities remain equally likely. Figure 12.1 separates the information arrival from final
