The June maturity disclosure adds a different measurement of the obligation. Under its heading
for undiscounted contractual and expected cash flows, the lease-liability row gives 1,515 within
three months, 5,885 in three to twelve months, 11,048 in one to five years and 485 beyond
five years. Those numeric cells sum to 18,933; the immediate and no-term columns contain
dashes (Ping An, 2019, pp. 164–165). The first two dated buckets sum to 7,400, but disclose
no payment dates within those buckets. The 18,933 total and the 17,212 carrying liability
can coexist because they measure different things. Their difference, 1,721, is not the interest
expense for the half-year and does not by itself determine a discount rate.
Even on the post-adoption basis, a change in a balance is not a complete account of the
transactions that produced it. The right-of-use asset falls by 17,390 −18,500 = −1,110 and
the liability by 17,212 −17,667 = −455. Additions, remeasurements, disposals and other
movements can accompany depreciation or payments. The first difference therefore does not
identify depreciation, and the second does not identify cash paid. The consolidated cash-flow
statement’s finance-leasing business borrowing and receivable lines concern the group’s financing
activities as a business; their labels do not make them payments on its own lessee obligations
(Ping An, 2019, pp. 106–107).
The same care prevents a false narrative conflict. In its major-contract discussion, the company
says that it has no reportable custody, contracting, lease or other major-contract matters for
the period (Ping An, 2019, p. 86). Preserving the company reference and the reportable-major-
contract qualification gives a different assertion from saying that the consolidated group has
no leases. Removing those qualifiers would create the apparent contradiction with the balance
sheet.
For the proposed representation, the useful result is a set of dated, source-supported balances,
policy elections and maturity restrictions. A query for the reported June carrying liability
can return 17,212 with its basis and precision. A query for future scheduled payments must
keep the disclosed maturity buckets and leave their internal dates unresolved. A query for the
complete transition journal or the half-year’s lessee cash payments must retain the missing
inputs. These distinct answers let actual disclosures constrain histories without pretending that
every balanced completion is the company’s history.
