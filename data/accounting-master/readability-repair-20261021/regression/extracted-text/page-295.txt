12.11.2
A changed loss estimate affects both groups without netting them
Before any service or time passes, reduce expected claims on the covered contracts from 48
to 44, leaving other assumptions unchanged. Issued fulfilment liability falls from 120 to 116.
Paragraph 50(b) first reduces the loss component, from 20 to 16, and recognises a loss reversal
of 4; there is no new issued CSM while that loss component remains.
Expected held recovery falls from 24 to 22. Under paragraph 66(c)(i) (European Commission,
2023a, IFRS 17.66(c),66(bb),B119F), this change follows the underlying change that did not
adjust the issued CSM, so it reduces the held asset and creates expense 2 rather than being
hidden in held CSM. Held fulfilment asset becomes 22.5, cost CSM stays 6 and total held asset
becomes 28.5. The covered loss is now 4, so B119F’s recoverable loss-component limit is 2
and the loss-recovery component falls from 4 to 2. That component change tracks the same
recognised recovery loss; posting a second expense for it would double count the event. Equity
becomes 123.5 + 28.5 −116 = 36, an increase of 4 −2 = 2.
If all remaining services are then provided and claims equal these revised estimates, issued
liability 116 is released through insurance revenue 100 and loss reversal 16; claims expense
and payment are 114. Issued service-period profit is 2. Held coverage asset 28.5 is released
as premium allocation 26.5 and reversal of loss recovery 2, with incurred recovery 22 and its
collection recorded separately. Held service-period loss is 6.5. The entity ends with cash and
equity 123.5 −114 + 22 = 31.5 and no group balances. Initial combined loss 16, estimate-change
gain 2 and service-period loss 4.5 sum to lifetime loss 18.5, equal to the change from opening
equity 50. Reinsurance has not removed or netted away the issued obligation.
B119F requires later loss-recovery components to reflect changes in the underlying loss component
and never exceed the portion expected to be recovered. Track separately adjustments already
included in fulfilment-cash-flow changes and reversals under 66(bb) that are not. The cap
does not authorise inventing recovery income. Under paragraph 67, a change in reinsurer
non-performance risk does not adjust CSM as future service; preserve collateral and dispute
assumptions required by paragraph 63. Transferred-risk adjustment must be measured under
paragraph 64, not mechanically copied as a fraction of the issued adjustment.
12.11.3
Changed terms and changed estimates are different events
For a treaty change, retain the old and new legal terms, effective date, covered-contract links
and consideration. An exercise of a right already contained in the contract is not a modification
under paragraph 72. A new treaty requires its own recognition and grouping decision and
cannot be backdated to create initial-loss recovery. For a modification, apply paragraph 72’s
tests (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.72–77) to the actual changed contract: changed
scope or separated components, a substantially different boundary, a different initial group,
changed direct-participation status, or loss of eligibility for a previously used premium-allocation
approach can require derecognition and a new contract. If none applies, paragraph 73 treats
the resulting cash-flow changes as estimate changes, with the held-contract modifications in
paragraphs 63–68 where applicable.
For required derecognition, paragraphs 76–77 remove the affected cash flows and risk adjustment,
adjust the remaining group’s CSM under the applicable model and revise remaining coverage
units. A transfer or qualifying modification uses paragraph 77’s actual transfer consideration
or equivalent new-contract premium adjustment rather than simply deleting the old balance.
The equivalent premium is a required valuation input, not the old carrying amount chosen for
convenience. Missing terms or that valuation leaves the particular modification unresolved. The
generic tests identify the required procedure; they do not price an unspecified changed treaty.
