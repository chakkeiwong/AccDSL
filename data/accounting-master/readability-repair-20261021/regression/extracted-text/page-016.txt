2.5
One economic scenario, several complete futures
For 2025, assume demand for 30 units at price 10, unit purchase cost 5 and a target closing
inventory of eight units. Opening inventory is four units, so the purchase decision is 30+8−4 = 34
units, costing 170. Deliveries cost 150, leaving inventory of 40. Collect 90% of current sales and
10 of old receivables: 270 + 10 = 280, leaving receivables of 55. Pay old suppliers 30 and 130 of
the new purchases, leaving payables of 40. Payroll is 50, capital expenditure of 20 occurs at
year-end and dividends are 8.
These decisions are inputs to a conditional forecast. They are not estimates of demand or
optimal management responses. No new borrowing is available. Under the existing asset
schedules, depreciation is 10+10/5+12/12 = 13. The new year-end asset begins depreciation in
the next period. Let α = F/100. With the floating rate resetting to 7%, interest is I = 7 −2α.
Profit before tax is 300 −150 −50 −13 −I = 80 + 2α, giving tax 16 + 0.4α and profit 64 + 1.6α.

The joint symbolic result is cash 93.6 + 1.6α, profit 64 + 1.6α and equity 146.6 + 1.6α, with
the same α ∈[0, 1] throughout. Selecting the smallest cash and largest equity independently
would describe no feasible company. A requested distribution requires a measure over that same
historical family; the accounting equations do not supply one.
The later method schedules use the floating share x rather than the fixed share α used here:
x = 1 −α. Both parameterisations describe the same joint family. Its 2025 opening cash is 74.6
and equity is 90.6; the forecast spends 20 on period-end capital expenditure, distributes 8 and
settles its stipulated tax in the same year. These teaching assumptions are retained when the
later lease example introduces a different tax-payment schedule.
2.6
Keeping the same company through every calculation
The first inventory purchase shows what a reproducible company calculation must retain. Before
the purchase, Northstar has six units costing 30 and owes suppliers 20. It buys fourteen more
units for 70 on credit. The journal debits inventory and credits trade payables by 70; the
resulting inventory is twenty units costing 100, payables are 90, and cash remains 60. A balanced
journal accompanied by twenty-one units of inventory is wrong under the stipulated unit cost
of 5. The quantity change and the monetary posting must therefore be checked against the
same purchase and opening state.
