3.14
A revised fact changes a conclusion without rewriting its
history
A correction can change the amount that a model now explains while leaving both its earlier
explanation and the company’s committed journals intact. The bank and insurance examples
later in this proposal make the distinction concrete. The bank needs the credit-risk baseline of
its irrevocable commitment even after a loan is drawn. The insurer needs to know whether its
reinsurance treaty preceded the recognition of an onerous group. Neither fact can be replaced
by the time a document happens to arrive.
Consider a constructed bank record that supplies no original commitment baseline. The analyst
can retain its cash flows and conditional loss scenarios, but cannot establish the required
original-to-current credit comparison. A later record identifies the original commitment risk
and supports the separately supplied finding of significantly increased credit risk. With the
other assumptions in Section 11.6, the funded allowance is 4 and equity is 16. The additional
record resolves the missing history; it does not reset the baseline to the draw date. The relevant
AASB 9 provisions retain the irrevocable commitment as the initial comparison and treat the
resulting asset as its continuation (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, paragraph
5.5.6 and B5.5.47). The probability tree remains an authored assumption, and the credit finding
is not inferred merely because a probability doubled.
Suppose, for this constructed record, exact bytes were publicly available on 2 January, ingested
into the system on 5 January, and accepted for the required reading on 6 January. An investor-
information reconstruction with a 3 January cutoff may use those bytes if their public availability
is verified, even though this particular system obtained them later. A system-as-of query for 3
January cannot use them. On 5 January a raw system query may retrieve the document, but a
query for an accepted reading still lacks the 6 January decision. Later extraction is compatible
with the earlier investor cutoff only if its interpretation introduces no later information. These
dates are authored specification fixtures; they establish no actual historical availability.
