With unchanged expectations, first-year interest is 50 × 10% = 5, leaving carrying amount
55. Now suppose a supported credit reassessment raises expected terminal collection to 88.
With one year remaining, its present value at the original rate is 88/1.1 = 80. The increase
80 −55 = 25 is an impairment gain. The following year’s interest is 80 × 10% = 8, bringing
carrying amount to 88 if expectations remain unchanged. Improved credit does not replace the
original rate or switch this POCI asset to the ordinary gross-interest method. The cure rule in
paragraph 5.4.2 refers specifically to assets that became impaired after initial recognition under
paragraph 5.4.1(b).

The last expression compares current and initial expected shortfalls at the same reporting date.
It explains both signs. At year one, expected collection of 44 gives N1 = 40 and L1 = 15, an
impairment loss of 15. Expected collection of 88 instead gives N1 = 80 and L1 = −25, an
impairment gain of 25. That negative allowance is a favorable adjustment to the asset, not a
liability owed by the bank. Paragraph 5.5.14 expressly requires favorable lifetime-loss changes
to enter profit even when expected losses fall below those reflected at acquisition. Imposing a
zero floor would leave carrying amount at 55 and suppress that gain.
The passage of time must also respect the interest basis. Over the next unchanged year, gross
accretion is rG, allowance unwind is rL, and net interest is their difference:
rG −rL = r(G −L) = rN.
In the favorable case, these amounts are 5.5, −2.5 and 8. One balanced decomposition debits
the gross asset 5.5 and the signed allowance account 2.5, and credits interest 8. The allowance
becomes −27.5, so gross 60.5 less allowance −27.5 gives net 88. The 2.5 is discount unwind
within this interest reconciliation; it is not a second credit-revision gain. The impaired case with
net 40 instead earns interest 4, while gross accretion 5.5 and allowance unwind 1.5 give net 44.
Finally, suppose the favorable case collects only 70 at maturity, with no reasonable expectation
of further recovery. Measuring the remaining net asset at the known receipt produces a final
impairment loss of 88−70 = 18. Total interest is 5+8 = 13, net impairment gain is 25−18 = 7,
and total profit is 13 + 7 = 20 = 70 −50. The cash reconciliation agrees: starting with cash and
capital of 200, purchase and collection leave 200 −50 + 70 = 220, equal to capital plus profit.
Immediately before collection, gross carrying amount is 60.5 and the signed allowance is −9.5,
giving net 70. Receiving cash 70 and removing those two balances clears the financial asset
without another gain. The contractual amount not collected is nevertheless 121 −70 = 51.
The no-further-recovery finding supports financial derecognition under paragraph 5.4.4; it does
not establish that the debtor’s legal obligation has been discharged. The record retains that
contractual shortfall and leaves legal release unassessed.
