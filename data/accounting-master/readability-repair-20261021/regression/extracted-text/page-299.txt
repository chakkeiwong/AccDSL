Consider a separate group with remaining-coverage liability 100, loss component 20 within that
liability, and no CSM. Cash is 200 and equity is 100. Assume no new estimates, investment
components, acquisition-cost presentation or tax, and present all finance in profit. For this
group, supply a supported fixed proportional allocation of one fifth. Let L denote the liability
and K its loss component, so k = K/L = 1/5. Positive finance expense h increases the liability;
expected claims and risk-adjustment releases c and a reduce it. Under this declared allocation,
L′ = L + h −c −a,
K′ = K + kh −kc −ka = kL′.
The last equality explains why exhausting the supported liability releases also exhausts the
loss component. It does not prescribe proportional allocation for every group. The finance
allocation kh remains a finance movement; the service reversal is k(c + a). Revenue from these
releases is (1 −k)(c + a), excluding that loss reversal.
In the first period, finance expense 10 raises liability to 110 and its loss component to 22.
Expected claim release 50 and risk-adjustment release 5 then release 11 from the loss component
and produce revenue 44. Actual claims of 50 are incurred and paid separately. Liability closes
at 55, loss component at 11, cash at 150 and equity at 95. Service profit is 44 + 11 −50 = 5;
finance expense 10 makes period profit −5.
In the second period, finance income 5 reduces liability to 50 and the loss component to 10.
Claim release 45 and risk-adjustment release 5 exhaust both: revenue is 40 and loss reversal is
10. Paying actual claims 45 leaves cash and equity of 105. Service profit is again 5; finance
income makes this period’s profit 10. Across both periods, service profit 10 less net finance
expense 5 gives total profit 5. Treating all releases of 105 as revenue and then adding loss
reversals of 21 would overstate profit by 21. The separate categories, rather than final cash
alone, reveal the mistake.
The held treaty needs the same discipline. Recall the covered-loss example: issued liability 120
contains loss component 20, while a timely half-quota treaty covers contracts with loss 8. Held
fulfilment asset 24.5 and cost CSM 6 give asset 30.5, containing a loss-recovery component of 4.
With cash 123.5, equity is 34. Reducing covered expected claims by 4 reduces issued liability
to 116 and loss component to 16. Held recovery falls by 2, giving held asset 28.5, unchanged
cost CSM 6 and loss-recovery component 2. Equity rises to 36. Paragraph 66(c)(i) puts the
held fulfilment change in profit because the related issued change did not adjust issued CSM.
Paragraph 66(bb) addresses reversals not already included in held fulfilment changes; the same
2 cannot be charged again (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.66(bb),66(c)(i),B119F).
Subsequent issued and held service, claims of 114 and recoveries of 22 close at cash 31.5, as
before. Tracking the recovery component does not create a second asset or a second expense.
12.12.3
A change in holdings leaves an old OCI schedule
Current insurance finance and the reclassification of old OCI answer different questions. Under
paragraph 89(b), profit finance on a direct-participation group matches the profit income or
expense of the underlying items actually held. Suppose total insurance finance expense is H,
and those items produce profit income A. Insurance profit expense is then A, while insurance
OCI income is A −H. Asset income and insurance expense remain separately presented, with
net profit finance zero (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.89–90,B134).
