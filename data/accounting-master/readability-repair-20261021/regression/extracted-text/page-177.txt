Chapter 10
Financing, ownership and the group
boundary
A company can preserve the same operating assets while changing who supplies its funding
and who shares its results. The following cases move from debt cash flows to ownership, then
to the reporting boundary of a group. Each numerical example states its own contract; the
coupon-bearing convertible below and the later zero-coupon compound example are different
instruments.
10.1
Debt and hybrid financing
The contract layer for a debt instrument stores issuer and holder perspective, currency, issue and
settlement dates, issue proceeds and legal face amount. Its payment terms include coupon/in-
dex/spread, reset and fixing dates, day-count convention, payment calendar, amortisation
schedule and maturity. Its rights and protections include seniority, collateral, covenants, options,
conversion terms, guarantees and linked hedges. Missing terms are explicit unknowns. A single
carrying amount cannot stand in for this record.
The accounting layer stores the classification decision and its evidence, initial measurement,
carrying amount, accrued interest, effective-rate method, unamortised fees or discounts, fair-
value disclosures, impairment or credit adjustments where applicable, and presentation mappings.
Contractual interest, effective-interest expense, realised cash payments and fair-value changes
are separate quantities. The zero-coupon example in Chapter 8 provides a minimal conformance
case for this separation.
The lifecycle must support issuance, recognition, accrual, reset, coupon, principal repayment,
modification, derecognition, default, recovery and conversion.
A modification event first
determines which accounting operation the selected standard requires; it does not always mean
issuing a new loan. An embedded option can affect valuation and classification without being
exercised. A derivative linked as a hedge requires both the economic contract and any applicable
accounting designation; economic risk reduction alone does not establish hedge-accounting
eligibility.
