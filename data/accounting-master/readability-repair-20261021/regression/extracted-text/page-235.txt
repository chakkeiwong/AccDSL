10.19.1
Losing control removes the whole subsidiary
Suppose an unanticipated transaction immediately after reporting transfers control to a buyer.
No earlier held-for-sale condition is assumed in this event slice; a real planned disposal needs
that assessment. Compare two alternatives: sell the whole 80% interest for USD 169, or sell
60% for 126.75 and retain 20% with independently admitted fair value 42.25. Both transactions
remove the seller’s substantive power. The equal price per share is an illustrative valuation
assumption, not an accounting requirement. It makes proceeds plus retained value equal to 169
in each alternative.

any retained investment at fair value. If C is cash consideration and V the retained fair value,

For the full sale, debit cash 169 and NCI 31.2, credit subsidiary cash 156 and goodwill 20.8, and

126.75 and retained investment 42.25. The subsidiary still holds its own bank account; removal

IAS 21.48–48B and IFRS 10.B99 then reclassify the parent’s entire accumulated exchange
reserve of 10.4 into profit: debit that reserve and credit disposal gain. Total disposal gain is
23.4+10.4 = 33.8. NCI’s reserve of 2.2 is already within the NCI balance of 31.2 removed above;
it is derecognised without being recycled to profit. Retaining 20% does not justify recycling
only the fraction corresponding to the shares sold. Paragraph 48A treats a loss of control as a
disposal even when a minority interest remains.
