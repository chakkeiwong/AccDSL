Recovering movements without inventing invoices
Suppose the annual report discloses sales of 260, cost of sales of 165 and the opening and closing
working-capital balances, while the collection and purchase totals are hidden. Receivables rise
from 20 to 35, inventory falls from 30 to 20, and trade payables rise from 20 to 30. Let C
denote collections, P purchases and S supplier payments, all in currency units over the same
year. Under Northstar’s explicit no-write-off, no-acquisition and no-currency-effect scope, the
three bridges are

Substitution gives C = 245, P = 155 and S = 145. These are identifiable aggregate movements
within this model. They do not identify which invoice was paid on which day. If acquisitions or
write-offs are admitted, their movement variables enter the same bridges and these totals may
cease to be unique.

The model’s historical slots are one delivery, collection, purchase, supplier settlement, payroll,
plant addition, depreciation, interest, tax payment and dividend per half-year. Each slot
represents a classified aggregate event. The interim filing supplies the information for allocating
the annual movements between the two half-years; the annual bridges alone identify only their
totals. The template for collection contributes +C to cash and −C to receivables; its state
effect reduces the unpaid receivable pool by C. The purchase template adds inventory quantity
P/5, inventory cost P and a payable P. A cash-balance identity by itself cannot substitute for
either template.
