10.6
A compound bond exposes classification dependencies
Consider an issuer receiving 100 for a two-year bond paying annual coupons of 5 and principal
100 at maturity, with a holder option to convert that principal into ten ordinary shares. Assume
its currency is the issuer’s functional currency, no other options, fees or contingent settlements,
and that the conversion feature qualifies as equity under the selected rule. A comparable liability
without conversion yields 10%. The inspected IAS 32-equivalent text requires separate liability
and equity components and measures equity as the residual (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2022c, paragraphs 28–32, AG30–AG32).

Issue debits cash 100 and credits these two components. Under the stated effective-interest
schedule, the first coupon leaves liability 1.1L0 −5 = 1050/11 ≈95.4545; after the second
coupon it is 100. Redemption debits liability and credits cash 100. Alternatively, conversion
at maturity debits liability and credits equity 100 while issuing ten shares; the original equity
component remains in equity. AG32 supports the absence of a gain or loss on that maturity
conversion. Exact rational valuations are kept before the declared monetary rounding.
A variable-number share settlement, cash alternative or other clause can change classification.
The contract record must therefore retain these terms; it cannot select liability/equity accounts
independently of the coupon, conversion and settlement rules. Early repurchase and induced
conversion invoke separate operations: the inspected AG33–AG35 text describes allocation and
the induced-conversion consideration difference. They are not implemented by changing the
maturity date of the simple example. US-GAAP and ASBE classification require their own
dated authority; the IFRS-oriented split is never silently reused under another basis.
10.7
Schedules and initial classification
The following liability and ownership methods use selected dated AASB 9 and AASB 132
provisions (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h,c). Issuer, holder, contractual cash
flow and accounting measurement remain separate throughout the schedule.
DebtContract contains parties, currency, principal, issue/settlement dates, payment calendar,
fixed/index/spread/reset terms, compounding/day-count, business-day adjustment, principal
changes, fees, options, security, priority and legal default/remedy terms.

evidence/admission provenance. The economic schedule emits due cash independently of the

A market-value adapter may value that schedule but cannot choose

acquisition cases distinguish FVTPL expensed fees, amortised-cost fees, original EIR and POCI
credit-adjusted EIR. No rate method inferred from market value can overwrite the contract
