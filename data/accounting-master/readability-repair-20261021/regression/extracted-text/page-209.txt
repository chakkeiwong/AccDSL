10.12.5
Forward elements and currency basis spreads
AASB 9.6.5.16 (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, 6.5.16 and B6.5.34–39) permits
the corresponding treatment when the forward element is excluded from a spot-designated
forward, or a foreign-currency basis spread is excluded. The accounting election is explicit;
designation of spot alone does not automatically make the excluded component an OCI reserve.
Apply the transaction/time-period distinction and aligned-value rules in B6.5.34–39. For the
forward element, the inception comparison uses absolute actual and aligned amounts. Retain
their signed fair values and the changes used for the reserve; do not compare signed inception
amounts as though a negative forward element were necessarily smaller in economic magnitude.
B6.5.39 carries the same guidance to currency basis spreads. The exact valuation of unmatched
forward elements and collars remains a required method for those applications.
10.12.6
Rebalancing preserves the history of the hedge
A changed hedge ratio can preserve the original risk-management objective. It does not erase
the accounting before the change. Under 6.5.5–6.5.6 and B6.5.7–21 (Australian Accounting
Standards Board, 2022h, 6.5.5–6.5.6 and B6.5.7–21), first measure and recognise ineffectiveness
under the existing designation, then adjust designated quantities prospectively. Distinguish
ordinary fluctuations around a valid ratio from a changed relationship that requires a new ratio.
A changed objective requires discontinuation of that relationship; a counterparty default is not
repaired by changing quantities. Update the documented expected sources of ineffectiveness
after rebalancing.
Before a change, cumulative derivative gain 12 and offsetting item loss 10 give reserve 10 and
profit 2. Immediately before rebalancing, admitted values 18 and negative 14 instead give
reserve 14 and cumulative profit 4. Record the period’s derivative gain 6 as reserve gain 4 and
profit gain 2 before adjusting the quantities. A reset to equal amounts at the new designation
date would hide the accumulated ineffectiveness.
Store each retained, added and removed volume with its designation date and valuation reference.
An added item layer begins at the rebalancing date; the earlier layer keeps its old reference.
The source’s example has 100 tonnes designated at forward price 80 and adds 10 tonnes
when the price is 90. In a zero-discount illustrative comparison at price 95, their changes
are 100(95 −80) + 10(95 −90) = 1550. Applying the old price to all 110 tonnes gives 1650,
inventing a gain of 100 on the added layer. Actual measurement must use the admitted risk,
dates, discounting and contractual terms.
Adding an instrument similarly starts its change at the new designation date without resetting
the old instrument. Decreasing designated instrument volume leaves any retained undesignated
derivative at fair value through profit or loss unless another qualifying designation applies. For
example, removing 10% of a homogeneous instrument from the designation leaves its total
carrying value unchanged; of a subsequent total fair-value gain 10, the undesignated 1 enters
profit and the designated 9 follows the continuing hedge’s effectiveness test. This split does not
assert that all 9 is effective. Decreasing item volume invokes partial-discontinuation rules for
the removed part. Whether its forecast cash flows remain expected determines the reserve’s
later release; removal from designation alone does not mean cancellation.
