11.10.1
A repayment can restore capacity without erasing credit history
A facility has both a funded balance and a right to request more funding. Record the legal
limit, draw requests and actual settlements separately, together with the replenishment rule,
expiry or cancellation conditions, currency, original credit-risk comparison date and current
credit assessment. If the facility permits repeated drawings, paying down one loan may restore
capacity. It does not establish a new low-risk borrower or reset the original credit history.
Determine the resulting loan’s classification and initial measurement from its business model,
cash-flow terms and value, rather than assuming every funded amount qualifies for amortised
cost.
For a partial draw, remeasure funded and remaining undrawn losses using the information
available after the draw. Do not allocate the old provision by the percentage drawn unless
the admitted loss model actually implies that result. AASB 9.5.5.6 and B5.5.47 (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, 5.5.6 and B5.5.47) preserve the commitment’s impairment
history. Where the two losses can be identified separately, AASB 7.B8E (Australian Accounting
Standards Board, 2022g, B8E) presents the loan allowance and commitment provision separately.
Where they cannot, the combined loss is recognised against the funded asset, with any excess
over its gross carrying amount presented as a provision. Inseparability is a measurement fact,
not an election to conceal a missing exposure model.
