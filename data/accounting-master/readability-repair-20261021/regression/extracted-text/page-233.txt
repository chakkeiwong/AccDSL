Functional currency versus group presentation
EntityCurrencyState names the functional currency and its evidenced rationale, foreign-currency
nominal balances, monetary/nonmonetary classification, historical or fair-value measurement
dates, rates and sources. IAS 21.21–23 and .28 require transaction-date translation and closing-
rate remeasurement of monetary items; historical-cost nonmonetary assets retain transaction-
date rates. A functional currency change is prospective under .35–37. Hyperinflation, lack of
exchangeability and hedge accounting require separate applicable methods; do not import the
historical .26 text into a later amended period without checking it.
Example: buy goods for foreign 100 at functional 1.2 per foreign unit; debit inventory 120/credit
payable 120. Closing rate 1.3 gives payable 130 and FX expense 10, while inventory remains
120 before any NRV test. Pay at 1.25: debit payable 130/ credit cash 125/credit FX gain 5.
Cumulative expense 5 matches 125 cash less 120 asset cost. Translating the inventory again to
130 would be wrong for this declared basis.
Group presentation is a different operation under IAS 21.39–41. A subsidiary has opening net
assets 100, equity 100, no debt or distributions, annual profit 20 entirely earned at rate 1.25,
and closing assets 120. Opening historical rate 1.2 and closing 1.3 give translated assets 156,
contributed/opening equity 120 and profit 25; translation OCI 11 reconciles the difference. It
is explained as 100×(1.3−1.2)+20×(1.3−1.25)=11, not a free balancing plug. If ownership
is 80%, parent/NCI shares of profit are 20/5 and OCI 8.8/2.2 under the stated equal rights.
Use transaction rates; an average is acceptable only as a justified approximation, not when
significant fluctuation invalidates it. Goodwill and fair-value adjustments follow IAS 21.47’s
foreign-operation treatment, rather than freezing them automatically in the parent’s currency.
Group reports retain the translation reserve by entity, ownership and event. Loss of control
requires the appropriate reserve reclassification under IAS 21.48 and IFRS 10.B99; a no-control-
loss change transfers the relevant attribution in equity under applicable conditions. Cash
translation effects reconcile cash separately from operating/investing/financing movements. An
intercompany monetary balance’s exchange result cannot simply be eliminated as if no currency
exposure existed; IAS 21.45 and net-investment .32 require the substantive classification.
