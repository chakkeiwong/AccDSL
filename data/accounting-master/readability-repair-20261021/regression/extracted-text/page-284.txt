Suppose instead that R is entered into immediately after B’s recognition, with no intervening
service, insured event or financing and otherwise the same prospective coverage. It is a valid
different history. It cannot recover the original recognised loss under paragraph 66A because it
fails B119C’s ordering condition. R then starts with cost CSM 5 and asset 70, with no loss-
recovery component. Opening equity is 70. Its year-one closing asset is 33 + 2.5 + 2.75 = 38.25,
giving total equity 74.75. Year-two cash and equity still finish at 88.3. The difference is the
timing of recognition, not a difference in cash. Paragraph C20A’s transition exception does not
apply to this ordinary new-business example.
Incorrectly pooling A and B produces a third history of reported amounts. Their combined
premiums 220 equal claim present value 200 plus the two supplied risk adjustments 20. A
pooled calculation would therefore show neither CSM nor initial loss, conceal B’s loss of 30,
and suppress the timely treaty’s recovery adjustment of 15. It reports equity 100 initially and
91.25 after year one, although final cash is again 88.3. Those accounts balance but violate the
separate-group recognition requirements. Likewise, adding the recovery component to the full
asset overstates opening equity by 15, while omitting financing from loss allocation makes the
component run out before coverage ends. Intermediate group identity, component movements
and recognition time are essential checks; terminal cash alone cannot distinguish these mistakes.
Actual use also requires the treaty’s boundary, collectability, measurement inputs and eligible
contract mapping. Partial coverage needs the systematic loss allocation in B119E; a nominal
quota cannot supply it. Changes in those facts or in the estimates require new measurement
events under paragraphs 66–67, rather than extending this fixed schedule unchanged. The shared
event procedure commits journal and component movements for each dated event together,
while preserving separate issued and held group identities. (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2022f, paragraphs 24, 63–67, 78–86 and B119C–B119F)
