# Chapter 3 — preserving assertions and their history

Exact Version 3.46, physical pages 62–73, read in segments 061–066,
067–072 and 073–078. Chapter 2 left a concrete representation problem:
reported amounts, derived movements, qualitative assertions and conditional
histories cannot occupy one undifferentiated record. This chapter gives them
separate identities, contexts, dates and dependencies.

The progression is intelligible. Observation fields precede meaning mappings;
the machine-in-service example introduces the different clocks; changing group
scope then motivates dated relationships. Coverage and narrative translation
procedures explain how a retained page can remain semantically unresolved.
Northstar's duplicate classification axes demonstrate invalid aggregation,
and the revised-assertion procedure selects eligible versions without using
ingestion order as a truth rule.

The source-admission cases test the consequences of those definitions. FIFO
does not supply lots or every applicable accounting rule. JPMorgan's wrong
page reference can be transcribed faithfully while being rejected as a
navigation target; the proposed correction invalidates dependent searches
without mechanically invalidating unrelated financial amounts. Conditional
rounding and the publication-date diagram distinguish assumptions from known
precision and known instants. These examples make partial answers specific
rather than treating every unknown as either zero or a global failure.

The final bank and treaty cases connect information changes to accounting
consequences. Economic event order remains distinct from document arrival.
Candidate-set changes can require a new selection even when an old assertion
or final number is unchanged. Earlier explanations retain their original view,
and a revised conclusion does not itself authorize a correcting journal or
change an exact retry's original commitment. That conclusion prepares the
literature and architecture discussion without claiming an implemented engine.
