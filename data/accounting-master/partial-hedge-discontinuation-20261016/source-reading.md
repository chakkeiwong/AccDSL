The supported reduction identifies a homogeneous portion of an initially
single-origin purchase and the matching proportional entire derivative. The
remaining portion continues to satisfy all qualifying conditions. The stopped
purchase portion has ceased to be highly probable. Its actual identification,
expectation and loss recoverability are supplied findings, not conclusions from
the financial amounts.

Proportional allocation is a local derivation with explicit prerequisites.
For identical per-unit exposures, let a be the removed fraction, with 0<a<1.
The removed cumulative derivative/item values are aD and aH. Since
`min(a|D|,a|H|)=a min(|D|,|H|)`, its reserve is aR and its prior earnings
are a(D−R). The complementary portion carries (1−a) times each amount.
This preserves every book total at discontinuation without a new gain or
resetting the original measurement date. It is not an allocation rule for
heterogeneous layers, unmatched changes or an unidentified aggregated reserve.

Thereafter the remaining portion uses its original cumulative origins. The
stopped derivative portion remains owned: its new gains/losses go to earnings,
while its reserve retains the amount measured on discontinuation, subject to
the separate expectation/recoverability rules. Cumulative earnings equal
continuing ineffectiveness + stopped prior ineffectiveness + stopped subsequent
fair-value change + signed amounts released from its reserve. If the purchase
occurs, only reserve still attached to that purchase enters inventory cost.

The audit passes for this finite matched-portion method. Numerical split
conservation will challenge the homogeneity derivation, and false/unknown
homogeneity will refuse; no full general partial-discontinuation allocation is
claimed. No tax, pricing, settlement fees, bank/insurance join or universal
cash-flow activity classification is inferred. The original rules and baseline
remain immutable; current applicability and actual facts need separate evidence.
