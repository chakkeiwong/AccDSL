# The annual panel leaves little room for nested temporal evaluation

The source inventory contains eight annual labels per company, 2018–2025,
for 72 annual reports. Under the selected strict pre-start horizon and
adjacent full fiscal years, the immediately preceding year's realised OCF is
unfinished at the cutoff. Even assuming every older label is instantly usable,
three training labels plus two earlier inner validation origins leave only
outer label 2025 per company. Five training labels plus two validation origins
leave none. These are conditional same-company feasibility bounds, not model
results or a universal bound for pooled/external training.

Six training/validation sensitivity combinations are recorded in
`feasibility.json`. They span zero to six potential outer years per company.
The proposed start-year grouping loses another endpoint because Apple's fiscal
label differs: with only outer label 2025 there is no complete common
start-year cohort. Sixteen exact positive cases and six invalid-input refusals
check the counting and supplied-timestamp boundaries. No model was fitted and
all 192 historical byte admissions remain false.

`design-proposal.md` sets out a concrete, provisional annual cohort policy,
uniform weights over a specified finite future grid, release-aware inner and
outer fitting, a matched baseline ladder and explicit failure status. It
distinguishes a finite future expected target from unsupported transport of
historical losses to that target. Window, grid, lead time, resources, probability
law and inference remain unselected scientific choices; counts cannot select
them. A zero-tuning count does not excuse the tuned classical baseline.

Hard evidence concerns timing/count limitations and invalid supplied inputs.
No forecasting candidates have been screened, and no statistically supported
ranking exists. Cell counts are explanatory only. A defensible comparison
needs admitted sources and forecasts, a justified fitting/tuning protocol,
appropriate uncertainty and sufficient temporal evidence under that protocol.
No default or whole requirement is promoted; manuscript 3.48 / 401 pages /
not_ready remains frozen.

Red team: the strongest alternative is that pooled or external training could
reduce the same-company history need. That possibility remains viable, but
changes the fitted policy and dependence assumptions and requires its own
target-specific evaluation. The upper bound would also change with a broader
source horizon or different forecast timing; neither is silently substituted.
The weakest evidence is transport to future reporting/economic regimes.
The result repairs a naive experimental design rather than rejecting the
underlying idea of preserving alternative histories.
