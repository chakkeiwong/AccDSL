This document specifies a system that translates company disclosures into traceable evidence,
represents several compatible economic and accounting histories, and simulates their future
journals and financial statements under explicit economic scenarios.
The proposed data contracts, methods and acceptance cases specify the intended implementation.
Current source and domain limitations remain explicit; this candidate is not a completed product
or a claim of full proposal readiness. Proposed libraries can be replaced while preserving those
contracts. Sector rule packages require their own accounting review before they can be described
as compliant with a reporting standard.
Research materials, source qualifications and review records accompany this document in
docs/design/research and docs/design/review.

26.19
A complete small acquisition makes the adjustments visi-
ble
The unknown amounts in the preceding section protect the real financing question from invented
measurements. A separate synthetic example can now test whether the proposed accounting
machinery combines those amounts correctly. Every amount in this example is stipulated in one
currency unit. It is not a valuation of the creditor or debtor. The example assumes an ordinary
business acquisition, a 75 per cent present ownership interest, aligned reporting periods, no
prior interest or relationship, and a gain of control on 1 July 2025. Tax recognition and the
measured tax amounts are supplied premises; the calculation tests their propagation.
At the start of the year the parent has cash 300 and capital 300. Immediately before acquisition,
the subsidiary has cash 50, equipment 70, payables 20, contributed capital 80 and pre-control
accumulated profit 20. Its net assets are 100. The parent pays 120 in cash and records an
investment 120, leaving its own cash 180. Those legal accounts remain separate and balanced.
The subsidiary’s accumulated profit 20 belongs to the acquired equity; it does not become
current post-control group profit.
The stipulated acquisition basis changes equipment from 70 to 90 and introduces a deferred-tax
liability 5. Acquired recognised assets are therefore 140 and liabilities 25, giving net assets 115.
With proportional non-controlling interests, Q = 115/4 and the positive excess in equation (26.6)
is 120 + 115/4 −115 = 135/4. Conditional recognition of that excess is an explicit premise
of this positive-excess example. The consolidated acquisition worksheet removes the parent’s
investment 120 and the subsidiary’s acquired capital 80 and accumulated profit 20. It adds
the equipment adjustment 20, the tax liability 5, goodwill 135/4 and NCI 115/4. Each entry
retains its own origin; none is an unexplained balancing amount.
The resulting acquisition balance can be reconstructed from cash 230, equipment 90 and
goodwill 135/4, against payables 20, deferred tax 5, parent capital 300 and NCI 115/4. The
legal books have not changed. Only the separate consolidation worksheet contains the acquisition
adjustments.
26.19.1
The closing report follows the controlled part of the year
After control the subsidiary receives external revenue 40 in cash and records legal depreciation 7.
Its legal closing cash is 90 and equipment 63; its post-control legal profit is 33. The acquisition
basis requires an additional depreciation 2 and a stipulated release of tax liability 1/2. Thus
the group profit contribution is 33 −2 + 1/2 = 63/2. The original pre-control profit 20 is still
excluded. This is a comparison of complete dated accounts, not a proportional allocation of a
whole-year income number.
The closing group holds cash 270, equipment 81 and goodwill 135/4. Its payables are 20 and
deferred-tax liability 9/2. Assets total 1539/4, liabilities 49/2 and equity 1441/4. Owners
receive three quarters of profit, 189/8, while NCI receives 63/8. Closing owners’ equity is 2589/8
and closing NCI 293/8; together they equal 1441/4. The expense and tax adjustments therefore

26.19.2
An equity-funded purchase need not consume cash
Hold total stipulated consideration at 120 and vary only its composition. A cash-only purchase
uses cash 120 and no new parent equity. An equity-only purchase issues parent equity 120 and
uses no cash. A mixed purchase pays cash 50 and issues equity 70. This controlled comparison
isolates the accounting consequences of the supplied components; it does not establish that the
securities have equivalent market values.
The subsidiary brings cash 50 into the consolidated perimeter in each case. Net acquisition
cash is therefore −70, 50 or 0 for the cash-only, equity-only and mixed purchases. With the
same later operating receipt 40, closing group cash is 270, 390 or 340. In the equity-only case,
acquired cash 50 appears despite the absence of a purchase cash payment. Issuing shares does
not create a fictitious cash receipt followed by a payment. The issued equity is retained as a
separate noncash consideration component and increases parent capital, while the cash report
retains cash paid and cash acquired separately (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026b,
paras. 39–44B).
This newly issued parent equity must not be confused with the financing example’s prior
creditor claim. The cash/equity comparison assumes no prior relationship. Extinguishing
or converting an existing debt requires the separate consideration, financial-instrument and
pre-existing-relationship analysis discussed earlier. Renaming the debt as an equity payment
would bypass that unresolved analysis.
26.19.3
A later measurement changes the worksheet and the information
view
Suppose the synthetic acquisition equipment amount was provisional. A later proposed mea-
surement changes it from 90 to 100, together with explicitly reviewed dependent amounts:
the acquisition tax liability becomes 15/2, additional controlled-period depreciation becomes
3, and the tax release becomes 3/4. These values are supplied together; a revised equipment
amount cannot silently inherit unrelated tax or expense assumptions. With proportional NCI,
net assets become 245/2, NCI 245/8 and goodwill 225/8. Post-control profit becomes 123/4,
owners’ profit 369/16 and NCI profit 123/16. In the cash-only case, closing assets are 3105/8,
liabilities 107/4 and equity 2891/8. Owners’ equity 5169/16 plus NCI 613/16 gives the same
total. All original legal accounts remain unchanged.
Those financial effects are conditional on the proposed information being eligible to revise
acquisition-date measurements. A later event, a nonprovisional item, a different effective date
or information outside the applicable measurement period cannot be treated as the same
adjustment merely because the proposed worksheet balances (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2026h, paras. 45–50). The selected reference receives those dispositions as explicit
premises; it does not decide their truth.

Availability introduces a different question. In the example the new information is discovered
on 15 January 2026 and reaches the selected actor on 1 February 2026. A 10 January query
retains the original even if the exact delivery time is unknown, because delivery cannot precede
discovery. A 20 January query likewise retains the original when the February delivery is
known. At delivery, eligible supplied information can select the revised conditional report. If
delivery is unknown after discovery, both information views may remain possible. If the supplied
disposition is a later event, unknown delivery does not make retrospective acquisition adjustment
an eligible financial alternative; only the original worksheet remains, with another-method work
conditional on the actor receiving that evidence.
The original report must also survive an unsupported proposed method. An undiscovered
revision might produce a negative excess that the current positive-excess reference cannot
handle. It cannot make the earlier report invalid. Once the revision could be available and
eligible, the system retains the original and the complete proposed request with its refusal and
missing-method requirement. It does not select the original as if the new information had been
resolved, and it does not fabricate a revised result. This distinction preserves history while
making the next accounting-method obligation visible.
26.19.4
The comparison remains narrower than company accounting
The selected comparisons inspect every original account, acquisition item, adjustment origin,
adjusted account and report field, including cash and parent/NCI equity movements. The
repaired revision reference also rebinds the embedded source and control queries to the requested
actor and cutoff. Earlier passing candidates were rejected when discriminating cases exposed a
pre-discovery alternative, an ineligible adjustment under unknown access, and a future failed
candidate that erased the original. Those failures remain in the evidence history.4
This remains a four-item synthetic acquisition with stipulated tax treatment, one currency,
ordinary rights, one actor and one proposed revision. Prior interests, actual debt conversion,
loss or later ownership changes, intragroup trade, general item mappings, foreign exchange,
revision chains and complete statutory disclosures need further methods and evidence. The
next comparison must add post-control intragroup transactions without leaking pre-control
activity or losing the acquired measurement basis.
