12.8
Reconstructing balances at transition
A disclosed restatement tells us what balances an insurer reported under the new basis. It
does not reveal the historical contract groups, assumptions or services needed to reproduce
their opening measurement. The three transition methods address different limits on that
reconstruction. The retained December 2022 AASB 17 compilation provides a useful, explicitly
scoped account of those methods: its stated applicability covers annual periods beginning from
1 January 2023 to before 1 July 2026. These provisions explain a design requirement; they do
not establish the rules governing Ping An’s ASBE groups (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2022d, Appendix C).
First distinguish the dates. Initial application begins the annual period in which the entity
first applies the standard. Transition normally begins the immediately preceding annual period.
Thus a calendar-year entity first applying the standard in 2023, with only 2022 presented as an
adjusted comparative, starts its transition calculation on 1 January 2022. If it elects to present
earlier adjusted comparatives, paragraph C25 instead takes transition back to the beginning
of the earliest such period. Neither date establishes when the resulting report became public
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022d, paragraphs C2 and C25–C27).
Full retrospective application identifies and measures each group as if the standard had always
applied. Acquisition-cash-flow assets are considered separately, balances that would not exist
under the new rules are removed, and the resulting net difference is recognised in equity. This
route needs the historical information required to reconstruct grouping, cash flows, measurement
assumptions and service already provided. It is required unless application is impracticable or
the specific paragraph C5A exception applies (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022d,
paragraphs C3–C5).
When full retrospection is impracticable for a group, the alternatives are modified retrospection
and fair-value transition. Modified retrospection aims for the closest retrospective result possible
using reasonable and supportable information available without undue cost or effort. Each
specified modification is allowed only to the extent that the necessary retrospective information
is missing.
If the information needed even for this modified method cannot be obtained,
paragraph C6(a) requires the fair-value approach. Missing history therefore does not license an
unrestricted choice of present assumptions (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022d,
paragraphs C5–C8).
For example, the modified route can reconstruct earlier expected cash flows from estimates at
transition, or an earlier determinable date, adjusted for known intervening cash flows, including
those of contracts that have expired. Its discount-rate approximations require the specified
yield-curve evidence; its risk adjustment and allocation of past service require their own records.
The source also prescribes nil amounts for particular acquisition-cash-flow items when the
necessary information is unavailable. That narrow instruction cannot be generalized into a rule
that every missing amount is zero (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022d, paragraphs
C12–C16).
Fair-value transition takes a different starting point. Paragraph C20 determines the CSM or loss
component from the difference between the fair value of the insurance group and its fulfilment
cash flows, both measured at transition. The insurance group’s fair value is distinct from the
value of its underlying investment pool. Replacing one with the other would compute a different
quantity. The calculation needs a supported group valuation as well as the cash-flow, discount
and risk-adjustment inputs; C20 expressly excludes the demand-feature rule in AASB 13
paragraph 47. Its permitted information-date, grouping and accumulated-OCI choices also have
specified conditions (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022d, paragraphs C20–C24B).
