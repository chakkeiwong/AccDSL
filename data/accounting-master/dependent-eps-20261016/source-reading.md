# IAS33 consequential and contingent effects

Inspected retained EU2023/1803 original text, IAS33.33–47 at15318–15446 and
49–57 at15455–15550, including paragraph57's final sentence. These are
historical accounting principles; operative issuer law and actual terms remain
supplied. The original PDF and text are retained locally.

33(c),34–35 require every resulting income/expense change and related tax;
35 explicitly names nondiscretionary employee profit-sharing after reduced
interest expense. The threshold bonus and flat tax in the examples are local
supplied terms. Their joint earnings formula is a local deduction; the standard
does not provide that bonus contract or prove a nonlinear selection algorithm.

41–44 require continuing-operations dilution, prohibit antidilutive assumed
conversion, and assess individual issues/series.49–50 address convertible debt.
An unconstrained minimum across hypothetical worlds does not override those
requirements. Nonadditive world outputs are therefore conditional calculations,
with final diluted selection expressly unevaluated.

45–47 supply the treasury-share method and the average-market-price screen.
52 treats the reporting-period end as the contingency end where conditions
remain outstanding.56 applies the period-end status to nonfinancial conditions.
57 first applies52–56, then the appropriate instrument method, and finally
forbids assumed exercise/conversion unless similar outstanding instruments that
are not contingently issuable are also assumed exercised/converted.

The finite example uses a store-count condition and a single admitted similar
outstanding option. Similarity is explicitly supplied and is not inferred from
different strike prices. If that assessment is unknown, there is no admitted
answer. Neither future stores nor a hypothetical isolated EPS can override57.
The two option series have no earnings effects, no future-service proceeds,
no participation or further dependencies and full-period weighting. Their zero
earnings/share ratios tie; test the prerequisite option before its dependent.
With positive earnings, every eligible positive increment is dilutive; with
zero or negative earnings, neither is strictly dilutive. Thus this dependency
does not require an unsupported general graph-ordering theorem.
