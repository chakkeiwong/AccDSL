## S1 early conversion

New lead: IFRIC19.1–3 and5–10, source lines61627–61723. Paragraph3(c) excludes
equity settlement in accordance with the original liability terms from the
interpretation. Therefore its fair-value equity measurement and extinguishment
gain/loss rule cannot be imported into the existing original-term early
conversion example. That exclusion is a useful new boundary, not an affirmative
measurement rule for the excluded transaction.

Re-inspected AASB132 AG31–35, retained standards/aasb132.txt lines1364–1400,
and corresponding historical IAS32 source. AG32 explicitly treats maturity
conversion; AG33/34 treats early redemption/repurchase, and AG35 an inducement
through amended terms. These are different triggers. The manuscript's9,200
early-conversion transfer remains a supplied EPS illustration. The original
conversion option's equity classification, exact exercise terms and absence of
inducement need support before even that analogy can be considered.

IAS8.10–12, EU source lines3455–3500, supplies an accounting-policy judgment
hierarchy in the absence of an applicable specific rule. A candidate policy
could reason from the original split and maturity conversion, but the hierarchy
does not itself prove this treatment. Keep the method source-dependent; no
general earlier-conversion rule was established. Next useful evidence is an
identified authoritative interpretation or original technical treatment with
the exact original-terms/inducement distinction, plus actual contract facts.
Two web searches returned no inspectable results; no absence inference follows.

## S1 all-zero CGU reversal

IAS36.118–125, lines17937–18000, retains pro-rata allocation by current carrying
amount, asset-specific ceilings and the prohibition on goodwill reversal.
If all eligible carrying amounts are zero, the stated proportional expression
has denominator zero. The ceiling is a bound, not a replacement weighting
formula. IAS8.10–12 is a new policy lead, but supplies no unique numerical
allocation. Equal weights, old costs and no-impairment amounts can give different
outcomes while all satisfying an aggregate ceiling. None becomes required by
choosing it in code. Existing refusal remains correct for this unsupported
algorithm; it does not assert that a real entity may never reverse impairment.
An authoritative allocation interpretation or separately supported policy is
still needed for admission. No repeated endpoint campaign is justified.
