## Verdicts

The existing shared-symbol and Boolean-universal claims are **correct within
their stated scope**. Existing nonempty-family and mixed-versus-undecided
rules were inspected and retained. A blanket interpretation of every certain
answer as a table describing the complete schedule would be **wrong relative
to that target**. The old text did not explicitly assert that algorithm, so
this is a clarification of an underspecified returned answer, not proof that
an implemented engine returns wrong answers.

The new two-creditor example is a **project derivation**. The actual computed
objects are (a) two complete schedules, (b) their tuple intersection and union,
and (c) per-history Boolean and sum results. The intersection is empty but
every schedule has one payable and total 100. Thus it is different from the
answer family and cannot replace it for existence or aggregation. Exhaustive
finite checks support this example only. Adding a zero-payable history
explicitly changes the result, demonstrating the scope assumption.

For any nonempty H and total Boolean P, H has no counterexample to P iff P
holds for every h in H. With exact constraint representation F of H this is
equivalent to unsatisfiability of F and not-P; the analogous no-witness check
establishes refutation. This is ordinary local logical equivalence, not an
algorithmic completeness or termination theorem. A sampled subset cannot
establish full-family absence of counterexamples.
