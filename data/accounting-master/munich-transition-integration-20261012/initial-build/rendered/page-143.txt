12.9
What an insurer’s transition disclosure reveals
Munich Re’s 2023 report shows how much a public disclosure can resolve. Its consolidated
statements apply IFRS as adopted by the European Union, together with the stated German
commercial-law requirements. The first application of IFRS 17 is 1 January 2023, with transition
at 1 January 2022 and restated insurance comparatives. Financial-instrument comparatives
generally remain under IAS 39, subject to the disclosed classification overlay. A single year
label therefore does not imply a single measurement basis for every line in the comparative
balance sheet (Munich Re, 2024, pp. 155 and 183–184).2
The report identifies full retrospection for certain property-casualty contracts and parts of
life reinsurance. Where historical information is insufficient in life reinsurance, it mostly uses
fair-value transition; it also uses that method for specified property-casualty reinsurance groups
and German primary business. Modified retrospection and fair value together account for over
90% of net insurance liabilities at transition. That is a statement about a liability denominator
at a particular date. It supplies neither a count of contracts nor the assignment and supporting
history of every group (Munich Re, 2024, p. 183).
The opening-equity bridge makes the scale of the different effects visible. The reported margin
component is much larger than the net equity reduction because the other measurement changes
also enter the reconciliation. The following amounts reproduce the report’s signs and billion-euro
scale (Munich Re, 2024, p. 184).

The five displayed change components sum to −2.5 billion, whereas the displayed endpoints
differ by −2.4 billion. The report warns that rounding can cause such deviations; these figures
are compatible under rounding to the displayed precision. The representation should retain
both the reported endpoints and the bridge, with their precision, instead of changing one to
force an exact identity (Munich Re, 2024, pp. 1, 155 and 184).
The separate margin tables also clarify which contracts are being followed. At 1 January 2022
the reported issued-contract CSM is −22,688 million and the held-reinsurance net cost/gain is
368 million. Adding those reported totals gives −22,320 million, consistent with the rounded
−22.3 billion margin component above. The issued-contract transition table follows groups in
force at transition, and its CSM amounts concern the general measurement model and VFA;
the associated revenue also includes PAA contracts. Its later balances therefore cannot be
substituted for the whole subsequent portfolio or used as if CSM and revenue had identical
populations (Munich Re, 2024, pp. 210–211 and 254–255).
