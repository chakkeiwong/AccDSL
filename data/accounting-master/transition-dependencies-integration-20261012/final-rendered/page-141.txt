The source also prescribes nil amounts for particular acquisition-cash-flow items when the

Fair-value transition takes a different starting point. Paragraph C20 determines the CSM or loss
component from the difference between the fair value of the insurance group and its fulfilment
cash flows, both measured at transition. The insurance group’s fair value is distinct from the
value of its underlying investment pool. Replacing one with the other would compute a different
quantity. The calculation needs a supported group valuation as well as the cash-flow, discount
and risk-adjustment inputs; C20 expressly excludes the demand-feature rule in AASB 13
paragraph 47. Its permitted information-date, grouping and accumulated-OCI choices also have
specified conditions (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022e, paragraphs C20–C24B).
To interpret that group valuation, consider what would be paid to transfer the liability in an
orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date. The obligation
remains outstanding, and the transferee must fulfil it; the thought experiment is not an
immediate settlement with the policyholder. AASB 13 therefore calls for assumptions that
market participants would use, in the accessible principal market or, in its absence, the most
advantageous market.
The unit being valued still comes from C20: the insurance group
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2021, paragraphs 2–3, 9, 13–24 and 34).
A quoted price is not required in every case. The valuation should maximise relevant observable
inputs, while supported unobservable inputs can supply the participant assumptions for which
market observations are unavailable. The insurer’s own data can be a starting point, but require
adjustment when reasonably available evidence indicates different participant assumptions or
entity-specific features unavailable to other participants. A technique must also fit the circum-
stances and available data. These requirements make the source, date and interpretation of each
valuation input consequential (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2021, paragraphs 61–67
and 87–89).
There is a further reason to retain separate fair-value and FCF calculations. AASB 13 includes
non-performance risk, including the issuer’s own credit risk, in liability fair value and assumes
that risk is unchanged by the hypothetical transfer. AASB 17 expressly excludes the issuer’s
own non-performance risk from fulfilment cash flows for issued insurance contracts. Even when
the two measurements draw on some of the same cash-flow records, their treatment of this risk
differs. Neither a single imported FCF balance nor the value of the underlying investments
supplies both C20 inputs (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2021, paragraphs 42–44)
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022e, paragraph 31).1
