# Cash-flow transition: technical mechanism supported, issuer effects open

Two official AASB sources now support a precise technical comparison of the
June2024 cash-flow amendments associated with AASB18/IFRS18. Twelve source
anchors and two actually viewed redline pages distinguish changes to the
indirect reconciliation from classification of interest and dividends.
Five exact synthetic examples confirm that reclassification can raise or
lower OCF while total cash stays fixed, whereas a changed reconciliation
starting point need not change OCF.

The specified-main-business rules are consequential for the panel. New34B–D
require evidence about the reporting entity and related profit-or-loss
categories, with a whole-category policy choice in the mixed-category case.
The group assessment can differ from that of a subsidiary. These inspected
requirements support a dependency in the future OCF protocol, not a selected
policy or quantified effect for any company.

No forecasting candidate is rejected or promoted and no ranking is supported.

Red team: the strongest alternative to a material target discontinuity is
that a company's existing classification already matches the amended rule.
Actual policy and first-adoption disclosures could establish a zero change
for the relevant components. The weak point is issuer/jurisdiction
applicability, which this AASB inquiry does not establish. This limitation
requires further source work; it does not invalidate the local algebra or
justify abandoning reported-basis forecasting.
