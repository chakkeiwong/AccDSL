17.26
A revised report and the economic history it describes
A financial report can change between the two decisions without changing the economic event
it describes. Suppose an earlier assertion reports an operating receipt of 7 and a later assertion
corrects it to 6. Selecting the available assertion at each date answers an information question.
It does not yet determine whether the receipt was always 6, whether an agreement actually
changed, or what information the manager received. Those questions must be separated before
the selected assertions can support a simulation.
The joint source calculation can now retain a different financial head at each decision. A
correction available between the dates selects the old assertion first and the corrected assertion
second. With an interval-valued availability time, the supported sequences can be old/old,
old/new and new/new. The reverse new/old sequence is impossible under this one-way correction
history, even though it would appear in an unconstrained Cartesian product of the two marginal
selections. Conflict and retraction retain unresolved content. None of these source selections,
by itself, specifies a financial policy.18

Before the receipt arrives, the complete states under R = 6 and R = 7 coincide in this
example. That includes the book, contractual memory and recorded flows. Attaching the
receipt-6 continuation to the identical pre-receipt prefix therefore reconstructs exactly the
complete receipt-6 path. Rejecting every change of a receipt parameter would miss this valid
correspondence. The correspondence can support an explicitly assumed correction of the same
event; it cannot establish that the revised report is true.

17.26.2
An explicit interpretation selects a complete world
For a supplied same-event correction, the analyst can declare the corrected head, a fixed receipt
and a contractual-right family. This declaration supplies a hypothesis about the whole economic
history. The selected heads at both dates must lie in the corrected head’s receipt-only ancestry,
and the right family must remain unchanged. Retaining the superseded assertion as evidence
does not impose both 7 and 6 as simultaneous exact facts about one receipt; imposing those
incompatible facts would leave no world.
The binding then selects the complete financial rows for the declared world while retaining
the dated source pairs, their supports and any unresolved temporal remainder. Receipt 6 with
the committed right binds 980 action-region rows; receipt 7 binds 968. These are complete
action-region row families, not counts of feasible policies. Their populations agree with the
protected reference problems. Unknown interpretation, actual amendment, changed rights,
uncovered ancestry, conflict or retraction, inconsistent clocks and incomplete search receive no
financial row set.20
The corrected head may be available to the analyst who specifies the hypothesis without having
been available to the earlier manager. Neither that head’s identity nor the assumed world’s
identity is automatically an observable message. An adaptive policy still needs an explicit
account of the information available at each decision and must choose the same action whenever
that information is the same. The next comparison must therefore preserve the distinction
between the analyst’s economic hypothesis and the manager’s dated observations.
