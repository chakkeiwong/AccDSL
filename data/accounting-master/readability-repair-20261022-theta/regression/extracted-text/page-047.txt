2.16.2
The remedy, jurisdiction and procedural stage matter
The Digital Markets Act discussion reports two investigations opened by the European Com-
mission on 25 March 2024. Article 5(4) concerns developers’ communication and promotion of
offers and conclusion of contracts with end users; Article 6(3) concerns default settings, app
uninstallation and a browser choice screen on iOS. The Commission’s 24 June announcement is
described as preliminary findings alleging an Article 5(4) breach and the opening of a third
investigation into new contractual requirements for third-party developers and app marketplaces.
Apple states that it believes it complies and continues to change its compliance plan in response
to Commission engagement. The preliminary allegation and Apple’s position remain separate
assertions.
Both filings say that, if the Commission makes a final violation determination, it can issue a
cease and desist order and may impose fines up to 10% of Apple’s annual worldwide net sales.
They also say an order would apply immediately during an appeal unless a stay is granted.
This preserves a conditional consequence, its stated sales base and the stay qualification. The
percentage is not a fine already imposed, an expected loss or a booked liability.
Even a
conditional fine calculation would need the applicable operative law, relevant sales period and
base, and assumptions about the determination and remedy. None is supplied by multiplying a
convenient annual sales figure by the reported cap.
The Department of Justice discussion reports the civil antitrust lawsuit filed on 21 March 2024
in the District of New Jersey by the DOJ and state and district attorneys general. It alleges
monopolization or attempted monopolization in the markets for “performance smartphones”
and “smartphones.” The DOJ seeks equitable relief; related state and federal civil litigation
seeks monetary damages and other nonmonetary relief. Apple states that it has substantial
defenses and intends to defend itself. Treating all these proceedings as one quantified damages
claim would erase both the different requested relief and the absence of disclosed amounts.
Epic’s case contains a reported court finding as well as unresolved procedure. Apple says the
Northern District of California court found certain App Store Review Guidelines provisions
to violate California’s unfair competition law and issued an injunction against prohibiting
developers’ external purchase links. The stated scope is the U.S. storefront of the iOS and
iPadOS App Store. Apple reports implementing a compliance plan and filing its statement
on 16 January 2024. Epic’s motion disputing that plan and seeking enforcement is reported
as pending, opposed by Apple; Apple also reports filing a motion to narrow or vacate the
injunction on 30 September 2024. These passages do not establish that the disputed plan was
judicially accepted or quantify its sales effect. An EU-wide scenario cannot inherit the U.S.
injunction merely because both matters concern the App Store.
The interim bodies repeat the substantive DMA, DOJ and Epic account, with acronym or
trademark presentation differences. Repetition is evidence of what Apple reported in each filing,
not proof that every intervening legal fact was unchanged. The September 30 motion also falls
after the annual September 28 period-end. Event date, reporting period and the date on which
the report became available must therefore remain distinct in a historical information query.
