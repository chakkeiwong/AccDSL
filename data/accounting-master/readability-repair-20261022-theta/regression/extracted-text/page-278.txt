Chapter 12
Insurance service, finance and claims
An insurer receives cash before much of its service is delivered. Cash premium, service revenue
and profit therefore follow different schedules. A short coverage example first connects gross
insurance to reinsurance.
The subsequent two-year cases introduce unearned profit, loss
allocation and finance one change at a time.
12.1
Insurance: cohorts and future service
An insurer requires policy cohorts or groups with issue dates, coverage, premiums, benefits,
expenses, lapses, claims development, guarantees and reinsurance relationships. Asset portfolios
need their own contracts and valuations. The liability state must distinguish expected future
cash flows, discounting, risk adjustments or margins, unearned profit measures where applicable,
incurred claims and movements between opening and closing states. The IFRS 17 overview’s
distinction between measuring future cash flows and recognising profit as service is provided
motivates this separation (IFRS Foundation, 2026b).
The module API accepts policy/cohort state, economic assumptions, non-economic assumptions
and information date. It returns a measurement vector, movement attribution and proposed
recognition events. Experience differences, changes in assumptions, finance effects and service
effects remain separately identifiable. Reinsurance held has its own record and measurement; it
is not an automatic percentage offset to every insurance liability.
Scenarios can alter discount curves, mortality, morbidity, lapse rates, inflation, catastrophe
losses and policyholder behaviour. Their joint dependence and cohort exposure must be declared.
A portfolio total can match history while hiding very different guarantee and duration profiles.
This makes alternative economic histories especially valuable, but also limits what public-data
reconstruction can identify. A full insurance package needs actuarial and accounting review
beyond the core release.
