4.3
Inverse problems, identification and probability
Stuart (2010), Section 2.2, starts from an observation equation y = G(u) and distinguishes
inversion, regularisation and Bayesian inference. The discussion makes explicit that a regular-
ising norm or prior is an additional assumption. AccDSL has a similar observation problem,
although its unknowns include discrete accounting interpretations and event structures as well
as continuous parameters. The infinite-dimensional well-posedness theorems in that paper are
not automatically applicable to this mixed, potentially discontinuous model.
The relevant issue is often identification rather than computation alone. Giacomini and Kitagawa
(2021), Sections 2.1–2.6 and Theorem 1 of the archived 2020 working version, show how a
class of priors can preserve uncertainty within an identified set. A likelihood that is flat over
the unresolved part does not learn the conditional prior over that part. Posterior lower and
upper probabilities summarise a class of posterior beliefs. The paper’s regularity conditions
and application to structural vector autoregressions do not prove that an arbitrary accounting
program admits the same algorithms or guarantees. The design borrows the distinction between
learnable quantities and assumption-dependent distributions; the simple accounting bounds in
Chapter 6 are derived directly.
Hoeting et al. (1999), equations (1)–(3), give Bayesian model averaging as a weighted sum
of model-conditional distributions, with weights determined by model priors and marginal
likelihoods. Sections 3 and 5 discuss computation and prior specification. This supports an
optional weighted mode, provided AccDSL specifies the likelihood, priors and candidate model
space. It does not justify giving equal mass to every syntactically different journal reconstruction.
Two encodings of the same economic history must not gain extra weight by duplication. Model
averaging also remains conditional on the considered model family, rather than repairing missing
mechanisms.
Scenario semantics need similar care.
Pearl (2009), Section 3.2.1 and equations (5)–(6),
distinguishes an intervention that replaces a structural equation from ordinary conditioning.
AccDSL will distinguish a supplied exogenous path, an observational conditional forecast and
an intervention. A rise in rates imposed by a stress test is not automatically distributed as the
collection of historical periods in which rates happened to be high. Identifying a causal effect
requires assumptions beyond reproducing accounts.
