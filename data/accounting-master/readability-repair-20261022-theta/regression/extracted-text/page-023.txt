At the start of the next period, a newly enacted law changes the current and recovery rates
to 30%. The previously recognised 40 of recoverable loss is now worth 12, so remeasurement
adds an asset and tax benefit of 2. This is a rate effect on the existing supported quantity. It
does not establish that more of the original loss is recoverable. Both rate and quantity must be
traced separately (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 12.47,56,60,79–82).

the remeasured asset of 12. The other 20 had no recognised asset to reverse, although its use

The subsidiary again earns 20, incurs tax of 6 and pays the previous liability of 5. Its closing
cash is 187.5 and its current liability is 6. Paying the old liability creates no new tax expense.
The parent closes with cash 481.5, plant 50 and the deferred asset of 9, as well as its unchanged

The parent’s shareholders receive profit 59 + 0.9(14) = 71.6, while non-controlling interests
receive 1.4. Earnings per share are 7.16. The first period’s asset of 10 and loss of 90 remain
in its comparative figures; later information changes the current estimate, not the old report
(European Commission, 2023a, IAS 8.34–38).
In the last year the parent receives 60, pays costs of 20 and depreciates plant by 20. Pretax
profit of 20 uses another 20 of the loss. The remaining 20 expires at year-end. Of the asset
of 9 brought forward, 6 is consumed by the deduction actually used and 3 is lost when the
remaining supported deduction expires. Deferred tax expense is 9, leaving parent profit of 11.
The subsidiary earns 20, incurs tax of 6 and pays both that amount and its opening liability
of 6. Group profit is 25; 23.6 belongs to the parent’s shareholders, giving earnings per share
of 2.36. Cash 717 and plant 30 are now the group’s only assets, with equity 747 and no tax
liability or loss asset. The unused loss of 20 remains in the history as an expired right, not as
an unreported zero.
The complete cash bridge provides a separate check on the three closes. Receipts of 240 less
operating cash costs of 150 and tax payments of 17 increase cash by 73, from 644 to 717.
Depreciation of 50 explains why cumulative profit is only 23. That profit increases equity from
724 to 747. The parent’s retained earnings finish at 41.5 and the subsidiary’s at 95.5. At
group level, parent retained earnings are 125.35 and the earlier ownership transaction remains a
separate equity reduction of 7.9. Adding these to parent capital and original compound equity
of 610 gives parent equity 727.45; non-controlling interests hold the remaining 19.55.
