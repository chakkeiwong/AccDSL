The documentary HedgeInventoryClose/1 report reconstructs the accepted hedge before checking
the purchase population, price, date and settlement amount. It retains purchase, settlement,
basis transfer and sale journals separately, with pre-sale books and gross cash origins. All
acquisition costs, recognition conditions and net realisable value support are supplied findings.
Missing findings leave the result unresolved; partial purchases, different valuations or insufficient
cash under the declared payment order require another method. The report is cumulative and
pre-tax: these examples do not determine an annual tax charge, non-controlling interests or a
complete company’s consolidated results.

Selling part of the hedged inventory
The purchase has absorbed the entire hedge reserve, but some goods may remain on the shelf
when the company next reports. Their cost stays in inventory until sale, subject to a write-down
if net realisable value falls below that cost. The sale does not release another portion of the
hedge reserve: that reserve was already removed when all the goods were acquired.

Now suppose reliable evidence supports net realisable value of 70 per unit for the 78 units
still held. This is a supplied estimate after completion and selling costs, with the same selling
circumstances for every unit. Inventory falls from 6480 to 78 × 70 = 5460, and the company
recognizes a write-down of 1020. IAS 2.28–31 requires the estimate to reflect the goods and their
purpose; different contracts or selling circumstances would require a more detailed population.
Retaining gross adjusted cost 6480 and an allowance 1020 makes the next sale easier to follow.
Sell 26 of these units for 2900. IAS 2.34 expenses their current carrying amount, 26 × 70 = 1820.
The sale removes 2160 of gross cost and 340 of allowance. The 52 units still held therefore
have gross cost 4320, allowance 680 and carrying amount 3640. If later evidence of changed
circumstances raises net realisable value to 90 per unit, IAS 2.33 permits a recovery only up
to original cost. Inventory returns to 4320, so the reversal is 680. Reversing the original 1020
would restore 340 relating to goods already sold and would overstate both remaining inventory
and profit.
