Multi-employer information is a classification decision
IAS 19.32–36 (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 19.32–41) requires classification from actual
plan terms, including constructive obligations. With adequate reliable allocation, a participant
in a defined-benefit multi-employer plan recognizes its share of obligation, assets and cost. If the
specific paragraph 34 information condition holds, account as if defined contribution and retain
the paragraph 148 disclosures. Insufficient information is not proof that the plan economically
has no defined-benefit exposure.
Paragraph 37 separately requires any contractual surplus/deficit-funding asset or liability and
resulting profit effect. A synthetic agreed deficit payment 121 due in two years, with a supplied
appropriate discount rate 10%, has liability 100 and expense 100 initially, followed by interest
10 and 11 and settlement 121. Ordinary current-service contributions are distinct. Do not
omit the deficit contract because ongoing contributions use defined-contribution accounting or
recognize the nominal 121 immediately while also accruing interest.
Withdrawal or wind-up liabilities follow IAS 37 under paragraph 39. A plan among entities
under common control is not a multi-employer plan under paragraph 40; paragraph 41’s plan-
wide information and separate-entity charge/allocation rules must be applied instead. The
record must therefore retain employer relationships, allocation reliability, contributions, deficit
agreements, withdrawal terms and required disclosures. Unknown relationship or contractual
terms block the classification, not the preservation of the underlying evidence.

Opening work carries both cost and completed effort
The earlier production example began with an empty factory account. An opening work-in-
progress balance changes both the cost available and the work still needed. Materials may
already be present even though conversion remains unfinished. Treating the opening units as
entirely new would charge that effort twice; ignoring their costs would lose part of the inventory.
Consider homogeneous units with all materials added at the start and conversion effort incurred
evenly. Ten opening units contain material cost 100 and conversion cost 50 and are halfway
through conversion. The factory starts another 90 units, incurring material cost 900 and
conversion cost 510. It completes 80 units and leaves 20 units halfway through conversion.
Assume the opening units are completed first, no spoilage, no idle-capacity cost, and net
realizable values above cost. Physical units reconcile as 10 + 90 = 80 + 20; available cost is
100 + 50 + 900 + 510 = 1560. These are supplied production facts, not estimates derived from a
reported inventory total.
