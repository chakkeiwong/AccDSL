To determine the contract balance, count consideration already paid or unconditionally due
once. If A is cumulative advance cash, adding it to gross invoices would count credited advances
twice. Subtract K to obtain T = G + A −K. Compare this with cumulative earned revenue R:
T = G + A −K,
CA = max(R −T, 0),
CL = max(T −R, 0).
At invoicing, T = 80 + 30 −30 = 80, giving the liability 80 −55 = 25. Collection leaves T
unchanged: cash replaces a right that was already unconditional. Together these relations imply,
for cumulative paid costs E and opening capital 100,
cash + AR + CA −CL = 100 + R −E.
Substitution shows why: cash is 100 + A + C −E, the receivable is G −K −C, and the net
contract asset is R −G −A + K. The advance, invoice, credit and collection terms cancel,
leaving earned profit in equity. The cancellation checks the arithmetic; the contractual findings
determine which amounts belong in it.
The May amendment now adds construction revenue of 6 without a receipt, reducing the liability
from 25 to 19. A new, separately identified advance of 50 raises cash to 158 and the liability to
69. Two further visits earn 10 and cost 4, leaving cash 154, receivables 30, contract liability 59
and profit 25. The remaining gross invoice of 90 credits the new advance of 50. Its net right
of 40 increases total receivables to 70; paid-or-due consideration reaches 170, so the contract
liability becomes 170 −71 = 99.
Collect the first invoice’s remaining 30, then complete construction and the last three visits
for cash costs of 66. Completion earns the remaining 99 and removes the contract liability.
Cash is 118, the second invoice still carries 40, and equity is 158. Collecting that 40 leaves
cash and equity of 158, as in the prepaid example. Customer cash is advances 30 + 50 plus
collections 20 + 30 + 40, totalling 170. Paid costs are 112 and profit is 58. The gross invoices of
170 describe the rights created, not an additional receipt.
Full prepayment provides a useful limiting case. If the final invoice credits all 170 already
received, it creates no receivable and no monetary journal; it still records which advances the
invoice consumes. Conversely, invoicing before any work can create a receivable and contract
liability together. Both schedules preserve the same earned amounts under these terms. The
finite method retains these billing identities alongside the two performance calculations. Nonzero
impairment, refundable excess payments, contract losses and tax require their own measurements
before this example can be joined to a more general company book.

The production record adds started/completed/scrapped quantities, material insertion point,
conversion stages, opening WIP by component, observed progress, actual eligible costs, normal
capacity, fixed/variable overhead classification, NRV, and the costing-policy version. Equivalent
units are a derived policy justified by this homogeneous process; IAS 2 (European Commission,
2023a, IAS 2.12–18) prescribes rational cost allocation and the normal-capacity constraint, not
this exact spreadsheet convention.
