10.12.3
The actual option and the hedged price need separate calculations
The time-value comparison also has to fit the risk being hedged. An actual option may have
a different exercise price from the price level the company has designated. Its payoff then
differs from the one-sided change in the hedged purchase. IFRS 9.B6.5.5 permits a hypothetical
derivative to measure that item but prohibits importing features that belong only to the actual
instrument. B6.5.32 similarly measures aligned time value using an option whose critical terms
match the item (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 9.B6.5.5, B6.5.32–33). The following
complete transaction uses the retained historical EU compilation; it is a distinct conditional
example from the preceding AASB-profile illustrations.
Consider a one-unit inventory purchase with a designated one-sided price risk above 110. The
company buys a call with strike 100. At designation, spot is 90 and both calls have zero intrinsic
value. The actual option costs 6, entirely time value; the hypothetical call with strike 110 has
aligned time value 4. These component values and the qualifying designation are supplied. The
company pays only the actual premium. The hypothetical option is a calculation of the hedged
risk, not another acquired asset.
At a later spot of 115, actual intrinsic value is 115 −100 = 15, while the hedged item’s aligned
intrinsic value is 115 −110 = 5. Suppose actual and aligned time values are both 3. The actual
option is worth 18 and the hypothetical option is worth 8. The effective intrinsic gain is 5; the
remaining actual intrinsic gain of 10 enters profit. Because initial actual time value exceeded
aligned time value, B6.5.33(a) gives a cost reserve change of 3−4 = −1 and time-value mismatch
profit of (3 −6) −(−1) = −2. Thus total reserve is 4 and profit is 8, together accounting for
the actual option’s gain of 18 −6 = 12.
The distinction can be expressed directly from the contractual price levels. Let x be the observed
price, q the common quantity, KA the actual strike and KH the hedged price level. The two
intrinsic values are
IA = q max(x −KA, 0),
IH = q max(x −KH, 0).
With zero initial intrinsic values, these are also the cumulative intrinsic gains. In the supplied
qualifying relationship, the intrinsic reserve is RI = min(IA, IH), and intrinsic mismatch profit
is IA −RI. Writing actual and aligned time values as TA, TH, with designation values TA0, TH0,
the admitted cumulative-loss cases give
