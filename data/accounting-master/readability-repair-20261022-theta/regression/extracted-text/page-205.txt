Suppose a qualifying inventory hedge begins with cash and equity of 200. Buy an option for
6, entirely time value. Cash is 194 and the option asset is 6; there is no initial expense. The
first supplied valuation is intrinsic value 12 and aligned time value 4. The option is worth 16.
Its increase of 10 comprises an effective intrinsic gain of 12 and a time-value loss of 2, both in
OCI but in separate reserves. At purchase and expiry, intrinsic value is 20 and time value zero.
The option is worth 20, the intrinsic reserve is 20 and the cost reserve is negative 6. The last
period’s change of 4 comprises intrinsic gain 8 and time-value loss 4.
Buy the inventory for 120 and settle the option for 20. Settlement removes the option asset and
leaves cash 94. The intrinsic reserve reduces inventory cost by 20. Removing the negative cost
reserve adds 6 to inventory cost:
120 −20 + 6 = 106.
Both direct transfers affect the asset and equity; neither is a second cash flow or an OCI
reclassification. Cash 94 plus inventory 106 equals equity 200 after the transfers. Selling for 130
gives profit 24 and cash 224. The cash bridge is 200 −6 −120 + 20 + 130 = 224. The equity
bridge is opening 200, profit 24, cumulative OCI 14, and a direct basis transfer of negative 14.
Omitting the premium would report cost 100 and overstate profit by 6. For a forecast sale
instead, the accumulated cost enters earnings when the sale does. An unrecoverable portion
enters earnings immediately under 6.5.15(b)(iii), rather than waiting for a transaction that
cannot recover it.
10.12.2
Protection over time and imperfectly matching terms
Consider separately an option that protects an existing inventory holding for two equal periods.
Admit aligned initial time value 6 and a rational even allocation over those periods. Intrinsic-
value and inventory movements are zero in this illustration, isolating the protection cost. After
one period, supplied time value 2 gives an OCI loss of 4. Amortisation of 3 is reclassified to
expense, leaving the cost reserve at negative 1. After expiry, the final time-value loss of 2 and
second amortisation of 3 leave no reserve; cumulative expense is 6. Immediately expensing each
observed time-value fall would instead charge 4 and 2. That is a different timing policy. If the
intrinsic-value hedging relationship is discontinued after the first period, 6.5.15(c) requires the
remaining net reserve loss of 1 to enter earnings immediately. The remaining option asset of 2
is still an asset; discontinuation does not destroy it.
When critical terms differ, B6.5.32 requires an aligned option valuation using terms that perfectly
match the hedged item. The hypothetical option is a measurement input for the related cost,
not a replacement of the actual contract. Under B6.5.33(a) (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2022h, B6.5.32–33), if actual time value exceeds aligned time value at inception, base the
cost reserve on aligned changes and put the difference between actual and aligned changes in
profit or loss. For initial actual/aligned values 6/4 and later values 3/3, actual loss 3 separates
into OCI loss 1 and profit loss 2. At expiry, cumulative losses 6/4 leave OCI loss 4 and profit
loss 2, before the required transfer or reclassification.
