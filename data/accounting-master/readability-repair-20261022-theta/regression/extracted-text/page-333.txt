To extend the opening balance sheet, supply the missing total explicitly. Assume a separate
complete measurement gives a reinsurance asset of 50, already including the loss-recovery
component of 12, against an old asset of 45. These are additional conditional inputs; the
multiplication above does not derive them. Other assets remain 500 and the two issued liabilities
still increase from 220 to 230. Total assets therefore rise from 500 + 45 = 545 to 500 + 50 = 550,
while equity moves from 545 −220 = 325 to 550 −230 = 320. The asset increase adds 5 to
equity against the issued-liability increase of 10, giving a net reduction of 5. An illustrative
reinsurance entry debits the new asset 50, credits the old asset 45 and credits opening equity 5.
Adding another asset of 12 would count a component of the supplied 50 twice and overstate
equity by 12. The net transition change belongs in equity under C4(b)–(c), rather than being
inferred as current recovery income from the ordinary recognition rule (Australian Accounting
Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs C4–C5 and C20A).
The component also imposes a useful later check.
If B’s loss component falls to 14 and
the recoverable portion remains 60%, paragraph B119F limits the reinsurance loss-recovery
component to 14 × 0.60 = 8.4. Leaving it at 12 would violate that bound. The bound alone
neither selects a unique new component nor determines the full reinsurance asset or period’s
profit; the actual changes and allocation rules still matter. Similarly, q = 0 gives no loss-recovery
component and q = 1 gives 20, without erasing the issued obligation or determining the entire
asset (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 66–67 and B119F).
