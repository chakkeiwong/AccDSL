2.16.3
A partner’s remedy can change Apple’s revenue
The risk discussion connects these matters to operations. Both filings report changes to U.S.
developer communications and EU changes to distribution, payment processing, fee structures
and developer tools. They also describe licensing revenue from Google and other search providers,
whose arrangements are subject to investigations and proceedings (Apple Inc., 2024a, Risk
Factors, printed pp. 14–15) (Apple Inc., 2025c, Part II, Item 1A, printed pp. 19–20). The
interim adds that Google was found to have violated U.S. antitrust laws on 5 August 2024
and that the DOJ proposed remedies ranging from restrictions on licensing agreements to
prohibiting commercial search-distribution terms with Apple for up to ten years. Apple says
that, if implemented, those remedies could materially affect its ability to earn that licensing
revenue. The finding is an earlier event newly described in this retained comparison; the
remedies are proposed, not reported here as imposed.
That channel differs from an Apple fine. The affected economic state would include the search
agreement, its revenue terms, duration, geography and substitution possibilities. Likewise, an
App Store remedy requires the affected storefront, developer terms, fees, customer purchases
and behavioural response. The filings identify these channels without supplying the contract
economics needed to calculate them. They do not justify applying one loss percentage to all
Services revenue or treating every customer as affected.
A conditional calculation should consequently specify which matter and remedy it assumes,
when that remedy takes effect, which contracts it changes and how the parties respond. A
monetary-loss calculation additionally needs recognition facts, any opening accrual and the
settlement and payment schedule; expense recognition and cash payment can occur in different
periods. The State Aid tax charge and escrow settlement, developed in Section 9.7, retain
their own tax records and must not be counted again as a DMA loss. Until the missing facts
and applicable treatments are supplied, the model can retain the reported proceedings and
affected queries as unresolved, without assigning probabilities or producing numerical journals.
This preserves useful knowledge about future exposure while keeping the distinction between a
known assertion and a supported financial consequence.
2.17
When later disclosures change the historical query
A later filing can reveal more about an earlier period without identifying a different economic
history. For example, a new segment-expense disclosure may make a previously hidden cost
visible, while the transactions that generated that cost remain the same. To use that information
correctly, a model must retain the original report, the later presentation and the date at which
the additional detail became available. Apple’s announcement and its later segment tables
show the difference between a planned disclosure and the comparative information eventually
reported.
