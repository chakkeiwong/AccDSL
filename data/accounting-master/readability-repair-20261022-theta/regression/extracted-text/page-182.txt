10.2.2
Attribute earnings before testing potential shares
Suppose consolidated profit is 15,000 from continuing operations and 18,000 in total. Non-
controlling interests receive 1,000, all from continuing operations. A cumulative, nonparticipating
preference class, classified here as equity, has a current-year entitlement of 2,000. Deduct that
entitlement even though it has not been declared. A payment of 500 of prior-year arrears is
not another deduction from this year’s earnings. These are supplied attribution facts; the EPS
calculation does not infer them from cash dividends (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 33,
paragraphs 12–14 and A1).
Earnings attributable to the parent’s ordinary shareholders are thus 15000 −1000 −2000 =
12000 from continuing operations and 18000 −1000 −2000 = 15000 in total. Discontinued
operations contribute 3,000. Basic continuing EPS is 12000/D ≈5.3914; basic total EPS is
15000/D ≈6.7393. Their difference, approximately 1.3479, is basic discontinued EPS. Profit
already includes the interest and tax effects described below; adding interest again at this stage
would be wrong.
There are three potential-share series. First, cash options have been outstanding all year: 200
shares at an exercise price of 4 before the split, and 400 at 2 afterward. Use a supplied average
market price of 5 on the post-split basis. Earlier prices must be converted to that same basis.
The options require no future employee services and contain no performance condition, debt
tender, redemption requirement or cash-settlement alternative.
Assumed exercise would provide 400(2) = 800. At the average market price, 800 corresponds
to 160 fairly priced shares. Only the remaining 400 −160 = 240 shares enter the diluted
denominator; there is no earnings adjustment. Adding all 400 shares would ignore the assumed
proceeds.
IAS 33 describes this as the difference between assumed issue and the shares
represented by proceeds at the average price (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 33, paragraphs
45–47 and A4–A5). The calculation records neither cash of 800 nor an actual share issue.
Second, a convertible debt series is issued on 1 July. Its original terms give the holder a voluntary
right to convert at any time before its 1 January 2026 maturity into 600 post-split ordinary
shares. Assume the debt is denominated in the issuer’s functional currency and the conversion
right is equity-classified, with no additional derivative features. Conversion settles solely in those
shares; the unconverted debt retains the cash payments specified below. No terms are changed
and no additional consideration is offered to induce conversion. For this case, supplied issue
proceeds of 10,000 divide into a liability of 9,200 and conversion equity of 800. The recognized
effective-interest expense for the remaining year is 800, with a related tax benefit of 200 and no
other earnings consequences. The original terms pay a coupon of 400 on 1 October and principal
of 9,200 plus a final coupon of 400 on 1 January 2026. The supplied effective rate is 1/23 per
92-day period: discounting those payments gives 400/(24/23) + 9600/(24/23)2 = 9200. Interest
of 400 first raises the liability to 9,600; the October coupon returns it to 9,200, and another 400
of interest gives a year-end liability of 9,600 before January settlement. The intervening payment
explains why the two interest amounts are equal. These supplied amounts include the relevant
cost and discount effects; substituting a coupon alone need not do so. Instrument classification
and component valuation are separate accounting inputs, illustrated by the compound-bond
example below.
