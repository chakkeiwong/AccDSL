Partial goodwill and the non-controlling interest
IAS 36 Appendix C (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 36.C1–C9) distinguishes the consolidated
carrying amount from the amount used for an impairment test. When acquisition-date NCI was
measured as its proportionate share of identifiable net assets, the reported goodwill omits NCI
goodwill, while the unit’s recoverable amount includes its contribution. C4 therefore requires a
notional gross-up before the test. C8 prevents recognizing an impairment of goodwill that was
never recognized. Neither the notional goodwill nor its notional impairment is posted as an
asset or expense.
Retain the acquisition’s NCI measurement election, ownership and profit-allocation history, unit
allocation and evidence for the notional amount. Division of parent goodwill by ownership is a
conditional illustration when proportional goodwill is justified; it must not silently assume away
a control premium or differing synergies. For a subsidiary that is itself a unit, C6 allocates loss
on the same basis as profit. C7 separately allocates a larger unit’s goodwill losses by the parts’
relative goodwill and identifiable-asset losses by their relative net identifiable carrying amounts,
then allocates within each part. Preserve the NCI-bearing and parent-only parts rather than
applying one ownership fraction to the entire group.
Assume the parent owns 80%, acquired proportionate-share NCI, has recognized goodwill 40
and supports proportional notional goodwill 50. Identifiable unit assets carry 200. If recoverable
amount 220, test 250 against 220 and allocate loss 30 entirely to goodwill. The recognized
goodwill loss is 24; the NCI notional loss 6 is not recorded. Reported goodwill falls from 40
to 16 and reported assets total 216; adding the remaining notional NCI goodwill 4 gives test
carrying amount 220. Demanding the reported amount equal 220 would improperly capitalize
that 4.
If recoverable amount instead falls to 180 in a separate branch, test loss 70 removes notional
goodwill 50 then identifiable assets 20. Recognized loss is 60: parent goodwill 40 plus asset
loss 20. Of the recognized loss, 56 belongs to the parent and 4 to NCI. The unrecognized
NCI-goodwill loss 10 never enters either profit or NCI equity. Closing identifiable assets 180
and goodwill 0 reconcile the reported decrease from 240 to 180. Goodwill impairment cannot
subsequently reverse under IAS 36.124; a rising valuation is not a new recognized goodwill asset.
A larger-unit branch adds a wholly owned part with goodwill 20 and identifiable assets 100.
Total test carrying amount 370 includes subsidiary notional goodwill 50 and parent-only goodwill
20. At recoverable amount 270, loss 100 first removes all goodwill 70, then allocates 30 to
identifiable assets in their 200 : 100 ratio. Recognized loss 90 comprises goodwill 60 and assets
30; attribution is parent 86 and NCI 4. The notional 10 remains unrecognized. Asset floors and
allocation of remaining goodwill must be respected when a branch does not exhaust goodwill.
