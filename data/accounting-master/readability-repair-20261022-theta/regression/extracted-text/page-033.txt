The note describes collection shortly after control transfer or the start of service delivery. It does
not specify one collection lag for every contract. Products revenue also reflects expected returns,
price protection and customer incentives. Revenue excludes taxes collected for remittance to
governments (Apple Inc., 2024a, Notes 1–2, printed pp. 34–35). Together these qualifications
prevent a simple conversion of reported revenue into gross customer receipts.
Presentation creates a second distinction. Where Apple obtains control of a third-party product
before sale, it reports the gross amount billed. For third-party applications sold through the App
Store, its stated treatment records only the retained commission in Services revenue. Services
also already includes recognition of the deferred value of services bundled with products (Apple
Inc., 2024a, Note 2, printed p. 35). Adding that recognition to reported Services a second time
would double count it; treating Services as gross App Store customer spending would change
the measured quantity.
The product and geographic tables describe different divisions of the same sales. The five
product/service rows sum to 391,035 million dollars in fiscal 2024 and 124,300 million in the
quarter ended 28 December 2024. The geographic segment sales sum to those same totals. They
must not be added together. Apple says the product mix is generally similar across segments,
except that iPhone represents a moderately higher share in Greater China. That qualitative
statement does not identify a product-by-region matrix (Apple Inc., 2024a, Notes 2 and 13,
printed pp. 35–36 and 46–47) (Apple Inc., 2025c, Notes 2 and 10, printed pp. 6 and 11).
2.11.1
The movement that the note leaves unidentified
At 28 September 2024 Apple reports total deferred revenue of 12.8 billion dollars. At 28
December it reports 13.2 billion, and says that quarter’s net sales include 3.7 billion recognised
from the deferred balance at the September date (Apple Inc., 2025c, Note 2, printed p. 6). The
balance sheet’s December current deferred-revenue line is 8,461 million, with 8,249 million at
September (Apple Inc., 2025c, printed p. 3). Those are current liabilities; the note’s larger
amounts are totals. The precision differs too: the total is displayed to tenths of a billion,
whereas the current line is displayed in millions.
Let D0 and D1 denote opening and closing total deferred revenue. During the quarter, let
A be additions to deferred revenue, Ro revenue released from the opening balance, and Rn
revenue released from amounts added during the quarter. Let M collect other signed movements,
positive when they increase the liability. These symbols describe a balance reconciliation; M is
not an assertion that a particular adjustment occurred. In billions of dollars,
D1 = D0 +A−Ro −Rn +M,
A−Rn +M = D1 −D0 +Ro ≃13.2−12.8+3.7 = 4.1. (2.21)
The source identifies Ro, not the whole release Ro + Rn. The 4.1 billion residual therefore
measures net additions after new-origin releases and other movements at the displayed amounts.
Calling it gross new deferrals, billings or cash collected would be wrong relative to what has
been calculated.
Rounding also matters. If each of the three displayed inputs is rounded to the nearest 0.1
billion, a closed outer envelope for that residual is [3.95, 4.25] billion: the lower endpoint uses
13.15 −12.85 + 3.65, and the upper uses 13.25 −12.75 + 3.75. This is a conditional precision
calculation, not a verified issuer rounding rule or a bound on gross collections. Its endpoints
leave the treatment of exact rounding ties unspecified.
Two constructed histories make the missing movement visible. For this illustration alone, use
the displayed amounts as exact inputs and set M = 0. Keep the reported quarter’s total revenue
at 124.3 billion. The table supplies two different choices for A and Rn; other recognised revenue
is the remainder needed to match that total.
