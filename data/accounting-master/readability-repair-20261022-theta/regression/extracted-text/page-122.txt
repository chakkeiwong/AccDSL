If the additional six units instead cost 60 and meet paragraph 20’s standalone-price condition,
the addition is a separate contract. The first four still earn 40; the six remaining original
units earn 60 and the new six earn 60. Both contracts keep their own allocation records. The
difference between these cases is the admitted price and scope assessment, not permission to
choose whichever result produces the preferred margin.
An integrated promise can have the opposite effect on past cumulative revenue. Suppose a
customer controls work in progress as the company creates it, satisfying paragraph 35(b). The
company has one integrated performance obligation, and its remaining work is not distinct
from work already supplied. Original price 100 and supported output progress of 40% yielded
revenue 40. An approved enforceable amendment changes that same promise to price 130; the
revised faithful output measure is 30% of the expanded work. Under 21(b), both the price and
progress change the cumulative amount earned:
revised cumulative revenue = 130 × 0.30 = 39,
catch-up = 39 −40 = −1.
The new work makes the completed work a smaller share of the promise. Revenue decreases by
1 at amendment even though the total contract price increases. The old 40 remains attached to
its original information view; the modification does not establish that the earlier estimate was
an error.
For a complete prepaid example, start with cash and equity 50. Receive 100 and pay service
costs 30 before amendment. Recognition of 40 leaves cash 120, liability 60 and equity 60. At
amendment, receive the additional 30 and debit revenue 1 while crediting the liability 1. Cash
is now 150, the liability is 91 and equity is 59: cumulative revenue 39 less costs 30 has earned
profit 9. Operating cash since opening is 100, also 9 + 91. Completing the promise next period
releases revenue 91 and incurs further paid service costs of 60. Period profit is 31, operating
cash is 31 −91 = −60, and closing cash and equity are 90. Lifetime profit is 130 −90 = 40.
The output progress percentages were independently supported; they were not inferred from
these cash costs.
Both modifications need the approved rights, effective date, previous and revised promise,
consideration, performance measurements and source versions. The distinct-goods method
freezes the fulfilled deliveries and replaces the remaining allocation; the integrated method
proposes only the difference between revised cumulative revenue and the amount already
recognised. Economic state and journals change atomically. Different billing schedules can
require a contract-asset adjustment instead of the prepaid liability entries used here. A request
that has not been approved leaves the existing contract operative under paragraph 18. Unknown
enforceability stays unresolved; approved scope with undetermined price requires paragraph
19’s variable-consideration estimate and constraint, not a price of zero.
