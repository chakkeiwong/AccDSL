First put the actual tax benefit on a consistent basis for the EPS allocation. Reported profit is
P + H when that benefit is recognised in profit, or P when it is recognised directly in equity.
Adding the latter benefit for this allocation gives adjusted profit G = P + H in either case.
Subtract all declared class dividends, including the ordinary tax-benefit dividend:
R = G −aD −bD −H = P −D.
This signed residual is the base profit still to allocate under the hypothetical distribution. It can
be negative when the actual dividend includes earlier years’ earnings. The participating residual
is aR, the ordinary base residual is bR, and the ordinary holders’ further tax adjustment is taR.
Adding declared and residual amounts gives
NO = bD + H + bR + taR,
NP = aD + aR,
EPSbasic = NO/n,
where NO and NP are the ordinary and participating earnings allocations and n > 0 is the
supplied weighted-average ordinary denominator. These are the example’s three steps: adjust
and subtract declared dividends, allocate the remaining profit and its tax consequence, then add
the declared allocations back to each class (IFRS Interpretations Committee, 2017b, Steps 1–3,
pp. 3–5).

In year 3 the ordinary dividend is 60 + 180 = 240. Its remaining base allocation is −50, and
the tax adjustment on that residual is −150, giving 240 −50 −150 = 40. Setting the negative
adjustment to zero would give 190 and count part of a benefit already attributed to earlier
earnings again. The −150 is an EPS-allocation reversal; it is neither a negative cash dividend
nor evidence that every tax loss creates a recognised asset.
The dependence on payout timing cancels under these fixed rights. Substituting H = taD and
R = P −D into the class allocations yields the local identities
NO = bD + taD + (b + ta)(P −D) = (b + ta)P,
NP = aD + a(P −D) = aP,
NO + NP = P + taP = G + taR.
