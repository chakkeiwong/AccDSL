11.9
Collateral received after maturity
Collateral changes expected receipts through its amount, timing and enforcement costs. Consider
a separate loan advancing 100 on 1 January 2024 and requiring 121 on 1 January 2025. Its
contractual half-year accumulation factor is 1.1, equivalent to 21% effective annual interest.
This rate convention differs from the preceding annual 10% loan.
The supplied initial law assigns probability 3/5 to full payment at maturity and 2/5 to a
default first observed within twelve months. In the default branch, collateral receipts arrive
on 1 July 2025, one half-year after maturity. Possible net receipts 121, 66.55 and 12.10 have
conditional probabilities 1/4, 1/2, 1/4, respectively. Their conditional mean is 66.55. The bank
has supported reasonable prospects of fuller recovery before the sale; 66.55 is not a known upper
limit. These probabilities are illustrative assumptions.
The expected loss compares the present value of contractual cash with that of all expected receipts.
The full-payment value is 121/1.12 = 100; the default-branch mean has value 66.55/1.13 = 50.

The extra half-year belongs in the discounting. Omitting receipts after maturity produces the
wrong loss 40. B5.5.55 expressly includes collateral cash beyond maturity and deducts obtaining
and selling costs; B5.5.43 limits twelve-month losses by the default date, not by the recovery
date. Here all possible defaults occur in the first year, so twelve-month and lifetime losses
coincide (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, B5.5.43–44 and B5.5.55).
Start with cash 200, deposits 180 and equity 20. Advance 100 and recognise allowance 20.
On 1 July 2024, first accrue gross interest 10. Rolling the original expectation forward gives
net 88 against gross 110, so allowance rises 20 to 22 through impairment expense 2. A separately
observed qualitative credit-impairment event then establishes the collateral-only expectation:
66.55/1.12 = 55. Increase allowance 22 to 55 through impairment expense 33. The event is an
admitted financial-distress finding, not an invented missed instalment before any instalment is
due.
During the next half-year, original-rate interest on net 55 is 5.5. Debit gross loan 11, credit
interest 5.5 and allowance 5.5. At contractual maturity gross 121 less allowance 60.5 leaves
net 60.5. There is no contractual post-maturity interest, unamortised fee or premium. The
initial 100-to 121 difference has been fully amortised. Over the final half-year the expected
receipt value rises 60.5 to 66.55, giving net interest 6.05. Under these terms, gross remains 121
and allowance falls 60.5 to 54.45: debit allowance 6.05 and credit interest 6.05. This follows from
the Appendix A definitions and B5.5.33’s gross-minus-discounted-receipts measurement. It is a
local derivation for these terms, not a rule that every overdue contract stops accruing contractual
interest (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, Appendix A, paragraph 5.4.1(b),
B5.5.33).

included in the forecast, and remits 66.55. Debit cash 66.55 and credit gross loan 66.55. Gross
