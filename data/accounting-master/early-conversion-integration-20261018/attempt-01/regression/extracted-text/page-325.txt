12.25
From transition measurements to opening equity
The fair-value approach produces a margin or a loss component, but those two outcomes enter
the liability differently. Consider two distinct groups of issued contracts for which the grouping
and eligibility for this transition method have already been established. Use the historical
provisions just described and assume that both groups contain only remaining coverage. All
amounts below are authored currency units at the same transition date. There are no incurred
claims, reinsurance, acquisition balances, tax, accumulated OCI or other transition adjustments
in this example. These simplifications isolate the effect of the two measurements; they do not
supply actual insurer valuations or evidence of eligibility.
For group A, suppose the measured fulfilment cash flows are 90 and the separately measured fair
value is 110. The FCF amount already includes the discounting and nonfinancial risk adjustment
required by the measurement rules. Paragraph C20 makes the difference, 110 −90 = 20, the
CSM. This is unearned profit associated with future services. Keeping it within the liability
gives 90 + 20 = 110; recording it as immediate profit would contradict that role (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 32–38, 40(a) and C20).
Now reverse the ordering of the inputs. For group B, let FCF be 120 and fair value 100. The
difference identifies a loss component of 20, while CSM is zero. The liability is 120. Paragraphs
49–50 describe the loss component as part of the liability for remaining coverage, with its own
subsequent allocation and reversal rules. Adding another 20 to the FCF balance would count
that component twice. Replacing the liability by fair value, 100, would instead understate the
required FCF balance (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 40, 47, 49–50
and C20).
Write F for measured future-service FCF, V for the group’s fair value, C for CSM and K for
the nonnegative loss component. Positive amounts denote liabilities. The two sign cases give
the following local derivation:
C = max(V −F, 0),
K = max(F −V, 0),
L = F + C = max(F, V ).
Here L is the recognised remaining-coverage liability. The loss component K is tracked within
L and is not added to it. Thus C −K = V −F, and at V = F both components are zero.
These relations apply to the stated transition case; later changes in FCF, service provision and
finance effects follow the subsequent-measurement rules.
