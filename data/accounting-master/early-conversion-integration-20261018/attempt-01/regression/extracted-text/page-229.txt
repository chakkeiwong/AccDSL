10.13.8
An unpaid participating dividend can change ordinary earnings
Assumed conversion is not the only hypothetical transaction that changes earnings per share.
Allocating undistributed profit between ordinary and participating shares can itself have a
tax consequence. Suppose participating dividends are tax deductible and the resulting benefit
belongs to ordinary shareholders. An allocation that divides reported profit solely by the
base participation ratio misses that additional ordinary entitlement. The June 2017 IFRS
Interpretations Committee decision requires the ordinary numerator to include the ordinary
holders’ share of the tax benefit from the hypothetical distribution required by IAS 33.A14.
This applies whether an actual benefit is recognised in profit or directly in equity (IFRS
Interpretations Committee, 2017a).
The accompanying example makes the consequence visible before any dividend is paid. Partici-
pating and ordinary holders share base distributions in the ratio ten to one. The tax deduction
is 30% of the participating distribution, and its entire benefit belongs to ordinary holders. In the
first year, profit before that benefit is 330 and no dividend is declared. The ordinary base share
is 330/11 = 30, while participating holders’ hypothetical allocation is 300. Distributing that
300 would create a benefit of 90 for ordinary holders. Their basic-EPS numerator is therefore
30 + 90 = 120, although no cash dividend or actual distribution-related tax benefit arose in that
year (IFRS Interpretations Committee, 2017b, pp. 1–5).
