10.13.5
Participation allocates the numerator before division
IAS 33.A13–A14 (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 33.A13–A16) allocates earnings according
to the actual dividend and undistributed-earnings rights of the classes. First deduct the declared
and contractually required dividends from the period’s allocable result. Allocate the remainder
as if it were distributed, using the participation formula and any caps. Add each class’s dividends
back to its allocation, then divide by the instruments to which that allocation belongs. A class’s
right to profits does not establish a right or obligation to share losses; preserve the actual terms.
Unknown participation rights block the allocation.
Suppose parent-attributable profit is 120, 100 ordinary shares participate with weight one, and
ten nonconvertible participating shares have weight two. Assume all are outstanding throughout
the period and there are no senior claims, caps or other instruments. Declared dividends are
20 and 4. Remaining profit 96 allocates over total weight 100 + 2(10) = 120: ordinary shares
receive 80 and participating shares receive 16. Adding dividends gives class earnings 100 and
20, so per-share earnings are 1 and 2. Allocating all 120 to the ordinary shares would overstate
their EPS as 1.2. Dividends are an allocation of this profit, not a second expense. A capped
or senior participation formula needs its own piecewise allocation; the simple weights cannot
replace it.
Diluted EPS then applies the relevant potential-share rules and dilution tests; it is not the
same calculation as award expense. Under paragraphs 47A–48 (European Commission, 2023a,
IAS 33.47A–60), future goods or services form part of the assumed proceeds for employee options.
A separate case with profit 100, 100 ordinary shares, ten options, cash exercise price 5, period-
average future service value 2 per option and average share price 10 has proceeds 70. Seven
shares are assumed repurchased, so incremental shares are three and diluted EPS is 100/103.
Omitting future services would instead add five. This comparison assumes the awards are
outstanding for the whole period and pass the applicable dilution test; performance-conditioned
awards also require paragraphs 52–57’s period-end contingency assessment.
Under paragraphs 58–60, an entity cash/share choice presumes share settlement if dilutive,
whereas a holder choice uses the more dilutive outcome. Required numerator adjustments
preserve the profit effect of assumed equity treatment. For a separate nonparticipating contract,
let reported profit be 90 on 100 shares after liability-remeasurement expense 10 that would be
absent under wholly equity classification, and let assumed share settlement add 20 shares. With
no tax effect or other changes, cash gives EPS 0.9 and share settlement gives 100/120 = 5/6.
The share outcome is more dilutive. Adding shares while leaving profit at 90 would produce 0.75
and compute the wrong assumed world. Continuing-operations control, instrument ordering,
time weights and antidilution remain the earlier EPS method’s requirements, not assumptions
waived by this example.
10.13.6
A senior dividend and a cap change who receives the next unit of
profit
The proportional example leaves every additional unit of earnings to the same sharing rule.
A senior entitlement and a cap change that allocation. Consider 100 ordinary shares and 20
nonconvertible participating preference instruments, all outstanding throughout the year. The
supplied terms give the preference class a cumulative current-year entitlement of 20, then equal
participation per instrument in positive residual earnings, up to total class earnings of 40.
The ordinary shares receive what remains. The preference class does not share residual losses.
