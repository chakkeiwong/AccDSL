An asset floor changes the allocation of a unit’s loss
A cash-generating unit can lose value while one of its assets retains a substantial disposal value.
Allocating the unit’s loss in proportion to asset balances just once may then reduce that asset
too far. IAS 36.104–105 first allocates loss to goodwill, then to the other assets pro rata, while
protecting each asset from a reduction below the highest of zero, its fair value less costs of
disposal if measurable, and its value in use if determinable. A share that cannot be assigned to
one asset is redistributed to the others (European Commission, 2023a).
Consider a synthetic cost-model unit containing equipment A with carrying amount 60, equip-
ment B with carrying amount 40, and allocated acquired goodwill 20. Cash 80 lies outside
the tested unit. With no liabilities, opening equity is 200. The unit boundary and goodwill
allocation are assumed to have been admitted under paragraphs 66–80; there are no shared cor-
porate assets, noncontrolling interests or liabilities requiring an adjustment to this comparison.
In a real case those judgments precede the calculation. In particular, paragraph 78 requires
consistent treatment of a liability that is included in the unit’s recoverable value; subtracting it
from only the carrying amount would compare different populations.
At measurement date T0, admitted value in use is 70 and fair value less disposal costs is 65, so
the unit’s recoverable amount is 70. Individual values in use are supported as not determinable,
while the individual disposal values are 55 for A and 5 for B. These are supplied valuation
assumptions, not values inferred by an allocation routine. They establish floors of 55 and 5.
A missing valuation would be a different state: the program may omit a term because its
non-determinability is admitted, but may not replace an unexamined input with zero.
The unit’s loss is 60 + 40 + 20 −70 = 50. Goodwill takes the first 20. An unconstrained
allocation of the remaining 30 would assign 30 × 60/100 = 18 to A and 30 × 40/100 = 12 to B.
A, however, can absorb only 60 −55 = 5. Its excess share of 13 passes to B, whose total loss
becomes 12 + 13 = 25, within its capacity 40 −5 = 35. The closing balances are therefore
A = 60 −5 = 55,
B = 40 −25 = 15,
goodwill = 20 −20 = 0.
Together they equal the unit’s recoverable amount of 70. Debit impairment expense 50, credit
goodwill 20, and credit the separate asset impairment allowances by 5 and 25. Cash 80 plus
equipment 70 equals equity 150. The unconstrained 18/12 entry would also balance, but would
leave A at 42, below its admitted floor of 55. Clipping A’s loss to 5 without redistributing the
excess would instead recognize only 17 of the required remaining 30.
For an implementer, each round has a remaining amount and an active set of assets. Compute
shares from their pre-allocation carrying amounts. Fix each share that reaches an asset’s
available capacity, remove that asset from the active set, and redistribute the remaining amount.
