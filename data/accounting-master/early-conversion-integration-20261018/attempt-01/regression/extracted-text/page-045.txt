2.15.2
Selling, manufacturing and owning are separate relationships
Apple India Private Limited illustrates the distinction between incorporation and sales attribu-
tion. The exhibit places its incorporation in India, while Apple’s segment definition places India
in Europe. Under the general customer-location rule, an India-customer sale belongs in that
sales region; neither statement identifies its legal seller or assigns all India sales to that sub-
sidiary. Even a complete subsidiary list would not supply the missing seller-by-customer-region
relationship.
The Business discussion also separates distribution channels: direct sales through Apple’s stores
and sales force accounted for 38% of fiscal 2024 net sales, and indirect channels for 62%. The
latter include third-party cellular carriers, wholesalers, retailers and resellers (Apple Inc., 2024a,
Markets and Distribution, printed p. 2). These are sales channel shares, not ownership shares
or counts of companies. A trading relationship does not by itself establish that a counterparty
belongs in the consolidated group.
Production adds a further distinction. Apple says substantially all its manufacturing is performed
in whole or in part by outsourcing partners, primarily in China mainland, India, Japan, South
Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam. It has also invested in manufacturing process equipment, much
of which is held at certain outsourcing partners, and made prepayments under long-term
supply agreements (Apple Inc., 2024a, Risk Factors, printed p. 8).
Its MD&A describes
partners manufacturing subassemblies and performing final assembly and testing, alongside
purchases from component suppliers (Apple Inc., 2024a, Manufacturing Purchase Obligations,
printed p. 25). Equipment custody, a supply contract and corporate control are thus separate
relationships. A disruption at a partner may affect product supply, equipment recoverability
and prepayments without supplying evidence that the partner should be consolidated. The
affected assets, contracts and counterparties must be identified before amounts can be assigned
to that scenario.
2.15.3
Tax and currency queries need facts beyond incorporation
The jurisdiction column also leaves entity tax residence and functional currency unresolved.
Functional currency is the currency in which an entity measures its accounts; it is a different
question from the dollars in which Apple presents its group statements or the currency of a
particular invoice. The group foreign-exchange discussion describes Apple as a net receiver
of currencies other than the U.S. dollar, with pricing and hedging responses to exchange-rate
movements. It supplies no entity-by-entity functional-currency assignment (Apple Inc., 2024a,
Foreign Exchange Rate Risk, printed p. 27). An Ireland incorporation row therefore does not
justify entering euros as the entity’s functional currency, just as the India row does not establish
rupees.
Tax evidence has its own dates and units of analysis. Annual Note 7 describes the historic tax
allocation of profits of Irish branches of two subsidiaries without naming those subsidiaries in
that passage. The MD&A describes the resulting group payment obligation and restricted escrow
(Apple Inc., 2024a, Note 7, printed pp. 39–40; State Aid Decision Tax Payable, printed p. 26).
The four Ireland rows in Exhibit 21.1 do not identify which entities had those branches, their
historic tax residence or the jurisdictional tax bases used in current accounts. A name match or
country match would bypass the legal and temporal facts needed for the tax calculation.
