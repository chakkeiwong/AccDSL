Chapter 11
Credit losses and the bank balance
sheet
The funding and default distinctions become central in a bank. A loan creates an asset, customer
payments affect settlement liquidity, and a forecast of credit loss affects an allowance before
cash is lost. The first case connects funding to a simple allowance; the later dated loan schedule
separates credit stages, interest recognition and eventual recovery.
11.1
Banks: accounting capital is not regulatory capital
A bank package needs instrument and cohort states for loans, securities, deposits, wholesale
funding, repos, derivatives, collateral and commitments. For interest-rate analysis, currency and
repricing buckets matter alongside remaining maturity. For credit analysis, exposure, default
state, loss severity, recoveries, guarantees and their dependencies matter. For liquidity analysis,
deposit behaviour, collateral eligibility, encumbrance and payment timing matter. These states
can be represented at an explicitly chosen aggregation level; the public record rarely identifies
every contract.
11.1.1
A reported group boundary
JPMorgan Chase’s filings make the boundary between a bank, its parent and its securities
issuers concrete. In the 2022–2025 reports, Item 1 identifies JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. as
the principal bank subsidiary and describes an intermediate holding company, JPMorgan Chase
Holdings LLC (the IHC). The reports also describe J.P. Morgan Securities LLC as a principal
non-bank subsidiary and place J.P. Morgan Securities plc and J.P. Morgan SE beneath the
bank. These are reported relationships for the stated dates, not a complete legal-entity graph
(JPMorgan Chase & Co., 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026).
The attached Exhibit 21 tables qualify their scope: they list significant legal-entity subsidiaries,
some parents of significant subsidiaries and certain entities designated for resolution planning,
while excluding subsidiaries that would not be significant in aggregate under the stated SEC
definition. Their indentation shows how the issuer presents the rows, but it does not supply
ownership percentages, tax residence, functional currency or unrestricted transfer rights. A
model therefore keeps a reported display relationship separate from a confirmed control edge.
