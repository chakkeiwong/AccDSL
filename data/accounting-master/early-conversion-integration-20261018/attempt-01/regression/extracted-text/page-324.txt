12.24.2
Prescribed missing-information treatments preserve actual cash
Missing acquisition allocation has a narrower consequence than missing cash.
Return to
the 30-payment case. Suppose the 10 payment is known, but reasonable and supportable
information for the C14B allocation is unavailable. C14D prescribes nil for the specified group
CSM adjustment and acquisition assets (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.C14B–C14D).
The modified initial CSM becomes 120 −90 −6 = 24, leaving 16 at transition. Liability is 80
and the acquisition asset is zero, while actual cash remains 180. Equity is therefore 100. The
payment was not erased; a specified transition measurement rule changed its asset and margin
consequences. If the payment itself were unknown, this cash reconstruction would remain
unresolved.
An onerous group brings reinsurance into the same calculation. Lower the premium to 80 and
restore the supported acquisition allocation 6 and 4. Initial loss is 90 + 6 + 6 −80 = 22, with no
CSM. For this unchanged, zero-rate group, the declared systematic allocation releases one third
of the loss component with the first period’s expected claim and risk releases. At transition
the loss component is 44/3, contained within FCF 64. It is not added to the liability a second
time. Cash is 140, so the acquisition asset of 4 gives equity 140 + 4 −64 = 80. C16 requires a
systematic allocation under the modified route; the full route preserves the supported historical
allocation (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.49–52,C16).
Suppose a timely treaty covers half of all these claims. Its premium is 49, with separately
supplied initial transferred risk adjustment 3 and transition risk adjustment 2. Ordinary initial
cost CSM is 49−45−3 = 1. The initial loss recovery is 22/2 = 11, making total held cost CSM 12.
After the first of three equal service periods, held cost CSM is 8. Remaining recovery 30 plus risk
adjustment 2 gives held FCF 32 and total held asset 40. The remaining loss-recovery component
22/3 sits within that asset. This computation keeps ordinary treaty cost and recovery of the
onerous loss as separate origins (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.66–66B,B119C–B119F).
The treaty pays actual recovery 15 on the first-period claims. Combined cash is 140 −49 + 15 =
106, and transition equity is
106 + 4 + 40 −64 = 86.
Under the admitted modified route, C16A gives the same remaining recovery component
(44/3)(1/2) = 22/3. Adding it to ordinary remaining cost CSM 2/3 gives held cost CSM 8.
Timely entry, covered-contract allocation and the expected claims-recovery percentage remain
required findings; the numerical agreement does not establish them (European Commission,
2023a, IFRS 17.C16A–C16B).
If the specific information needed for C16A is unavailable, C16C instead requires that no
loss-recovery component be identified. Suppose ordinary held valuation remains supported.
Held FCF is still 32 and ordinary remaining cost CSM is 2/3, giving held asset 98/3 and equity
106 + 4 + 98/3 −64 = 236/3. This rule does not make the held asset zero or supply a missing
ordinary valuation (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 17.C16C).
