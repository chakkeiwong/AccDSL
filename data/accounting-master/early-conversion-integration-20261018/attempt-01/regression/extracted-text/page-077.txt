Merigoux et al. (2021), Sections 3–4 and Figures 8–9, provides Catala’s scopes, exceptions,
default calculus and translation into a conventional functional target. Its explicit empty and
conflict outcomes are valuable precedents for accounting rules with exceptions. They are different
from probabilistic uncertainty. AccDSL will preserve this distinction: conflicting applicable rules
produce a diagnostic, while an evidence-supported alternative reading produces a conditional
model branch. The paper’s compiler correctness results do not establish correctness of our
accounting rules or of an automatic translation from disclosures.
The inspected LegalMath code offers reusable ideas in its shared typed interpretation, partial
observations, source references and validity/knowledge times. Its ordinary frontend execution
selects a reading. AccDSL needs an additional execution mode that retains a family of readings,
continuous unknowns and economic state. A bounded candidate generator is useful for search,
but its candidate limit does not prove completeness.
Peyton Jones et al. (2000), Sections 3–5, builds complex financial contracts from combinators
and separates their compositional meaning from a chosen valuation model.
The ACTUS
technical specification similarly separates contract states, scheduled events, state transitions and
payoffs, with risk factors supplied through an interface (ACTUS Financial Research Foundation,
2026). These are strong reasons to represent contractual obligations independently of accounting
classifications. Neither contract cash flows nor a derivative price determine the recognition,
measurement and presentation rules of a complete IFRS or US GAAP book.
4.5
Extraction and financial reasoning
The document literature supports a staged ingestion process. PubTables-1M (Smock et al.,
2022), in its canonicalisation procedure and evaluation section, distinguishes table detection,
structure recognition and functional analysis. Its GriTS evaluation compares reconstructed
table structure. TableBank (Li et al., 2020), Sections 3–4, uses document structure to obtain
weak supervision for table detection and recognition. GTE and FinTabNet (Zheng et al.,
2021), Sections 3–5, provide a financial-document setting and company-level dataset splits.
These sources help select extraction components and evaluation units. Their table scores
do not establish that a debt note has been interpreted correctly across years or that every
off-balance-sheet exposure has been found.
The Docling report (Auer et al., 2024), Section 3, describes PDF parsing, page layout analysis
and table structure recognition within a document-conversion pipeline. The locally archived
version lists Christoph Auer first; this version difference is recorded in the catalogue. Docling
is an ingestion candidate whose output must retain source locations and undergo accounting
validation.
Its code and model licences must be considered separately when packaging a
deployment.
