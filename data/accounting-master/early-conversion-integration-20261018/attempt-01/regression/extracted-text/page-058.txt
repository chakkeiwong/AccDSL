2.23.1
Service provision requires continuing access
Adequate components and saleable inventory still leave a service question: can the company
continue offering the software and content its customers want? Apple says its products and
services require third-party intellectual property, while giving no assurance that the necessary
licences will be available on commercially reasonable terms or at all.
Its digital-content
arrangements can be short and need not renew on similar terms. Content providers can also
require new rights-management or security technology before access can continue (Apple Inc.,
2024a, Risk Factors, printed pp. 9–10). The relevant resource is therefore partly a dated right
to offer particular content or functionality. Physical capacity alone cannot establish that right.
Developer support has a separate economic cause. Apple believes customers’ hardware purchases
depend partly on available applications, whose continued development and maintenance are not
assured. It describes developers comparing platforms by market position, anticipated revenue,
expected growth and the cost of developing and updating software (Apple Inc., 2024a, Risk
Factors, printed p. 10). An application can consequently become unavailable or unsuitable even
while the hardware remains usable. A scenario needs the affected applications and developers’
response; an estimate of component capacity cannot supply either.
Access also depends on how a service uses data and takes payment. Apple reports restrictions
on collecting, using and transferring personal information, including transfers within its in-
ternational group. It separately warns that failure to follow payment-card security standards
could increase transaction costs or remove the ability to process cards (Apple Inc., 2024a, Risk
Factors, printed p. 15). These issuer descriptions identify possible constraints. Which constraint
applies depends on the data, entity, jurisdiction, agreement and effective rule.
Suppose a content agreement permits a specified offering only until its expiry date. Under one
explicitly assumed history it renews; under another it expires without replacement. Identical
physical resources then permit different offerings. The comparison needs the service, customer
population, territory, contract term and allowed substitutions, together with any new cost and
demand response. A financial calculation must apply the relevant revenue, payment and remedy
terms to the service actually provided. Apple’s risk statements supply neither those contract
facts nor probabilities for the alternatives. The historical record must preserve the continuing
relationships as well as the reported balances if it is to support these different futures.
2.24
A useful answer before the company’s history is complete
The Apple examples now give us enough information to pose a more demanding question: what
can one connected answer say about the reported company and its possible cash requirements?
The answer must carry both the reported amounts and the limits of the histories used to explain
them. Otherwise a complete-looking statement could conceal a missing liability, a hypothetical
retirement or an unavailable settlement date.
For the retained annual source, the selected statements report net income of 93,736 and other
comprehensive income of 4,280, giving comprehensive income of 98,016 million dollars. Assets of
364,980 less liabilities of 308,030 give closing equity of 56,950. The following quarter reports net
income of 36,330, other comprehensive income of 383 and closing equity of 66,758 (Apple Inc.,
2024a, 2025c, consolidated financial statements). These are source-located readings of displayed
amounts. Their units, periods, group perimeter and statement populations accompany them. A
share count or a per-share amount keeps its own unit; it cannot enter a monetary total merely
because it sits next to one.
