Entity-specific tax, currency and intercompany questions require additional
records: legal counterparties, dated ownership and control, compatible entity books, functional
currencies, tax bases and matched transactions. Group eliminations then require the applicable
accounting methods as well as those facts. The later IFRS group examples explain their own
assumptions; they do not establish the operative US GAAP treatment for Apple. Keeping those
dependencies explicit preserves useful source knowledge while leaving the unanswered entity
questions open.
2.16
Legal proceedings beyond the booked liability
Knowing the companies within a group does not identify all the events that could change their
earnings. A legal proceeding can require a payment, but it can also change how a company sells
its products or earns licensing revenue. Apple’s reported App Store injunction illustrates the
second channel: it concerns developers’ external purchase links on the U.S. storefront, rather
than a disclosed amount of damages. A forecast that represents legal exposure only as a liability
balance would leave that operating consequence outside the model.
The annual Item 3 and interim Part II Item 1 each discuss Digital Markets Act investigations,
the Department of Justice lawsuit, Epic Games and other legal proceedings. These are dated
issuer reports, with different kinds of statements inside each matter: allegations, reported
decisions, pending motions, management’s position and possible consequences. Keeping those
distinctions allows a model to use what is reported without deciding the case itself (Apple Inc.,
2024a, Item 3, printed p. 18) (Apple Inc., 2025c, Part II, Item 1, printed p. 18).
2.16.1
A loss assessment answers a narrower question than exposure
Annual Note 12 and interim Note 9 give the same qualified assessment: “In the opinion of
management, there was not at least a reasonable possibility the Company may have incurred a
material loss, or a material loss greater than a recorded accrual, concerning loss contingencies
for asserted legal and other claims” (Apple Inc., 2024a, Note 12, printed p. 46) (Apple Inc.,
2025c, Note 9, printed p. 11). The sentence identifies whose assessment it is, concerns an
incurred material loss, and allows for a recorded accrual. It does not disclose a total legal
accrual, allocate an accrual to one of the named matters, or assert that all exposure is zero.
Nor does it supply a numerical probability or materiality threshold.
The annual MD&A separately reports Apple’s recognition policy: the company records a liability
when it is probable that a loss has been incurred and the amount is reasonably estimable, a
determination requiring significant judgment (Apple Inc., 2024a, Legal and Other Contingencies,
printed p. 26). That policy explains why a pending proceeding and a recognised liability are
different facts. It is issuer-policy evidence; applying the operative US GAAP requirements to a
particular case still needs the relevant authority and case facts. An allegation cannot supply
those facts, and a possible change in future sales is not itself the incurred loss described by the
policy.
