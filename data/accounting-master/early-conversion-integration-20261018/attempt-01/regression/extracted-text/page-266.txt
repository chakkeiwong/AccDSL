Dates also change the numerical problem. Fractional powers such as f are generally irrational,
so a rational calculator must preserve a bound rather than label a rounded decimal exact.
Reduce the day fraction to p/q and choose rational endpoints a and b on a declared decimal
grid. The following integer-power comparisons establish containment:

All quantities are positive, which justifies taking the monotone qth root. Interval arithmetic then
carries these bounds through discounting and each balance; the requested scale and accumulated
widths remain part of the result. A leap-day interval uses its actual number of days, rather
than an implicit annual-anniversary convention. Zero and negative rates above −1 are admitted
under the same convention.
For positive future receipts, present value decreases continuously as the rate increases. Certified
endpoint signs can therefore enclose a unique effective rate. Such a bracket is useful numerical
evidence, but its midpoint is not an exact rate. The two financial examples require an exactly
established initial PV identity and use bounds for the intervening dates. Propagating an
uncertain rate through a general financial schedule remains a separate method.
