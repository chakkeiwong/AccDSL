The statement supplies neither an indemnified party nor a particular agreement. It also leaves
the covered conduct, trigger, duration and limit unknown. To connect a third-party claim to
a dated obligation, the model would need those terms and the facts that activate them. The
existence of indemnification language somewhere in Apple’s agreements cannot establish that a

facts remain distinct from the management loss assessment examined in Section 2.16.
The full annual passage also describes unresolved ordinary-course claims and investigations, an
increase over time in their number and alleged magnitude, and patent claims concerning cellular-
enabled products. Apple reports defending infringement actions in U.S. and international
courts, whose plaintiffs frequently seek broad injunctions and substantial damages. These
are issuer statements about claims and requested relief. They do not identify a complete
matter population, decide the infringement allegations or quantify the payments and product
restrictions that will result.
Defense is a separate source of cost. The filing states, “Regardless of the merit of particular
claims, defending against litigation or responding to government investigations can be expensive,
time-consuming and disruptive to the Company’s operations.” Thus a scenario in which Apple
successfully resists a claim need not be a scenario with no defense effort or disruption. The
model therefore needs the response services, their amounts and dates, and related payment
obligations. The damages outcome cannot identify those expenses or payments.
Settlement can alter recurring operations as well as resolve a dispute. Apple says it may enter
agreements or other arrangements to settle litigation, without assurance of acceptable terms
or that litigation will not occur. Those agreements can increase cost of sales and operating
expenses, change business practices and limit products and services. A conditional settlement
therefore needs its payment terms and its continuing effects on royalties, costs or offerings.
An adverse resolution can additionally involve monetary damages, disgorgement, corporate
measures or an injunction. The annual passage explicitly includes a resolution against an
indemnified third party above management’s expectations. Neither that possibility nor the
separately reported quarterly settlements supplies a contract, amount or probability for a
forecast.
2.18.1
Insurance describes a possible recovery, not a net loss
Apple says it maintains insurance for certain types of claims, while warning that coverage may be
insufficient for all losses or all claim types. The qualification leaves both the covered population
and the amount recoverable unresolved. A model cannot deduct an assumed insurance payment
from damages or an accrued liability merely because insurance exists. It needs the policy
period, insured party and beneficiary, covered event, exclusions, deductible, limit, insurer and
recovery terms, together with facts about the claim. The applicable recognition and presentation
treatment must also be established before a recovery amount becomes an accounting entry. The
passages identify none of those amounts or terms.

discussion warns of recovery expenditures and lost sales, especially when critical components

(Apple Inc., 2024a, Risk Factors, printed p. 6). The data-security discussion says coverage is

2024a, Risk Factors, printed p. 12). The trade receivable discussion instead says a substantial
