Finally, a correct purchase must survive an uncertain acknowledgement. The request identifies
Northstar, its book and branch, and the particular purchase; it also fixes the opening state,
expected revision and all input versions. Suppose the client sends the request but receives no
response. If the store committed nothing, an exact retry may commit the purchase. If the store
already committed it, the same retry returns the original receipt without another purchase.
The client cannot distinguish these possibilities from a timeout.
Receipt lookup therefore precedes comparison with today’s head, after the original request has
passed its input and authorization checks. A later, separately identified purchase may have
advanced the book, but it does not erase the first receipt. Replaying the first request still
returns that receipt. Changing its request identifier or budget changes its full fingerprint and
conflicts with the already recorded business event. A new event presented against an obsolete
opening is instead rejected as stale. Every rejection leaves the committed states, journals,
quantity changes and receipt index unchanged.
Review decisions, saved search progress and imports have their own owners and receipts.
Correcting the reported revenue invalidates dependent current uses, including a selection that
depended on the old set of eligible readings; it does not rewrite committed purchases. Saving
one searched region of [0, 1] preserves the other region and the next position to visit. Resuming
with a larger budget updates that saved work, while a changed model requires a different
calculation. These distinctions make the complete company example reconstructible from its
inputs and record references. The operation-specific fields and exact documentary exchanges
are described in Section A.1; they still require implementation and actual fault testing before
any claim of database durability.
2.7
One company close across several accounting methods
Separate examples can each balance while disagreeing about the cash they share. A return
settlement may draw on the same bank account that must redeem a bond; an internal sale may
increase a parent’s earnings without increasing the group’s earnings. To see these interactions,
consider a second synthetic company: a parent, P, and its subsidiary, S. P owns 80% of S at
the beginning of 2025 and controls it throughout the year. Both use USD, the same calendar
and the same accounting policies. All amounts are currency units. The opening balances are
supplied facts; the example begins after acquisition and debt issuance.
P opens with cash 500, inventory 60, plant 100, a return-recovery asset 16, an investment in S
of 80 and a deferred tax asset of 10. It owes a refund liability of 40, matured debt of 100 and a
deferred tax liability of 4. Its capital 600, original compound-instrument equity 10 and retained
earnings 12 give equity 622. S opens with cash and capital of 100. Eliminating P’s investment
against its share of S’s opening capital leaves non-controlling interests (NCI) of 20. The group’s
opening assets are 786, liabilities 144 and equity 642.
This case has a deliberately narrow tax law: a supplied enacted rate of 25%, deductible wages,
matching book and tax depreciation, and the return timing specified below. Recovery of the
stated deferred tax assets is supported by a supplied finding. P controls the timing of reversal
of taxable outside-basis investment differences, with no reversal expected in the foreseeable
future, so the IAS 12.39 exception applies conditionally. These are teaching premises, not a
statement of any jurisdiction’s current law. The recognition, consolidation and cash-flow rules
use the retained historical standard text (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 12.24,39,46–51;
IFRS 10.B86–B94).
