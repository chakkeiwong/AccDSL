An assumed series adds the same ai to either numerator and bi to
either denominator. If current EPS is r ≥0, both resulting fractions fall below r exactly
when ai −rbi < 0. If that expression is positive, the previously active fraction rises; equality
leaves EPS unchanged. Thus order complete gross effects by ai/bi, recalculate the allocation,
and include a series only when it strictly lowers ordinary EPS. IAS 33.41–44 provides the
continuing- operations control and separate-series dilution objective (European Commission,
2023a, IAS 33.33–44); the formula here is a local derivation for the stated rights and additive
gross effects.
The ordering also finds the minimum over this family’s combinations. For any trial bound
r, requiring both fractions to be at most that bound imposes two inequalities with the same
varying sum P
i(ai−rbi). Including every negative term minimizes that sum in both inequalities,
so a minimizing combination exists among the prefixes in ascending ai/bi order. An included
series leaves EPS above its own ratio, and all later ratios are at least as large. Once a series no
longer lowers EPS, later ones cannot restore dilution. Below the senior entitlement, nonnegative
earnings additions and positive share additions cannot deepen the ordinary loss per share.
Equality is excluded by the strict dilution condition.
The read-only CappedParticipationEPS/1 method uses exact rational amounts and returns the
allocation before and after each trial assumption. It requires explicit rights, complete effects,
context and period findings; unknown findings leave the answer unresolved, and unsupported
or contradictory terms produce no partial EPS result. Its 18 authored cases include both cap
regimes, zero and negative earnings, equality, reversed input order and the nonparticipating
limit. An independent calculation enumerates all 49 finite exercise combinations. This supports
the specified calculation, while the existing fixed ordinary-numerator method retains its separate
assumptions.
Complete gross effects are the consequential restriction. IAS 33.35 points out that removing
interest expense can increase a nondiscretionary employee profit-sharing expense. If that expense
depends on the combined conversions, single-series effects need not add. Conditional exercise
or changed rights can alter the possible assumptions as well. Those cases need their own joint
earnings and eligibility calculation before any dilution ordering is justified.
10.13.7
Conversion can change another expense and depend on another
instrument
Reallocating a fixed amount of earnings is only one way that assumed conversion can change
the numerator. Removing interest expense may increase the earnings on which employee
remuneration depends. IAS 33.35 expressly requires the resulting change in nondiscretionary
profit-sharing expense to enter the EPS calculation, together with the related tax effects under
paragraphs 33–34 (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 33.33–35). Calculating each conversion
once in isolation can therefore miss a consequence of converting two instruments together.

and complete tax expense is one fifth of profit after the bonus. These are supplied terms for
the example. There are 100 ordinary shares, with no participation rights or other attribution;
continuing and total earnings coincide. The reported bonus is zero, tax is 18 and earnings are
72. Each of two full-year convertible debt series would remove interest expense of 20 and add
20 ordinary shares. Assume all other conversion consequences have been accounted for.
