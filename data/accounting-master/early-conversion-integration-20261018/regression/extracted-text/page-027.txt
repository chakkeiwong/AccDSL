Apple: source labels do not identify exposure
The real reference package is Apple’s 2024 Form 10-K and first-quarter 2025 Form 10-Q, for
fiscal dates 28 September and 28 December 2024 respectively (Apple Inc., 2024a, 2025c). The
annual balance sheet also supplies 30 September 2023. The annual filing is archived as the
company’s PDF from AnnualReports; the interim filing is archived from SEC EDGAR with
its inline XBRL. Apple’s Note 1 describes a 52/53-week year ending on the last Saturday of
September. Calendar-year assumptions would therefore misstate the periods.
The package inventory preserves every annual PDF page and every interim top-level section, all
thirteen annual notes and all ten interim notes. It records internal cross-references and externally
incorporated material, including the proxy statement, as distinct obligations. Statement and
debt facts below are structured; policies, other notes and exhibits retain their source locations
and specific modelling obligations. This is complete inventory coverage of the acquired files,
with partial semantic formalisation. It does not claim to reconstruct every Apple contract or to
have acquired every incorporated external document.
Apple’s annual balance sheet reports assets 364,980, liabilities 308,030 and equity 56,950, in
millions of US dollars. Note 9 reports term-debt principal 97,341, unamortised discount/premium
and issuance costs of −321, and hedge accounting adjustments of −358. Thus its carrying
amount is 97,341 −321 −358 = 96,662, split into current 10,912 and non-current 85,750. Fair
value is separately disclosed as 88.4 billion. None of these three measurements may replace
another merely because a table calls it debt.
Note 9 identifies the notes as fixed-rate, then states that interest-rate swaps effectively convert a
portion to floating rates. Note 4 reports interest-rate contract notional of 14,575 at the annual
date. A notional is neither a liability carrying amount nor sufficient information for an exact
interest sensitivity: reset timing, remaining periods, curve conventions and hedge coverage still
matter. The cross-reference connects the derivative to the borrowing without erasing either
contract.
