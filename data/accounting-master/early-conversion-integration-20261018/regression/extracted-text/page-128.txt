For 2024, the revalued amounts are depreciated over the remaining four and three years. The
charges are 120/4 = 30 and 24/3 = 8, leaving carrying amounts 90 and 16. The company then
revalues the whole class to 45 and 22. A falls by 45: its surplus absorbs 40 through OCI and
the remaining 5 is a profit-or-loss expense. B rises by 6, reversing its own earlier loss through
profit. After eliminating accumulated depreciation, gross PPE is 45 and 22, with surplus zero.
Profit is −38 −5 + 6 = −37 and OCI is −40. Cash 60 plus PPE 67 equals closing equity 127.
A now retains a prior revaluation loss of 5; B retains none.
In 2025, the remaining three-year life of A and two-year life of B give depreciation 45/3 = 15
and 22/2 = 11. Carrying amounts become 30 and 11. At 31 December their assumed fair values
are 50 and 11. A’s increase of 20 first reverses its loss of 5 through profit, then adds 15 to OCI
and surplus. B’s valuation is included even though its monetary adjustment is zero. Before sale,
cash 60 and PPE 61 equal equity 121.
Immediately after that measurement, the buyer obtains both machines and pays their same-date
values, 50 for A and 11 for B. Debit cash 61 and remove PPE 61; the disposal gain is zero.
The company elects the transfer permitted by paragraph 41: debit A’s surplus 15 and credit
retained earnings 15 directly. This transfer creates no income, new OCI or cash. Routing it
through profit would falsely raise 2025 profit from −21 to −6.
