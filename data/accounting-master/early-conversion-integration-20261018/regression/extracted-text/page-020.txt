2.7.3
Buying more shares changes who owns the equity
On 1 July P pays 20 to the outside shareholders for another 10% of S. P now owns 90%, and
control is unchanged. In P’s separate books, its cost-policy investment increases by 20 and cash
decreases by 20. S records nothing: the payment was between its shareholders. The group’s
accounting must instead describe a change in the relative claims of P’s owners and NCI.
Immediately before purchase, S’s equity is 121 and NCI owns 20%, carrying 24.2. P buys half
of that interest. The NCI reduction is 12.1, not the cash price 20. IFRS 10.23 and B96 put the
difference directly in parent equity:
∆NCI = −(0.90 −0.80)121 = −12.1,
(2.7)
∆EP = −(20 −12.1) = −7.9.
(2.8)
No gain, goodwill or remeasurement is introduced. IAS 7.42A–42B classifies the group cash
payment as financing, while the parent’s separate purchase of an investment is investing under its
own cash-flow presentation (European Commission, 2023a, IFRS 10.23,B96; IAS 7.16,42A–42B;
IAS 27.10).
The purchase date matters for later earnings. First-half NCI profit remains 4.2; it is not
recomputed at 10%. The new fraction applies to subsequent profit. Recording only an ending
ownership percentage would lose that history and allocate the year’s earnings incorrectly.
