2.12.2
Why a fall in commitments does not identify cash spent
The purchase note and management discussion disclose different views. Note 12 lists 11,226
million of future payments under unconditional purchase obligations whose remaining terms
exceed one year. Its fiscal-2025 row is 3,206 million (Apple Inc., 2024a, Note 12, printed p. 45).
The term restriction selects contracts; it does not place every payment beyond the coming
year. The note describes these as off-balance-sheet commitments, primarily involving supplier
arrangements, intellectual property, content and distribution rights.
Management’s discussion provides additional near-term evidence. At the same September
date it reports manufacturing purchase obligations of 53.0 billion, of which 52.9 billion is
payable within twelve months. Other purchase obligations total 12.0 billion, with 4.1 billion
within twelve months; that description includes capital assets as well as supplier, content and
rights arrangements (Apple Inc., 2024a, printed p. 25). A model confined to Note 12 would
miss those reported aggregates. Conversely, subtracting 11.226 from 12.0 and labelling the
difference short-term contracts is unsupported: the category and term populations have not
been reconciled, and the precision differs.
At 28 December 2024 manufacturing obligations are 39.8 billion, all payable within twelve
months. The interim report says contractual cash requirements have not changed materially
since the annual filing except for commercial paper, manufacturing obligations and the State
Aid tax payable (Apple Inc., 2025c, printed p. 16). That qualitative statement is not an
exact December lease measurement or evidence that every lease remained unchanged. The two
manufacturing totals are dated observations over changing orders and payment horizons. Their
13.2 billion difference does not identify purchases or cash paid during the quarter.
To see what is missing, consider an explicitly simplified model in billions of dollars. Let O0 and
O1 denote a comparable population of undelivered fixed-price orders at the two dates. Let N be
new orders and F the value removed when goods are delivered. Assume no cancellation, price or
currency change. Treat the displayed 53.0 and 39.8 as exact inputs for this illustration. These
assumptions define the example; the disclosure does not establish this roll-forward convention
for every reported obligation. The balance relation is
O1 = O0 + N −F,
F −N = 53.0 −39.8 = 13.2.
(2.22)

same closing undelivered amount of 39.8. The source balances alone cannot choose between
these conditional movements; actual contract changes and delivery evidence could do so.
