Apple, Caterpillar and JPMorgan Chase each contribute one annual and three quarterly reports
per year; the other six issuers each contribute one annual and one half-year report. Thus
3 × 8 × 4 + 6 × 8 × 2 = 192. This is source acquisition, not semantic closure or a representative
random sample. The 244 retained original versions include historical alternatives to the active
panel. Apple remains a detailed development package, with Caterpillar supplying a contrasting
inventory policy; bank, insurer, IFRS and ASBE reports are also retained.
Publication dates remain unverified for 136 of the 192 accepted reports; 56 have filing-date
metadata.
A historical forecasting population needs the actual byte-availability evidence
described in Section 3.13, not imputed publication dates. The 31 provisional annotations
across four reports are exposed development material, not heldout answers. Policies, note
links, incorporated documents, changed comparatives and adjudicated meanings still require
task-specific preparation. Synthetic revisions provide controlled cases without substituting for a
required real conflict. The corpus protocol and active panel preserve their distinct populations
and versions.
Annotators first record literal/context readings without seeing the model’s predictions, then
mark source conflicts, mapping relationships, narrative qualifications and admissible alternatives.
A second accounting-aware reviewer adjudicates material disagreements while preserving both
initial annotations. Reports from the same issuer family and duplicated tables cannot be split
between development and heldout evaluation. Forecast tests freeze the actual publication cutoff;
later revisions are separately labelled outcomes and may not enter predictor inputs. Extractor
upgrades rerun the same frozen corpus.
For the initial supported release, loss of a source span, wrong entity/unit/ period/basis, invented
evidence, hidden financing, dropped feasible branch, incorrect cash reconciliation or unreported
unsupported treatment blocks acceptance. All selected critical facts and operations must have
exact reviewed answers or the expected unresolved response. These zero-tolerance conditions
follow from the product’s meaning-preservation requirements. Non-critical extraction quality
is reported by stratum and error category; no arbitrary overall accuracy threshold certifies
whole-report understanding. An extraction benchmark needs its own sample size and uncertainty
protocol before making statistical accuracy claims.
The DSL and typed-library comparison uses identical packages, evidence, schemas and runtime.
Users perform the same tasks: correct a context, add a shared uncertainty, represent a restate-
ment, add a supported event and explain a forecast difference. Task order is counterbalanced and
experience recorded. Both frontends receive the same validation and error messages. Measure
successful completion, material semantic errors, time and maintenance effort with uncertainty
appropriate to the participants; report no winner from the current syntax checks. A textual
language earns its maintenance cost only if that comparison supports its use.
14.11
Workload limits and release evidence
The first integration workload is one local company package, up to 10,000 observations, 100
declared aggregate event slots per period, 64 materialised finite-choice cells and a 30-second
budget per solver operation. These are provisional engineering limits for bounded failure
behaviour, not measured capacity or scientific defaults. They must be configurable and recorded.
Reaching a limit yields partial coverage with the unvisited formula/cells, never an exhaustive-
history claim. Peak memory and latency are measured only after an implementation exists; a
slower but semantically correct result does not become a scientific failure.
