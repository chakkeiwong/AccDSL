The entries also distinguish impairment from a cash payment. Debit impairment expense 5 and
credit the G1 acquisition asset 5 for the first test. Then debit impairment expense 20 and credit
each group’s asset by 10 for the second. Cash does not move: the acquisition costs were already
paid. For a small balance-sheet slice starting with cash and equity of 100 and no liabilities,
paying the allocated costs leaves cash 40 and acquisition assets 60. After impairment, cash
remains 40, acquisition assets are 35 and equity is 75. Paragraph 79 includes these acquisition
assets within the related issued-contract portfolio carrying amounts; the separate records used
here do not prescribe a separate line on the published balance sheet (Australian Accounting
Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 28E and 79).
Recoverability can improve. At a later reporting date, suppose the full-group net inflows are
45 and 30, with renewal contributions of 35 and 15. Assume no intervening cash movement or
recognition of either group. The allocation assumptions have been reviewed under B35B and
remain unchanged; only the measurement inputs improve. Against the original allocations of 40
and 20, the remaining shortfalls are now 5 for each group. Paragraph 28F permits reversal to
the extent the impairment conditions have improved. Reverse 10 for G1 and 5 for G2: debit the
acquisition assets 15 and credit the impairment reversal in profit or loss 15. Assets become 35
and 15, totalling 50; cash stays 40 and equity becomes 90. The improvement restores 15 of the
earlier loss of 25 without creating cash or recognising the expected renewals early (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 28F and B35B).
If the impairment conditions later disappear altogether, with these other facts unchanged, only
the remaining loss of 10 can be reversed; the original allocated cost of 60 is the ceiling in this
example. When related contracts are actually recognised and the acquisition cash flows enter
their group measurement, the corresponding acquisition asset must be derecognised. B35C
provides for partial incorporation when contracts are added over several periods. Keeping
both the old asset and its incorporated amount would double count the same cost. The record
must therefore preserve its origination cohort, future group, allocation history, impairment and
reversal history, and the amount already incorporated into recognised contracts (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 28C–28F and B35B–B35C).
These are ordinary reporting-date impairment and reversal entries. They do not use the separate
transition rules for acquisition assets. The historical edition, actual cost eligibility and supported
renewal estimates must be checked before applying the example to an insurer.
