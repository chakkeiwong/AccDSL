The same passage describes facilities in disaster-prone locations and asserts that climate change
is increasing the frequency or intensity of certain natural disasters and extreme weather. It also
identifies fire, power shortages, nuclear and other industrial accidents, hostile acts, ransomware,
labor disputes and health emergencies. These are Apple’s stated exposures, without facility-
specific hazard estimates. Its requirement that suppliers maintain safe workplaces is separate
from whether an accident occurs. The company reports that major public-health issues, including
COVID-19, have adversely affected demand, operations and distribution, and warns of possible
future effects. Travel restrictions, freight limitations and interruptions to production ramps
supply concrete mechanisms; the paragraph does not quantify an historical loss or a future

Consider two explicitly assumed histories for a particular product. In one, the supply route
remains available but customers reduce orders.
In another, orders remain but a critical
component cannot arrive.
The first requires a demand response; the second requires an
inventory and delivery response. They can also occur together. To compare the histories,
the model needs product-level orders, available components and finished goods, lead times,
contractual commitments and feasible management actions. An assumed alternate supplier
must have capacity and suitable terms before it becomes an available response. A sales delay
or lost order follows from those facts and assumptions; it cannot be obtained by applying one
percentage to every reported balance.
Recovery adds another date. Apple warns that an interruption can require substantial time
and expenditure to resume operations, with lost sales and greater consequences where critical
components have single or limited sources. Note 12 separately explains that even components
available from several suppliers can face industry-wide shortages and commodity-price fluctua-
tions (Apple Inc., 2024a, Note 12, printed p. 46). Ending an assumed interruption therefore need
not restore production or sales immediately. Recovery spending can precede or follow resumed
deliveries. The model must retain the dates of the interruption, restored capacity, deliveries,
expenditure and settlement separately; the applicable accounting treatment then determines
the journals. The insurance qualification already examined in the litigation discussion does not
collapse those dates into a net loss or guarantee reimbursement.
The reported receivables, investments, debt and liquidity resources constrain particular dated
histories, but they do not determine these operating responses. The investment sensitivity
discussed above measures a specified interest-rate effect. Chapter 10 examines the distinct
debt and currency measures; none identifies the broader disruption response. The interim’s
incorporation of annual risk factors, except for the changes it sets out, likewise does not establish
unchanged facilities, orders or funding conditions (Apple Inc., 2025c, Part II, Item 1A, printed
p. 19). For the historical record, the useful distinction is now precise: retain Apple’s assertions,
the relationships needed to interpret them, and the analyst’s scenario assumptions as separate
information. Alternative demand, supply and recovery histories can then remain visible until
evidence narrows them.
