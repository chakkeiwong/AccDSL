The text supplied to table recognition also needs an identity.
The retained docling-ibm-
models 4.0.2 consumer uses OTSL cell and span tokens, converts them to HTML, and merges
text from separately supplied PDF cells (Docling IBM Models contributors, 2026). This is a later
representation than the original paper’s HTML sequence. The Docling report explicitly describes
a subsequent token-language refinement (Auer et al., 2024, Section 3); neither that statement
nor a model-card score identifies the later files with the paper’s experimental checkpoints.
The proposed adapter therefore retains the source page and crop, the supplied text cells and
their locations, the predicted table cells, and the assignments and structural changes made
during matching. If a correction combines columns or adds a cell for unmatched text, a reviewer
must be able to recover the earlier structure and the text that prompted the change. Text
obtained from an OCR engine also retains that engine’s identity and outcome. A resulting
table remains a proposal until its cell content, context and accounting interpretation have been
checked; a well-formed HTML table alone cannot settle those questions.
Token syntax and cell geometry require separate checks too. In the inspected author version
of OTSL, Lysak et al. (2023, Sections 4.1–4.3) use five tokens: C starts a cell, L joins the cell
to its left, U joins the cell above, X joins both, and NL ends a row. Neighbor restrictions and
equal row lengths allow invalid tokens to be detected during generation. The authors explicitly
distinguish a syntactically valid sequence from a correct prediction.
Even those local rules need a further condition to ensure rectangular merged cells. Consider two
rows, C L NL followed by U C NL. The L has C to its left, the U has C above, and the first-row,
first-column and equal-length rules are satisfied; no X occurs. Yet the first cell joins the top-left,
top-right and bottom-left positions, leaving the bottom-right position to the second C. It is
L-shaped. Representing it with both a two-row and a two-column HTML span would also
occupy the second cell’s position. This finite example shows why the six printed rules alone do
not establish a nonoverlapping partition into rectangular cells. The proposed adapter checks
that each merged cell occupies a complete rectangle, that cells do not overlap, and that every
declared grid position belongs to a cell. Passing those checks still leaves correspondence with
the source table to be established.
The later decoder needs its own verification. In the retained 4.0.2 source, a correction described
as applying to the first row uses a row counter that is initialized to zero and never updated.
It therefore also rewrites an affected token on later rows (Docling IBM Models contributors,
2026). This is a specific source-code defect relative to that stated scope; its frequency and effect
on actual model predictions have not been measured. Neither the paper’s syntax argument nor
these partial corrections establish that a deployed converter enforces all the proposed checks.
The retained Apple annual filing makes the dependency problem concrete. Its issuer archive
includes the instance, the issuer’s schema and its calculation, definition, label and presentation
linkbases. Those files also refer to US GAAP and SEC Reporting Taxonomy definitions, SEC
schemas and XBRL definitions held elsewhere. The issuer archive alone therefore cannot
supply every definition needed to interpret its tagged facts. For this particular archive, a static
inspection now resolves the recorded imports, link references and typed-domain references to
retained files, checks target namespaces and fragment identifiers, and checks that instance facts
have declared concepts, contexts and units. This establishes a source dependency map. XML
Schema and XBRL validation, the issuer’s interpretation of each concept, and the completeness
of the wider disclosure package remain separate questions.
