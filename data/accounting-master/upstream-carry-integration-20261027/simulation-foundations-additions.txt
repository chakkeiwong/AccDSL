Equal recognised-tax trading contributions happen to make several profit totals match the
downstream example. That numerical coincidence is not a reason to forget which company

26.23
Carrying the subsidiary’s margin into the next year
At the first upstream year end, suppose the parent still holds the whole lot. The subsidiary’s
legal profit is 81/2: operating profit 33 plus internal margin 10 less current tax 5/2. Closing
its legal income accounts therefore puts 81/2 into post-control retained profit. The parent has
earned no trading profit. These legal retained amounts cannot simply be copied into group
equity.
With the inventory deferred tax asset recognised, the subsidiary’s consolidated result is 63/2.
The group close leaves 189/8 of that result in owners’ retained profit and adds 63/8 to acquisition
NCI of 115/4. Opening NCI is consequently 293/8. The account-by-account opening bridge
reconciles these closed group accounts with the closed legal books, preserving the investment
elimination, acquired basis, inventory adjustment and tax. It does not acquire the subsidiary
again. Without the inventory deferred tax asset, the corresponding subsidiary result is 29:

26.23.1
The parent’s sale releases profit attributed to the subsidiary
In year two the parent sells the full lot outside the group for 40 cash. Its legal books recognise
cost 30 and current tax 5/2, leaving its own after-tax trading profit 15/2. The group opening
inventory adjustment still reduces the parent’s inventory by 10. Releasing it requires a debit
to the parent’s inventory and a credit to the subsidiary’s current cost of sales. The debit
removes the carried adjustment after the legal sale; the credit locates the realised margin in the
subsidiary seller’s earnings. A second charge to opening retained profit would duplicate the
opening bridge.
Where the inventory deferred tax asset was recognised, its 5/2 reversal is also attributed to the
subsidiary under the supplied premise. The subsidiary’s trading contribution is therefore 15/2.
Continue the explicit equipment schedules: legal depreciation 14, additional acquired-basis
depreciation 4, and acquired tax-liability release 1. They contribute a net subsidiary expense of
17, so its total second-year result is −19/2. NCI receives −19/8; parent owners receive 3/8 after
adding the parent’s own trading result. Group profit is −2. The negative NCI result remains
part of the allocation (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026c, paras. B88, B94).
Without a recognised inventory deferred tax asset, there is no asset to reverse. The subsidiary
instead contributes margin 10 less equipment expense 17, a loss of 7. NCI receives −7/4 and
parent owners receive 9/4; group profit is 1/2. This branch earned 5/2 less in the first year and
5/2 more in the second. After full realisation, the two branches therefore reach the same closing
equity under the stipulated tax assumptions. Their yearly earnings are different.
Both branches end with cash 290, equipment 63, goodwill 135/4, and no inventory or inventory
deferred tax asset. Assets are 1547/4. Liabilities of 57/2 include the original payable 20,
remaining acquired deferred tax liability 7/2, and unpaid current taxes 5. Equity is 1433/4,
split between owners 324 and NCI 137/4. The prior tax payable is carried; no settlement is
invented to obtain these balances.
Complete opening and closing accounts distinguish the attribution even where aggregate profits
coincide. Partial resale retains the appropriate margin and tax; an unresolved opening or
continuing tax method retains the prior result and proposed continuation without a second-year
final report.8 These acquisition examples all started with no prior interest in the subsidiary.
When an investor already holds an interest before obtaining control, the opening investment,
acquisition consideration and any remeasurement must be distinguished before the same
consolidation logic can be applied.
