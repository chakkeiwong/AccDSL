17.28
When the financial history itself cannot be settled
The receipt-6 contradiction contains an economic impossibility that additional information
cannot remove. Its two second-stage refusal rows share the exact rate point 19/320 and 43/800.
Under the existing floating-coupon formula, these rates give I1 = 95/16 and I2 = 43/8. Checking
all four action sequences at this one point avoids confusing two different financial histories that
happen to produce the same message.
Opening cash 11/2 = 88/16 cannot pay the first coupon 95/16, so either sequence beginning
with pay is refused immediately. Drawing supplies one unit and costs 1/4, leaving first-settlement
cash 5/16. Receipt 6 followed by repayment of the borrowed principal leaves 85/16, while the
second coupon is 86/16. Draw/pay therefore fails by 1/16. Draw/draw also fails: the once-only
facility has already been used, and the second coupon is below its strict draw trigger. All four
sequences fail even for a manager who knows both coupons in advance.
At the same rate point with receipt 7, draw/pay succeeds and ends with cash 15/16. This is an
explicit counterfactual, not a replacement for the assumed receipt-6 world. The earlier viable
receipt-6 examples also remain valid. The diagnostic establishes one impossible point in the
original family, rather than impossibility at every point. Later algebraic cash from a sequence
whose first payment was refused is not an executed outcome.23

For receipt 6, the sum boundary is 45/4. Equality is viable: the second payment leaves exactly
zero cash. Equality at the first trigger is also viable, because I1 = 11/2 can be paid without
drawing. For receipt 7, the sum boundary is 49/4 > 12, above the largest possible coupon sum,

The result concerns the continuous coupon square. Exact affine certificates check every inherited
guard of pay/pay in the first region and draw/pay in the second, and exclude every action
sequence in NR. The three regions are exhaustive and disjoint because they split first on
I1 ≤11/2 and then on the sum inequality. Rational boundary examples illustrate the result,
while the region certificates establish it.24
On the viable subset, the original refined first trigger suffices for a common rule: draw first
exactly when that trigger is true, then pay second. The manager does not need the future
coupon to choose the first action. This rule cannot settle the excluded points, which remain part
of the original model. The result does not cover absent or mixed borrowing rights, arbitrary
receipts, or a changed facility contract.
A financially impossible settlement is still a possible company history to represent. The unpaid
coupon, the earlier draw and repayment, and the dates of failed payments must remain available
to subsequent accounting and recovery events. Refusing a payment instruction must therefore
preserve the outstanding obligation and the failed history rather than remove it from later
statements.
