This document specifies a system that translates company disclosures into traceable evidence,
represents several compatible economic and accounting histories, and simulates their future
journals and financial statements under explicit economic scenarios.
The proposed data contracts, methods and acceptance cases specify the intended implementation.
Current source and domain limitations remain explicit; this candidate is not a completed product
or a claim of full proposal readiness. Proposed libraries can be replaced while preserving those
contracts. Sector rule packages require their own accounting review before they can be described
as compliant with a reporting standard.
Research materials, source qualifications and review records accompany this document in
docs/design/research and docs/design/review.

26.25
One debt exchange, two sets of accounts
Suppose a company extinguishes debt of 100 by issuing its own shares, valued at 70, to the
lender. The borrower removes a liability and issues equity; the lender removes a financial asset
and receives a different asset. Neither side receives cash. A gain for one party and a loss for the
other can arise, but the two parties need their own classifications, measurements and journals.
The shares received also raise a separate question about the lender’s subsequent relationship
with the borrower.
26.25.1
The debtor must first establish the scope of the exchange
AASB Interpretation 19 addresses a debtor’s renegotiated financial liability settled by issuing
equity. It does not address creditor accounting. It also excludes an existing shareholder acting
as an owner, common control before and after the exchange together with equity-distribution
or contribution substance, and share settlement in accordance with the liability’s original terms
(Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2020, paras. 2–3). An original contractual conversion
therefore cannot enter this debtor example merely because it produces shares.
The compound conditions matter. An existing shareholding alone does not say that the creditor
acts as an owner in this transaction. Common control alone does not establish the specified equity
substance. The conditional scope calculation keeps yes, no and unknown assertions separate. A
known exclusion remains effective even when another condition is unknown; otherwise missing
scope facts leave the method unresolved. The same discipline applies to source availability at
the stated information cutoff.
For the finite example, supply a renegotiated, whole-liability extinguishment within the Inter-
pretation’s scope, a financial liability and an issue of own equity. The borrower opens with cash
30, equipment 100, debt 100 and contributed capital 30. There is no other activity, interest,
transaction cost, current or deferred tax, OCI or cash leg during the illustrated interval. Each
absence is an explicit synthetic premise.
When the issued equity’s fair value can be reliably measured, the debtor uses that fair value.
Only when it cannot be reliably measured does the Interpretation direct measurement by
reference to the extinguished liability’s fair value (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2020,
paras. 6–7). Carrying amount is not a substitute for missing fair value. At the extinguishment
date, the difference between the liability carrying amount and consideration paid enters profit
or loss (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2020, paras. 5, 9).
At the supplied equity value 70, the debtor debits debt 100, credits newly issued equity 70 and
credits gain 30. Closing cash remains 30 and equipment 100; liabilities are zero. Equity of
130 comprises opening capital 30, new equity 70 and current profit 30. Operating, investing
and financing cash flows are all zero, while the noncash debt-for-equity exchange is identified
separately. The Interpretation’s conceptual analysis using an issue for cash followed by repayment
does not establish actual cash movements in this example (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2020, BC13, BC25).
An equity value of 100 gives no gain; 120 gives a loss of 20. If equity fair value explicitly cannot
be measured reliably and liability fair value is independently supplied as 60, the measured
equity is 60 and the gain is 40. All four cases leave assets and total equity at 130 but change
the division between contributed equity and earnings. A partial exchange needs an allocation
between the extinguished portion and any modification of the remaining debt; it is outside
this complete whole-debt worksheet (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2020, paras. 8,
10–11).

26.25.2
The creditor must separate impairment from disposal
Now consider the lender’s books. Supply a loan measured at amortised cost, with the relevant
business-model and contractual-cash-flow conditions, followed by expiry of the entire contractual
claim. These are distinct accounting premises (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026i,
paras. 3.2.3, 4.1.2). For the shares received, stipulate no control, significant influence or joint
control, an AASB 9 FVTPL classification and no OCI election. No-control alone would again
be insufficient to establish that investment method.
The creditor opens with cash 30, gross loan 100, no loss allowance and capital 130. Before the
exchange, an independently supplied expected-loss assessment requires an allowance of 30. The
creditor debits impairment expense 30 and credits the allowance 30, leaving a net loan balance
of 70. An allowance update belongs in impairment profit or loss; it must not disappear into
a later net disposal calculation (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026i, paras. 5.2.2,
5.5.8).
At exchange, shares with supplied fair value 70 replace that net claim. The creditor debits
shares 70 and the allowance 30, and credits gross loan 100. There is no disposal gain or loss.
Total profit for the interval is a loss of 30, entirely from impairment. Closing cash 30 plus
shares 70 gives assets 100; opening capital 130 less the loss gives equity 100. Cash flows remain
zero. The complete record shows both journals and the intermediate assessed loan, so a correct
closing total cannot conceal an omitted impairment step.
To see why this matters, supply a different assessment: allowance zero before receiving the same
shares worth 70. The disposal then gives a loss of 30 and the total loss is again 30. This is a
different conditional accounting history. The illustration does not infer expected losses from the
share value or certify either assessment for an actual borrower. With allowance zero, shares worth
100 or 120 instead produce zero disposal result or a gain of 20. With allowance 30 and shares
worth 60, impairment loss 30 is followed by disposal loss 10. Whole-asset derecognition and
amortised-cost gains and losses have their own requirements (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2026i, paras. 3.2.12, 5.7.2).
Initial share recognition also needs a measurement distinct from the debtor’s fallback. The
example supplies share fair value and initial transaction price as equal, with unchanged fair value
at closing. A difference between initial fair value and transaction price invokes the additional
conditions in AASB 9; the finite worksheet leaves it unresolved rather than automatically
recognising a day-one gain (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026i, paras. 5.1.1–5.1.1A,
B5.1.2A). Missing share value cannot be replaced by the debtor’s liability value. Zero tax, fees,
interest, other activity and cash legs remain supplied premises.
The matched 70, 100 and 120 examples give opposite total debtor and creditor results under
their specified carrying amounts, share values and absence of other effects. That agreement
does not make the accounting methods identical. It is the complete component accounts,
measurement premises and dated source bindings that permit the comparison.10
If the new shares confer control, the creditor’s noncontrolling worksheet no longer supplies the
acquisition accounting. The debt settlement, consideration for the business and any pre-existing
relationship must be distinguished before goodwill or consolidated statements can be measured.
The next question is how to preserve that separation through the control-changing exchange.
