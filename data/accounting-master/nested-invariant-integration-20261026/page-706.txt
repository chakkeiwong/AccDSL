A.54.21
Keep each assessment attached to its historical terms
A claim can remain outstanding while its due date changes. Checking its current balance
and current due date is therefore insufficient to explain an earlier overdue assessment. In the
retained synthetic example, a claim issued on 2 January 2027 is initially due on 31 January.
An imported assessment dated 28 February records 20 remaining and 20 overdue. Prospective
extensions become effective on 10 March and 10 April, moving the due date first to 30 April
and then to 31 May. Applying the current 31 May due date to the February assessment would
incorrectly turn its overdue amount into zero. The historical assessment must remain attached
to the initial term.
The term history is stored inside the claim. Each term has its own local identifier, effective
date, due date, predecessor and event identity. Each assessment has a date, referenced term,
remaining amount, overdue amount, origin and event identity. Local identifiers alone do not
identify these records: another claim may use the same identifier. Nor does insertion order
establish chronology. A declaration must name the parent claim and select a term using its
effective date before comparing the financial amounts.
