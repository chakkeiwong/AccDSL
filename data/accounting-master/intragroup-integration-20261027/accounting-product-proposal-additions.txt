This document specifies a system that translates company disclosures into traceable evidence,
represents several compatible economic and accounting histories, and simulates their future
journals and financial statements under explicit economic scenarios.
The proposed data contracts, methods and acceptance cases specify the intended implementation.
Current source and domain limitations remain explicit; this candidate is not a completed product
or a claim of full proposal readiness. Proposed libraries can be replaced while preserving those
contracts. Sector rule packages require their own accounting review before they can be described
as compliant with a reporting standard.
Research materials, source qualifications and review records accompany this document in
docs/design/research and docs/design/review.

Availability introduces a different question. In the example the new information is discovered
on 15 January 2026 and reaches the selected actor on 1 February 2026. A 10 January query
retains the original even if the exact delivery time is unknown, because delivery cannot precede
discovery. A 20 January query likewise retains the original when the February delivery is
known. At delivery, eligible supplied information can select the revised conditional report. If
delivery is unknown after discovery, both information views may remain possible. If the supplied
disposition is a later event, unknown delivery does not make retrospective acquisition adjustment
an eligible financial alternative; only the original worksheet remains, with another-method work
conditional on the actor receiving that evidence.
The original report must also survive an unsupported proposed method. An undiscovered
revision might produce a negative excess that the current positive-excess reference cannot
handle. It cannot make the earlier report invalid. Once the revision could be available and
eligible, the system retains the original and the complete proposed request with its refusal and
missing-method requirement. It does not select the original as if the new information had been
resolved, and it does not fabricate a revised result. This distinction preserves history while
making the next accounting-method obligation visible.
26.19.4
The comparison remains narrower than company accounting
The selected comparisons inspect every original account, acquisition item, adjustment origin,
adjusted account and report field, including cash and parent/NCI equity movements. The
repaired revision reference also rebinds the embedded source and control queries to the requested
actor and cutoff. Earlier passing candidates were rejected when discriminating cases exposed a
pre-discovery alternative, an ineligible adjustment under unknown access, and a future failed
candidate that erased the original. Those failures remain in the evidence history.4
This remains a four-item synthetic acquisition with stipulated tax treatment, one currency,
ordinary rights, one actor and one proposed revision. Prior interests, actual debt conversion,
loss or later ownership changes, intragroup trade, general item mappings, foreign exchange,
revision chains and complete statutory disclosures need further methods and evidence. The
next comparison must add post-control intragroup transactions without leaking pre-control
activity or losing the acquired measurement basis.
26.20
An internal sale becomes group profit only through an
outside transaction
The acquisition worksheet now has a second task: keep two valid legal books from counting a
transaction within the group as income from outside it. Continue the synthetic 75 per cent cash
acquisition, but add inventory costing 20 and matching capital of 20 to the parent’s opening

credit. The parent records revenue 30, cost of sales 20 and a receivable 30. The subsidiary
records inventory 30 and a payable 30. Each legal book balances, yet their sum includes a profit
of 10 before any customer outside the group has bought the goods.

26.20.1
Removing profit does not remove an external tax obligation
Tax depends on more than the consolidated carrying amount. AASB 112 compares that amount
with the appropriate tax base, which may depend on consolidated or separate tax returns. Its
illustrative examples include intragroup profit eliminated from inventory without an equivalent
adjustment for tax purposes (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2023b, para. 11 and
illustrative examples, B11). The accounting rule does not tell us which tax law applies to this
company.
To expose this dependency, stipulate that the parent’s margin of 10 is taxed now at one quarter,
that the subsidiary keeps a tax base of 30, and that the same rate applies when it uses the
deduction. The parent therefore has a current tax expense and external payable of 5/2. The
group inventory has carrying amount 20 and tax base 30, leaving a deductible temporary
difference of 10. If the supplied recoverability conditions permit recognition, the deferred tax
asset is also 5/2. Its income offsets the current expense in this example; the external payable
remains. If recognition is explicitly withheld, the asset is zero and group profit is lower by 5/2.
The reference retains these alternatives separately. If the tax treatment is unknown, it retains
the pretax worksheet and requires the missing tax method before selecting a final report.
These are supplied tax and attribution premises. Recognition requires the appropriate taxable-
profit assessment, and measurement depends on enacted or substantively enacted rates and
expected recovery (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2023b, paras. 24–29, 46–51 and
58). In this downstream example the parent’s current tax and the consolidation tax adjustment
are attributed to parent owners. That premise does not resolve upstream transactions or every
group tax arrangement. The retained source editions support the conditional design; they do
not select an actual entity’s reporting package or tax law.
With the deferred asset recognised, the unsold-inventory case closes with assets 1629/4, liabilities
27 and equity 1521/4. Parent equity is 2749/8 and NCI is 293/8. Group cash remains 270,
whether the internal payable is settled or outstanding. Inventory and the extra opening parent
capital account for 20 of the change from the acquisition-only case; the separate tax asset and
payable account for 5/2 on each side. No balancing amount is needed.
26.20.2
An outside sale releases only the corresponding part of the margin
Now let the subsidiary sell half the lot to an outside customer for 20 cash before the year
closes. The example stipulates a qualifying customer contract, transfer of control and a fixed
price, with no returns or additional obligations. A cash receipt alone would not establish those
facts (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paras. 9, 31–38 and 47). The subsidiary
recognises revenue 20 and inventory cost 15. Inventory cost is expensed when the related revenue

The group still eliminates the full internal sale of 30. Only half of its margin remains in unsold
inventory, so the closing unrealised-profit adjustment is 5. The remaining group inventory is
10, while the sold half costs the group 10. The outside sale thus adds group pretax profit of
10: revenue 20 less the original group cost 10. Of this increment, the subsidiary’s own sale
contributes 5 and the release of the parent’s margin contributes 5.
Continue the supplied tax premises. The parent’s current tax remains 5/2; the subsidiary’s
profit of 5 adds current tax 5/4. Only the unsold margin supports the remaining deferred
asset, now 5/4. Relative to the acquisition case without inventory trading, the group earns
an additional after-tax profit of 15/2. Half arises in the subsidiary after its current tax, and
half belongs to parent owners after the downstream consolidation adjustments. Ordinary NCI
therefore participates in the subsidiary’s 15/4 increment, not in the parent’s released margin.
The full closing comparison gives group profit 39: owners receive 483/16 and NCI receives
141/16. Assets are 416, liabilities are 113/4, and equity is 1551/4. Parent equity of 5603/16
plus NCI of 601/16 gives the same total. Cash is 290, including the outside receipt of 20. That
operating receipt remains in the group cash report even if the separate internal payment of 30
is also made.
26.20.3
The dated journals make the closing report explainable
The comparison retains every legal entry, including the supplied opening trial balances, the
explicitly stipulated pre-control receipt and interim close, acquisition consideration, controlled-
period receipts and depreciation, internal sale and settlement, outside sale and current taxes.
Replaying these dated journals recovers the opening, acquisition and closing accounts. Their
origins distinguish an outside receipt from a reciprocal internal payment; the consolidation
entries remain in a separate worksheet. A set of closing balances could not establish that history
on its own.
No resale, half resale and full resale are compared under each selected NCI election and
consideration mix. Unknown tax preserves the missing dependency instead of completing
the report with zero. These results remain a one-lot, same-period downstream example with
supplied revenue, tax and ordinary-rights premises.5 Carrying unsold inventory into another
year creates a new question: the next report must release profit from opening group equity and
carry its tax basis, rather than repeat the original acquisition or eliminate a sale that occurred
in the prior year. That requires a separate two-year comparison.
