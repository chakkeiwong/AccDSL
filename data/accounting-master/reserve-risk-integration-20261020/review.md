## Eleven displayed calculations

1. **Relative allowance:** total measure less the same law's mean. This
   separates expected payments from compensation without choosing preferences.
2. **Left quantile:** the infimum definition and domain `(0,1]` handle atoms.
   The example identifies 4 at level 3/5; it does not claim a unique inverse.
3. **Expected shortfall:** the quantile integral retains exactly
   `F(q)-alpha` of the boundary atom. With positive masses summing to one,
   the example gives 31/4; the separately defined alpha=0 endpoint is the mean.
4. **PH survival integral:** nonnegative losses and rho at least one are
   explicit. At rho=1 it is the ordinary mean; signed inputs need another method.
5. **PH finite weights:** decreasing transformed survival gives nonnegative
   differences summing to one. Recombining identical outcomes preserves the
   integral. The 0/4 counterexample identifies the inspected printing error.
6. **Expected-SD bound:** conditional Jensen and total variance require nested
   information and finite second moments. It is not a pathwise or quantile bound.
7. **Conditional total variance:** the nonnegative conditional-mean variance
   explains the second inequality; the four-outcome witness gives 30=5+25.
8. **CDR substitution:** paid amounts cancel exactly when reserves equal true
   conditional expected remaining payments. An upward ultimate revision is a
   negative profit-sign CDR. A fitted reserve need not meet the premise.
9. **Reverse-CDR sum:** telescoping gives `M_t-U`, whose conditional mean at t
   is zero. Total variance relates its time-zero variance to expected `V_t`.
10. **Additive CDR variances:** iterated expectation gives orthogonality under
    true conditional means. The example's covariance is zero but increments
    are dependent. Origin aggregation requires covariance terms.
11. **Cost-of-capital sum:** capital at start of year t incurs a year-end
    charge discounted by `d(0,t+1)`. The surrounding prose explicitly requires
    an expectation and joint dependence if the inputs are random. No charge
    rate, discount basis or capital measure is supplied by the formula.

## Cold-reader answers

1. A payment law describes possible payments and their probabilities; an
   entity must still choose how it values bearing that uncertainty.
2. Upper forty-percent ES includes all mass at 10 and 3/20 at 4. Omitting or
   retaining the entire boundary atom changes the question. Coverage 3/4 at
   31/4 does not make 31/4 a quantile of a law whose values are only 0, 4 and 10.
3. A transformed mass is the fall in transformed survival across an outcome.
   The inspected formula reverses that subtraction after the first outcome;
   the two-point rho=1 counterexample gives negative mass and the wrong mean.
4. `F_0` is today's information; `F_t` includes later news and paid amounts.
   Expected future SD is 2, root expected variance is sqrt(5), and today's SD
   is sqrt(30); the upper branch's SD 3 exceeds sqrt(5).
5. True conditional means give zero-mean martingale differences, permitting
   the variance sum. Arbitrary fitted estimates can break those premises.
6. A real calculation still needs the justified law, horizon, units, sign,
   conditioning, entity preference, capital basis, charge/discount timing and
