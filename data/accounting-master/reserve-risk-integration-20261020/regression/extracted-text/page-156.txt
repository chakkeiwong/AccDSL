Consider a deliberately limited asset book to test a tempting reconstruction. Let G0, G1 be
opening and closing gross cost and B0, B1 accumulated depreciation, recorded as positive
amounts. Let A be gross additions, R the original cost of retired assets, d the depreciation
charge and S the accumulated depreciation removed with those assets. Assume there are no
other movements and all depreciation is expensed. For this illustration, use the displayed
amounts as exact, including d = 8,200 million. The two balances must then satisfy
G1 = G0 + A −R,
B1 = B0 + d −S,
0 ≤S ≤R.
(9.1)
The inequality requires a nonnegative carrying amount for the retired assets in this cost
model. If additions are equated with the 9,447 of cash payments, the gross balance requires
R = 9,447 −4,529 = 4,918. The depreciation balance requires S = 8,200 −2,564 = 5,636. The
proposed book removes more accumulated depreciation than original cost, by 718 million. It is
infeasible under these combined assumptions. That finding rejects the proposed history; it does
not establish an error in Apple’s filing or identify which assumption must change.
The result is not sensitive to small rounding differences under a specified rounding model. If
the million-dollar balances and payment are rounded to the nearest million, and 8.2 billion to
the nearest 0.1 billion, the excess d −9,447 + (45,680 −43,715) lies in the closed outer envelope
[666.5, 769.5] million. Its lower endpoint is 8,150 −9,447.5 + 45,679.5 −43,715.5 = 666.5. This
is a conditional precision check, not a verified issuer rounding rule; the exact tie convention
remains unknown.

Both histories satisfy equation (9.1); their gross additions can also be allocated to match the three
reported asset classes. For History 1, acquisition debits PP&E cost 10,529 and credits cash 9,447
and a capital payable 1,082. Depreciation debits expense and credits accumulated depreciation
by 8,200. Retirement debits accumulated depreciation 5,636 and loss 364, while crediting gross
PP&E cost 6,000. Net PP&E rises 1,965 and cash falls 9,447, so total assets fall 7,482. Liabilities
rise 1,082 and equity falls 8,564 through the two expenses: 1,082 −8,564 = −7,482. History 2
has the same asset change but liabilities rise 2,082 and equity falls 9,564. These are coherent
subsystem consequences, not a fit to all of Apple’s statements or an exhaustive family of real
asset histories. Neither history receives a probability or preference from this arithmetic.
