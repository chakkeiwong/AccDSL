10.19
A foreign subsidiary from acquisition to disposal
The preceding examples establish acquisition, translation and disposal separately.
Their
interaction matters because a foreign subsidiary carries more than translated net assets into a
sale: goodwill and accumulated exchange differences also have owners and dates. A sale can
leave total equity correct while putting the wrong amount in profit. We follow one subsidiary
through these steps using the selected historical IFRS 3, IFRS 10, IAS 21 and IAS 7 provisions
in the retained EU text (European Commission, 2023a).
A parent starts with USD 300 of cash and equity. USD is its functional and presentation
currency; the subsidiary’s functional currency is EUR. At acquisition the parent pays EUR
96 for 80% of equal ordinary interests, when each euro buys USD 1.20. It obtains substantive
power over the relevant activities, exposure to variable returns and the ability to affect them.
The other owners have no substantive rights that defeat that control. Neither investment-entity
treatment nor a common-control transaction applies.
The acquired business already produces outputs and includes a skilled organised workforce
performing a process critical to continuing them, as required by the assumed IFRS 3.B12C(a)
facts. The optional concentration test is not elected. Identifiable fair values are EUR 20 of
cash, inventory of 140 and a payable of 60. There is no other separately identifiable asset, tax
difference, previous holding, contingent consideration or acquisition cost in this synthetic case.
The workforce itself is subsumed into goodwill under B37; calling a residual goodwill would not
justify omitting an identifiable customer relationship or other asset in a real acquisition.
Identifiable net assets are 20 + 140 −60 = 100 euros. The parent chooses proportionate-share
measurement for the non-controlling interest (NCI) under IFRS 3.19. NCI is therefore EUR
20 and goodwill under paragraph 32 is 96 + 20 −100 = 16 euros. This recognised goodwill
belongs to the acquired parent interest; no NCI goodwill has been recognised. The parent’s
separate entry debits investment and credits cash by USD 115.2. The consolidation adjustment
debits acquisition equity 120 and goodwill 19.2, and credits the investment 115.2 and NCI 24.
Immediately after acquisition, group cash is 208.8, inventory 168, goodwill 19.2 and the payable
72. Net assets of 324 equal parent equity 300 plus NCI 24.
The subsidiary then sells all the inventory for EUR 160, receives cash, and pays its EUR 60
payable. Both events occur at USD 1.25 per euro. The specified event interval has no further
expense, impairment or distribution. In the subsidiary’s books, revenue 160 less cost of sales
140 gives profit 20; cash closes at 20 + 160 −60 = 120 euros. Translated revenue is 200, cost of
sales 175 and profit 25. Parent and NCI shares of profit are 20 and 5. The translation worksheet
translates the opening balances to the event rate before recording these movements; it does not
charge cost 175 against unadjusted acquisition inventory 168 and leave a temporary negative
stock. These are presentation adjustments, not a remeasurement of historical-cost inventory in
the subsidiary’s own functional currency.
At reporting, and at an immediate disposal considered below, the rate is USD 1.30 per euro.
Subsidiary cash translates to 156. IAS 21.47 also treats the acquisition goodwill as an asset of
the foreign operation: its EUR 16 becomes USD 20.8. To explain the currency reserve, translate
the opening net assets and the subsequently earned profit from their respective rates:
