The cancellation uses DN = ND and does not divide by earnings, which may be zero or
negative. It consequently handles a loss without a separate sign shortcut. For example, if
N = −20, D = 10, a = −15 and b = 5, the candidate changes EPS from −2 to −35/15 = −7/3:
it increases the loss per share and is dilutive. A zero earnings increment with the same positive
share increment would reduce that loss and be excluded. Equality in the comparison is neutral
and is also excluded under the strict dilution rule (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 33,
paragraphs 41–44).
Sort candidates by their exact ratio a/b in ascending order. Break equal ratios by a stable series
identifier, then test each against the current N, D. On admission, replace N, D with N +a, D+b
before proceeding. Use rational arithmetic for this decision; rounding an incremental ratio to
the displayed precision can change an equality into an apparent decrease. The debt in the
example has the exact ratio 365/184, and the preference shares are compared with 7665/1684,
not with the earlier basic ratio. The share increment already contains its time weight; the
selector must not apply the issue-date fraction a second time.
Why does this order suffice under the additive assumptions? Admission makes the new ratio a
weighted average of the old ratio and a/b, strictly between them. The new ratio remains above
the admitted ratio. Every earlier admitted ratio is therefore below the final ratio, say q. At the
first exclusion, the next ratio is at least q; every later ratio is at least as large and is excluded
too. If S is the selected set and N0, D0 the basic amounts,

Each selected summand is negative; every unselected summand is nonnegative. Replacing S by
any other subset can only increase the left side. Dividing by that subset’s positive denominator
shows that its continuing EPS is at least q. This is a local derivation for fixed independent
effects, not a claim that arbitrary instruments satisfy those assumptions. Neutral series can
create other subsets with the same minimum; strict admission excludes them.
Total earnings have their own supplied increment for each series. Carry those increments
through the same selected population, then subtract continuing earnings to obtain discontinued
earnings. Selecting again on total profit would contradict the continuing-operations control just
illustrated (European Commission, 2023a, IAS 33, paragraph A3).
