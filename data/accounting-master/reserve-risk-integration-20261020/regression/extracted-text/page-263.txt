Buying a claim whose credit loss is already in the price
The preceding loan became credit-impaired after origination. A purchased or originated credit-
impaired asset, abbreviated POCI, has a different starting point: expected credit losses already
enter the cash flows used to determine its credit-adjusted effective interest rate. Charging those
same initial losses again would reduce the asset twice.
Suppose the bank buys a fixed claim of 121, due in two annual periods, for 50. Its initial expected
collection is 60.5. Assume separately established POCI status, amortised-cost classification
and acquisition fair value equal to the price, with no fees, interim payments, modifications,
foreign exchange or tax. Assume that reasonable prospects of fuller recovery remain until the
terminal assessment, so no earlier full or partial write-off is required. Expected cash below the
contractual amount does not establish that some portion has no reasonable prospect of recovery.
Paragraphs 5.4.4 and B5.4.9 require that separate assessment. The original credit-adjusted rate
r, with positive compounding factor 1 + r, must discount expected collection to the amount
