Acquisition costs and the timing of reinsurance
An acquisition asset can enter a group that is onerous at recognition. Reinsurance then raises a
second question: how much of that initial loss may be recognised as recoverable, and when?
Following the two groups separately prevents a recoverable loss from becoming either a second
asset or a reduction of insurance revenue.
Consider a separate general-measurement group with opening capital and cash of 50. Before
recognition, the insurer pays acquisition costs of 10, allocated wholly to that future group. At
the prior assessment, expected premium 100, claims 80 and risk adjustment 5 give expected net
inflow 15. The asset of 10 is therefore recoverable under the stated assumptions, leaving cash
40, acquisition asset 10 and equity 50. No renewal allocation is involved (Australian Accounting
Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 28A–28F and B35A–B35D).
The expected claims increase to 100 when the contracts are recognised; premium remains 100
and risk adjustment remains 5. The example has no intervening unrecorded onerous period,
discounting, tax, investment component or OCI. Before premium receipt, fulfilment cash flows
are 100 + 5 −100 = 5. Removing the acquisition asset adds 10 to the initial loss, making 15,
with CSM zero. The entry debits loss 15 and credits acquisition asset 10 and remaining-coverage
liability 5. Receiving the premium then gives cash 140, liability 105 and equity 35 (Australian
Accounting Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 28C, 38(c)(i) and 47).
The loss component of 15 is part of the liability of 105. Acquisition cash has already left the
company: putting it back into future cash outflows would count the cost twice. Its original
asset also disappears when it enters the group measurement.
A separate held treaty covers half of every claim of this group and costs 55. Assume no
commissions, limits, disputes or counterparty shortfall. Expected recovery is 50; risk transferred
is separately valued at 2.5.
That supplied risk adjustment need not equal half the gross
adjustment in a different contract. Ordinary cost CSM is 55 −50 −2.5 = 2.5, so the held
asset after premium payment would be 55 (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022f,
paragraphs 62–65).
If the treaty was entered into at or before recognition of the onerous group, the specified initial
loss recovery is 15(0.5) = 7.5. It increases held cost CSM to 10 and the measured asset to
50+2.5+10 = 62.5. The standard multiplies the recognised loss by the expected claims-recovery
percentage; this does not mean the treaty separately reimburses half the acquisition invoice.
The loss-recovery component of 7.5 is contained in the asset of 62.5. After paying the treaty
premium, cash 85 plus that asset equals liability 105 plus equity 42.5 (Australian Accounting
Standards Board, 2022f, paragraphs 66A–66B and B119C–B119F).
At the end of the single service interval, release the issued liability of 105 as revenue 90 and loss
reversal 15. The ratio 15/105 = 1/7 allocates the expected-claim and risk-adjustment releases to
the loss component. Acquisition-cost presentation adds revenue 10 and an equal service expense
10 under B125. It creates neither cash nor a new acquisition asset. Recognising and paying
actual claims of 100 therefore gives issued-group service-period profit 100 −100 −10 + 15 = 5.
Including the initial loss of 15, its lifetime loss is 10 (Australian Accounting Standards Board,
2022f, paragraphs 49–52 and B120–B125).
The held asset releases as premium allocation 55 and loss-recovery reversal 7.5, with incurred
recovery and collection of 50 recorded separately. Its service-period result is 50−55−7.5 = −12.5;
the initial recovery of 7.5 makes its lifetime result −5. The company’s final cash and equity are
both 35:
50 + 100 −10 −55 −100 + 50 = 35.
All issued and held balances are zero. Total operating cash is −15, profit is −15, and OCI is zero.
