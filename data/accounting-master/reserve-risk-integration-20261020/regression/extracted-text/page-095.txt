A continuous example whose posterior can be checked
A linear Gaussian model supplies a tractable comparison for later numerical inference. Assume
the unknown economic quantity has a Gaussian prior z ∼N(m0, V0) with positive definite
covariance V0. Let the observation be y = Hz + ϵ, where ϵ is independent Gaussian noise with
positive definite covariance R. The measurement-error assumption is substantive: it describes
this example, not rounding in every financial statement.

The prior and observation contribute precision and precision-weighted information. An im-

For m0 = 0, V0 = 4, H = 1, R = 1 and y = 2, posterior precision is 1/4 + 1 = 5/4, variance
is 4/5 and mean is (4/5)2 = 8/5. A conditional cash quantity 100 + 10z has mean 116 and
variance 80. A second independent measurement of 2 would give precision 9/4, variance 4/9
and mean 16/9. Reusing the first measurement would produce those same numbers but would
be wrong for the stated information. The analytic example can distinguish numerical error
from information double counting.
For a bounded non-Gaussian scalar model, quadrature requires a stated domain or tail truncation
and sufficient integrand error bounds to enclose the normalizer and requested moments. Without
a positive lower normalizer bound, the ratio remains unresolved. Mixed models can enumerate
finite choices and integrate each conditional continuous model, but combining branches requires
their evidence factors, not draw counts. Optional MCMC needs target-specific parameterisation,
tuning, independent chains, tractable correctness comparisons, convergence and Monte Carlo
error checks. This proposal supplies no generally validated sampler or universal tolerance.
Nor can a more elaborate inference engine identify a quantity that the observation model
never sees. If reports constrain only total debt 100, the likelihood may be constant in its
floating share x. Its conditional prior then remains unchanged. A uniform prior is a chosen
assumption, not a discovery from those reports. The inspected working version of Giacomini and
Kitagawa (2021, Sections 2.1–2.3, equations (2.2)–(2.4)) motivates retaining posterior classes
where a conditional prior cannot be revised. For Northstar there is a simple direct deduction:
under all probability measures on [0, 1], the expectation of 5 + 2x lies in [5, 7], with endpoints
attained by point masses. This deduction imports no asymptotic theorem from that paper’s
structural-vector-autoregression setting.
