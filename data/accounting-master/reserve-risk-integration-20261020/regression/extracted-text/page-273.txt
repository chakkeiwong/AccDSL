When an eligible part is derecognised, 3.2.13–14 allocates the old carrying amount by the
relative fair values of the transferred and retained parts at the transfer date. Retained servicing
rights enter that analysis; inadequate servicing compensation can require a servicing liability. A
nominal principal ratio is not a substitute for relative fair value.
Assume an eligible identified cash-flow part, completed transfer and admitted derecognition. The
larger asset carries 100; transferred and retained parts have supplied fair values 45 and 55, and
cash consideration is 46. Allocate carrying amounts 45 and 55. Debit cash 46, credit the asset
45 and recognise gain 1. The retained asset carries 55; net assets rise from 100 to 101. If the sold
principal fraction happened to be 40%, using that fraction would report retained carrying value
60 and gain 6, despite the supplied relative values. This example excludes servicing, fees, tax
and prior OCI and assumes no separate allowance at the measurement instant; those features
need their own retained allocations, not silent deletion from a real transfer.
If substantially all risks and rewards are retained, 3.2.15 instead keeps the entire asset and
recognises a liability for the proceeds. Receiving 46 then gives cash 46, unchanged loan 100 and
liability 46, with no sale gain. The same cash receipt can therefore have a different balance
sheet. Subsequent asset income and liability expense are recognised separately, not offset.
11.10.4
Continuing involvement preserves the guarantee exposure
Where neither substantially all risks nor rewards are transferred or retained and control remains,
3.2.16–22 requires continued recognition to the extent of involvement and an associated liability.
A guarantee case follows B3.2.13(a) (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2022h, B3.2.13(a)):
the retained asset is the lower of its carrying amount and the maximum consideration that
could be repaid; the initial associated liability is that guarantee amount plus the fair value of
the guarantee. Subsequent loss allowance reduces the retained asset, while the initial guarantee
fair value enters profit as the obligation is satisfied under AASB15 principles. Options and
partial retained interests require their own measurement branches; the guarantee formula is not
a general transfer formula.
Admit a transferred asset carrying 100, cash received 103 including fair value 3 for a guarantee,
maximum repayment 20, and the required risk/reward/control findings. Retain an asset of
20 and recognise associated liability 23. The entry debits cash 103 and the retained asset 20,
credits the old asset 100 and liability 23, and has no initial gain. Cash 103 plus asset 20 less
liability 23 preserves net assets of 100. Derecognising everything would wrongly report gain 3
and omit the exposure.
At a later date, admit satisfaction of one third of the guarantee service and loss allowance 2.
Release guarantee income 1 by reducing the associated liability to 22; recognise impairment
expense 2 and allowance 2. Net assets are 103 + (20 −2) −22 = 99, matching net expense 1. If
the guarantee then expires with no claim and no remaining obligation, reverse that allowance 2
and remove retained asset 20 against liability 22, recognising the remaining guarantee income 2.
Cash and net assets are 103 and cumulative net income 3. An actual claim, changed valuation
or unsatisfied obligation would require its actual settlement analysis; expiry cannot be presumed
from a convenient reporting date. The continuing asset and liability are presented separately.
