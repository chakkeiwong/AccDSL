A query for the selected displayed statement tokens can therefore finish even though the question

The distinction matters: an unresolved feasible remainder does not mean that no history exists,

the reading task does not.
Preserving a source token also requires restraint in arithmetic. The annual cash-flow row for
debt issuance displays a dash. Its observation remains a reported dash. The retained financing
reconciliation separately states the narrow convention under which that dash contributes zero
to its displayed sum. This convention does not turn all dashes into zeros. Similarly, the annual
cash-flow opening amount of 30,737 differs from the opening balance-sheet cash amount of
29,965 by 772. That population difference is an explanatory residual, not permission to create a
receipt or payment for 772.
2.24.1
Matching annual rows leaves different liabilities to settle
Two conditional asset histories illustrate why the connected result must retain journals as well as
totals. In H1, gross additions of 10,529 are paid with 9,447 of cash and a capital payable of 1,082.
Retirements remove gross cost of 6,000 and accumulated depreciation of 5,636, leaving a loss
of 364. H2 adds and retires another 1,000 of gross cost: additions become 11,529, retirements
7,000, loss 1,364 and capital payable 2,082. Depreciation is 8,200 in both branches. The extra
acquisition and retirement cancel in net property, plant and equipment; cash is unchanged. The
asset journals alone leave H2 with 1,000 more liability and 1,000 less profit and equity.
Now suppose a distinct non-tax expense liability exists: its amount is q in H1 and q −1,000
in H2, with q ≥1,000. Each branch debits expense and credits that liability. This is an
independent economic premise, not an event inferred from a statement residual. Suppose also
that the retirement loss and this expense share the product-cost row, both liabilities share the
same other-current-liability bucket, the extra asset cost has the same gross class, and current
and deferred tax, all other activities, shares and distributions are unchanged. Then the expense
difference of −1,000 offsets the extra retirement loss, and the liability difference offsets the extra
capital payable.
The indirect cash-flow presentation still needs a specified allocation. Let α ∈[0, 1] allocate the
expense-liability adjustment once between the noncash adjustment and the working-capital
adjustment. If ∆means H2 minus H1, the two displayed-row differences become
∆other adjustments = 1,000 + α(−1,000) = 1,000(1 −α),
(2.25)
∆other liabilities = (1 −α)(−1,000) = −1,000(1 −α).
(2.26)
The two differences sum to zero for every allowed α. At α = 1, each is zero and all 102 selected
annual row differences vanish under these assumptions. At α = 0 or 1/2, the same total
operating cash has a different presentation in those two rows. Here α is a reporting assumption,
not a probability over histories. Matching differences also does not construct an absolute feasible
remainder for the company. The illustrative journals remain conditional subsystem histories.
