11.1.7
Reconciling accounting equity to regulatory capital
Regulatory capital is computed in a separate prudential layer with its own perimeter, eligibility,
deductions, risk weights and transition provisions. Accounting equity feeds a reconciliation
to capital; it is not automatically Common Equity Tier 1. Liquidity and capital constraints
influence the management policy, which in turn generates asset sales, funding or capital events.
A breached ratio can be an economically meaningful scenario outcome. No hidden capital
injection is permitted to repair it.
The United States national-bank rules show why that reconciliation needs records for each
instrument. Under the common-stock criteria in the retained January and April 2026 texts,
classification as equity under GAAP is only one condition. The instrument must also satisfy
conditions concerning payment and direct issuance by the bank, priority in insolvency, loss
absorption, permanence, dividend discretion, purchase financing and guarantees. The rules
distinguish common equity Tier 1, additional Tier 1 and Tier 2, each with its own eligible elements
and deductions. A security’s name and its balance-sheet category therefore cannot supply its
regulatory treatment (Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, 2026c, 12 CFR 3.20(a)–(d)).
Regulatory recognition can also change while a debt remains outstanding. The general Tier 2
instrument criteria require an original maturity of at least five years. At the beginning of
each of its last five years, the amount eligible for Tier 2 falls by 20% of the original amount,
net of redemptions; the instrument is excluded when less than one year remains. Calls and
early redemptions have further conditions, including OCC approval. This reduction in eligible
capital does not itself reduce the accounting debt. The contract, issuance and redemption
history must support both amounts separately (Office of the Comptroller of the Currency,
2026c, 12 CFR 3.20(d)(1), including footnote 7).
Deductions require similarly specific records. Section 3.22 deducts goodwill and specified other
assets from common equity Tier 1, with separate rules for their tax effects. Its general rule
permits an associated deferred tax liability to reduce an asset deduction only if that liability
would be extinguished when the asset is impaired or derecognized. The same liability cannot
offset two assets; separate conditions govern offsets against deferred tax assets, and the chosen
netting treatment must be consistent across reporting periods unless the OCC approves a change.
Accumulated other comprehensive income has its own adjustments and election conditions,
including limits on which institutions may opt out and when they must elect. Reconstructing
capital consequently needs asset-specific tax links, instrument facts, institution classification
and the actual election history. The reported equity and aggregate deferred-tax balances do not
determine that reconstruction (Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, 2026a, 12 CFR 3.22(a),
(b), (e)).
