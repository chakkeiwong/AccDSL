10.8
Modification, extinguishment and default
At a modification, retain original and replacement terms, counterparty, fees, effective date,
legal-release evidence and original EIR. Determine derecognition before measurement. AASB
9.3.3.1–3.3.3 and B3.3.1–B3.3.6 govern the selected issuer-liability cases. The quantitative
ten-percent test compares the PV of new terms, including specified borrower/lender fees, at
original EIR against remaining original PV. It is not a universal asset test, nor proof that every
change below ten percent is immaterial; changed currency, rights and other qualitative terms
need the applicable analysis. Insufficient evidence remains unresolved. Third-party payment
without legal release does not extinguish debt.
On the first anniversary above, old remaining 121 has PV 110. Replace with 133.1 due at the
original maturity date, one year after the modification, with no fees and an explicit supported
substantial-terms conclusion: PV 121 differs by exactly 10%, satisfying the inclusive test.
Assume separately supported new fair value 118; debit old debt 110, debit extinguishment loss
8, credit new debt 118. The new EIR is 133.1/118−1, not automatically the old rate. If the
replacement is instead a stated non-substantial same-currency reduction to 115.5, no fees or other
changes, PV 105 leads to debit debt 5/credit modification gain 5 under the B5.4.6 recalculation
mechanism, then interest 10.5 and settlement 115.5. The actual change below ten percent still
needs the qualitative finding. Repurchase of carrying amount 110 for 108 extinguishes the
repurchased obligation: debit debt 110/credit cash 108/credit gain 2. Unamortised fees belong
in the carrying amount before that comparison, not a second gain adjustment.
If cash available is 80 against debt 121 due, record the due obligation and an unpaid/default
economic state; payment 80 leaves contractual unpaid 41 only when that partial payment
actually occurs. Do not cancel the remaining liability, recognize a waiver or issue a loan unless
a separately admitted event authorizes it. Covenant acceleration, cross-default, cure periods,
collateral realization and restructuring require the actual legal terms and priority waterfall. A
scenario may explore specified alternatives with explicit assumptions; it may not silently select
the cheapest settlement as an accounting fact.
