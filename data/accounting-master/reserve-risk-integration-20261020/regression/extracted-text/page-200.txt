For a qualifying fixed-for-fixed issuer conversion option in functional currency, AASB 132.28–32
separates liability fair value and residual equity. Assume total issue proceeds 100, standalone
debt fair value 90, zero coupon, maturity two years and cash redemption 100 or fixed 10 ordinary
shares; no extra derivative, tax or fee. Issue debit cash 100/credit debt 90/credit conversion-
equity 10. Effective interest accretes debt 90 to 100. At maturity conversion under original
terms debits debt 100 and credits issued-share equity 100; the original equity 10 stays in equity
(a permitted internal transfer is not income). Shares increase 10 and no redemption cash leaves.
Redemption instead debits debt 100/credit cash 100; unexercised equity is not remeasured into
profit. AG 32 supports no conversion gain/loss in that stated case. Variable share count, cash
alternatives, foreign currency and changed conversion terms require classification/modification
analysis; they cannot be forced into this example.
Financial statement mappings separate interest, modification/extinguishment results, owner
transactions and cash settlement. A financing-liability note reconciles opening carrying amount,
cash proceeds/repayments, effective interest, fees, conversions, modifications and FX. Total
shares and earnings-per-share denominators need dates and dilution conditions; outstanding
shares at year end are not a substitute for a weighted period denominator. Full option valuation,
hedge accounting, EPS and legal-capital regimes remain explicitly additional methods; the
proposed records preserve their required inputs.

Changed conversion terms change the accounting question
A maturity conversion, an early repurchase and an inducement to convert all remove or alter
debt, but they do not allocate the resulting consideration in the same way. The issuer must retain
the old conversion rights, the revised rights, the date of amendment and the actual settlement. A
record that says only converted loses the facts needed to distinguish these events. The following
cases use the retained Australian standards for explicit calendar-2024/2025 for-profit Tier 1
illustrations, without optional later amendments. They are pretax: tax consequences remain
uncomputed.
For an early repurchase with unchanged conversion privileges, the issuer allocates the considera-
tion between the liability and equity components using the method of the original separation.
The liability allocation uses its current fair value; the difference from its carrying amount enters
profit or loss, while the equity allocation stays in equity (Australian Accounting Standards
Board, 2022c, AG33–34 and IE39–46). The standard’s example has a face amount of 1,000,
half-yearly coupons of 50 and ten payments remaining. The original half-year discount rate is
5.5%; the contemporaneous comparable nonconvertible debt rate is 4%. For either rate r, the
