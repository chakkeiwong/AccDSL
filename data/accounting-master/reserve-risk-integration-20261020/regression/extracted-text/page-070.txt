Public availability and system possession are different views
Northstar’s correction explains why a result needs a reporting vintage. A historical forecast
also needs to establish when the exact document bytes were available. A report downloaded
today may carry an old date on its current web page; that does not prove that the same bytes
were public on that date. Public availability evidence and an ingestion receipt answer different
questions.
An investor-information query supplies a cutoff K. Each admitted source must have supported
public availability no later than K and evidence linking the selected bytes to that availability.
Later ingestion is allowed. Later extraction review can read eligible old bytes, but an interpreta-
tion that uses later information fails the cutoff on its own dependencies. This view establishes
public availability, not actual investor awareness or the ability to trade.
A system-as-of query instead supplies an instant S and requires an immutable receipt showing
possession of those bytes by S. Publication cannot replace that receipt. An accepted-layer query
also requires review decisions visible by S; a raw-layer query may return a then-unreviewed
document. A query asking both what was public and what the system possessed must pass
both filters. Unknown publication alone need not block a pure possession question.
Archival exploration permits inspection of retained sources with incomplete timing. With a
cutoff, unknown publication yields an unresolved candidate, not a cutoff-qualified selection.
Without a cutoff, the archive may display the retained lineage, explicitly without a historical-
knowledge claim. A known later version is excluded from an earlier view and cannot suppress
an eligible old assertion. An unresolved potentially relevant version keeps the affected answer
unresolved even when an older assertion is displayed.
Date-only evidence requires particular care. For publication date D in a named timezone, let
[aD, bD) be the interval from local midnight to the next local midnight. A cutoff before aD
excludes the source; a cutoff inside the interval leaves availability unresolved; a cutoff at or after
bD supports admission on this date evidence. No arbitrary noon or fixed 24-hour duration is
inserted. Missing timezone leaves the instant comparison unresolved.
