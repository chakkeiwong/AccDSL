2.8.2
Currency and ownership follow different dates
The exact first-period transaction rate is 1.1 and the closing rate 1.2 USD/FCU; the second
rates are 1.25 and 1.3. Opening loan origination uses the opening rate 1. The foreign recognition
and cash events use these supplied transaction rates; closing translation follows them at the
stated closing rates. No annual average is inferred. Under IAS 21.39–41, closing assets and
liabilities use closing rates, income uses transaction-date rates, and the resulting translation
difference enters OCI (European Commission, 2023a).
With no foreign capital contributions or dividends in the period, a subsidiary whose opening
equity is E, after-tax profit is p, and opening, event and closing rates are r0, re, rc, has translation
difference
OCI = E(rc −r0) + p(rc −re).
(2.16)
To derive it, subtract translated opening equity Er0 and translated profit pre from translated
closing equity (E + p)rc. The first term revalues the opening net assets; the second reconciles
the two rates applied to this period’s retained profit. Thus B’s first translated profit is 8.8 and
OCI is 100(0.2) + 8(0.1) = 20.8. I’s translated profit is −4.4, with OCI 100(0.2) −4(0.1) = 19.6.
Together with P’s loss 0.8, group profit is 3.6 and OCI 40.4.
Only after those first-period results does P buy another 10% of B for USD 15.

2.8.3
Cash translation does not equal translation OCI
At the first close, I’s deposit 20 appears as 24 in translated cash and as 24 in B’s translated
deposits. The group eliminates both; neither legal book changes. Group cash is 485 + (253 +
150 −20)(1.2) = 944.6. Adding loan 66 and DTAs 1.4 gives assets 1012; liabilities are (200 +

then no loan or DTA.
