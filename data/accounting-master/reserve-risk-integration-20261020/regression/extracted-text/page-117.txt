The minus K prevents the applied advance from appearing both in the cash payment and in
the bill. Since CA −CL = R −T, the control
(A + C) + (G −K −C) + CA −CL = G + A −K + R −T = R
recovers the earned amount. This derivation uses a single contract, with 0 ≤K ≤min(A, G),
0 ≤C ≤G −K and 0 ≤R, T ≤100. It provides no authority to offset different contracts or to
ignore impairment, financing, tax, cancellation or refund terms.
Each event must carry its contract, identity, date, currency and admitted facts.
In this
restricted extension, advances precede all performance and billing; later payments settle identified
receivables. A revenue increment first releases an existing contract liability and then creates a
contract asset. For a new gross bill g that applies unused advance credit k, only g −k creates a
receivable. That amount releases the existing contract asset first; any remainder increases the
contract liability. The invoice consumes its identified credit even if the advance has already
been earned. A duplicate credit application, an overcollection or missing unconditional-right
evidence cannot pass by adjusting a balancing account.
Billing before performance gives the same final balances in the example but a different inter-
mediate history. The invoice creates a receivable of 50 and adds 50 to the existing contract
liability of 30; earning 60 then releases 60 of that liability. After the later collection of 20,
both paths have the balances above. Completing the service adds revenue 40, releasing the
remaining liability of 20 and creating a contract asset of 20. A final bill for 20 and collection of
the remaining 50 leave cash 100 and no contract or receivable balance. The actual sequence is
retained even where endpoint amounts agree.
A wholly prepaid invoice exposes another implementation requirement. If its gross amount
and applied advance are both 30, it creates no new monetary right and needs no accounting
lines. It still consumes an identified credit. The proposed authored method therefore declares
a state-only journal proposal: empty lines, zero account changes and explicit changes to the
invoice and advance allocations. Authorization checks that the net new right is zero; the same
atomic commit and replay rules protect the allocation from reuse. An empty journal cannot
carry a nonzero monetary effect. The native acquisition schema requires nonempty lines and
does not implement this case; an unavailable authored schema returns an unsupported-operation
result without changing state.
Take the wholly prepaid invoice in a synthetic book containing only cash 30 and a contract
liability of 30. No performance has occurred. Advance A1 has 30 unused; there is no invoice yet.
The new invoice I1 records gross billings of 30 and applies all of A1. Cumulative G and K both
rise from zero to 30, so T = G + A −K remains 30. Cash, revenue, receivables and contract
balances do not change. But A1 is now fully used and I1 is paid. Omitting this transition would
let a later invoice claim the same credit again.
