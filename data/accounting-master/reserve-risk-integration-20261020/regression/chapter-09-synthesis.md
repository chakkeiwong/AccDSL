# Chapter 9 — the histories behind operating balances

21 October 2026. Version 3.45, physical pages 113–176, read in order across
the recorded whole-document chain. This is provisional author evidence;
independent, domain and human acceptance remain pending.

The chapter makes the common execution procedure depend on substantive
accounting facts. Customer advances, performance obligations and returns
separate cash, revenue and recovery rights. Inventory and production methods
retain cost populations and completed effort. Equipment, restoration and
employee benefits require a history of components, obligations, measurement
changes and their profit or OCI origins. Later events cannot be computed
reliably from a single closing balance.

The numerical cases generally earn their added detail by exposing a concrete
failure. A production cash bridge fails if depreciation and stock adjustments
are counted on inconsistent populations. An impairment cap cannot supply an
allocation weight. A shorter useful life can follow genuinely new information
or reveal an old transcription error; the two histories require different
depreciation, tax and equity movements. The correction is posted at discovery
while reports preserve their original publication and availability dates.

The Apple cases extend that argument from supplied facts to real disclosures.
Gross PPE, accumulated depreciation and cash payments reject one proposed
asset history without identifying its replacement. Two locally feasible asset
books require different company remainders. Their totals can be made equal
under further assumptions, but the detailed cash-flow rows, tax consequences,
notes and later settlement dates remain independent constraints. The tax case
similarly retains unexplained movements rather than assigning them to invented
currency, equity or cash transactions.

The standards comparison then asks whether the proposed method is applicable.
Historical source support, operative authority, enterprise scope and adoption
elections remain separate. ASBE lease and deferred-tax adoption have different
clocks, and tax transition includes comparative effects after the opening
entry. Ping An's report demonstrates why an adoption balance, a later carrying
amount and a maturity table answer different questions. A dash does not prove
no prior contract; an asset/liability difference does not identify an equity
adjustment; a maturity-minus-carrying difference does not identify interest.
