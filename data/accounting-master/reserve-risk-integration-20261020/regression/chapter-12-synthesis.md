Premium cash arrives on a different schedule from service and profit. That
opening distinction grows into a complete accounting argument: future service,
incurred claims, risk adjustment, unearned margin and loss components each
retain their own movement. The zero-rate and nonzero-rate examples establish
that finance changes the amount later released. Connected issued groups and
reinsurance then show why company aggregation must preserve separate group
measurements. Equal final cash cannot validate initial recognition, pooling
or interim loss allocation. A loss component is already within a liability
or held asset; adding it again can produce a balanced but wrong journal.

The event-publication section carries this financial dependence into software.
Finance, service, incurrence and settlement have different intermediate
balances. Group measurements, journals and component deltas must agree before
publication. Exact retries return the original receipt without replaying the
financial effect or replacing the current head with old state. An absent
acknowledgement leaves commit status unknown. These are specified observable
behaviors, with actual transaction isolation and durability still untested.

PAA eligibility, acquisition-cost treatment, premium finance and claim
settlement use different clocks. A one-year policy can leave a discounted
claim payable much later. The LIC finance allocation can use the claim's
incurrence rate rather than group inception. Reinsurance needs its own
coverage, entry date, collectability and risk adjustment. Direct participation
also needs inception findings; a minimum payment can coexist with it.

The guarantee example supplies the important bridge from contractual payoff
to valuation. Its two-state replicating portfolio derives the price, while a
different physical law governs forecast frequencies and mortality. The
replicating portfolio is not silently treated as the insurer's actual hedge.
Complete conditional outcomes then carry that valuation through service,
investment repayment and claims. Later variable-fee examples distinguish
future-service changes, margin exhaustion, recovery and qualified mitigation.
Partial treaties apply recovery percentages only after covered losses have
been identified. Changed legal terms require a different analysis from
changed estimates; equivalent replacement premium is not a second cash receipt.

Finance presentation preserves another kind of history. A revised benefit can
require a new remaining-duration allocation, while a holdings change leaves an
old OCI schedule under frozen assumptions. New actual market values cannot
rewrite that schedule. Derecognition depends on the origin of OCI. The
homogeneous examples do not settle early termination during a mixed-origin
policy-change runoff; that source question remains open.

The claims and reserving material makes the probability problem explicit.
Identical single-date distributions can imply different nonlinear treaty
recoveries. A point reserve does not identify its estimation uncertainty, and
repeated fitted means do not supply future payment variation. The bootstrap
discussion preserves shared-fit dependence, process-law choice, failed draws,
sparse-data limits, measurement horizon and conditioning information. Its
backward-law counterexample and reserve-centering derivation explain precisely
why convenient substitutions can compute a different target. The inspected
source versions and unseen software remain distinct.

The newly added risk-measure section fits that argument: even a justified

The reserving exchange preserves exactly the distinctions those methods need.
A zero payment, structural absence and failed cell cannot share one coerced
value. Complete joint draws retain one fit and an explicit law. Failed or
unattempted work leaves the requested full result unresolved. A seed is not a
durable checkpoint, and computational completion is not probability admission.

Acquisition and transition cases return these methods to dated company
accounts. Two recoverability tests deduct overlapping losses once. Asset

Prescribed nil treatments do not erase known payments. Group fair value,
fulfilment cash flows and investment holdings measure different quantities.
The fair-value transition calculation must be applied by admitted group
before aggregation, and a recoverable loss component does not value the whole
held contract.
