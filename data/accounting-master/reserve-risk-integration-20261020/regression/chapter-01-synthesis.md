# Chapter 1 — why contract uncertainty must survive

Version 3.45, physical pages 10–12. This synthesis uses the five specific
judgments in segment-007-012.json, already recorded after actual viewing; it
adds no new page credit. Independent and human acceptance remain pending.

The chapter makes the product question financial before making it technical.
Two loans can produce the same historical interest while responding differently
to a rate reset. The fraction of fixed-rate principal therefore belongs to the
economic history even when the historical journal cannot identify it. The
interest expression then extends the two endpoints to an entire feasible
family, without giving that family a probability measure.

That example earns the subsequent requirements: source and temporal meaning,
joint histories, economic responses, accounting consequences and reproducible
results must remain connected. The first demonstrator is deliberately bounded
but still has three dates, a revision and complete affected statements. A small
balanced journal alone would not answer the question that opens the chapter.

The sequence is necessary. Architecture before the borrowing example would
ask the reader to accept machinery before understanding its purpose. The
worked company is now the right next step: it must demonstrate the full chain
and show which facts are given, recovered, assumed or still unknown.

No new confirmed defect appears in the recorded chapter judgments. The evidence
supports local author assessment of the argument and the stated arithmetic,
not product implementation, forecast validity or human comprehension.
