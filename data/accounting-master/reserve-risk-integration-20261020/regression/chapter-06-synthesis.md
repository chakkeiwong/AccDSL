# Chapter 6 — histories, laws and computational meaning

Version 3.45, physical pages 84–96, inspected in consecutive order. This is
author review; independent, domain and human acceptance remain pending.

The chapter supplies the mathematical meaning promised by the architecture.
Context-specific observations constrain a declared family of histories. Its
payable example shows why an empty intersection of rows cannot erase a
liability known to exist, while the nullspace calculation explains why
aggregate statements leave movements unresolved. Unknown contract terms add
another source of ambiguity even when movements are identified.

Balanced posting conserves a signed ledger total. The separate recognition,
reporting and economic-state requirements prevent that proof from becoming a
claim of accounting correctness. The forward sequence then separates behavior,
events, accounting and state transition. A conditional future law belongs to
each history; a mixture requires an additional justified measure. The variance
decomposition is derived under that measure, and uncertain weights receive
their own probability bounds.

The practical qualifications carry real consequences. The rounded-debt upper
bound is not attainable. Equal cent displays can hide different input domains;
equal terminal cash can hide different treaty or commitment state. An
unresolved branch retains its probability. The positive-payment derivative
justifies a unique bracketed rate only in its stated domain. The Gaussian
example supplies an exact posterior while exposing repeated information as a
different target. Finally, conditioning and intervention produce different
answers in the same elementary structural model.

All displays were legible. No new confirmed defect was found in this chapter.
The relational and inference citations retain explicit conditions and limits;
this reading does not constitute a new technical audit of all cited papers.
Continuous numerical methods, real accounting authority and implementation
conformance remain separate obligations. The next chapter must specify the
construction and forecasting procedures that preserve these definitions.
