# Chapter 11 — bank rights, measurement and settlement

21 October 2026. Version 3.45, physical pages 244–277, recorded in the
continuous whole-document chain. All chapter nodes have author judgments.
This synthesis draws on those recorded readings, including the supplemental
correction extending the debt subsection through page 251. Independent,
domain and human acceptance remain pending.

The chapter connects a bank's legal entities, contractual rights, accounting,
prudential capital and settlement liquidity. Equal reported totals do not
make the underlying rights or histories interchangeable. The JPMorgan examples
distinguish reported relationships from a complete control graph, parent income
from cash, consolidated restricted cash from parent resources, and permission
to pay a dividend from a completed transfer. Publication, adoption, declaration
and payment dates belong to different decisions. Reported precision and a
proposed amendment cannot supply missing facts.

Debt adds another set of distinct quantities and events: carrying value,
principal, guarantees, payment, default notice, remedies, amendments and
successor rights. The chapter preserves the limits of the inspected sources;
an original indenture and its supplements remain uninspected where stated.
Loan origination can create a deposit before any external cash settlement.
A commitment, expected draw, allowance and carrying amount therefore need
their own populations. Regulatory capital is a separate assessment: the gross
exposure base precedes the allowance cap and excess deduction, average assets
need evidence beyond closing assets, and a capital ratio supplies no cash for
settlement.

Classification comes before amortised-cost arithmetic. Two assets can deliver
the same disposal proceeds while producing different interim asset, profit
and OCI paths; equity FVOCI cannot inherit debt FVOCI treatment. Expected
credit losses measure discounted expected cash shortfalls. The twelve-month
horizon selects default events, rather than cutting off recovery cash at the
end of twelve months. Funding a commitment conditions the probabilities on
the observed draw while retaining the original credit baseline and the
supported SICR horizon. Keeping the old draw probability or restarting that
horizon can produce the same wrong allowance through different mistakes.

The ordinary and POCI schedules distinguish initial priced loss, later signed
allowances, interest unwind, credit revisions and write-off. A favorable POCI
revision can produce a negative allowance without creating a bank liability.
Write-off requires a separate no-recovery finding and does not forgive the
legal debt. Fractional dates and an irrational original rate then expose a
computational issue: certified bounds must follow one shared rate through the
whole loan. Their endpoints are not freely combinable economic histories or
independent journal amounts.

Modified terms, an impairment cure and reversal of SICR require different
findings even when eventual wealth agrees. Collateral received after maturity
still belongs in expected losses, with enforcement proceeds counted once.
Replenished revolving capacity does not reset credit history. Integral fees
change yield while service fees follow the service. These examples extend the
history argument by showing which state must survive a superficially fresh
transaction.

Transfers require identification of eligible rights before relative fair
values allocate carrying amounts. Retained risks may create borrowing instead
of a sale. Continuing guarantees, calls, puts and collars preserve different
exposures. The recognised asset and associated liability already represent
the option; adding another option duplicates the right or obligation. Under
amortised-cost continuing involvement, the asset and liability can require
different rates.

The final facility case returns these measurement distinctions to cash.
Capacity of 8, a request for 8 and confirmed receipts of 8, 6 or 0 are
different states. Unpaid deposits stay within deposit liabilities rather than
being added again. A lost acknowledgement cannot establish nonreceipt. The
chapter consequently prepares the reader to ask separately what happened,
what is known, which rights remain, and how the applicable measurement uses
those facts. Insurance service, finance and claims follow next.

No new manuscript defect was confirmed in this chapter's recorded reading.
The subsection-span correction repairs review evidence, not the manuscript.
Actual calibration, applicable authority, contract and tax facts, general
allocations and company-wide integration remain open. The chapter's many
counterexamples are consequential, but their pacing and comprehension have
not received independent or human acceptance. No whole requirement closes.
