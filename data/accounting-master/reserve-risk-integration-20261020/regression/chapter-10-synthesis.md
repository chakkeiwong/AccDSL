# Chapter 10 — financing rights and the reporting group

21 October 2026. Version 3.45, physical pages 177–243, read in the continuous
whole-document chain. All chapter nodes have explicit author judgments. This
is provisional author evidence, with independent, domain and human acceptance
pending.

The chapter extends the operating-company history to claims on the company and
to the boundary of the reporting group. Liability or equity classification,
compound instruments, award conditions and ownership rights determine which
obligations and earnings belong to whom. The earlier company and Apple cases
keep synthetic accounting illustrations separate from actual contract terms;
a later agreement does not prove that an earlier award was amended.

The financing calculations repeatedly show why a closing amount is inadequate.
An inducement has an amendment date distinct from an offer or share delivery.
A purchased derivative has an acquisition value that is not a hedge gain.
Adding a designated item layer preserves its own price origin, while reducing
an instrument leaves earlier gains intact and changes only future attribution.
Stopping a purchase portion introduces another distinction: its reserve can
remain after hedge accounting stops, depending on expectation and recoverability.

The option and inventory cases carry these distinctions into complete
transactions. Actual and hypothetical options must measure their own strikes.
A matched zero-cost collar can have signed interim reserves and two gross
cash flows. A reserve enters inventory basis before goods are sold; later
write-down reversals apply only to goods still held. Some wrong methods give
the correct final profit after full disposal, so intermediate inventory,
reserve and earnings states are essential evidence. The examples state the
valuation and qualification assumptions on which their arithmetic depends.

The EPS discussion likewise preserves the rights behind the numerator. Senior
participation and caps mean assumed shares can change ordinary earnings as
well as the denominator. The restricted ordering proof requires independent,
complete gross earnings effects. Joint bonus and tax consequences violate
that premise; their conditional worlds are computed, but admissible final
selection remains unresolved. Participating-dividend tax benefits can affect
EPS without creating an actual dividend, tax asset or journal.

The foreign-subsidiary case joins acquisition, goodwill, translation, NCI and
disposal. Translation of cash measures a different population from total OCI.
Losing control removes the entire subsidiary even if an investment remains,
and parent and NCI currency reserves have different treatment. The implementation
section then gives these distinctions exact book owners, branch origins,
currency units and dependency views. A consolidation removal cannot become a
fictional payment in the parent's legal book. Atomic publication and retries
are substantive software requirements in this part of the design.

The closing group methods explain why an eliminated loan can retain currency
exposure, why matching balances need an identified transaction, and why
hyperinflation restatement precedes translation. Exchangeability is assessed
for a dated purpose under the inspected amendment; a quoted rate or a numeric
consequence does not prove that the estimate meets the required objective.

No additional defect was confirmed in this chapter's author reading. This
does not establish every imported provision, actual-company treatment or
general method. Nonadditive EPS selection, heterogeneous or unmatched hedge
attribution, actual valuations, applicable authority and successor-profile
integration remain open where stated. The chapter is long and dense, but its
local counterexamples provide distinct consequences rather than interchangeable
summaries. Human pacing and comprehension remain untested. The next chapter
applies the history and measurement discipline to banks and credit losses.
