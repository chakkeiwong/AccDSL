# Conditions for interpreting the archive evidence

Question: exact complete evidence retention with ordered relationships and atomic
bundle publication. The comparator is the original supplied JSON text and the
unchanged P07 transaction store, not an expected state generated by the archive.
Native propose/apply provides the separate semantic comparator in the forged
balanced-proposal case. Archiving that proposal is allowed; claiming it is valid
would be wrong, and the explicit output boundary prevents that inference.

Primary criteria are byte equality, exact ordered descriptors and dependency
edges, complete receipt visibility, and original semantic refusal where required.
Test counts, timing, SQL integrity alone and content hashes alone are explanatory.
Tampering, half-published groups, rewritten payloads or false admission veto the
reference. Failed probes trigger design repair; protected-input changes stop
continuation. No scientific/default ranking is being made.

Inherited choices: P07 one-writer SQLite FULL/DELETE settings, immutable nodes
and hash identities. They are baseline primitives, not universal defaults.
The additional exact-byte envelope is justified by observed Unicode/order
aliases and float refusal. Explicit caller limits bound the reference; no
convenient hidden recommended size is installed. Hard-link publication addresses
the observed initialization boundary without altering P07. Its platform scope
and orphan-staging behavior are retained as limitations.

Pre-recording review found an important integrity detail: P07 add_node returns
an existing matching identity without rereading every stored payload. The archive
now performs full receipt read verification inside its transaction, and checks
the marker before writing. Adverse cases corrupt an existing payload, retry,
and require refusal with no open transaction. They also check changed receipts,
dependency edges, unknown versions, unrelated stores and reduced read limits.

Failure injection includes exceptions after each record, subprocess exit before
and after group commit, and before and after initial publication. These are local
process-recovery checks. They cannot establish behavior under power loss, disk
corruption, hostile modification, concurrent writers or an untested filesystem.
The existing three P07 recovery/retry/migration methods retain their original
older-engine scope; they do not turn this archive into that engine's new adapter.
