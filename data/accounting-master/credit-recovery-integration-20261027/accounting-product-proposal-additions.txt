This document specifies a system that translates company disclosures into traceable evidence,
represents several compatible economic and accounting histories, and simulates their future
journals and financial statements under explicit economic scenarios.
The proposed data contracts, methods and acceptance cases specify the intended implementation.
Current source and domain limitations remain explicit; this candidate is not a completed product
or a claim of full proposal readiness. Proposed libraries can be replaced while preserving those
contracts. Sector rule packages require their own accounting review before they can be described
as compliant with a reporting standard.
Research materials, source qualifications and review records accompany this document in
docs/design/research and docs/design/review.

What a later recovery would require
A receipt after financial write-off raises a new accounting question. The original EU source used
by the retained examples treats write-off as a derecognition event (IFRS 9, paragraph 5.4.4), while
its disclosure requirements explicitly contemplate written-off assets still subject to enforcement
(IFRS 7, paragraphs 35F(e) and 35L). These provisions explain why the history of a removed
portion should remain available. They do not determine the legal position of a particular
borrower or supply evidence of a later receipt.
The distinction also matters when choosing a recognition rule. The IFRS Interpretations
Committee’s March 2019 decision on curing a credit-impaired financial asset addresses the
adjustment of a recognised loss allowance, including discount unwinding.
It requires the
difference considered in that request to be presented as a reversal of impairment losses rather
than interest revenue. Applying that answer to cash received after full write-off would require
a further argument: the checked decision does not explicitly address that fact pattern. The
retained purchased or originated credit-impaired cases also need their own applicability analysis.
Exact inspected passages, source versions and unresolved questions are retained in data/
accounting-master/credit-recovery-scope-20261027/source-map.md.
Before a later event can affect the book, the model needs the receipt’s amount, currency,
transaction and information dates, source availability and allocation.
An allocation to a
previously written-off portion must identify that portion; an allocation to a different supported
claim must preserve that distinction. The applicable recognition and presentation treatment
remains a separate proposal. Cash-flow classification also depends on the entity and the nature
of the receipt, so a synthetic operating or investing label cannot answer it.
The first intake therefore records missing evidence explicitly. For each of the nine closed histories,
it retains the complete dated nominal record and leaves the new receipt and its allocation
unknown. Zero-valued historical components remain visible; an unknown new receipt is not
replaced by zero. Actual prior collection identities submitted as new receipts are rejected
because those collections are already represented. The checked intake has no financial effects
and accepts no positive recovery in this version. Its 50 adverse controls test preservation
and refusal boundaries, not recognition of an actual recovery. The reproducible result is in
data/accounting-master/credit-recovery-intake-20261027/result.md.
This limited interface makes the remaining work concrete. A positive recovery method needs
authentic receipt evidence, a supported allocation and a justified post-derecognition treatment.
Those additions must preserve the original write-off finding and financial history, rather than
retrospectively rewriting them to match a later outcome.
