17.20.3
Resume the original question and preserve its proofs
The checkpoint binds the complete source request, actor, trust declarations, timing assumptions,
method identities and execution allowance. On reading it, a separate checker reconstructs each
branch’s constraints from the original domain and its path. It does not accept a saved mutable
constraint list as a substitute. It verifies all completed witnesses or contradictions, the exact
complement structure, source outcomes, support paths and work counts.
The reference uses a fixed left-first depth-first order. This makes the resumed tree directly
comparable with the existing uninterrupted calculation. It also allows the checker to reject a
purported completed branch placed after an earlier unfinished one. The order is a comparison
convention, not a claim that this schedule is the fastest way to find useful financial conclusions.
A step allowance specifies how much new work one call may perform. A separately bound total
execution cap determines when further work must stop. Reaching that cap retains the pending
tree and reports a capped calculation; it does not turn unresolved cells into completed results.
The test with a cap of two preserves the same original question and performs no repeated
solver work when resumed at that cap. Changing the cap or the source assumptions requires an
explicitly different, checked request to the continuation interface.
Across the 47 selected temporal requests, step allowances of one, two, seven and 4,096 produce
exactly the same final proof trees, outcomes and source supports as uninterrupted search. Earlier
certificates remain unchanged at every pause. A separate reread checks 1,060 saved checkpoints
and rejects altered partition cells, proofs, source premises and completion declarations. These
comparisons establish the selected finite continuation property.10
This representation avoids a separate explicit node for every real-valued event-time assignment.
It does not remove exponential growth in the number of distinguishable revision combinations.
Nor does installing all children before a logical pause establish crash-safe file storage, authenti-
cated evidence or actual actor knowledge. Larger revision families, better schedules, justified
approximate reduction and durable storage require their own arguments and comparisons. The
useful advance here is narrower: unfinished computation retains both what has been proved
and every domain that has yet to be checked.
17.21
Fixing the policy question before the search finishes
A manager who may pay an obligation or draw on a facility needs a rule for acting on the
information available at each decision.
The preceding search can establish some possible
information histories while leaving others unexplored. To compare policies during that search,
their meaning must remain fixed. If each newly discovered history changes what a policy is
allowed to observe, an apparent reduction in the feasible set may instead reflect a change in the
question.
The first requirement is therefore a declaration of possible messages before temporal search.

In the finite example, start with the original registry of financial assertions and observation

those financial and observation assertions conservatively. Some combinations may later prove

The declaration binds the manager, decision dates, source records and meanings of pay and

messages to the manager. A retraction supplies no replacement assertion payload, but it does
not justify removing every message associated with the original assertion before its timing has

every listed message.
17.21.1
A fixed domain makes partial restrictions comparable
Let U denote the declared finite set of decision keys, including the decision stage and the
required own-action memory. A total rule assigns either pay or draw to every key in U. Let ΠU
be the set of these rules. Assignments to keys that are never reached still belong to a total rule;
they have no effect on that rule’s realised path. Consequently, several total rules can represent
the same policy on the reachable histories.
At search stage t, let Ht contain only the selected financial and information histories supported
by completed temporal branches. For a history h, write Vh(π) when following rule π selects
no refused action in that history. This condition uses the complete finite action rows for that
selected history, including first-decision refusal and second-decision refusal after the first action.
The rules surviving the known restrictions are
Ct = {π ∈ΠU : Vh(π) for every h ∈Ht}.
(17.33)
These are sets of decision rules, not monetary balances or probability distributions. The
relation is a local definition under a fixed message declaration and the supplied action semantics.
Unresolved source outcomes are retained separately; they cannot supply a selected history or
establish its financial constraints.
Resuming the same checked search retains the previously completed histories. Thus Ht ⊆Ht+1.
A rule satisfying every condition for the larger set also satisfies every condition for its subset, so
Ct+1 ⊆Ct.
(17.34)
This inclusion explains the asymmetric conclusions. Once known constraints eliminate every
rule, adding more histories cannot restore a rule under the same declaration. If some rules
survive, a pending branch may still eliminate them. In the coarse/refined correction example,
the known constraints already eliminate all rules after four of the 25 temporal nodes have been
resolved. The baseline instead retains four total rules and establishes conditional feasibility only
when the search completes with selected source outcomes throughout.
17.21.2
Three reasons that a surviving rule may still be unresolved
Unfinished search, unresolved source meaning and an inadequate domain allowance are different
limitations. A two-node search cap retains 15 pending branches in the baseline. Those branches
have not all been shown to contain possible histories, but they must remain represented until
checked. The surviving rules therefore remain candidates.

A search can also finish while a required assertion remains unavailable, retracted or in conflict
with another head. Unknown access leaves four rules surviving the known constraints, yet
one source outcome remains unresolved. Retraction and conflicting-head cases likewise do not
become feasible merely because all temporal branches have been processed. An inconsistent
clock system is different again: it admits no temporal assignment. The 512 unconstrained rule
assignments in that example do not establish a feasible policy for a possible company history.
Finally, the conservative domain may exceed the stated computational allowance. The finite
profile permits at most 12 binary decision keys, hence at most 4,096 total rules. Renaming only
the low message in one correction produces 12 keys and eight surviving total rules. Renaming
all correction messages requires 18 keys, so the reference retains the original request and returns
an unresolved domain budget without truncating the keys or enumerating a preferred subset.
The extra rule count in the smaller example is not evidence of better information or better
economic performance.
The checked partial calculation binds each result to the original request, declaration, domain,
methods and temporal frontier. It retains the supporting branches, unresolved source outcomes
and pending temporal domains. Changing a message, source, actor or domain cannot reuse the
old result as the answer to the new question. A future extension rule may justify some reuse,
but that relation must itself be stated and checked.11
This construction joins a fixed decision problem to an unfinished search without treating
uncertainty as missing arithmetic. Longer horizons, additional actions, changing declarations
and broader observation languages still require their own comparisons.

26.26
Separating consideration from the other parts of an ex-
change
Obtaining control through a debt exchange raises a question that the separate debtor and
creditor worksheets cannot answer alone: which amounts belong to the acquisition of the
business? A transaction may also settle an existing relationship or pay for services. Before
measuring goodwill, the company must distinguish consideration for the acquired business from
effects accounted for separately. The acquisition guidance requires that separation and discusses
the reasons for a transaction, who initiated it and its timing; no one indicator by itself settles
the classification (Australian Accounting Standards Board, 2026h, paras. 51–53 and B50–B53).
Consider an explicitly supplied example in one currency at one measurement date.
Four
identified components are assigned values of 80 for acquisition consideration, 20 for separate
debt settlement, 5 for acquisition costs and 3 for another separately accounted transaction.
Their inventory sums to 108, while the declared consideration bucket contains only 80. The
difference is not a residual to be forced into goodwill. Nor does this example infer the 20
settlement amount from a loan’s face or carrying value: both the amount and its assigned
purpose are supplied premises.
The finite calculation gives each component a distinct identity and retains its source, availability
date, amount and assigned accounting purpose. It requires an explicit assertion that the
supplied inventory is complete for the question. Reordering the four components leaves the same
records and sums. An unknown settlement amount is not zero; missing allocation, coverage or
source-timing premises leave the requested totals unresolved. Relabelling debt as a previously
held equity interest or as acquisition consideration cannot establish the missing accounting
premise.
These checks establish arithmetic conditional on the supplied partition. They do not determine
the appropriate measured allocation for the intended conversion-to-control transaction. The
inspected acquisition paragraphs explain separation and settlement of pre-existing relationships,
but do not by themselves establish that loan-specific allocation. The remaining work is to
support the relationship treatment, transaction assignments and measurement basis before
constructing goodwill and acquired-basis statements.11
