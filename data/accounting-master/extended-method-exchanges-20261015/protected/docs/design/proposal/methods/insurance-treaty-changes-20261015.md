# Partial reinsurance and changes in insurance estimates

The following methods use IFRS 17 in the retained September 2023 EU
compilation. They extend the preceding nonparticipating general-measurement
examples. Contract rights, group assignments, recovery percentages, risk
adjustments and discount rates are explicit conditional inputs. A change in
one of these inputs must retain its cause: changed service expectations,
financial risk, counterparty performance and a new legal agreement do not
have interchangeable accounting consequences.

## Only the covered contracts support loss recovery

IFRS 17.62–66B keeps a held treaty separate from the issued contracts, with
its own entry, coverage and recognition dates. Proportionate coverage can
delay recognition until an underlying contract is recognised. B119C permits
the initial onerous-loss recovery only if the treaty was entered into before
or at the same time as the underlying onerous contracts were recognised.
The record must distinguish these dates; the treaty's later cash payment
does not identify when it was entered into.

When a treaty covers only some contracts within an onerous issued group,
B119E requires a systematic and rational allocation of the group's loss to
the covered contracts. B119D then multiplies that covered loss by the
percentage of their claims expected to be recovered. A treaty's headline
quota percentage must not be applied to uncovered losses. Preserve the
contract-to-issued-group and contract-to-treaty joins, covered risks and
periods, attachment points, limits and exclusions, the loss-allocation method,
expected claims/recoveries and the separate transferred-risk adjustment.
For excess-of-loss or capped cover, expected recoveries require the admitted
claim distribution and treaty payoff; a constant quota share cannot be used
without support.

Take an issued group with premium 100, expected claims 118 and risk adjustment
2, all measured with zero discounting at recognition. Initial loss is 20 and
CSM is zero. A source-supported allocation in this synthetic contract set
identifies a covered portion with premium 41, claims 48 and risk adjustment
1, giving loss 8; the uncovered portion has premium 59, claims 70 and risk
adjustment 1, giving loss 12. These amounts sum to the group; they are not
separate groups created to improve the result.

A treaty entered into on time covers half of the covered contracts' claims.
Expected recovery is 24, transferred-risk adjustment is a separately supplied
0.5, and premium paid is 26.5. After payment, the ordinary fulfilment asset is
24.5 and cost CSM is 2. Loss recovery is

$$8\times 50\%=4,$$

so cost CSM rises to 6 and the measured held asset to 30.5. The loss-recovery
component of 4 sits inside that asset; it is not another receivable. Starting
from cash and equity 50, receive issued premiums 100 and pay treaty premium
26.5. Cash 123.5 plus held asset 30.5 equals issued liability 120 plus equity
34. Applying 50% to the entire loss would recognise 10 and overstate equity
by 6. If this same treaty had been entered into only after the underlying
recognition, the initial loss-recovery adjustment would not apply.

## A changed loss estimate affects both groups without netting them

Before any service or time passes, reduce expected claims on the covered
contracts from 48 to 44, leaving other assumptions unchanged. Issued
fulfilment liability falls from 120 to 116. Paragraph 50(b) first reduces
the loss component, from 20 to 16, and recognises a loss reversal of 4;
there is no new issued CSM while that loss component remains.

Expected held recovery falls from 24 to 22. Under paragraph 66(c)(i), this
change follows the underlying change that did not adjust the issued CSM,
so it reduces the held asset and creates expense 2 rather than being hidden
in held CSM. Held fulfilment asset becomes 22.5, cost CSM stays 6 and total
held asset becomes 28.5. The covered loss is now 4, so B119F's recoverable
loss-component limit is 2 and the loss-recovery component falls from 4 to 2.
That component change tracks the same recognised recovery loss; posting a
second expense for it would double count the event. Equity becomes
$123.5+28.5-116=36$, an increase of $4-2=2$.

If all remaining services are then provided and claims equal these revised
estimates, issued liability 116 is released through insurance revenue 100
and loss reversal 16; claims expense and payment are 114. Issued service-period
profit is 2. Held coverage asset 28.5 is released as premium allocation 26.5
and reversal of loss recovery 2, with incurred recovery 22 and its collection
recorded separately. Held service-period loss is 6.5. The entity ends with
cash and equity $123.5-114+22=31.5$ and no group balances. Initial combined
loss 16, estimate-change gain 2 and service-period loss 4.5 sum to lifetime
loss 18.5, equal to the change from opening equity 50. Reinsurance has not
removed or netted away the issued obligation.

B119F requires later loss-recovery components to reflect changes in the
underlying loss component and never exceed the portion expected to be
recovered. Track separately adjustments already included in fulfilment-cash-flow
changes and reversals under 66(bb) that are not. The cap does not authorise
inventing recovery income. Under paragraph 67, a change in reinsurer
non-performance risk does not adjust CSM as future service; preserve collateral
and dispute assumptions required by paragraph 63. Transferred-risk adjustment
must be measured under paragraph 64, not mechanically copied as a fraction
of the issued adjustment.

## Changed terms and changed estimates are different events

For a treaty change, retain the old and new legal terms, effective date,
covered-contract links and consideration. An exercise of a right already
contained in the contract is not a modification under paragraph 72. A new
treaty requires its own recognition and grouping decision and cannot be
backdated to create initial-loss recovery. For a modification, apply paragraph
72's tests to the actual changed contract: changed scope or separated
components, a substantially different boundary, a different initial group,
changed direct-participation status, or loss of eligibility for a previously
used premium-allocation approach can require derecognition and a new contract.
If none applies, paragraph 73 treats the resulting cash-flow changes as
estimate changes, with the held-contract modifications in paragraphs 63–68
where applicable.

For required derecognition, paragraphs 76–77 remove the affected cash flows
and risk adjustment, adjust the remaining group's CSM under the applicable
model and revise remaining coverage units. A transfer or qualifying
modification uses paragraph 77's actual transfer consideration or equivalent
new-contract premium adjustment rather than simply deleting the old balance.
The equivalent premium is a required valuation input, not the old carrying
amount chosen for convenience. Missing terms or that valuation leaves the
particular modification unresolved. The generic tests identify the required
procedure; they do not price an unspecified changed treaty.

## Future service consumes CSM before creating a loss

For issued contracts without direct participation, paragraph 44 and B96–B97
distinguish future nonfinancial service changes from financial changes and
incurred-claim experience. Use initial-recognition discount rates for the
specified CSM adjustments under B72(c), while fulfilment cash flows use
current rates. Changes in current rates, financial risk and incurred-claim
estimates do not enter CSM by simply labelling them future cash flows.
Preserve the current and initial-rate valuations and the reason for each
change; their difference is part of the finance analysis, not an unrecorded
balancing amount.

To isolate the nonfinancial rule, assume no passage of time, zero rates,
no new contracts and no service release. A group has fulfilment liability
80, CSM 20 and total liability 100. An adverse future-service estimate of
15 raises fulfilment liability to 95 and reduces CSM to 5, with no immediate
profit effect. A further adverse change of 10 raises fulfilment liability to
105, exhausts CSM and creates loss 5 and a loss component of 5. A subsequent
favourable change of 8 first reverses that loss 5; only the remaining 3 restores
CSM. Closing fulfilment liability 97 plus CSM 3 is 100. Crediting all 8 to
profit would overstate the reversal by 3. The loss component is an internal
allocation of the liability, never an additional liability of 5.

## Finance presentation preserves total income and expense

For the nonparticipating case in paragraphs 87–90, a portfolio-level policy
can present insurance finance entirely in profit or disaggregate it using
the required systematic allocation. Under B129–B131, the choice and the
contract characteristics determine the method. If financial assumptions do
not substantially affect the nominal amounts paid, the systematic allocation
uses the initial-recognition rates specified by B72(e)(i). OCI is the difference
between that allocation and total finance, not a discretionary smoothing
reserve. Over the group's duration it totals zero under B130.

Consider a GMM group with a now-incurred, fixed claim of 121 payable in two
years, no remaining coverage or CSM, and zero risk adjustment because no
nonfinancial uncertainty remains in this illustrative obligation. Initial and
current discount rates are 10%, so liability is $121/1.1^2=100$. The next
year's current rate falls to zero; one year remains, and current liability
becomes 121. Total first-year finance expense is 21. Under the admitted OCI
policy, the initial-rate allocation is 10 in profit and the difference 11
in OCI. In the second year, with rate zero unchanged, current liability stays
121, so total finance is zero. The initial-rate allocation accretes the
alternative carrying amount 110 to 121, giving profit expense 11 and OCI
income 11. Paying 121 extinguishes the liability. Cumulative profit finance
is 21 and OCI is zero.

With opening cash 150, liability 100 and equity 50, first-year equity falls
to 29 and stays 29 in the second year because profit expense and OCI income
offset. Final cash and equity are 29. Presenting all finance in profit gives
expenses 21 and zero instead, with the same total equity and cash. The OCI
choice changes presentation, not the contractual claim or current measurement.
This is the B131 fixed-payment case; variable-payment B132, VFA/underlying-item
B134–B136, PAA and actual curve/RA/process valuation retain their separate
methods. A successful fixed-claim example does not establish those models.
