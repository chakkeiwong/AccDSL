# Revolving capacity, loan fees and the rights retained after transfer

These conditional methods extend the preceding full-draw example using the
retained historical AASB9 and AASB7 compilations for explicit calendar-2024/2025
Australian for-profit Tier 1 cases. Legal rights, initial values and credit
loss estimates are supplied assumptions. None is inferred from a facility
label or from the fact that a receipt reached the bank's account.

## A repayment can restore capacity without erasing credit history

A facility has both a funded balance and a right to request more funding.
Record the legal limit, draw requests and actual settlements separately,
together with the replenishment rule, expiry or cancellation conditions,
currency, original credit-risk comparison date and current credit assessment.
If the facility permits repeated drawings, paying down one loan may restore
capacity. It does not establish a new low-risk borrower or reset the original
credit history. Determine the resulting loan's classification and initial
measurement from its business model, cash-flow terms and value, rather than
assuming every funded amount qualifies for amortised cost.

For a partial draw, remeasure funded and remaining undrawn losses using the
information available after the draw. Do not allocate the old provision by the
percentage drawn unless the admitted loss model actually implies that result.
AASB9.5.5.6 and B5.5.47 preserve the commitment's impairment history. Where the
two losses can be identified separately, AASB7.B8E presents the loan allowance
and commitment provision separately. Where they cannot, the combined loss
is recognised against the funded asset, with any excess over its gross carrying
amount presented as a provision. Inseparability is a measurement fact, not an
election to conceal a missing exposure model.

Start with cash 200, deposits 180 and equity 20. A revolving limit of 100 has
an admitted undrawn provision of 2. No interest accrues between the following
ordered events. The customer draws 40 externally; cash becomes 160 and gross
loan 40. Conditional on the draw, supplied funded ECL is 3 and remaining
undrawn ECL is 1.5. Debit impairment expense 2.5 and the old provision 0.5,
and credit allowance 3. The result is allowance 3, provision 1.5 and equity
15.5. Cash 160 plus net loan 37 equals deposits 180, provision 1.5 and
equity 15.5. The undrawn capacity is 60; it is not an asset of 60.

The borrower repays 10 externally. The contract restores that capacity, so
cash is 170, loan 30 and available capacity 70. New supplied ECL values are
funded 2.4 and undrawn 1.8. Reduce allowance by 0.6, increase provision by
0.3 and recognise a net impairment reversal of 0.3. Then a new draw of 20
gives cash 150, loan 50 and capacity 50. Supplied funded and undrawn ECL of
4 and 1.2 require allowance increase 1.6, provision reduction 0.6 and expense
1. Total loss balances are 5.2, cumulative impairment is 5.2 and equity is
14.8. Cash 150 plus net loan 46 equals deposits 180 plus provision 1.2 plus
equity 14.8. Reusing the first funded estimate or treating the second draw
as a new pristine commitment would compute a different exposure.

For a separate inseparable-loss example, gross funded loan 4 and combined ECL
6 require allowance 4 and provision 2. The rule does not create a negative
net loan of 2, nor does it write off the contractual claim of 4. The reporting
record preserves the combined estimate and the reason a separate attribution
cannot be made.

A revolving facility does not necessarily use its short cancellation notice
as its loss horizon. Under AASB9.5.5.20 and B5.5.39–40, some jointly managed
drawn/undrawn facilities expose the lender beyond that notice period because
normal risk-management actions do not prevent those losses. Admit the exposure
period from the facility's features, comparable historical exposure/default
experience and expected risk actions. A one-day cancellation clause and a
three-year assumed horizon are both insufficient alone. Missing evidence
leaves the horizon unresolved; the mechanical capacity rollforward does not
calibrate it.

## Fee purpose determines whether it enters yield

Under AASB9.B5.4.1–4, fees integral to origination or a probable specific
lending arrangement enter effective interest for instruments measured at
amortised cost. A fee's name does not settle its purpose. Servicing fees,
commitment fees when a specific loan is unlikely, and qualifying syndication
fees instead follow AASB15. Preserve the services promised, probability of a
specific loan, instrument classification, timing, recipient and any allocation
between distinct purposes. For instruments at fair value through profit or
loss, the source's treatment of integral fees differs; do not copy the
amortised-cost deferral.

Suppose a probable specific loan attracts commitment fee 2. Receive cash 2
and defer the fee; receipt alone is not service revenue. On origination,
advance contractual principal 100 and incorporate the deferred fee in the
loan's carrying amount, giving 98. A single contractual receipt of 107.8 one
year later gives effective interest

$$r=107.8/98-1=10\%,\qquad 98r=9.8.$$

Accrete 9.8 and collect 107.8. Net cash from the fee and loan is
$2-100+107.8=9.8$, equal to interest income. A supplied zero loss allowance
isolates fee timing in this example; fee receipt does not imply zero credit
risk. If the commitment instead expires without a loan, B5.4.2(b) recognises
the fee as revenue on expiry. This branch contains no loan carrying amount.

If a fee is outside effective interest, determine revenue under the separate
customer-service method. Calling it a service fee is not permission to
recognise it immediately. For a separately admitted two-period stand-ready
service with equal service each period and total consideration 2, the same
cash receipt releases revenue 1 per period from a contract liability. A
different obligation or satisfaction pattern changes that timing. The exact
services must be supplied before this branch can be applied.

## Selling a part requires identifying what was sold

AASB9.3.2.2 first decides whether the derecognition test applies to a part or
the entire asset. Eligible parts include specifically identified cash flows,
fully proportionate shares of all cash flows, and fully proportionate shares
of specified cash flows. The first or last collections from a pool do not
become a qualifying pro rata part merely because their nominal amount is 40%.
Then establish a transfer or qualifying pass-through arrangement and evaluate
risks and rewards. If neither substantially all risks and rewards are retained
nor transferred, evaluate control through the transferee's practical ability
to sell. The retained legal and cash-flow evidence must support each branch.

When an eligible part is derecognised, 3.2.13–14 allocates the old carrying
amount by the relative fair values of the transferred and retained parts at
the transfer date. Retained servicing rights enter that analysis; inadequate
servicing compensation can require a servicing liability. A nominal principal
ratio is not a substitute for relative fair value.

Assume an eligible identified cash-flow part, completed transfer and admitted
derecognition. The larger asset carries 100; transferred and retained parts
have supplied fair values 45 and 55, and cash consideration is 46. Allocate
carrying amounts 45 and 55. Debit cash 46, credit the asset 45 and recognise
gain 1. The retained asset carries 55; net assets rise from 100 to 101.
If the sold principal fraction happened to be 40%, using that fraction would
report retained carrying value 60 and gain 6, despite the supplied relative
values. This example excludes servicing, fees, tax and prior OCI and assumes
no separate allowance at the measurement instant; those features need their
own retained allocations, not silent deletion from a real transfer.

If substantially all risks and rewards are retained, 3.2.15 instead keeps
the entire asset and recognises a liability for the proceeds. Receiving 46
then gives cash 46, unchanged loan 100 and liability 46, with no sale gain.
The same cash receipt can therefore have a different balance sheet. Subsequent
asset income and liability expense are recognised separately, not offset.

## Continuing involvement preserves the guarantee exposure

Where neither substantially all risks nor rewards are transferred or retained
and control remains, 3.2.16–22 requires continued recognition to the extent
of involvement and an associated liability. A guarantee case follows
B3.2.13(a): the retained asset is the lower of its carrying amount and the
maximum consideration that could be repaid; the initial associated liability
is that guarantee amount plus the fair value of the guarantee. Subsequent
loss allowance reduces the retained asset, while the initial guarantee fair
value enters profit as the obligation is satisfied under AASB15 principles.
Options and partial retained interests require their own measurement branches;
the guarantee formula is not a general transfer formula.

Admit a transferred asset carrying 100, cash received 103 including fair value
3 for a guarantee, maximum repayment 20, and the required risk/reward/control
findings. Retain an asset of 20 and recognise associated liability 23. The
entry debits cash 103 and the retained asset 20, credits the old asset 100
and liability 23, and has no initial gain. Cash 103 plus asset 20 less
liability 23 preserves net assets of 100. Derecognising everything would
wrongly report gain 3 and omit the exposure.

At a later date, admit satisfaction of one third of the guarantee service and
loss allowance 2. Release guarantee income 1 by reducing the associated
liability to 22; recognise impairment expense 2 and allowance 2. Net assets
are $103+(20-2)-22=99$, matching net expense 1. If the guarantee then expires
with no claim and no remaining obligation, reverse that allowance 2 and
remove retained asset 20 against liability 22, recognising the remaining
guarantee income 2. Cash and net assets are 103 and cumulative net income 3.
An actual claim, changed valuation or unsatisfied obligation would require
its actual settlement analysis; expiry cannot be presumed from a convenient
reporting date. The continuing asset and liability are presented separately.
