The result has three distinct dispositions: completed views with no pending

retains every complementary child and performs no repeated work when resumed at

cap is separately externally bound. Completion does not follow from agreement
among the currently known outcomes.

The stored tree contains pending paths, checked empty terminals, checked nonempty

fixed depth-first prefix as well as coverage; a recomputed internal digest cannot
excuse missing siblings, duplicated paths, bad proofs, wrong source/trust/actor,
changed methods, forged counts, outcomes, supports or premature completion.

Post-run red-team: the47 baseline requests have small trees, so the result does
not establish that exponential growth is practical at larger revision counts.
The inherited six-revision scope and fixed traversal constrain the claim. The
proof checker shares the admitted input preparer and exact edge format with the
baseline, but reconstructs partitions and outcomes separately. Agreement of
whole trees is stronger than count agreement, yet still conditional on supplied
chronology, access routes and trusted observations. Checking an external expected
digest detects altered bytes relative to that expectation; it authenticates no
source and proves no human's knowledge. In-memory complete-child installation is
not a crash-safe storage protocol. General cap extensions, scheduling, approximate
compression and tag-induced temporal refinement remain separate work.
