## Selection passages and examples

Re-read retained EU 2023/1803 original IAS 33.33–47 and predecessor reading
of 49–57. Paragraphs 33–35 require consequential earnings/tax changes;
41–44 require the continuing-operations control number, prohibit antidilutive
conversion and consider individual series in increasing earnings per incremental
share order. Those duties must all survive a proposed generalization.

Read AASB 133 illustrative Example 9, printed pp. 28–30, in full. Its source
references paragraph 44 and 10/12/19/31–33/36/41–47/49–50. Options add 20,000
shares and no earnings; bonds add 3,000,000 earnings and 2,000,000 shares;
preference shares add 6,400,000 earnings and 1,600,000 shares. Fixed incremental
ratios are 0, 1.5 and 4. After options and bonds, continuing EPS is
13,000,000/4,020,000 = 650/201. Adding preferences would raise it to 970/281,
so preferences are excluded. Total EPS is 9,000,000/4,020,000 = 150/67.
This supplies a concrete sequential additive comparator. It contains no
threshold profit-sharing expense or state-dependent earnings increment.

Read Example 7, printed pp. 25–26, including its footnotes: period-end
conditions govern contingent shares; forecast earnings cannot replace observed
conditions; continuing operations can be positive while total earnings are
negative. Read Example 12, printed pp. 32–38, including all quarterly/annual
calculations and explanatory notes. It applies actual conversion dates,
time-weighting, the continuing-operations control number and fixed dividend/
interest additions. Neither example resolves the nonadditive ordering question.
Example 12 expressly excludes liability/equity classification; its called-bond
conversion is not support for S1's missing early-conversion measurement.

## Interpretation inspected and new consequence

IFRS Interpretations Committee, *IAS 33—Tax arising from payments on
participating equity instruments*, June 2017, complete one-page decision:
IAS 33.A14 requires allocation as if all period profit were distributed.
Ordinary earnings include the ordinary holders' share of a tax benefit caused
by that hypothetical distribution, regardless of whether the actual benefit
is recognized in profit or directly in equity.

The complete accompanying example, pp. 1–5, specifies a 10:1 participating-to-
ordinary ratio, a 30% deduction, and tax benefits accruing only to ordinary
holders. Profits before this benefit are 330, 550 and 110. Actual participating
dividends are 0, 300 and 600; ordinary dividends including the benefit are
0, 120 and 240. The final ordinary EPS numerators are 120, 200 and 40.
The negative residual allocation in year 3 reverses earlier hypothetical
allocations; it is not evidence that a dividend tax loss asset can always be
recognized. Page 2 footnote (a) says that different tax-benefit rights require
the relevant allocation ratio. It supplies no worked arbitrary capped/circular
tax-benefit allocation. Preserve that distinction.

This decision concerns **basic** EPS allocation. It neither admits the raw
minimum over conversion worlds nor specifies nonadditive dilution ordering.
It does expose a concrete internal F1 method obligation: the earlier supplied-
after-tax-earnings class allocation does not describe this distribution-induced
tax consequence or its reversal across reporting periods. That method can be
developed without solving nonadditive conversion selection.

## Candidate-rule disposition

For the inherited P80 example, basic EPS is 16/25; either individual conversion
gives 2/3; both together give 22/35. Fixed standalone ranking with actual world
recalculation and sequential marginal ranking both reject the first conversion.
The unconstrained subset minimum selects both. No inspected passage authorizes
overriding individual screening merely because that combined result is lower.
Calling a subset “admissible” without deriving its accounting admissibility
does not repair this problem.

For P90, individual worlds have earnings 84 and the combined world 92.
Freezing the two standalone earnings increments produces 96 and is **wrong
relative to the supplied bonus/tax contract**. Both sequential candidate rules
can recalculate 92 here. With only two issues this case does not distinguish
fixed ranking from repeated marginal ranking, and it cannot prove either
general rule. Selection remains `not_evaluated` in the admitted method.

The strongest alternative reading is that paragraph 44 already requires a
standalone ranking followed by recalculation of each accepted cumulative world.
The inspected examples support that procedure when increments are fixed;
they do not specify when to re-rank changing increments, revisit rejected
issues or handle tied, mutually consequential series. That missing argument
vetoes a general claim, not the conditional world calculations or further F1
work. A new explicit technical interpretation or a derivation resolving these
conditions would reopen this precise boundary.
