# Additional corporate methods and their measurement inputs

Ordinary company accounts can contain obligations that the teaching Northstar
case omits. These proposed methods preserve that breadth without assigning an
unsupported zero. They use the common EventBasis/authorization/journal boundary
and the retained EU2023/1803 provisions identified below. The original 2023 text
is historical inspected support, not a complete current amendment census or a
claim about any particular company's compliance. MC13, MC15–MC20, MC25–MC29
and MC35 apply. Numbers are synthetic, with sources supporting conditions and
mechanisms rather than the selected example assumptions.

## X1. Employee benefits

DefinedContributionState retains service periods, promised contribution formula,
contributions due/paid and applicable legal terms. For earned service requiring
contribution10, debit employee expense10/credit payable10; payment settles it.
Calling a plan defined contribution needs the limited-obligation facts; a
guaranteed benefit or additional funding obligation cannot disappear by name.

DefinedBenefitState retains employee/cohort service, benefit formula, projected
salary, retirement/turnover/mortality assumptions, discount-curve currency/term,
obligation, plan-asset fair value, asset ceiling, contributions, benefits and
remeasurements. IAS19.67–74 requires projected unit credit and the stated service
attribution; .83 supplies discount-rate requirements. Assumptions are separately
admitted measurements, not constants provided by the accounting rule. A one-employee
toy plan promises100 for each completed service year, paid after year two, with
certain completion, zero salary growth and supported10% discount. At year-one
end accrued benefit100 due in one year has PV1000/11. In year two that opening
obligation accretes100/11 and the second earned benefit adds100; obligation200
then settles200. Merely using current salary times years without timing would
give the wrong year-one obligation.

A separate funded-plan roll-forward supplies an independent statement example:
opening DBO100, plan assets80 and net liability20; current service12; interest5
on DBO, interest4 on assets at5%; contributions10 at year end; benefits8 paid
from plan at year end; actuarial obligation loss7; actual asset return6. DBO
closes100+12+5−8+7=116, assets80+6+10−8=88, net liability28. Expense is12+1=13,
OCI loss is7−(6−4)=5. Debit service expense12/credit net pension liability12;
debit interest expense1/credit liability1; debit OCI5/credit liability5;
debit liability10/credit cash10. End liability20+13+5−10=28. Plan-paid benefits
reduce both DBO and plan assets and do not cause a second employer cash payment.
IAS19.113, .120–127 supports these components; remeasurement OCI is not recycled
to profit under122. A surplus requires the asset ceiling and applicable minimum
funding analysis, not recognition of any calculated negative liability.

Unknown actuarial inputs give conditional obligations or measurement_unresolved.
Curtailment, settlement, amendment and multi-employer/insured arrangements need
their own fact conditions and measurement-date remeasurement. A buyout is not
settlement if obligations remain. Sensitivities vary one assumption only when
that conditional meaning is explicit; correlated risks require joint scenarios.

## X2. Impairment and provisions

ImpairmentState retains asset/CGU boundaries, carrying amounts, allocated goodwill,
corporate assets, recoverable-amount inputs and previous impairments/depreciation.
IAS36.9–10 determines indicators and mandatory annual tests. Recoverable amount
is the higher of value in use and fair value less disposal costs (.18); it is
not the lower of two pessimistic scenarios. VIU needs the supported cash flows,
forecast horizon/growth, discount and exclusion of inappropriate financing/tax
or uncommitted restructuring effects under33–57. Compute conditional values if
those estimates are unknown; do not infer them from historical profitability.

CGU assets excluding goodwill100 plus goodwill20, recoverable amount90: loss30
first removes goodwill20 and then reduces other assets10 subject to their floors
under104–105. Debit impairment30/credit goodwill20/credit asset allowance10.
If the asset floor is95 the requested reduction to90 is impermissible; apply
the remaining allocation/recognition conditions, not a forced plug. For a
standalone cost-model asset with carrying80, recoverable60, recognize20 loss.
At a later measurement before further depreciation, supported recovery to90
permits reversal only20 to the no-impairment80 ceiling (.117–119). Goodwill
impairment is not reversed (.124). Different dates require the hypothetical
unimpaired depreciation path, not the old nominal cost as cap.

ProvisionState retains obligating past event, present legal/constructive duty,
outflow assessment, estimate distribution, settlement dates, risk/discount basis,
reimbursements and evidence. IAS37.14 requires present obligation, probable
outflow and reliable estimate; .36–47 governs best estimate/risk/discount.
An intended future operating expenditure without that duty is not a provision.
Assume an established obligation to pay121 in two years, deterministic amount,
appropriate10% rate and no double-counted risk: recognize expense/provision100,
accrete interest10 then11, settle121. The passage-of-time increase is borrowing
cost under60. If the obligation is an eligible asset restoration cost, initial
debit follows that asset's method rather than always expense; later changes need
the applicable restoration interpretation. Contingent liabilities/disclosures
remain visible when recognition conditions are not met; no generic0.5 threshold
may substitute for the relevant standard's required analysis.

## X3. Share-based compensation

AwardState distinguishes equity versus cash settlement, grant/vesting/service
dates, granted units, grant-date fair value, service/nonmarket/market conditions,
expected units vesting, cumulative expense and changes/cancellations. IFRS2.7–11,
15–23 supports the employee equity-settled method. With100 units, grant value3,
two-year service condition and all expected to vest, year-one cumulative expense
150 and equity150. At vesting only80 satisfy the service condition: cumulative
240, second-year expense90. Debit employee expense and credit equity. Failure
of a market condition is different under21; if the employee satisfies other
vesting conditions, it does not automatically reverse the grant-date measured
service expense. Unknown conditions cannot be represented by a single forfeiture
percentage without their type and evidence.

Cash-settled awards remeasure the liability under30–33. Same100 expected units,
half service and current unit fair value4 gives liability/expense200 at year
one;80 actual units and value5 at settlement gives total400, second expense200,
then debit liability400/credit cash400. Reusing the equity grant value3 would
be wrong. Actual award valuation requires the specified model/market inputs,
option rights, volatility, dividends and calibration; the fair values3/4/5 are
case inputs. Modifications and settlement-choice arrangements require their
own conditions, with the original minimum expense and increment analyzed under
the applicable provisions rather than overwriting the original grant.

## X4. Fair value and capitalization boundaries

A ValuationRequest names the unit of account, market/date, asset/liability
characteristics, principal/accessible market, technique, input hierarchy,
counterparty/own non-performance risk where relevant, calibration and sensitivity.
IFRS13.24, .42–43 and .61–90 guide the selected measurement. Quoted identical
asset price7 for10 units gives70 in a qualifying Level1 case; a blockage discount
based only on holding size cannot reduce it to63 under69/80. A present-value
illustration111 due next year at an independently supported11% rate gives100;
that equality does not establish the rate, market comparability or accounting
classification. Historical EIR, an impairment discount, risk-neutral pricing
and a physical expected return answer different questions.

Borrowing-cost capitalization requires a qualifying asset, eligible costs,
commencement/suspension/cessation dates and the specific/general-borrowing
calculation, before moving interest into cost. IAS23.8–25 supplies these rules in
the retained historical EU text: for specific borrowing, eligible actual costs
less temporary investment income; for general borrowing, the applicable weighted
rate and cost ceiling. Commencement requires expenditures, borrowing costs and
necessary preparation activities; extended inactive periods can suspend it, and
substantial completion ends it. Assume specific borrowing100 outstanding during
a full eligible construction year at5%, with an admitted expenditure schedule
and temporary investment income1 on temporarily unused funds during that period.
Net capitalized cost is4. Real admission still needs the dated standard, actual
schedule and eligibility evidence. It is not a default5% add-on to all assets. Internally developed
intangibles likewise need research/development separation and all applicable
recognition criteria; a forecast of future sales does not establish an asset.

The extension interface returns the stated measured components, proposed journal,
economic delta, disclosures and unresolved inputs under M1–M4. Calculation
success does not permit another operation to fill a missing authority/fact with
a default. Real-company acquisition/tax/employee/valuation evidence and the full
exceptions remain explicit internal preparation, with proposed answers and
competing treatments retained for any later authorized domain review.
