17.26.2
An explicit interpretation selects a complete world
For a supplied same-event correction, the analyst can declare the corrected head, a fixed receipt
and a contractual-right family. This declaration supplies a hypothesis about the whole economic
history. The selected heads at both dates must lie in the corrected head’s receipt-only ancestry,
and the right family must remain unchanged. Retaining the superseded assertion as evidence
does not impose both 7 and 6 as simultaneous exact facts about one receipt; imposing those
incompatible facts would leave no world.
The binding then selects the complete financial rows for the declared world while retaining
the dated source pairs, their supports and any unresolved temporal remainder. Receipt 6 with
the committed right binds 980 action-region rows; receipt 7 binds 968. These are complete
action-region row families, not counts of feasible policies. Their populations agree with the
protected reference problems. Unknown interpretation, actual amendment, changed rights,
uncovered ancestry, conflict or retraction, inconsistent clocks and incomplete search receive no
financial row set.20
The corrected head may be available to the analyst who specifies the hypothesis without having
been available to the earlier manager. Neither that head’s identity nor the assumed world’s
identity is automatically an observable message. An adaptive policy still needs an explicit
account of the information available at each decision and must choose the same action whenever
that information is the same. The next comparison must therefore preserve the distinction
between the analyst’s economic hypothesis and the manager’s dated observations.
17.27
Dated observations and a common decision rule
The analyst’s receipt assumption does not tell us what the manager can observe. Two explicit
alternatives make the distinction testable. The first retains the original numeric observations:
the first coupon signal and the later trigger and cash-coverage signal. The second pairs each
numeric message with the receipt reported by the financial assertion selected at that date.
These are supplied information assumptions. A reported future receipt is neither cash already
received nor proof of the eventual amount.
For a correction arriving between decisions, the supplemented first message reports 7 and the
second reports 6, even when the analyst evaluates a complete receipt-6 world. Putting the later
correction into the first message would give the manager information too early. At the second
decision, the key still includes the first message and the manager’s first action. That memory is
needed even if the current numeric signal coincides with one reached after a different earlier
choice.
Several supplied source/world alternatives can then be evaluated together. The alternatives
share an actor, dates and action meanings. Their identities remain internal: equal visible
decision keys require the same action in every alternative. Finding a policy separately in each

17.27.1
An action assignment or a contradiction can settle existence
The two available actions give a smaller logical question than enumerating every rule. Associate
a Boolean variable xi with each complete decision key: 0 denotes pay and 1 denotes draw. A
financial row refused at the first decision forbids one assignment xi = a. A row refused at the
second decision forbids the joint choice of its first and second actions, xi = a and xj = b. A
rule avoids those refusals exactly when it satisfies, respectively,
xi ̸= a,
(xi ̸= a) ∨(xj ̸= b).
(17.40)
The second clause says that at least one of the actions leading to the refused row must change.
It is equivalent to the implications
(xi = a) ⇒(xj = 1 −b),
(xj = b) ⇒(xi = 1 −a).
(17.41)
A unary refusal similarly implies (xi = a) ⇒(xi = 1 −a). These clauses preserve the original
financial rows and observation keys. They change how existence is decided, without changing
which decisions are allowed.
A proposed total assignment is checked directly against every refused row. If none is reached
under that assignment, it supplies one common policy for the resolved financial family. It
does not enumerate other policies or choose an optimal one. Conversely, suppose implications
justified by refused rows lead from xi = 0 to xi = 1 and also from xi = 1 to xi = 0. Either value
contradicts a consequence of choosing it. The two paths therefore certify that no assignment
can avoid all those refusals. A graph calculation can discover the paths, but verification checks
each edge against its cited financial row.
In the between-date receipt-6 example, a three-edge contradiction has a direct decision interpre-
tation. At the first numeric message X paired with reported receipt 7, paying is refused, so
the rule must draw. After that action, a later message reports no trigger, inadequate coverage
and receipt 6. Paying is refused there, and drawing is also refused. The two second-stage
refusals make the required first draw impossible. This identifies incompatible requirements
in the supplied decision problem. It does not yet tell us whether a particular economic path
is impossible even with full information, or whether indistinguishable paths demand different
actions.
