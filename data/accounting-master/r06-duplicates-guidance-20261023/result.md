The note starts from XBRL2.1 duplicate-item identity and explicitly anticipates
future aspect-based OIM definitions. It excludes fractional facts. Complete
duplicates compare value-space values, numeric inferred decimals and effective
string language. Custom attributes and footnotes are expressly ignored by
that definition. This processing convention cannot justify deleting original
occurrences or their provenance from the proposed historical record.

Numeric consistency requires compatibility with one underlying value across
all intervals. The note uses closed intervals and additionally requires equal
reported values at the same accuracy. Its source example has intervals
[2450,2550] and [1500,2500], with shared range [2450,2500]. Its equal-accuracy
example rejects 2000 versus 3000 at decimals−3 despite their endpoint contact.
These are inspected source illustrations; no new arithmetic campaign ran.
The note's further assertion that closed intervals change the outcome only
when an infinitely precise exact value also appears remains attributed to the
source. Its derivation and relevant validity assumptions were not checked.

The retained OIM section 6.2.1 adopts the all-interval and same-accuracy rules
and cites this exact note. It also warns that consistency is not transitive.
Consequently, pairwise consistency with one representative does not justify
merging an entire class. Duplicate identity and value consistency remain
different relations. The diagram's complete-within-consistent drawing must
be read with the text's numeric domain: complete string facts do not thereby
become members of a numeric-only class.

The source-model boundary matters for languages. The note calls differing-
language string facts multi-language duplicates and says their equivalence
cannot be determined automatically. OIM treats language as a dimension and
calls dimensionally distinct language variants alternatives. The historical
terminology cannot be substituted for the later identity definition. Neither
text proves that two strings are translations of the same assertion.

For downstream processing, section 7.4 recommends retaining the highest-
accuracy consistent occurrence. Final corrected Calculations1.1 section5.5
instead requires the intersection of every duplicate interval. The completed
prior inquiry already preserves an exact witness distinguishing those
quantities; it was neither rerun nor re-labelled as a source conformance test.
Following the note's reduction is not enough to implement the final rule.

The outstanding normative questions remain unresolved. The note helps explain
the distinction between identity and consistency, but does not correct final
section5.4's reference to OIM duplicate facts where its surrounding discussion

Recommendation wording remain separate. No authority or local policy changed.
