## McCarthy (1982), REA

All 25 scanned pages, printed 554–578, were rendered and read. Resources,
events, agents, stock-flow, duality, control and responsibility appear on
printed 560–564; conceptual/global schema and local views on 556–558.
Double entry can be a derived external view (560). The ideal of indefinitely
disaggregated events is qualified (562, 572). Purchase/payment and vendor
integration occupy 565–567. Claims may be derived imbalances or stored base
entities (568–571). The model does not settle periodicity and measurement
choices (569–570). Equity requires ownership transactions, not merely the
balance-sheet residual (574–575). Implementation and complex validation
remain research questions in the paper (576).

Local conclusion: an economic event/contract representation with journal
views is a credible baseline. REA does not supply identification of a unique
history from reports, an observation likelihood or a simulation policy. This
is a scoped architectural comparison, not a performance comparison.

## Pflug and Pichler (2012)

Reuse the preceding dossier's reading of Definition 5.1, Theorem 6.1 and
proof (physical 6, 10–11). Conditional couplings preserve information. The
stability argument uses convex admissible decisions, convex cost in decisions
and a uniform Hölder condition in uncertainty. Conditional averaging and
Jensen cannot be transferred to an indivisible issuance decision without
another argument. The exact binary-action case rejects that transfer.

## Backhoff-Veraguas, Bartl, Beiglböck and Eder (2020)

Published Finance and Stochastics PDF, DOI 10.1007/s00780-020-00426-3,
physical 2–8, 19–23 and 27–29 inspected. Definitions 1.2–1.3, Theorem 1.5,
Lemma 3.9, Theorem 3.10 and the relevant proof establish scoped stability.
The semimartingale adapted metric is not literally the same expression as
every discrete nested distance. Weak transfer in Theorem 1.5 permits another
bounded predictable strategy; same-strategy transfer requires further
Lipschitz regularity and stronger distance/moment conditions. Remarks 3.11
and 5.1–5.3 give discontinuity/unbounded-strategy/superhedging limits.
No general hybrid-company or default-indicator theorem follows.

## Backhoff, Bartl, Beiglböck and Wiesel, adapted estimation

Retained arXiv 2002.07261; published identity DOI 10.1214/21-AAP1687.
Definitions 1.1–1.2, Theorems 1.3, 1.5, 1.7, Assumption 1.4 and Sections
5–7 inspected (physical 2–5, 13, 16–20). The setting is iid complete paths
on a fixed finite horizon and bounded state cube. Adapted empirical
quantisation pools histories; ordinary empirical paths can reveal the future
through a unique first observation (Example 7.1). Consistency does not need
Lipschitz kernels; quantitative rates do. Markov rates in Theorem 6.1 require
the process actually to be Markov; horizon still enters constants.
The main consistency proof and rate-proof assembly were read; not every
auxiliary lemma has been independently rederived. These rates cannot be
imported to one dependent issuer panel with hidden contracts.

## Chopin, Jacob and Papaspiliopoulos, SMC squared

Retained arXiv 1101.1528; methods physical 3–9, computational discussion
12–14 and Appendix A 24–25 inspected. Equation (7), Proposition 1/equation
(8) and Appendix A describe the extended target and its parameter marginal.
PMMH rejuvenation is in Section 3.2. Filtering arrays and complete
genealogies have different storage costs; this is not constant-cost exact

## Seaman, Galati, Jackson and Carlin (2013)

Electronic reprint, DOI 10.1214/13-STS415; physical 2–3 and 5–11 inspected,
including Theorems 1–3 and proofs, Section 5.4 and the appendix argument.
Realised MAR and everywhere MAR differ. Realised MAR plus parameter
distinctness gives likelihood factorisation/proportionality (Theorem 1).
The Bayesian result additionally uses prior independence (Theorem 2).
Repeated-sampling equivalence needs the corresponding everywhere condition;
observed versus expected information is qualified in 5.4. The conditions
are sufficient, not generally necessary. Equivalence under an assumed
missingness model does not establish that the model is true.

## Suijs (2007)

Publisher-layout article retained through Tilburg repository, DOI
10.1016/j.jacceco.2006.10.002. Model, equilibrium and discussion, physical
5–20, inspected with exact equation/proposition anchors; Appendix A derives
the four payoff cases for Proposition 2; Appendix B supplies multiple
partial-disclosure equilibria. The manager knows own return but not the
investor's private assessment of the outside opportunity. Equation (1)
updates the firm's probability of being relatively good; (2)–(3) compare
disclosure utility; (5) and Proposition 2 permit withholding high returns
under specified conditions. This is contrary evidence to imposing favorable
disclosure as a universal rule. The local simple cutoff example is a separate
derivation, not Suijs's model and not an inspected reproduction of Dye.

## Continuation: Hart–Moore scan completed

All 62 physical pages of the retained NBER3906 November1991 scan have now
been visually inspected: the earlier12 pages plus the remaining25 spreads
in hart-reading/. This is complete reading of this edition, not verification
of correspondence with the1994 journal article or independent reproof of every
comparative-static proposition.

Printed5–16 set deterministic returns/liquidation, zero interest, risk
neutrality, private savings outside seizure, single-creditor control, and an
exogenously specified offer game. Lemma1, equations(3.1)–(3.5), and Appendix
A1–A4 give the creditor outside value, repudiation constraints and cash
feasibility. The induction uses the offered second round and the restrictions
on return/liquidation timing; it is not an arbitrary recovery percentage.
Example1 (printed16) has two remaining returns2 and26 and liquidation rents8
and8: creditor value16, final credible payment13, hence wealth at least1 is
needed for continuation. With wealth below1 the project is liquidated despite
continuation value28 exceeding liquidation16. This is a reproducible reference
case, not a legal rule for distressed corporations.

Proposition1/equations(4.5)–(4.7), printed17–19, require both dated credible
repayment upper bounds and cash-feasibility lower bounds. Proposition2,
printed20–24, describes a set of repayment paths, not unique hidden contracts.
Printed27–28 explicitly distinguish long debt from rolling short debt: the
same path can require access to outside refinancing in the latter case.
The continuous-time limit (Proposition3, printed29–36, AppendixA5–A7) keeps
finite-horizon and monotonic timing assumptions. Proposition4 and Appendix
A7–A11 use controlled changes of durability, timing and bargaining; these are
not unrestricted cross-company comparative statics. Printed45–47 distinguish
asset replaceability from resale value, note the absence of an equity model,
and identify stochastic returns, consumption and multiple creditors as further
work. The paper's discussion is not empirical validation.

## Beiglböck–Pflügl–Schrott, retained2024 preprint

arXiv2406.19810v1, dated1July2024,22 pages; journal identity
10.1016/j.spa.2026.105032 is separately recorded. Inspected Definitions2.3,
2.6–2.8, Proposition3.6 and proof, Theorem3.9 and proof, Proposition3.13,
Theorems5.4/6.1 and proof assembly, Remark5.7, Remark6.2 and AppendixA.
The information process recursively includes conditional laws of its own
continuation. Markov lifts characterize equivalence; their existence supplies
neither a small finite sufficient statistic nor an identifiable one from
financial statements. Strategy transfer needs the stated stochastic-basis
conditions and a declared choice about independent randomization. AppendixA
constructs a counterexample to a broader historical amalgamation lemma using
nonstandard spaces; it does not invalidate the paper's discrete-time standard-
Borel transfer theorem. External dependency theorems are scoped, not silently
claimed independently rederived.

## Saldı–Yüksel, retained2025 kernel-topology preprint

arXiv2502.13486v2,37 pages. Inspected Definition4; Theorem4.1 counterexample;
Theorem4.2 and proof; Section5.1 Assumptions2–4, equation(11), Theorems5.1–5.2
and proof assembly; Remark12. Weak embedding convergence and strong norm
convergence differ. The square-wave example preserves a nonzero pointwise
kernel discrepancy while weakly converging. The discounted MDP application
uses bounded continuous costs, finite actions, weakly continuous kernels,
common domination and initial-law domination; the weak-to-strong route adds
bounded equicontinuous densities. This supplies a competitor for controlled
transition learning, not a theorem for discontinuous bankruptcy payoffs,
private multi-actor information or a changing feasible action set. The imported
Kara–Yüksel robustness theorem remains a dependency for any implementation.

## Kara–Yüksel2020: original kernel-control dependency

Retained arXiv1803.06046v7,1February2020, from the exact OpenAlex arXiv
location; DOI10.1137/18M1208058 independently matched. Read Assumption1.1,
Theorems4.2–4.4 with full displayed value-iteration/robustness arguments,
LemmaA.1, AppendixA.5, Remarks4.1–4.5 and Corollary5.1. The proof separates
finite value-iteration convergence from a uniform discounted tail. Policy
robustness requires optimality from all initial states; compactness yields
subsequential limiting actions satisfying the limiting Bellman equation.
A policy optimal only from the training initial state can behave arbitrarily
on states the approximate law never reaches. This is the relevant transfer
restriction, not evidence that all learned policies fail. Finite-state empirical
learning assumes every state/action pair is visited indefinitely. No such
coverage is established by company statements. General weak-convergence
lemmas and the entire partial-observation proof chain were not independently
reproved; source reading is scoped and journal correspondence remains open.

The printed Theorem4.4 proof calls a fixed measurable-policy operator a map
on C_b. Continuity of an arbitrary policy is not guaranteed by its assumptions.
The sup-norm contraction is naturally on bounded measurable functions; the
AppendixA.5 sequential argument supplies the required limit. No unqualified
assertion of continuous policy evaluation is imported.
