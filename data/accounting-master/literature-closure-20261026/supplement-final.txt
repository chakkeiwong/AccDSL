  Financial statements do not determine a company: learning,
       compression and financing in scenario simulation
                                 AccDSL research supplement

                                   26 October 2026
   Provisional technical synthesis; source and implementation limits stated locally


                                              Abstract
         Financial statements constrain the history of a company without identifying all its
     transactions, contractual exposures or future decisions. A simulator must preserve those
     distinctions, including differences that later information may never resolve. The computa-
     tional problem is therefore to share representations and calculations while retaining the
     information needed for future decisions. This supplement develops conditions for exact
     sharing, explains why approximate sharing needs separate policy and threshold arguments,
     and distinguishes hard evidence from probabilistic learning. It then connects debt proceeds,
     promised payments, recovery and shareholder dilution to dated financing choices. Primary
     work on accounting representations, adapted transport, sequential inference, disclosure and
     corporate finance supplies both useful methods and precise reasons that some tempting
     transfers fail. Exact synthetic cases and inspected author code support the local conclusions.
     They do not establish empirical forecast validity or completion of the general company
     simulator.

1    The company behind the reported numbers
A company can report the same debt balance under a fixed-rate loan and a floating-rate loan.
It can even have recorded the same historical interest payments if the floating rate has not yet
reset. A future rate shock separates their cash flows. Two different sequences of purchases
and disposals can also produce the same closing asset balance while leaving different assets
available for production or sale. The first example conceals contractual state beyond the
journal; the second conceals transactions within an accounting aggregate. Neither ambiguity
is removed by constructing one balanced journal.
    For a fixed linear reporting map, write reported amounts as y = Az, where z contains
transaction or account movements. If Av = 0, then z and z + v have the same reports.
They are both admissible only if they also satisfy the nonnegativity restrictions, dates, notes,
contractual conditions and accounting rules in the declared model. The null space alone is
not a generator of economically possible companies. More generally, let h contain opening
state, dated events, outstanding contracts, recognition and measurement choices, and source
support. With reporting map R and observed evidence e, define

     H(e) = {h : R(h) agrees with e and h satisfies the declared economic restrictions}.      (1)

Agreement includes units, rounding, dates and reporting perimeter. An assertion that a loan is
floating rate is as relevant as a reported numeric total. A missing note is recorded as missing
evidence; its contents are not inferred from the convenience of a candidate reconstruction.
    McCarthy’s REA model provides a useful architectural antecedent. Resources, events and
agents, linked through stock-flow, duality and control relations, organise economic activity
before particular accounting views are produced [1, pp. 560–570]. Double entry can be one
external view. Claims can be derived from imbalances or retained as base records when their
uses warrant it. The paper also recognises the practical need for aggregation and the fact that
measurement and periodicity are not settled by a database schema [1, pp. 568–576]. These
distinctions support an event-and-contract representation with journal views. They do not
imply that disclosures identify a unique event history, or supply the likelihood of observing a
disclosure.
    The proposed history family is therefore a family of hypothetical explanations, not multiple
legal books asserted to belong to the issuer. Revisions to actual reports form a separate version
history. Future behaviour is another separate choice: even complete historical contracts do
not identify how management will respond to a new liquidity shock. A run binds a history, a
behavioural model, an economic scenario and accounting rules, and retains that combination
in its results. Without defensible weights over histories, the result is a family of conditional
laws or feasible outcomes. Counting equivalent encodings of one history must not make that
history more probable.

2     Possibilities do not need individual tree nodes
Suppose twenty contractual clauses are unknown binary facts. The number of assignments
is 220 , but twenty variables and their joint restrictions need not require a million copies of
the company. Common events can be stored once; uncertain rates can remain symbolic;
alternative clauses can be guards on a shared calculation. This saves representation and
repeated computation. It does not guarantee that every query is easy: some queries genuinely
require exploring many combinations, and continuous uncertainty is not a finite list.
    A tree is useful when showing what is observed and decided over time. A stored node
may represent a region of feasible histories, with outgoing edges denoting observations or
economically meaningful events. A graph can share common prefixes and equivalent continua-
tions. Distinct provenance is retained even when execution is shared. The meaning of an edge
matters: logical dependence, source support and stochastic transition are different relations.
Drawing the same graph for them does not establish conditional independence.
    Consider a loan whose unknown fixed coupon is either 5 or 7 in each of two periods. The
admissible payment paths are (5, 5) and (7, 7). Taking the Cartesian product of the marginal
possibilities {5, 7} at each date adds (5, 7) and (7, 5). Those extra paths are wrong for this
contract. A change of terms requires a modification or refinancing event. The same error arises
if a robust dynamic program allows a new adversarial choice of an otherwise fixed contract
each period. A rectangular family can be appropriate for a different model, but it changes
this one. Dynamic consistency of preferences and factual persistence of a contract must be
assessed separately; the learning-under- ambiguity literature makes updating and restrictions
on priors substantive assumptions [5].

2.1    Exact sharing must preserve information as well as cash
Assume a fixed finite horizon and a transition model that is Markov in the augmented economic
state, including persistent terms and time. Let s be such a finite state and a an action available
to a specified actor. Write Ksa (B, o, z) for the joint probability that the next state lies in block
B, the actor observes o, and the requested current output is z. Two states can be placed in the
same block when they have the same feasible actions, the same already available information,
the same current outputs, and the same Ksa for every admitted action. Any terminal output
must also be constant on each terminal block. Persistent unweighted alternatives are kept as
separate types, with this comparison performed within the relevant conditional model rather
than by inventing a distribution over types.
    For a common policy that depends only on the preserved observation history, these
conditions preserve the joint law of future observations and outputs. To see why, begin at the
last date: the terminal output agrees within each block. One step earlier, the common policy
chooses the same action distribution at indistinguishable histories, and the joint transition into
successor block, observation and output agrees. Summing the common successor law gives the
same continuation law. Backward induction repeats that argument to the starting date. This
is a finite-state argument under the stated joint-kernel condition; it is not a general theorem
that equal balance sheets imply equivalent states.
    The observation-quotient work already present in AccDSL supplies exact finite path
comparisons and deliberately rejected partitions for this restriction. The present supplement
reuses that work. A query that adds another output, policy, horizon or observation can
invalidate a previously justified merge. An analyst may not know the loan type even though
management does; policies then use management’s information. Conversely, sampling next
year’s shock inside the simulator must not reveal it to management today.

2.2   A small example exposes the cost of lost information
Let tomorrow’s payoff driver Z be a fair binary variable. Today an actor chooses a ∈ {0, 1}
and receives 1{a = Z}. In one model, today’s signal reveals Z; in another, the signal is
constant. Both models have the same distribution of Z. The optimal expected payoff is 1 in
the revealing model and 1/2 in the uninformative model. A distance that compares only the
terminal distribution reports no discrepancy while the decision problem changes substantially.
The exact diagnostic enumerates every feasible policy in both models; it does not allow a
separate action for a hidden state.
    Nested and adapted transport distances address the temporal issue by restricting couplings
so that matching paths cannot depend on information that is unavailable at the relevant date.
Pflug and Pichler’s stability argument uses convex decision sets, convex loss in decisions and
uniform regularity in uncertainty [2, Definition 5.1, Theorem 6.1 and proof]. A projected
action is formed by conditional averaging. If the only permitted issuance sizes are 0 and 1,
an average of 1/2 is infeasible. The theorem therefore does not automatically justify merging
discrete issuance choices.
    Backhoff-Veraguas and coauthors obtain related stability results for financial optimization
with bounded predictable strategies and regular payoffs [3, Theorem 1.5; Lemma 3.7; Theo-
rem 3.10]. Their weak transfer permits a different strategy in the other model. Reusing the
same strategy needs additional regularity. Their semimartingale distance also has a different
cost functional from a simple sum of discrete path deviations; the paper explains why time
refinement matters. Its examples of discontinuous strategies and exact superhedging delimit
the guarantee. These are useful reasons to formulate the company decision problem precisely,
rather than reasons to attach one distance to every accounting query.


3     Approximation needs the error relevant to the decision
3.1   Thresholds require control near the boundary

decision date, Y its approximation under a specified joint coupling, and c the contractual
threshold. All three are in the same currency and date basis. A cash approximation of −ϵ
by +ϵ has error 2ϵ, yet changes 1{X < 0} from one to zero. Small cash error alone does not
imply small default-probability error.

3.3    Even sampling can accidentally reveal the future
With continuous first-period observations, independent sampled paths typically have distinct
first values. If each stored first value has only its single sampled continuation, that value becomes
an identifier for the sampled future. An optimizer can exploit this unintended information.
More paths do not by themselves repair the information structure of the empirical measure.
Backhoff and coauthors give an explicit optimal-stopping counterexample [4, Example 7.1].
Their adapted empirical measure quantizes observations so that paths share histories. Strong
consistency applies to iid complete paths on a fixed finite horizon and bounded state space;
quantitative rates additionally require Lipschitz conditional kernels [4, Definitions 1.1–1.2,
Theorems 1.3, 1.5, 1.7].
     A Markov modification improves the horizon exponent only when the process is in fact
Markov [4, Theorem 6.1]; constants still depend on the horizon. A company’s current balance
sheet generally is not a sufficient Markov state when hidden debt terms or asset vintages
matter. Nor are reports from one issuer an iid sample of complete company paths. The method
suggests how a deliberately generated simulation sample can retain information. Its rates
cannot simply be assigned to an observed issuer panel.


4     Learning without erasing persistent possibilities
Let θ denote fixed but unknown contract terms and Xt the evolving economic state. Their
roles differ. The transition for Xt can depend on θ, but absence of evidence does not authorize
resampling θ as a new physical contract. Management, lenders and the analyst can have different
observation histories Ita ; each action must be measurable with respect to the information of the

about that state.

The denominator must be positive and finite. Densities for exact continuous observations

4/(1 + 3p)2 is positive, so the posterior range is [4/7, 12/13]. These are derived bounds for this
specified likelihood, not confidence bounds inferred from financial reports. With no likelihood
justified, hard-compatible families and sensitivity analysis remain available.

The second expression selects type zero after signal zero and type one after signal one. It

type has physically changed along a realized path, yet dependence has changed across possible
paths. Preserving path support alone would miss the error.
    Shapiro defines rectangularity through equality between static worst-case evaluation and
its nested conditional counterpart [17, Definition 2.1, Theorem 2.1 and proof, Section 5].
His finite construction pastes conditional distributions across histories; it can enlarge the
family. A dynamic program for that enlarged family solves a changed robustness question. Its
convenience cannot justify claiming that it solved the original persistent-type problem.
    Terminology also requires care. Pichler and Shapiro call a product family a rectangular
setting, but explicitly show that its original worst-case functional and its composite conditional
functional can differ [18, Section 4.2, Example 4.7]. Computing a conditional law-based risk
measure can also differ from conditioning an unconditional ambiguity set. Take six equiprobable

states and an observed three-state group with losses 0, 1, 2. Conditional expected shortfall
at level 1/2 within that group is 5/3. The unconditional six-state risk envelope permits
probabilities at most 1/3 per state. It can assign 1/6 to the loss-2 state, zero to the other two
group members, and 5/18 to each state outside the group. This is an admissible probability
law; after conditioning on the group it assigns probability one to loss 2. The conditional worst
case over that envelope is therefore 2. The difference is between two precise conditional risk
definitions, not an arithmetic error [18, Example 3.2 and equations (3.1)–(3.5)].
    Policy consistency and equality of risk values are distinct as well. Xin and Goldberg give
moment-constrained inventory examples in which an initially optimal policy ceases to be
optimal after reoptimization, and another in which the optimal policy sets agree although the
two optimal values differ [19, Definitions 3.1–3.3, Observations 5–7, proofs in Sections 6.3–
6.5]. The company model must specify whether management can commit to a policy, what
information each later decision uses, and which family of joint laws is retained. For a finite
reference problem, enumerate complete admissible laws and observation-measurable policies
before choosing an approximation. For larger problems, a set of beliefs over persistent types or
an explicit precommitment formulation can retain those restrictions, but its recursive sufficiency
and computational cost need a separate derivation. Repeated worst-case optimization remains
useful as a stress test when the changed family is stated explicitly.

4.2   Sequential Monte Carlo is an approximation to a stated model
A particle approximation can reduce computation after a probability model is chosen. It
does not create that model. A type with probability 0.01 has probability 0.99100 > 1/3 of
disappearing from 100 independent multinomial resampling draws. No new evidence is involved
in this loss. Rare but material types therefore need explicit support checks, stratification or
separate conditional computations, with the resulting approximation stated.
    For state-space models with persistent unknown parameters, SMC2 combines parameter
particles and state filters, with particle-MCMC rejuvenation [6, Section 3.2, equation (7),
Proposition 1, Appendix A]. Its extended target has the desired parameter posterior as a
marginal under the source assumptions. Finite particles still approximate that posterior;
exactness of a target marginal does not mean that one finite run represents every possible type.
The unbiased full particle likelihood estimate used in this construction must be distinguished
from an arbitrary estimated likelihood ratio.
    The inspected author-linked modern implementation creates a state filter for each parameter
particle, runs that filter through the current observation date when evaluating a proposed
parameter, and incorporates a likelihood correction when increasing the state-particle count.
These operations have real time and memory costs. Retaining only filtering arrays differs from
retaining complete genealogies. This is a candidate for explicitly probabilistic filtering, not a
replacement for symbolic unweighted historical ambiguity.


5     Financing must connect cash, promises and choices
A financing transaction has three different descriptions. A contract creates rights and promises;
a market or bargaining model determines whether and on what terms it is available; accounting
rules determine recognition and later measurement. A posting that debits cash and credits debt
answers only the last description for an assumed executed transaction. It does not establish
that lenders would advance the required cash.

5.2   Prices depend on the policy that follows issuance
For longer-lived debt, lender receipts depend on future issuance, investment, priority changes
and default choices. A candidate policy π produces a price q π . If management optimizes while
holding an incompatible price fixed, the resulting policy may not be the one lenders priced. A
simultaneous solution must make the chosen policy optimal under its price and make the price
value that policy’s promised and default-state payments. A constrained contract-price input is
a useful alternative for mechanical stress testing, but its interpretation is conditional on that
price and availability.
    Benzoni and coauthors make this distinction explicit in a dynamic model with fixed issuance
costs [8, Section 2, Propositions 1–2, Appendix A]. Their debt quantity is face value, its price
is per unit of face, and shareholders choose default, issuance or inaction. Value matching and
smooth pasting generate a candidate barrier policy; off-policy deviations and a supermartingale
condition are required for equilibrium verification. The retained model assumes zero default
recovery and risk-neutral agents. It therefore illustrates the policy-price consistency problem,
but is not the positive-recovery solution required by the preceding example.
    The alternative managerial model of Lambrecht and Myers places investment, borrowing,
payout and rents in a dated sources-and-uses constraint and imposes a no-Ponzi restriction
[9, Sections 2–4]. Its safe-debt solutions depend strongly on managerial preferences and

                                                8
governance. In its exponential-utility example, the liquidation covenant protects lenders under
the stated diffusion dynamics. A jump across the boundary, delayed liquidation or costly asset
disposal would require a different recovery analysis. A closed-form policy derived under those
restrictions is a competitor to consider, not an identified default policy for every issuer.

5.3   Recovery, conversion and creditor disagreement
Suppose a liquidation estate has value A ≥ 0, senior promised payment S and junior promised
payment J. Under an expressly assumed strict priority contract with no liquidation costs or
other claims,
           RS = min(A, S),     RJ = min((A − RS )+ , J),     RE = (A − RS − RJ )+ .           (9)
At A = 70, S = 60, J = 50, creditors receive 60 and 10 and equity receives zero. Applying
min(A, J) independently to the junior claim would allocate the same estate twice. This
waterfall is a contractual illustration; actual priority, collateral, netting and insolvency costs
require the applicable facts and authority. Consolidation does not make assets of every
subsidiary available to every creditor.
    Conversion changes rights before a subsequent payoff is allocated. If junior debt of face 50
converts into 40% of residual equity, and the remaining enterprise value is 100 subject to senior
debt of 60, equity is worth 40. The converted holder receives value 16 and the prior equity
receives 24. The junior debt has been extinguished. Paying the old 50 promise as well as the
new shares would double count the conversion. A covenant trigger, an exercise decision and
the register update must be dated separately; a contract may specify a different conversion
ratio or priority.
    Recovery conservation does not settle bargaining. Consider an explicitly authored restruc-
turing example: liquidation yields 50 to a secured creditor and zero to an unsecured creditor;
continuation is worth 70 in total. An offer of 45 to the secured creditor and 25 to the unsecured
creditor has higher total value than liquidation but will be rejected by a secured creditor that
can obtain 50 by refusing. Whether the offer can proceed depends on voting, enforcement,
bargaining and financing rules, which this arithmetic does not supply. Thus a simulator must
not choose the maximum-total-value allocation and call it an implementable restructuring.
    Dewatripont, Legros and Matthews provide a primary contracting comparison [10, Sec-
tions 2–6 and Appendix B]. Their environment has observable but noncontractible effort,
limited liability, monotone investor receipts and efficient renegotiation. Under entrepreneur-
offer renegotiation, a debt contract can dominate a more general contract in the stated
pure-strategy class. Their appendix also supplies cases in which changed bargaining power,
third parties or mixed messages change that conclusion. Even their investor conversion option
need not be an ordinary equity security, as footnote 16 explains. Contract labels cannot replace
the exact contingent payments and exercise rights in the company model.

5.5    A rate shock changes both market access and ownership
An exact combined example shows why the financing steps must be dated. Two possible
companies each start with cash 16 and loan face 100. One pays a fixed coupon of 5; the other
pays 7 after the specified floating-rate reset. Each then makes an operating payment of 6.
A covenant tests cash of at least 5 immediately after these payments and before financing.
Breach forbids new borrowing but permits an equity cure. Management requires closing cash
8. These are assumed contract terms, not a claim about a standard covenant.
    The fixed-rate company has cash 16 − 5 − 6 = 5, meets the covenant and needs 3. A
candidate debt issue with maximum face 5, conditional solvency probability 4/5, recovery 2
and gross risk-free return 21/20 has proceeds 88/21. Holding that quoted per-face price fixed
for this conditional stress, issuing face 315/88 raises exactly 3. The floating-rate company has
cash 16 − 7 − 6 = 3, breaches the covenant and needs 5. Debt is unavailable; even an improper
borrowing override at the maximum face would raise only 88/21 < 5.
    Suppose the permitted equity cure has fee 1, pre-issue economic equity value 20 and 100
incumbent shares. A gross subscription of 6 provides net cash 5. The post-issue equity value is
25, new investors own 6/25, and the common share price is 19/100. Their 600/19 new shares
have value 6, while incumbents retain value 19. Both companies finish with cash 8, but their
new debt and ownership differ. Merging them on closing cash would conceal those differences.
Testing the covenant only after the cure would erase the earlier breach and wrongly admit the
borrowing alternative.
    This example holds quoted financing prices and the pre-issue equity valuation fixed to
expose event ordering and allocation. It does not solve the feedback from financing to default
and prices. A source correction introduces another interaction: withdrawing an unsupported
fixed-rate classification can reopen the floating-rate history, invalidating an earlier single-history
result. If management later receives a rate notice, its admissible policies can change even
when the analyst’s reported balances do not. Shared computations must therefore depend on
evidence version, actor information, contracts and the requested outcomes, as well as current
cash.


6     What is disclosed is part of the observation model
Imagine equal prior probabilities of a good and bad type. If good types disclose with probability
0.9 and bad types with probability 0.1, then disclosure raises the probability of a good type
to 0.9, while silence reduces it to 0.1. Treating a missing disclosure as no information would
retain 0.5 in both cases. The discrepancy comes from omitting the selection mechanism, not
from numerical approximation. These probabilities are an authored example, not estimates of
actual issuer disclosure behaviour.
    Let Y denote potentially observable information, M the pattern of observed components,
fθ (Y ) its model and gϕ (M | Y ) the publication or missingness mechanism. The likelihood of

6.1    A simple equilibrium of silence
A separate local illustration makes non-disclosure endogenous. Let value V be uniform on [0, 1].
A manager knows V with probability 1/2, independently of value; otherwise the manager
cannot disclose it. Informed disclosure is truthful and costless, the manager maximizes the
contemporaneous price, and risk-neutral investors price at conditional expected value. Suppose
an informed manager discloses above cutoff a. Silence occurs with probability (1 + a)/2. Its
unconditional value contribution is 1/4 + a2 /4, giving silence price

                                                        1 + a2
                                      Psilence (a) =            .                              (12)
                                                       2(1 + a)

An informed manager at the cutoff is indifferent, so√a = Psilence (a). Multiplying through and
collecting terms gives a2 + 2a − 1 = 0, hence a = 2 − 1 ≃ 0.4142. Types above that value
disclose and those below it prefer the silence price. The uninformed manager explains why
silence does not identify the worst type. This derivation is a local example; the blocked Dye
original is not claimed to have been reproduced.
    Nor is favorable selection universal. Suijs models a firm that knows its return but is uncertain
how investors compare it with an outside opportunity [12, Sections 3–4, Proposition 2 and
Appendices A–B]. Under the stated conditions a profitable firm can disclose lower returns and
withhold higher returns. Multiple partial-disclosure equilibria are possible. The implication for
AccDSL is to represent alternative disclosure mechanisms and test their consequences, rather
than hard-code one direction of selection.

6.2    Mechanical shocks, conditioning and intervention
A mechanical rate stress replaces an input while holding specified other relations fixed. A
conditional forecast computes outcomes among states where an observed rate has a chosen
value. A causal intervention changes the mechanism generating the rate and then propagates
the remaining structural relations. These calculations can disagree.
    For a finite counterexample, let U be a fair binary variable and observe X = U , Y = U .
An alternative structural model has X = U , Y = X. Both give exactly the same observed joint
distribution. Under an intervention setting X = 1, the first model retains E[Y ] = 1/2 while the
second gives E[Y ] = 1. Observational agreement does not identify the intervention response.
A forecast labelled as causal therefore needs the structural assumptions that distinguish these
possibilities, or must retain the set of responses. Source corrections and simulated observations
also have different time meanings: a hypothetical future disclosure updates an actor inside
that run, not the analyst’s actual present evidence.

7.1   What a contract engine establishes
A contract engine can generate scheduled events and payoffs from supplied terms, calendars
and risk observations. It need not determine which financing is available, choose management’s
action, infer undisclosed terms or price endogenous default. The retained ACTUS specification
identifies itself as v1.1, revision 843f7a3, dated 8 June 2020, aligned with dictionary v1.2 [13].
That historical pairing is more precise than treating a current website download as the latest
compatible runtime.
    Independent arithmetic on the retained official PAM examples checks two simple sched-
ules. A principal of 3000 at 10% over 31 actual days with A365 pays 3000(0.1)(31/365) =
25.479452 . . .. The discounted-issue A360 case advances 2800 against face 3000 and pays
3000(0.1)(59/360) on its first two-month interest date. Initial interest of zero and interest-
before-maturity ordering are retained from the explicit schedules. These calculations match 24
listed events within the stated decimal-serialization tolerance.
    This does not execute ACTUS. The inspected service requires ACTUS core version 1.1.0;
its Dockerfile copies and builds a local core directory. The attempted public core repository
and Maven lookup did not yield the matching implementation. A historically matched
dictionary and specification therefore remain insufficient to claim runtime conformance. Rate
resets, business-day adjustment, missing observations and other contract types need their own
implementation traces and comparisons before adoption.

7.2   The equation being minimized matters
Suppose an economic equation is E[r(θ, ϵ) | s] = 0. The squared conditional mean and
conditional mean square are different:
                              E[r2 | s] = {E[r | s]}2 + Var(r | s).                          (13)
The variance can depend on the policy parameters. In the exact example r = θ − 1 + θϵ, with
independent fair ϵ ∈ {−1, 1}, the intended loss is (θ − 1)2 , minimized at 1. The mean square
is (θ − 1)2 + θ2 , minimized at 1/2. Training can successfully optimize the wrong quantity.
    Conditionally independent draws ϵa , ϵb give
                            E[r(θ, ϵa )r(θ, ϵb ) | s] = {E[r(θ, ϵ) | s]}2 .                  (14)
Conditional independence, the conditioning state and differentiation assumptions matter.
Reusing the same draw returns the mean square. A finite product estimate can be negative
even though its expectation is nonnegative. Maliar, Maliar and Winant discuss solving economic
equations with deep learning [14]; the inspected author-linked Krusell–Smith notebook uses the
product of residuals from two future draws. That code is evidence for its equation construction,
not evidence that it solves a risky-debt issuance and recovery system.
    Gradients require the same care. If F (θ, q) = θq and q(θ) = θ, the total derivative is 2θ.
Differentiating with q detached gives θ. A deliberate alternating-price algorithm may use the
latter as one step, but it is wrong if described as the total gradient of the coupled objective.
Boundary default decisions and discrete issuance choices add feasibility and nonsmoothness
issues beyond this example.
    The notebook also contains differences between explanatory markdown and executed
parameter values and initialization, along with clipping and cross-sectional normalization of
shocks. Those change the problem being computed and must be included in a reproduction.
The separate audit retains exact cells and hashes. No neural training has been promoted here.
The Duarte paper’s announced replication library has not been matched to an author-linked
implementation in the inspected sources [15].

8    What would establish an estimated company policy?
Historical fit is only one restriction on a model. If two policies take the same action on
every observed state but diverge after refinancing is refused, the historical sample cannot
distinguish their response to that refusal. The observation model can create another equivalence:
infrequent disclosure of losses can resemble a lower loss frequency. A source-to-parameter map
should therefore identify what is measured, what is inferred under a model, and what remains
set-identified or a sensitivity assumption.
    A credible evaluation uses information as it was available at each forecast origin. Later
restatements and later-known covenant breaches remain unavailable to earlier forecasts. Issuer
exit and default must be retained rather than dropping failed companies from the comparison.
The benchmark ladder should include a naive persistence/accounting baseline, a tuned classical
policy or state-space model, the plain proposed model and any enhanced version, using the
same information, feasible actions and evaluation periods.
    The primary criterion must match the proposed use. For a probabilistic forecast of default,
the Brier loss (q − D)2 has expectation p(1 − q)2 + (1 − p)q 2 = p(1 − p) + (q − p)2 , uniquely
minimized by q = p. This identity is useful but does not validate the estimated p. Proper
scoring rules assess distributional forecasts under specified outcomes [16]; unweighted sets
require coverage and sharpness questions appropriate to sets, not arbitrary probabilities added
to permit scoring. Decisions additionally need realized cost or regret evaluation under a
defensible comparison.
    Repeated forecasts from one issuer and common macroeconomic shocks are dependent.
Paired comparisons, issuer/time blocking and an uncertainty method appropriate to that
dependence must be specified before ranking methods. Rare defaults may make precise tail
comparisons impossible in the available panel. Such results remain descriptive. Conservation
and feasibility errors can veto a candidate regardless of its predictive score; a good score does
not repair a wrong accounting or financing quantity.


9    A method with explicit limits
The resulting design retains joint economic histories, dated evidence and actor-specific infor-
mation. It uses symbolic constraints and shared records before approximation; exact merging
requires equality of the relevant continuation law. Where approximation is needed, cash,
threshold events and policy regret receive different error arguments. Probabilistic filters oper-
ate only after prior, transition and observation models have been stated. Financing connects
proceeds to contingent creditor payments, management decisions and ownership changes before
generating the corresponding journal.
    Several residuals are substantive. The historical planning sources still need source-specific
access and edition comparison. The full ACTUS runtime has not been matched. General
creditor bargaining, broad hybrid-state approximation and empirical issuer-policy identifica-
tion remain open. The acquired follow-up literature also contains work awaiting technical
inspection. These limitations prevent an all-gap closure claim. They do not erase the exact
corrections, source-supported restrictions and counterexamples that can already govern the
next implementation.
